# Schema exceptions: reproduction, seed and propagation

Part of the schema exceptions log. Rules, statuses and the full index are in
[`../schema-exceptions.md`](../schema-exceptions.md).

Entries marked "imported" came from earlier private notes kept before this log existed. Their **Seen in**
lists name crops, not checked Glinda docs: the facts have not yet been re-checked against the crop's
Glinda doc, and some of those crops have no Glinda doc yet. When a research run confirms one, replace the
plain name with the cropID, doc and Finding.

## 3 — Plant sex that has to be managed at planting

- **Status:** OPEN (logged 2026-11-24; imported crops added the same day).
- **What doesn't fit:** "Pollination" covers whether pollination is needed and how. It has no place for
  a crop whose plants come in several sexes that cannot be told apart at planting, so growers must
  over-plant and thin to the wanted sex, with the seed's sex ratio setting the stand.
- **Seen in:**
  - `papaya` (`papaya.md`, Finding 1): selfed Solo seed gives 2 hermaphrodites : 1 female; Hawaii plants
    3 seedlings per hole (5 for 'Rainbow', which splits 1:1) and thins to one hermaphrodite. Heat causes
    female sterility in hermaphrodites.
  - Imported: kiwifruit and date palm (dioecious; males are a non-bearing share of the stand); rambutan,
    pulasan; asparagus (male plants yield more; all-male hybrids; female plants seed volunteers); coontie;
    mountain papaya (three sexes); American persimmon (a plant's sex expression can change from year to
    year); Texas persimmon (wild stands about 9-10 males per female); nutmeg (dioecy forces vegetative
    propagation) vs bay leaf (dioecious, but grown for leaf, so it doesn't); allspice (dioecy disputed).
- **Possible home:** a "Sex system" bullet (monoecious, dioecious, polygamous; sex ratio from seed;
  how growers get the wanted sex: thinning, clones, sexed seed; whether it matters for the harvested
  product), next to "Pollination".

## 4 — Propagation material restricted by licence or intellectual property

- **Status:** OPEN (logged 2026-11-24).
- **What doesn't fit:** the "Invasiveness / biosecurity" bullet's "legal restriction on cultivation,
  import, or movement" is about biosecurity. It has no place for restrictions that come from licences,
  patents or plant-variety protection: who may plant the material, where, where the produce may be sold,
  and through which suppliers.
- **Seen in:**
  - `papaya` (`papaya.md`, Finding 4): GM papaya may be planted only in Hawaii, with seed bought only
    from the growers' committee, and fruit sold only to countries that accept GM papaya.
  - `peach` (`peach.md`, Finding 6): 'Guardian' rootstock is under US Plant Variety Protection
    No. 9400013 and sold only through licensed nurseries.
  - `peach_low_chill` (`peach-low-chill.md`, Agronomic data, Propagation): most current UF scion
    cultivars are patented ('UFBest', 'Flordabest', 'Gulfcrimson', 'UFO', 'UFSharp'), while older ones
    ('TropicBeauty', 'Flordaking', 'Flordadawn') are not.
- **Possible home:** a "Licensing / IP" line under "Propagation", separate from biosecurity rules.
  Other legal limits on the product or the harvest are entry 64.

## 17 — Planted pollinizers that are never harvested, and pollen as an input

- **Status:** OPEN (imported 2026-11-24; first Glinda doc added the same day).
- **What doesn't fit:** "Pollination" says whether a pollinizer is needed. It doesn't record that part
  of the planted area bears nothing saleable, or that pollen or pollinating figs are bought, harvested or
  applied by hand as a farm input.
- **Seen in:**
  - `pear` (`pear.md`, Finding 2): a counter-case. Pollinizers are a third of the area or 25-33% of
    trees, but their fruit is harvested and sold; bee hives are budgeted at $114/acre.
  - `yellow_passionfruit` (`passionfruit.md`, Finding 2 and Pollination): the self-sterile yellow form is
    hand-pollinated where carpenter bees are scarce; in the Huila budget hand pollination is 21% of
    labour; growers set out decaying logs to shelter the carpenter bees.
  - Imported: Japanese plum (about 11% pollinizer trees, never harvested); kiwifruit (male vines);
    seedless watermelon (up to 30% pollinizer plants, some non-fruiting); date palm (2-4% males; pollen
    collected and hand-applied, 15-20 g per female; the pollen parent shifts ripening by up to two
    weeks); fig (caprifigs carrying the fig wasp, for Smyrna types); muscadine (female cultivars, 3:1
    within 50 ft); American persimmon (one male per 23 or more females, of the same chromosome race).
- **Possible home:** a "Pollinizer share" line under "Pollination" (non-bearing fraction, layout,
  bought or hand-applied pollen).

## 18 — Pollination that lowers quality or changes the product

- **Status:** OPEN (imported 2026-11-24).
- **What doesn't fit:** "Pollination" assumes pollination is wanted. In some crops it adds unwanted
  seeds or changes what the product is.
- **Seen in (imported):** clementine and mandarin (cross-pollination makes fruit seedy); pomelo;
  parthenocarpic cucumber (seedy if pollinated); pineapple (seeds from hummingbird pollination, which is
  why hummingbirds are banned in Hawaii); Asian persimmon (pollination-variant cultivars lose astringency
  only when seeded).
- **Possible home:** a "Pollination effect on product" line under "Pollination", including isolation
  from pollinators.

## 19 — Seed that comes true to type, or seed that won't

- **Status:** OPEN (imported 2026-11-24; first Glinda doc added the same day).
- **What doesn't fit:** "Propagation" lists seed and vegetative routes. It doesn't say whether seed
  reproduces the parent (apomixis, nucellar embryos) or, the reverse, whether seed-grown plants lose the
  parent's chemistry so that clones are required.
- **Seen in:**
  - `peach_low_chill` (`peach-low-chill.md`, Agronomic data, Propagation): 'Flordaguard' rootstock seed
    doesn't reliably come true; about 6% of seedlings are outcrosses that lose nematode resistance and
    are culled by their lack of red leaves, so seed blocks are isolated and cuttings are taken only from
    grafted trees.
  - `pear_low_chill` (`pear-low-chill.md`, Propagation): pears do not come true from seed, so cultivars
    are sold budded or grafted onto Callery or *P. betulifolia* seedlings.
  - Imported: mangosteen and porang (apomictic); mango (polyembryonic cultivar groups come true, others
    don't); Key lime and citrus rootstocks (nucellar seed); oregano (seedlings don't keep the parent's
    chemotype).
- **Possible home:** a "True to type from seed?" line under "Propagation".

## 20 — Seed storage behaviour outside the normal range

- **Status:** OPEN (imported 2026-11-24).
- **What doesn't fit:** no bullet holds seed viability or storage class. Some seed dies when dried or
  within days; some lasts centuries.
- **Seen in (imported):** wild rice, avocado, mangosteen, breadfruit (recalcitrant; must stay moist);
  lychee (viable 4-5 days); parsnip (one-year seed life); lotus (seed viable for centuries); edible canna
  (very hard, long-lived seed, claim hedged); chuin (pods smoked over the hearth or sealed with camphor and
  ash; pods picked by the lunar calendar against a seed beetle).
- **Possible home:** a "Seed storage" line (orthodox, intermediate or recalcitrant; viability; farm
  storage practice).

## 21 — Seed dormancy and pre-sowing treatment

- **Status:** OPEN (imported 2026-11-24).
- **What doesn't fit:** nothing records a seed treatment needed before sowing (stratification,
  scarification, cleaning) or conditions that cause dormancy.
- **Seen in (imported):** American persimmon (2-3 months at 1-4 °C); date-plum (30-90 days); sweet cherry
  seedling stocks (90-180 days); Texas persimmon (no dormancy, but cold storage *induces* it; fruit pulp
  inhibits germination); swiss chard (dormant in seedbeds above 35 °C); velvet bean (hard seed coat);
  wasabi (winter cold breaks seed dormancy); carrot (seed debearding).
- **Possible home:** a "Seed treatment" line under "Establishment".

## 22 — Seed unit or seed size that sets labour

- **Status:** OPEN (imported 2026-11-24).
- **What doesn't fit:** "Establishment" takes spacing and method. It doesn't record that the seed unit
  itself (a multi-seeded cluster, or very tiny seed) forces thinning or special sowing.
- **Seen in (imported):** sugar beet, table beet, swiss chard (multigerm seed balls need bunching and hand
  thinning; about 109 h/ha in an early-1900s record; monogerm seed cuts that to about 5%); fonio, celery,
  watercress, carrot (tiny seed, about 0.8 mg for carrot).
- **Possible home:** a "Seed unit" line under "Establishment".

## 23 — Seed-production systems and isolation between rows

- **Status:** OPEN (imported 2026-11-24).
- **What doesn't fit:** "Pollination" has room for a cultivar-compatibility wrinkle. It has no place for
  the seed system (cytoplasmic male sterility, F1-only, gynoecious lines, self-incompatibility used for
  hybrids), for isolation from *other crop rows* of the same species (sometimes in another category), for
  crossing with a wild weed, or for a crop that is a donor of a hybrid system used in other crops.
- **Seen in (imported):** *Beta vulgaris* rows (sugar beet, table beet, swiss chard; isolation about
  3 km); *Brassica oleracea* rows; all *B. rapa* rows (turnip, bok choy, napa cabbage, mizuna, komatsuna,
  choy sum, rapini) cross with each other; *B. juncea* mustards (CMS hybrids dominate); radish (its Ogura
  cytoplasm is the source of a CMS system used in rapeseed); Florence fennel (a CMS donor for parsley);
  *Capsicum* species; cucumber and bitter melon (gynoecious F1); endive and chicory (one-way crossing);
  baby broccoli (F1 only, no seed-saving route); parsley and Hamburg parsley (same species in two
  categories); carrot (crosses with Queen Anne's lace); parsnip (with wild parsnip); salsify (wild crosses
  formed a new species); potato (hybrid true seed of a normally clonal crop); brown mustard (certified
  isolation 50 m, foundation 100 m) vs yellow mustard (self-incompatible).
- **Possible home:** a "Seed production" bullet: breeding system, hybrid system, isolation distance and
  which other rows or wild plants it crosses with.

## 24 — Planting stock with its own storage, dormancy and multiplication

- **Status:** OPEN (imported 2026-11-24; first Glinda doc added the same day).
- **What doesn't fit:** "Propagation" names the route, and "Storage" covers the harvested product. For
  vegetatively propagated crops the planting stock has its own storage regime, shelf life, dormancy and
  multiplication rate, and it can take up a large share of the land.
- **Seen in:**
  - `peach_low_chill` (`peach-low-chill.md`, Agronomic data, Propagation): 'Flordaguard' seed fruit must
    be picked before they drop because heat damages the embryo; seed is stratified; about 6% outcrosses
    and a further 10% runts are culled.
  - Imported: seed potato (stored at 3.3 °C, browning at 0 °C); Andean potato (no tuber dormancy;
    diffused-light seed stores give short sturdy sprouts and 15-30% more yield); yam (dormancy length by
    species); cassava (stems keep about two weeks, upright in shade); cocoyam (seed cormels buried in
    sand); horseradish (sets kept in moist sand); porang (16 h/day light for about a month breaks
    dormancy); konjac (most of the planted area grows planting stock: 3,230 ha planted vs 1,950 ha
    harvested for sale; 3.3-6 times enlargement per season; corm shape sets machine planting); elephant
    foot yam (multiplication 1:4, raised to 1:15 by minisetts); sweet potato (slips raised from stored
    roots).
- **Possible home:** a "Planting stock" sub-section under "Propagation" (storage, dormancy, shelf life,
  multiplication ratio, share of area).

## 25 — Crop grown mainly as rootstock or planting material for another crop

- **Status:** OPEN (imported 2026-11-24; first Glinda doc added the same day).
- **What doesn't fit:** "Propagation" covers this crop's own rootstocks. Some crops are grown, or matter
  most, as the rootstock or propagation source for a *different* crop.
- **Seen in:**
  - `peach_low_chill` (`peach-low-chill.md`, Finding 6): 'Flordaguard' is a peach grown for rootstock
    seed, recommended for low-chill peach, nectarine and plum.
  - `pear_low_chill` (`pear-low-chill.md`, Finding 5): the standard stock is *P. calleryana*, grown from
    seed; it is not a crop row in this project, and is now an invasive whose sale is banned in several
    states.
  - `quince` (`quince.md`, Finding 1 and Propagation): grown in dedicated stool beds as the classic
    dwarfing rootstock for pear and also used for medlar; a long-standing storage reference calls
    rootstock use the crop's main commercial purpose.
  - Imported: date-plum (rootstock seed for Asian persimmon); golden
    currant; sour orange (classic citrus stock; tristeza kills sweet orange on it); cherry plum; squash
    as a graft stock for oriental melon.
- **Possible home:** a "Role as rootstock for other crops" line under "Propagation".

## 26 — Graft compatibility rules

- **Status:** OPEN (imported 2026-11-24; first Glinda doc added the same day).
- **What doesn't fit:** "Propagation" mentions rootstocks but has no way to record which stock and scion
  pairs fail, failures that appear only years later, bridges, or crops that in practice can't be grafted.
- **Seen in:**
  - `pear` (`pear.md`, Finding 6): quince is the most widespread stock but its compatibility "is not good
    for all cultivars"; the imported 8-10-year delay and interstem bridge were not re-confirmed this pass
    (request `pear--quince-incompatibility`).
  - `quince` (`quince.md`, Finding 1 and Propagation): the stock side of the same incompatibility — pear
    scions 'Bartlett'/'Williams' and 'Bosc' are incompatible on quince and need a 'Beurré Hardy' or
    'Doyenné du Comice' interstem bridge; pear rootstocks in turn perform poorly on quince.
  - Imported: Asian persimmon (date-plum stock fails with some cultivars; graft take 44-45% vs 70%);
    Texas persimmon (incompatible with the rest of the genus); wet-tropics mangoes (incompatible with
    mango); pomegranate (grafting impractical); sweet cherry on Mahaleb (failure after about 6 years);
    Eureka lemon on trifoliate orange.
- **Possible home:** a "Graft compatibility" line under "Propagation".

## 27 — Rows separated by a single gene or a bud sport

- **Status:** OPEN (imported 2026-11-24).
- **What doesn't fit:** the cultivar-row gate check decides whether to split a row. Nothing records that
  two rows differ by one allele, so that seedlings segregate between them or a branch can sport from one
  row to the other.
- **Seen in (imported):** nectarine (peach with a recessive allele; peach branches sport to nectarine).
- **Possible home:** a note under "Cultivars" on the genetic link to the sibling row.
