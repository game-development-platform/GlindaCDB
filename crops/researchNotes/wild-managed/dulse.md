## Why it belongs in this category

- **Supply is wild-picked.** Commercial dulse is hand-picked from the
  foreshore, most famously on Grand Manan in the Bay of Fundy, where it has
  been harvested for more than a century [3].
- **Farming is only a partial alternative.** Rope culture at sea has
  worked but yields only about 0.3–0.9 kg per metre per season, and a 2011
  review judged it not feasible with then-current techniques [5]. Land-based
  tanks work (Scotland, Germany) [5], and the related Pacific dulse is
  tank-farmed commercially in Oregon [2]. So it is a mixed case: wild-managed
  today, with a tank route that could later move it to farmed.

## Fields a wild-managed template would need, with dulse values

- **Harvest rights / tenure:**
  - Ireland: a Foreshore Act 1933 licence for state foreshore. Under the
    2021 Maritime Planning Act every wild harvester must hold a Marine
    Usage Licence by July 2028; new applications are paused until 2027 [3].
  - Maine: personal picking up to about 22.7 kg a day (converted from
    50 lb); commercial picking needs a Marine Harvesting Licence for
    Seaweed [3].
  - Scotland: hand-picking needs only the landowner's permission [3].
  - Canada: a Fisheries and Oceans licence [3].
- **Season window:** Late spring to about November [1][3]. Growth is in
  winter and spring, with no or negative growth in summer and autumn [6].
- **Harvest method:** Hand-picking at low tide, timed to tides of up to
  5 m on Grand Manan [3].
- **Harvest rules / good practice (Maine guidance):** pick only attached
  plants, never drift; leave holdfasts, immature plants and older thick
  fertile plants; leave at least 25% of the biomass [3].
- **Workforce:** About 100 pickers on Grand Manan in 1973 [3]. No
  hours-per-tonne figure was found.
- **Post-harvest site use:** On Grand Manan the catch is spread in rows
  assigned to each harvester and sun-dried for about 24 hours. It needs
  about six hours of strong sun; fog and wet summers cause shortages (1973)
  [3].
- **Quality by location:** Dark Harbour dulse is considered the best [3].
- **Supply level:** One wholesaler buys about 10 t a summer and can't meet
  demand [3].
- **Regeneration:** Perennial; plants regrow from the holdfast each year
  [1][4]. Wild regrowth rate after picking and a sustainable harvest rate:
  Not sourced (the 25% rule is guidance, not data).
- **Climate pressure:** Bay of Fundy waters have warmed about 1 °C per
  decade over 40 years; Gulf of Maine autumn temperatures recently ran
  2.3 °C above the long-term average [3].

## Template questions this case raises

- Assigned drying rows are a second tenure layer (land for processing),
  separate from the right to pick.
- Weather risk falls on drying, not on the harvest itself.
- A species can be wild-managed in one region and farmed in another (or
  have a farmed close relative), so the template may need a "cultivation
  alternative" field.
