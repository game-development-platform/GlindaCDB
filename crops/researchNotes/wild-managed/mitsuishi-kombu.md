## Why it belongs in this category

- **Wild only.** Hidaka sells only wild-harvested kombu; there is no
  aquaculture there [2].
- **Managed, not just picked.** Fishers clear other seaweeds from the rock
  so zoospores can settle [2], the research station monitors stocks [2],
  and sales go through a regulated co-op system [2].

## Fields a wild-managed template would need, with Hidaka kombu values

- **Harvest rule / technique:** Cut harvest July to October; beach-cast
  collection year-round [2]. Boats go out only in good weather and sea [2].
- **Habitat:** Rocky shore of Hokkaido's Pacific coast (Hidaka, Tokachi)
  [2].
- **Stock trend:** Production down 45% in 20 years, to 1,979 t (2022) [2].
  Large loss after the 2023 marine heatwave [2].
- **Enhancement:** Clear competing seaweeds before the September–November
  zoospore release [2].
- **Monitoring:** Monthly at Hiroo since 2014 [2].
- **Who harvests:** Co-op fishers; drying by family, relatives and local
  helpers [2][3].
- **Tenure / licensing:** Through fishery co-ops (e.g. Erimo, Hidaka,
  Hidaka-chūō) [2]. Exact rights not sourced.
- **Marketing:** Joint sales through the Hokkaido fisheries federation
  since 1954 [2]; beaches graded by quality [3].
- **Postharvest:** Sun-dried on the beach, then sorted [2][3].
- **Bottleneck:** Drying labour, not stock alone [2].
