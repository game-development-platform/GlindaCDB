The `[n]` numbers below refer to that doc's Sources list.

## Why it belongs in this category

- **No farming yet.** No cultivation method has worked, despite repeated
  attempts. Fixed-fragment and pond culture were both tried; growth rate is
  the main obstacle [3]. Taiwanese pond culture has stopped, and Moroccan
  restocking failed [1].
- **Harvested from wild beds.** It grows on rocky seabeds in turbulent
  water, so it is taken by divers or after storms [4].

## Fields a wild-managed template would need, with Gelidium values

- **Harvest rights / tenure:**
  - Morocco: licensed divers and boats. Only 10–20% of the harvest comes
    from agar-company boats under official rules [1].
  - Portugal: weed is sold only to licensed concentrators restricted to one
    harvest zone. Harvest is currently confined to one of six zones [5][1].
  - Japan: gathering rights work like fishing rights, issued through
    fishermen's cooperatives [7].
- **Season window:**
  - Portugal: July–December [5].
  - Izu, Japan: opens in early spring and is cut short by sea conditions
    [7].
  - French Basque storm-cast: starts with the first autumn swells [5].
  - Morocco: a closed season of 1 October–30 June (from slides; low weight)
    [1].
- **Harvest methods:**
  - Divers pluck or cut: Japanese ama down to about 10 m, men with diving
    gear deeper, and Portuguese and Moroccan hookah divers [7][5].
  - Storm-cast collection: boats net floating weed, and tractors scoop it
    off beaches [5].
  - Direct harvesting is banned in the French Basque Country [5].
- **Harvest rate per worker:** 120–250 kg dry per diver per day. Japanese
  ama take up to 300 kg [7].
- **Quotas and quality rules:**
  - Morocco's landing quotas were 5,440 t (2010) and 6,040 t (2014), with
    20% exportable (slides; low weight) [1].
  - Up to 80% of Moroccan landings must be processed in-country [1].
  - Quality depends on method and depth. Deep-water plants are best, and
    storm-cast weed is poorer [7][1].
- **Standing stock and regeneration:**
  - El Jadida biomass was 13,700–16,800 t wet, with a mean of 800 g/m² [5].
  - Plants regrow clonally from creeping axes. Regrowth slows if those axes
    are destroyed [3].
  - Regrowth rate after harvest: Not sourced.
- **Overharvest failure mode:** Uncontrolled plucking from the base strips
  beds [5]. Overharvested beds lost 33% of biomass in four years and were
  taken over by another red alga, *Halopithys incurvus* [5].
- **Stand enhancement:** Japan throws rocks into productive beds, which are
  colonised at about 5 kg/m² [3].
- **Ecosystem and climate dependence:**
  - Warming, more light and more extreme waves have largely wiped out the
    Basque canopy [6].
  - Winter storms uproot 60–80% of Moroccan biomass [5].
  - The 2002 *Prestige* oil spill closed the Spanish harvest [1].
- **Market and trade mechanics:**
  - Morocco supplies 82% of global landings [1].
  - Its export quotas caused the 2015 lab-agar shortage, when prices
    roughly tripled [4].
