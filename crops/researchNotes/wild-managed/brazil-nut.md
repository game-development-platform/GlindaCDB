## Why it belongs in this category

- **Plantations aren't viable.** Brazil nuts have been grown in
  plantations, but production is low and not currently economic [1].
  Plantation yields in central Brazil are low, probably because of the
  specialised pollinators and the planting material used [2].
- **Pollination needs intact forest.** The trees fruit almost exclusively
  in pristine forest, because disturbed forest lacks the large-bodied bees
  that pollinate them [1]. Euglossine bees depend on particular orchids
  [3].

## Fields a wild-managed template would need, with Brazil nut values

- **Harvest rights / tenure:**
  - Madre de Dios, Peru: hundreds of smallholder concessions under
    long-term agreements [4].
  - Peruvian land is state-owned and rented to individuals and families
    for 2–10 years. In Bolivia and Brazil land is often private [5].
  - Brazil: extractive reserves are among the land-use types studied [2].
- **Season window:** The rainy-season harvest (zafra) [3][4].
- **Harvest method:**
  - Fallen pods are gathered from the forest floor and cracked open with
    machetes [6].
  - Each pod holds 10–25 seeds [5].
  - Falling pods are a serious injury risk to collectors [5].
- **Stand structure:** Trees grow in stands of 50–100 [5].
- **Regeneration and lag:**
  - Trees take about 15 years to mature, and at least 25 to reach full
    production [3].
  - A single tree can bear for about 150 years [7].
  - Seeds are dispersed by agoutis hoarding and forgetting nuts [3].
  - Collectors also hunt agoutis during the harvest [8].
- **Overharvest failure mode:** Delayed recruitment failure. Sites with
  moderate or heavy collection have few young trees [7]. Old trees keep
  producing while replacement fails. Gelidium is different: its stands are
  stripped directly.
- **Market and livelihoods:**
  - It is the only globally traded seed collected wild by forest
    harvesters [2].
  - In Madre de Dios it makes up 55–65% of forest income for concession
    households [2].
  - Bolivia exports nearly 28,000 t a year [6].
