## Why it belongs in this category

- **Wild only.** Not cultivated; it is trawled from natural forests [2][5].
- **Highly managed.** Rotating fields, yearly surveys, closures and
  five-yearly rule revisions (below) [2].

## Fields a wild-managed template would need, with Norwegian values

- **Harvest rule / technique:** Mechanical trawl that takes mainly large
  plants, leaving small ones and holdfasts [2].
- **Rotation:** Fields one nautical mile wide, lettered A–E. Each opens
  once in five years, then rests four [2]. Southern Nordland uses five or
  ten years [2].
- **Quota:** None; access is by open field [2].
- **Season:** Fixed dates set by regulation (e.g. 1 September – 31 August)
  [2].
- **Monitoring:** Yearly video and acoustic survey of fields due to open,
  against closed control areas [2].
- **Closure trigger:** Fields with canopy under 0.8 m and low biomass are
  advised closed [2].
- **Regulator:** Directorate of Fisheries, via regional J-notices; county
  working groups with industry, fishers and municipalities [2].
- **Who harvests:** Two licensed companies [2].
- **Recovery:** Canopy regrows in 2–6 years [4]; epiphyte communities lag
  beyond five years [2].
- **Threats:** Sea urchin grazing [2][4]; storms [2].
- **Other jurisdictions:** Scotland banned whole-plant commercial removal
  in 2018 [5]. Beach-cast stipe collection in Ireland, now lapsed [3].
- **Yield-like figures:** 13 kg/m² standing biomass; 14–30 t an hour
  trawled [2]; about 150,000 t a year nationally [2].
