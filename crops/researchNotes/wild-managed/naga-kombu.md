# Wild-managed resource notes: Naga kombu

**Temporary file.** It holds research for the planned wild-managed-resource

**Source doc:** `crops/raw-data/algae/naga-kombu.md` (researched
2026-11-15). The `[n]` numbers below refer to that doc's Sources list.

## Why it belongs in this category

- **Wild only.** No farming was found; it is cut from natural beds [1].
- **Actively managed.** Weed clearing is called indispensable [2]. GIS maps
  guide where to clear [2], and a co-op has tracked blade weights for years
  [1].

## Fields a wild-managed template would need, with naga kombu values

- **Harvest rule / technique:** Long-handled gear from boats, June–October,
  on plants in their second year or older [1].
- **Harvest stages:** Saomae (young, soft, early season) vs mature kombu
  [3]. Saomae is described as thinning [3].
- **Habitat:** Wave-exposed rocky shore, Kushiro to the Kurils [1][2].
- **Stock trend:** About 10,000 t of kombu a year regionally, slowly
  declining [1].
- **Enhancement:** Weed clearing, prioritised by growth-potential maps [2].
- **Monitoring:** Habomai co-op blade weights, May and June, 2000–2014 [1].
- **Tenure / licensing:** Co-op fishing rights. The Kaigara-jima grounds
  need a yearly private Japan–Russia agreement with a fee and catch quota
  (2026: ¥76.67 million, 3,024 t, 181 boats) [3].
- **Opening:** A fixed opening day (1 June), with a mass start at a signal
  [3].
- **Disruption risks:** Storms, drift ice, cold springs, tsunami and
  diplomacy [1][3].
- **Postharvest:** Family beach drying; frozen storage being trialled [2].
- **Bottleneck:** Drying labour [2][3].
