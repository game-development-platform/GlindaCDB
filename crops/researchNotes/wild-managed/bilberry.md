## Why it belongs in this category

- **No cultivation.** It has never been domesticated and is harvested
  only from wild forest stands [1][4]. Norwegian semi-cultivation trials
  have not yet raised yields [12].
- **Supply is set by access rules and labour, not land.** In Sweden anyone
  may pick, including large commercial crews [11]. In Finland, 75% of the
  wild berries sold in 2025 were picked by seasonal foreign workers [5].

## Fields a wild-managed template would need, with bilberry values

- **Harvest rights / tenure:** public access (Sweden's *allemansrätten*).
  There is no ban on commercially organized large-scale picking, provided
  it causes the landowner no loss or nuisance beyond the berries
  themselves. Protected areas can restrict it [11]. The landowner has no
  right to the berries.
- **Labour permits (new field):** the harvest depends on migrant pickers,
  so visa rules act as the supply control.
  - **Finland:** picking falls under the Seasonal Workers Act from 17
    February 2025 [5]. About 3,000 seasonal pickers came in 2025, against
    about 1,000 in 2024 [5].
  - **Sweden:** no work permits for forest berry picking from 1 June
    2026; seasonal permits with a Swedish employer remain [10].
  - **Effect:** Finnish commercial purchases were 1.16 million kg in
    2024 and 6.83 million kg in 2025 [5].
- **Season window:** July-September [4]; about 50 days after flowering
  [3].
- **Harvest method:** hand or berry comb. Picking with a comb once or twice
  a year reduced plant numbers, though twice-yearly combing raised
  reproduction value [1]. In Finland, 97% is sold uncleaned [5].
- **Stand yield and monitoring:**
  - National-forest-inventory models give Finland about 208 million kg a
    year and Sweden a mean of 336 million kg (142-565) [2].
  - Sweden makes annual seasonal yield forecasts from inventory plots [2].
  - Only about 3-6% of the crop is picked [2][5] (see the doc's
    Estimates).
- **Yield drivers:** frost at bloom, snow cover, summer moisture, stand
  age and forestry (clearcutting cuts cover; thinning raises it) [1][3].
- **Regeneration and lag:** rhizome spread of 5-10 cm a year; seedlings
  rare; seed production from about age three [3].
- **Overharvest failure mode:** unclear. With only a few percent picked,
  the main threats are forestry changes and climate (warmer winters, less
  snow, more bloom frost) rather than picking [1][3]. Comb damage is the
  one direct picking effect found [1].
- **Market and certification:**
  - Finnish buyers paid €2.39/kg on average in 2025 [5].
  - Finland had 6.9 million ha of certified organic wild-collection area
    in 2025 [5].
  - A storage aid supports buyers' stocks [5].
  - The extract trade is wild-supplied worldwide and prone to fraud [8].
- **Labour-conditions risk (new field):** picker housing, pay and
  trafficking investigations shape whether permits are issued [10][12].

## Suggested template fields from this case

- **public-access harvest right** (no tenure holder)
- **labour-permit regime** as the binding supply control
- **share of standing crop harvested**
- **national yield monitoring or forecast**
- **certified wild-collection area**
