## Why it belongs in this category

- **Supply is wild.** Commercial arame is gathered from wild beds in Mie,
  by ama divers and box-glass fishers, in a set summer season [2].
- **No farming found.** Hatchery seedlings exist but are used only to
  restore lost forests [3].

## Fields a wild-managed template would need, with arame values

- **Harvest rights / tenure:** Not sourced. Gathering is by local
  fishers and ama in Mie [2]; who holds the rights (presumably the
  fisheries cooperative) wasn't found.
- **Season window:** July–September in Mie [2].
- **Harvest method:** Ama free-diving, and box-glass gathering from the
  surface [2].
- **Harvest limits:** Not sourced.
- **Post-harvest site use:** Beach sun-drying in high season [2].
- **Workforce:** Ama divers and fishers [2]; numbers not sourced.
- **Regeneration:** Perennial; blades shed and regrow yearly [1][2]. (In
  Mie's sagarame, blades grow fastest in winter [3].)
- **Stock threats:** Warming (29 °C limit) and fish grazing in the south
  and west; urchin overgrazing in the north-east (tipping point about 15.5
  urchins per m²) [3]. Japan's seaweed beds shrank from about 200,000 to
  125,000 ha in 1978–2007 [3].
- **Linked fisheries:** The forests support abalone, urchin and turban
  shell fisheries; abalone landings fell when the Toshi forest vanished
  [3].
- **Restoration as management:** Cooperative members raise seedlings
  with the Toba City research institute, cover them with anti-grazing nets
  and transplant them after a year [3].
- **Species identity:** The commercial "arame" may be a different species
  (*Eisenia nipponica*, sagarame) from the one on the label [1][2].

## Template questions this case raises

- Restoration effort (seedlings, urchin removal, nets) as a management
  action that rebuilds the stock.
- Linked harvests: the kelp is both a crop and the habitat for another
  fishery (abalone, urchin).
- Diver-based harvest (ama) as a labour type distinct from shore picking
  or raking.
- A trade name that covers more than one species.
