## Naming and scope

This covers *Dunaliella salina*, a single-celled green alga of salt lakes
and saltworks, grown for natural beta-carotene [1][2]. It covers the
single cropID `dunaliella`.

**Names.**

- **Old and messy.** It was first seen in the salterns of Montpellier,
  France, in 1838, and described as a genus in 1905 [1]. The genus
  taxonomy is still unresolved, and many culture-collection strains turned
  out to be mislabelled [1].
- ***D. bardawil*.** The Israeli high-carotene strain is often treated as a
  form of *D. salina* ("*D. salina* var. *bardawil*") but sometimes as a
  separate species [1]. The Eilat producer calls its biomass *D. salina*
  var. *bardawil* [2].
- **Green vs red.** *D. salina* turns red with carotene; its green relative
  *D. viridis* lives alongside it at Hutt Lagoon [1].
- **Status-table name.** Kept as *Dunaliella salina*.

**Relatives flagged:** `haematococcus` (RESEARCHED) — the other
stress-to-ripen carotenoid crop; already cross-linked. No new rows
proposed.

**Supply is farmed** (Finding 1). Harvesting wild lakes isn't commercially
practical because cell densities are too low [2], so this is not a
wild-managed resource, even though the largest farms are built in natural
salt lakes.

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — farmed in pink salt lakes, mixed by the wind.**

- **Hutt Lagoon.** In Western Australia, a 12 × 2 km hypersaline lagoon
  less than 1 m deep, with salinity up to 320 g/L [2]. BASF's facility there
  has 450 ha of ponds surrounded by 3,500 ha of natural lagoon [2]. *D.
  salina* is the main cause of the lake's pink colour [2].
- **Whyalla.** BASF's second site, in South Australia, licensed since 2005
  [2]. The two sites make BASF the world's largest producer of algal
  beta-carotene [2]. South Australia's output was 647 t (A$4.85 million) in
  2020–21, and as high as 4,400 t (A$37 million) in 2015–16 [2].
- **Two pond types.**
  - **Extensive:** large ponds built on lake beds with earthen dykes and
    no mixing devices, mixed by wind [2].
  - **Intensive:** raceways with paddle-wheels [2].
- **Origins.** A plant with over 25 ha of ponds opened at Hutt Lagoon in
  late 1986, with 50,000 m² production ponds and no loss of productivity
  on scale-up [2]. Whyalla was built in 1985–86 [2].
- **Israel.** Nature Beta Technologies (owned by Japan's Nikken Sohonsha)
  has grown it near the Red Sea in Eilat since 1987, on about 100,000 m²
  of open ponds [2], producing about 25 t of biomass a year [2].
- **Why it matters.** A crop for land no other crop can use: brine,
  desert sun and salt flats.

**Finding 2 — salt is the weed control.**

- **Salinity.** Commercial brine is held at about 18–27% sodium chloride
  [3]. Growth is best at about 18–21%; carotene per cell peaks above about
  27%, and carotene per litre of brine at about 24% [3].
- **Why.** Few other organisms survive these salinities, which makes the
  culture nearly free of competitors [2][3].
- **But not grazer-free.** A predatory ciliate, *Stylonychia
  histriomuscorum*, was the main grazer in one open pond; adding 0.5 g/L
  ammonium nitrate controlled it without harming the alga, while raising
  salinity did not help [3].
- **Why it matters.** Salinity is both the crop's protection and its yield
  lever.

**Finding 3 — a wall-less cell that makes glycerol and carotene under
stress.**

- **No cell wall.** *Dunaliella* lacks a rigid cell wall [3]. To survive
  changing salinity it balances its inside with glycerol, up to about 50%
  of dry weight [3].
- **Carotene.** Under stress (strong light, high salt, nutrient shortage,
  extreme temperature) beta-carotene can reach about 10–14% of dry weight
  [3]. Its beta-carotene is up to half the 9-cis form, unlike synthetic
  beta-carotene [2].
- **Resting cells.** In winter at Hutt Lagoon it forms resting cells
  (aplanospores), which hold canthaxanthin rather than beta-carotene [1].
- **Why it matters.** No wall means easier extraction than
  *Haematococcus*; the glycerol is a possible co-product.

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Single swimming cells; year-round culture in
  warm, sunny climates [2]. Aplanospores form mainly in winter [1].
- **Harvest pattern:** Continuous harvesting from dilute brine [2].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** Not sourced.
- **Climate zones:** Hot, arid regions with little rain and few cloudy
  days [2]. Tolerates below 0 °C to above 40 °C, with optimum growth about
  21–40 °C depending on strain [3].
- **Composition:** Beta-carotene up to 10–14% of dry weight; glycerol up
  to 50% [3].
- **Edibility:** Eaten as dried algal powder or beta-carotene extracts [2].
- **Pollination:** Not applicable.
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** High light raises carotene [3].
- **Yield:** Not sourced per hectare. Eilat about 25 t a year from about
  10 ha (Estimates) [2].
- **Water use:** Brine (hypersaline water), not freshwater [2][3].
- **Propagation:** From strain stock; photobioreactors are used to make
  inoculum for raceways [2].
- **Labor:** Not sourced.
- **Soil:** Not applicable. Ponds built on salt-lake beds [2].
- **Storage (postharvest):** Not sourced.
- **Establishment:** Some designs use lower-salinity, nutrient-rich
  nursery ponds before high-salinity production ponds [2].
- **Cultivars:** Patented strains (e.g. DF15, DF40) deposited by the EU
  D-Factory project [2].
- **Weather response:** Cloud and rain reduce carotene; heat near 40 °C
  stimulates it [2][3].
- **Water regime:** Hypersaline (18–27% NaCl in culture; 320 g/L in Hutt
  Lagoon) [2][3].
- **Harvest stages / windows:** Harvest red cells; harvesting dilute
  suspensions economically was the key technical hurdle [2].
- **Soil biology:** Not applicable. Ciliate grazers (Finding 2) [3].
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** Cosmopolitan in salt lakes and salterns
  [1]; no invasive record found. Restricted to hypersaline water, so escape
  risk seems low (inference).
- **Controlled environment:** Photobioreactors for inoculum; production in
  open ponds [2].

## Hazards

- **Beta-carotene in smokers.** In two large trials, heavy smokers taking
  20–30 mg beta-carotene a day long-term had more lung cancer [4]. EFSA
  concluded that intakes below 15 mg a day as a food additive or
  supplement don't raise concern [4].
- **No other hazards found in this pass.**

## Uses and processing

- **Chain:** pond culture in brine → harvest dilute cells (flotation and
  similar methods) → extract carotene or dry to powder [2].
- **Uses:**
  - Beta-carotene in oil for food and pharmaceuticals [2].
  - Dried algal powder for prawn feed [2]; shrimp pigmentation and immunity
    [3].
  - Health food powder (Japan) [2].
  - Food colourant (beta-carotene, e.g. margarine) [2].

## Estimates and derivations

**Eilat yield, about 2.5 t per hectare a year.** Inputs: about 25 t
ash-free dry biomass a year [2] from about 100,000 m² (10 ha) of ponds [2].
25 / 10 = 2.5 t/ha. The two figures come from different sources and
years, so this is rough.
