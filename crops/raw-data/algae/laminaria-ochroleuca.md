## Naming and scope

This covers *Laminaria ochroleuca* Bachelot de la Pylaie, a warm-water
(Lusitanian) kelp of Atlantic Iberia and nearby coasts [1]. It covers the
single cropID `laminaria_ochroleuca`, promoted from PROPOSED (flagged in
`laminaria-digitata.md`).

**Names.**

- **English.** Golden kelp, named for its blade colour [1].
- **Spanish trade.** Sold as *kombu* (Galician kombu) [2]. "Kombu" in
  Galician trade also covers *L. hyperborea* and *Saccharina* [2].

**Why it gets its own row (gate check, 2026-11-16).**

- **A distinct species.** It looks like `laminaria_hyperborea`, but is more
  yellow and lacks that species' rough stipe [1]. It is a warm-water
  species where *L. hyperborea* is cold-water [3].
- **Its own food market:** Iberia's kombu (Finding 1) [2].
- **Its own management regime:** Galician co-management plans with daily
  catch caps (Finding 2) [2]. This differs from Norway's field rotation.
- **A climate-driven replacement for *L. hyperborea*** in south-west
  England (Finding 3) [3].

**Relatives flagged:**

- `laminaria_hyperborea` (cross-linked).
- `laminaria_digitata` (cross-linked).
- `sugar_kelp` (Galician "kombu de azúcar" [2]).
- No new rows proposed.

**Supply is wild-managed** (Finding 2). Saved to
`crops/researchNotes/wild-managed/laminaria-ochroleuca.md` (issue #2).

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — Iberia's kombu.**

- **Product.** Galician firms sell it dried, salted, fresh and canned as
  kombu [2]. One dried retail pack lists 100% *L. ochroleuca* [2].
- **Who.** Porto-Muiños (Cerceda, A Coruña) and the Algamar group (Mar de
  Ardora) both hold harvest plans that include it [2].
- **Portugal.** Called kombu there too, as an edible seaweed [4]. In
  northern Portugal it forms large populations in deep tide pools and
  sparse patches in the shallow subtidal [1].
- **Why it matters.** A European kombu, distinct from Japanese *S.
  japonica* (`kelp_kombu`).

**Finding 2 — harvested under Galician co-management plans.**

- **System.** Since the 1990s, Galicia has managed shellfish and seaweed
  harvests through plans co-run by the government and the fishing sector
  [2]. Plans are approved by triennial orders (e.g. 2025–2027) [2]. The
  legal basis is Decree 153/2019 [2].
- **Who holds plans.** Fishers' guilds (*confrarías*, e.g. Vigo, Baiona,
  Muros, O Grove, A Coruña) and processing firms [2].
- **Methods.** On foot at low tide, by breath-hold diving, and by
  surface-supplied diving from boats [2].
- **Example caps (Vigo plan, 2017)** [2]:
  - 1,000 kg wet weight per boat per day.
  - 175 kg per on-foot harvester per day.
  - At most 40 harvest days a year.
  - Open year-round.
  - Six on-foot harvesters and six boats (twelve crew). No new permits.
  - Catch checked at set control points and sold through the Canido and O
    Berbés fish markets.
- **Other plans.** Muros caps divers at 500 kg a day, combined with
  *Himanthalia*, also with 40 days a year [2]. Caps vary by plan [2].
- **Why it matters.** Control by daily cap and permit count, not by field
  rotation as in Norway (`laminaria-hyperborea.md`).

**Finding 3 — the kelp that is moving north.**

- **Range.** Its northern limit ran from Brittany to Devon, UK [3]. It was
  first recorded in the UK in the early 20th century [3]. It was first
  found in Ireland at Béal an Mhuirthead, County Mayo, in 2019 [3].
- **Deep in the south.** It grows below 50 m in the Straits of Messina [1].
- **Replacing *L. hyperborea*.** In Plymouth Sound it has proliferated and
  partly replaced *L. hyperborea* at some sites [3].
- **A poorer habitat.** Warming-affected forests held about half the
  epiphytic algae and a fifth of the mobile invertebrates per unit area of
  unaffected forests [3]. Its stipe is smooth, so fewer epiphytes settle
  (inference from [1][3]).
- **Temperature.** Sporophytes do best at about 12–22 °C and gametophytes
  at 15–18 °C [3]. Low weight.
- **Why it matters.** Climate change swaps one crop for another on the
  same rock: less habitat, but a food kelp.

**Finding 4 — iodine-rich.**

- **Iodine.** 2,780 mg/kg dry matter in Galician samples, second only to
  sugar kelp (4,131) [4]. A Catalan market survey found kombu (*Laminaria*
  spp.) averaged 3,970 mg/kg [4].
- **Feed limit.** Because of iodine, its inclusion in animal feed should be
  kept below 0.66% [4].
- **Arsenic.** Mainly arsenosugars, of low toxicity [4].
- **Protein.** About 9% of dry weight in wild Portuguese samples [4].

**Finding 5 — cultivation is experimental.**

- A Portuguese thesis tested hatchery methods [5]. Photoinhibition was the
  main problem for this species [5].
- No commercial farming found.

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Perennial kelp (lifespan not sourced).
- **Harvest pattern:** Hand-cut by divers and on foot, under daily caps;
  year-round in some plans [2].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** Not sourced.
- **Climate zones:** Warm-temperate north-east Atlantic and Mediterranean
  [1][3].
- **Composition:** Iodine 2,780 mg/kg DM [4]; protein about 9% DW [4].
- **Edibility:** Edible; sold as kombu [2][4].
- **Pollination:** Not applicable.
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Sensitive to photoinhibition in culture [5].
- **Yield:** Not sourced per area. See Estimates for cap scale.
- **Water use:** Marine.
- **Propagation:** Natural recruitment; experimental hatchery [5].
- **Labor:** Divers and on-foot harvesters [2].
- **Soil:** Not applicable. Rocky shore, tide pools and subtidal [1].
- **Storage (postharvest):** Dried, salted or canned [2].
- **Establishment:** Not applicable (wild).
- **Cultivars:** None.
- **Weather response:** Not sourced.
- **Water regime:** Marine; low shore to deep subtidal [1].
- **Harvest stages / windows:** Set by each plan [2].
- **Soil biology:** Not applicable. Supports fewer epiphytes than *L.
  hyperborea* [3].
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** Native in Iberia; range-expanding north
  [3].
- **Controlled environment:** Hatchery trials only [5].

## Hazards

- **Iodine:** High; limit intake (Finding 4) [4].
- **Arsenic:** Mostly low-toxicity forms [4].

## Uses and processing

- **Chain:** hand harvest → weighed at control point → sold through a fish
  market → dried, salted or canned [2].
- **Uses:** Kombu for broths, stews and salads [2]; feed ingredient within
  iodine limits [4].

## Estimates and derivations

**Vigo plan ceiling, about 280 t wet a year.** Inputs: 6 boats × 1,000 kg
× 40 days = 240 t; 6 on-foot harvesters × 175 kg × 40 days = 42 t [2].
Total about 282 t. This is the most the plan allows, not actual landings.
