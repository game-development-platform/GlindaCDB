## Naming and scope

This covers *Laminaria pallida* Greville ex J. Agardh, the split-fan kelp
of southern Africa's cold west coast [1]. It covers the single cropID
`laminaria_pallida`, promoted from PROPOSED (flagged in `ecklonia.md`).

**Names.** Split-fan kelp [1]. Its type locality is Table Bay, Cape Town
[1].

**Why it gets its own row (gate check, 2026-11-16).**

- **Different genus from `ecklonia`.** *E. maxima* (sea bamboo) is a
  floating canopy kelp with a hollow, gas-filled stipe [1]. *L. pallida*
  usually has a solid, stiff stipe and grows beneath the *E. maxima* canopy
  [1].
- **Its own range and dominance.** Northward along the coast and into
  Namibia, *E. maxima* is progressively replaced by *L. pallida* [1]. It is
  the dominant kelp in Namibia [1].
- **Shared uses but a distinct resource.** It is co-harvested with *E.
  maxima*, but in smaller amounts (Finding 2) [2]. This pass is kept short
  because the shared management is already in `ecklonia.md`.

**Relatives flagged:**

- `ecklonia` (co-harvested; cross-linked).
- `laminaria_digitata`, `laminaria_hyperborea`, `laminaria_ochroleuca`
  (same genus, North Atlantic).
- No new rows proposed. Older Namibian sources list a separate kelp,
  *L. schinzii* [3]; its status was not resolved in this pass.

**Supply is wild-managed** (Finding 2). Saved to
`crops/researchNotes/wild-managed/laminaria-pallida.md` (issue #2).

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — the understorey kelp that takes over in the north.**

- **Range.** From Danger Point (about 100 km east of Cape Town) to Rocky
  Point near the Kunene River, northern Namibia [1]. It also grows on
  Tristan da Cunha, Gough Island and Île Saint-Paul [1].
- **Where it grows.** Commonly below the *E. maxima* canopy [1]. A thesis
  photograph shows a young plant at 8 m on a granite boulder [1].
- **Shape changes northward.** Along 1,600 km from Cape Town to Swakopmund,
  *L. pallida* stipes become increasingly hollow to the north [1]. It may
  outcompete *E. maxima* northward because it tolerates low light better
  [1]. A hollow stipe may help it grow faster in length in shallow water
  [1].
- **Standing stock.** An old estimate put it at almost 218,000 t fresh
  weight between Cape Point and Cape Columbine [1]. No data exist for the
  rest of its range [1].
- **Why it matters.** Shade-tolerant and dominant where water is turbid.

**Finding 2 — a lesser partner in South Africa's kelp industry.**

- **South Africa.**
  - The industry is based mainly on *E. maxima*, and to a lesser extent *L.
    pallida* [2].
  - Beach-cast kelp of both species has been collected since at least
    1953 [2]. It is sun-dried, milled and exported, mainly for alginate,
    because there is no commercial extraction in South Africa [2].
  - Collection of wash-up for alginate has dropped to very small amounts
    in recent years [2].
- **Management.** Kelp is harvested in seaweed concession areas under the
  Marine Living Resources Act [2]. The coast from Port Nolloth to Port St
  Johns is divided into 23 harvesting areas [2]. Quotas are awarded to
  concession holders for 10 years after a government tender [2]. Harvest is
  not allowed in Marine Protected Areas [2].
- **Namibia.**
  - Beach-cast kelp, *E. maxima* and *Laminaria*, is collected around
    Lüderitz [3]. One operator collects by hand in the surf zone [3]. It
    cuts off blades and holdfasts and leaves them on the shore for
    wildlife, then sun-dries and chips the kelp for agriculture [3].
  - An environmental clearance certificate and park permits are required
    [3]. A 2025 dispute concerned a company accused of stripping beaches
    without one [3].
- **Why it matters.** Beach-cast collection is the main harvest, so supply
  depends on storms.

**Finding 3 — other uses and farming interest.**

- **Iodine.** Namibian scientists are looking at *Ecklonia* and *Laminaria*
  to treat goitre and prevent cretinism [4].
- **Fertiliser.** Namibian beach-cast *L. pallida* has been studied as a
  biochar fertiliser [4].
- **Farming.** A Western Cape small-scale project focuses on cultivating *E.
  maxima* and *L. pallida* [4]. Africa's first kelp farm is in a pilot
  phase in Namibia [4] (species not stated).

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Perennial kelp (lifespan not sourced).
- **Harvest pattern:** Beach-cast collection, mostly year-round with storms
  [2][3].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** Not sourced.
- **Climate zones:** Cool Benguela upwelling coast, South Africa to
  Namibia; South Atlantic and Indian Ocean islands [1].
- **Composition:** Iodine-rich (Not sourced as a figure) [4].
- **Edibility:** Not sourced.
- **Pollination:** Not applicable.
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** More low-light tolerant than *E. maxima*
  [1].
- **Yield:** Standing stock about 218,000 t fresh (Cape Point–Cape
  Columbine) [1].
- **Water use:** Marine.
- **Propagation:** Natural; cultivation trials [4].
- **Labor:** Hand collection on beaches [3].
- **Soil:** Not applicable. Rock, below the *E. maxima* canopy [1].
- **Storage (postharvest):** Sun-dried, milled or chipped [2][3].
- **Establishment:** Not applicable (wild).
- **Cultivars:** None. Stipe form varies with latitude [1].
- **Weather response:** Storms supply beach-cast [3].
- **Water regime:** Marine, subtidal.
- **Harvest stages / windows:** Not sourced.
- **Soil biology:** Not applicable.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** Native.
- **Controlled environment:** None.
