## Naming and scope

This is modeled as *Helianthus annuus* L. (common/annual sunflower),
family Asteraceae — the single cultivated sunflower species (other
*Helianthus* species, such as Jerusalem artichoke, are different crops
grown for a different organ). The one real internal split is
confectionery vs. oilseed type — not two species, but two cultivar
groups within the same crop, on the same plant architecture, differing
in seed size, hull proportion, and oil content (Finding 4). This file
keeps them as one entry with two cultivation paths, per this project's
distinctiveness-check convention (same growing method, same climate/
water/pest profile, only the end product and processing chain differ)
rather than treating them separately. Oil palm is a useful comparison
for general oil-crop catalogue conventions, though the two crops share
almost nothing biologically — sunflower is an annual achene (seed) crop,
oil palm a decades-long perennial tree.

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — this is an achene (seed) crop where the harvested organ
and the seed-crop organ are usually the same thing.** Unlike oil palm
(two separate oils from two separate fruit tissues), sunflower's whole
commercial product — oil, confectionery kernel, or planting seed — comes
from the same single-seeded fruit (achene), harvested once per plant per
season by combine. This makes sunflower a "grain-type" crop in the usual
seed-crop sense for the open-pollinated path, even though, unusually for
a grain-type crop, most of the commercial crop is actually F1 hybrid seed
(Finding 2), which is not the same as the food crop.

**Finding 2 — commercial planting seed is overwhelmingly F1 hybrid,
built on cytoplasmic male sterility (CMS), and the hybrid cross depends
on live bees, not hand pollination.** Hybrid seed production crosses a
male-sterile "A line" (the CMS seed parent) with a male-fertile
"restorer" (R) line (the pollen parent), planted in separate rows at a
set ratio in an isolated seed-production field [5][6]. Because sunflower
pollen is too heavy and sticky to travel by wind, honeybees (and in at
least one Indian trial, a larger bee species) do the actual pollen
transfer between the R-line and A-line rows [5][6]. Hand pollination
exists only as a costly fallback — one Indian study found an acre of
hybrid seed production needed around 42 skilled labourers for hand pollen
transfer, and caged/isolated seed-increase plots can lose up to 90% of
yield if pollinator access is inadequate [5]. This is a hybrid cross that
is insect-mediated rather than hand- or wind-pollinated, in contrast with
some other oil crops this project has researched.

**Finding 3 — the crop is genuinely heliotropic before flowering, and
this is mechanistically well understood (unlike most "sun-tracking
plant" folklore).** A 2016 peer-reviewed study found that young sunflower
plants track the sun east-to-west during the day and reorient overnight
to face east again by dawn, driven by a circadian clock producing
antiphasic stem elongation on the east and west sides [8]. This tracking
STOPS once the stalk stops elongating and the flower matures — mature
heads are fixed, facing east, which is why a sunflower field looks
uniformly east-facing without any of the plants still actually moving
[8]. The eastward-facing orientation appears to warm the flower faster
in the morning and is associated with more pollinator visits [8] —
directly relevant to Finding 2's bee-dependency. This is a genuinely
distinctive trait among crops this project has researched (a true
photoperiod-independent circadian tracking behaviour, distinct from
ordinary bolting/flowering triggers).

**Finding 4 — confectionery and oilseed types are the same species with
opposite priorities: seed size and hull thickness vs. oil content.**
Confectionery types are larger-seeded with a thick, often black-and-
white-striped hull and low oil content, suited to in-shell roasting or
dehulling for kernel snacks and baking; oilseed types are smaller, black,
high-oil achenes grown for crushing [3]. A Morocco-vs-Spain comparison
found NO difference between the two types in seed yield, hundred-kernel
weight, or kernel oil content when compared on a like-for-like basis —
the practical difference is almost entirely about hull proportion and
seed size, which drive the end market, not the underlying plant's
productivity [3]. Confectionery-type hulls run roughly a fifth to a
third of seed weight and must be removed efficiently for the dehulled-
kernel market [3][10].

**Finding 5 — oil extraction is a genuine two-stage industrial process,
and most research covers only the first stage (mechanical pressing).**
Expeller (mechanical) pressing alone still leaves 10-15% residual oil in
the press-cake; commercial mills typically follow with solvent (usually
hexane) extraction to recover the rest, producing a fine, low-fat (<2%),
high-protein meal rather than a high-fiber expeller cake [9]. Seed oil
content itself runs 46-51% in modern hybrids [1]. EU organic rules bar
solvent-extracted meal, so mechanically pressed cake is the only
organic-eligible option [9].

**Finding 6 — hull content, not genetics, is the main lever on sunflower
meal's feed value.** Whole-seed (undehulled) prepress-solvent extraction
gives a 25-28% crude-protein meal; partial dehulling raises this to
34-38%; fully dehulled meal commonly reaches 40%+ protein [9]. Growers
deliberately leave more hull in the meal for cattle feed (where fibre is
tolerated and extraction efficiency matters more) and remove more hull
for poultry feed (where fibre content directly hurts feed value) [9].
Cattle rations can safely run up to about 20% sunflower meal [9].

**Finding 7 — the main disease (Sclerotinia head/stalk rot) has no
reliable chemical control, and bird damage is mechanistically linked to
it.** Head-rot infection is favoured by extended rain in the weeks
before and during flowering, and sclerotia from infected heads return to
the soil and can re-infect sunflower or other susceptible crops in later
years — rotation, not fungicide, is the main real-world control, since
fungicide efficacy is described as limited in sunflower [7]. Heads
already damaged by birds, insects, or hail are more likely to then be
attacked by Sclerotinia [7]. Harvest timing is itself a disease-and-
bird-damage control: harvesting promptly at physiological maturity (head
back banana-yellow, bract tips browning), at or below 18-20% seed
moisture, limits both bird losses and head-rot/shatter loss [7]. Downy
mildew is a separate, cosmetic-only disease on mature plants (pale
upper-leaf mottling, fuzzy underside growth in cool wet nights/warm
humid days) that does not kill a mature plant [7].

**Finding 8 — genome-size sources are reasonably consistent, in
contrast with oil palm's documented split.** A 2005 reference gives 1C =
3.7 pg (≈3,619 Mb) for diploid *H. annuus* (2n=34) [11]; separate
genomic-assembly estimates of 2,871-3,189 Mb and roughly 3.3 Gb sit in
the same general range once converted, with >70% of the genome made up
of LTR retrotransposons and their derivatives [11]. Unlike oil palm's own
genuinely disputed, methodologically-explained 2x genome-size
disagreement, this is ordinary cross-study estimate variation around one
consistent figure, not a documented conflict.

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Warm-season annual. Cycle length 90-130 days:
  sourced range includes early hybrids at 94-99 days, an Iranian early-
  hybrid trial at 101 days, mid-early hybrids at 111-114 days, and a
  mid-late French hybrid at about 124 days [1].
- **Climate:** Temperate, warm temperate, Mediterranean, subtropical —
  a general warm-season annual range inferred from trial-site locations
  cited across sources (Slovakia, Ukraine, France, Iran, Egypt, China,
  Bangladesh, Texas), not independently re-sourced beyond those
  locations.
- **Food status:** Confectionery kernels and oil are eaten raw or
  lightly processed; oilseed-type achenes are not normally eaten whole.
  Crude sunflower oil is 8840-9000 kcal/kg [2]. No raw-seed or raw-kernel
  composition table was found this pass.
- **Pollination:** Bees materially raise seed set and are an absolute
  requirement for the CMS hybrid cross (Finding 2); open-pollinated
  varieties are more self-fertile but still benefit from bee visitation.
  Fruit set without pollination is reduced but not necessarily zero for
  self-fertile open-pollinated cultivars; the CMS hybrid cross
  specifically CANNOT set seed without pollen transfer, since the A line
  has no functional pollen of its own.
- **Yield / composition:** Good yield around 3,470 kg/ha (a 2019-2020
  Slovak multi-hybrid trial average) [1]; exceptional yield around 4,730
  kg/ha (one Ukrainian hybrid's breeder-catalog yield potential) [1]. No
  confirmed world-average yield figure was found this pass — the sourced
  figures are managed-trial or breeder-catalog numbers, almost certainly
  above typical world rainfed averages, so this is left explicitly
  unconfirmed rather than guessed. Seed oil content is 46-51% [1].
  Harvest moisture is 18-20% or below, dried to 9.5-12% for storage [7].
- **Water use:** A crop-coefficient curve cited secondhand via a Texas
  study gives 0.15 initial, 0.90-1.15 mid-season (full cover), declining
  to 0.35 at season end [4]. A Texas High Plains field study measured a
  HIGHER full-cover coefficient of 1.24 over 46 days, above that
  mid-season ceiling, and found accelerated development relative to the
  reference calendar [4]; a 2017 study on saline Chinese soils explicitly
  found its measured values "considerably different" from the reference
  curve and built its own salinity-level-specific curve [4]. Extended
  rain before/during flowering specifically favours Sclerotinia head
  rot — a disease effect of excess moisture, not a direct water-stress
  effect on yield (Finding 7). Sunflower has a general reputation as a
  relatively drought-tolerant field crop, not independently verified
  against a quantitative source this pass. Waterlogging tolerance is
  likely low by general field-crop expectation, not independently
  sourced.
- **Establishment:** Direct-seeded. Planting density varies by climate/
  salinity: 50 cm x 25 cm under one Bangladesh coastal trial, 35 cm x 50
  cm under one Chinese saline-drip trial, 31,250-35,714 plants/ha
  recommended under one arid fertigation study [1][4]. No seed rate in
  kg/ha was found this pass.
- **Soil:** pH is a genuine source conflict: a US grower guide states a
  6.5-7.5 optimum band; a separate, lower-quality source gives 6.0-7.0
  [4]. Both ranges overlap at 6.5-7.0, so the practical difference is
  mainly at the low end. Sunflower is moderately salt-sensitive; high
  salinity markedly reduces seed yield, though one US grower guide
  describes its performance on salinity-prone soils as "not exceptional
  but compares well with other commonly grown commercial crops"; density
  optimisation (closer spacing under saline conditions) can help offset
  the effect [4].
- **Genome:** Diploid, 2n=34; 1C ≈ 3.7 pg (≈3,619 Mb) per one 2005
  reference [11]; broadly consistent (not a documented conflict) with
  independent genomic-assembly estimates of roughly 2,871-3,189 Mb to 3.3
  Gb once converted (Finding 8).
- **Cultivars:** No sourced list of named commercial hybrid lines was
  found at a level of detail worth a full cultivar table beyond trial/
  catalog yield figures already noted above; the two real, well-
  documented cultivar groups are confectionery and oilseed type (Finding
  4), plus a high-oleic vs. conventional (linoleic) oil-chemistry split
  that cuts across both. **Oilseed type**: small, black, high-oil
  achene; predominantly F1 hybrid, open-pollinated available; the modern
  commercial mainstream for crushing; higher oil content (46-51%) and
  lower hull proportion than confectionery type. **Confectionery type**:
  large, black-and-white-striped (or albino/coloured) achene, thick
  hull; F1 hybrid and open-pollinated both used; the in-shell-snack and
  dehulled-kernel market standard; lower oil content, higher hull
  proportion (roughly a fifth to a third of seed weight), larger seed
  size; no measured yield, hundred-kernel-weight, or oil-content
  difference from oilseed type when grown and compared like-for-like.
  One Morocco-Spain study found Morocco's own food-use seed actually
  comes mostly from the oilseed type with low hull, while Spain's in-
  shell snack market draws on the striped confectionery type — a real
  regional-use split, not a universal rule [3]. **High-oleic oil
  chemistry** (a trait, not a market-type split): cuts across both
  oilseed and confectionery groups; a modern breeding development for
  oxidation stability; sold separately from "conventional" sunflower
  oil; oleic acid far higher (≥80% in one spec sheet), linoleic acid far
  lower (≤15%), polyunsaturated fat much lower, monounsaturated fat much
  higher than conventional sunflower oil [2].
- **Propagation:** The overwhelming majority of modern commercial
  plantings are F1 hybrid, consistently described as the industry
  standard across the hybrid-seed-production sources [5][6]. Open-
  pollinated/landrace varieties still exist and are grown, particularly
  in smallholder, organic, or heritage contexts, but are not the
  commercial mainstream. The CMS A line's pollen is non-functional, so no
  hand emasculation is needed; cross-pollination to the R line is
  carried out by honeybees in commercial field production because
  sunflower pollen is too heavy and sticky to travel by wind; hand
  pollination is used only as a costly, lower-throughput fallback,
  mainly in breeder nurseries or isolation cages (Finding 2). Because the
  A line is male-sterile by cytoplasm, not by a simple nuclear gene,
  saving seed from a commercial F1 hybrid's own harvest gives a highly
  unpredictable, largely male-sterile and commercially useless result
  unless the farmer also controls the R-line cross again — a stronger
  seed-saving trap than ordinary nuclear-gene F1 segregation.
- **Harvest:** Single stage, single window — one warm-season annual
  cycle per planting, combine-harvested once; threshing happens in the
  same pass. Harvest at physiological maturity, then dry to 9.5-12%
  moisture for safe long storage (Finding 7).
- **Disease/pest profile:** Sclerotinia head/stalk rot (Finding 7) and
  downy mildew (cosmetic-only on mature plants) are the two documented
  diseases.
- **Heliotropism:** Young plants track the sun east-to-west during the
  day and reorient to face east again overnight, by a circadian
  mechanism (antiphasic east/west stem elongation); tracking stops once
  the stalk stops elongating and the flower matures, after which the
  head stays fixed facing east (Finding 3).

## Hazards

No plant-toxin or food-safety hazard was found for sunflower seed, oil,
or meal in the sources checked this pass. The main real agronomic
problem, Sclerotinia head rot (Finding 7), is a crop disease, not a
human or animal health hazard, so no `TO BE ADDRESSED` entry is recorded
here.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain (oilseed type):** achene harvested (combine, single stage) →
  dried to 9.5-12% moisture → pressed (mechanical expeller, typically
  followed by solvent extraction) → oil (conventional/linoleic or
  high-oleic chemistry) + meal (hull content tuned for cattle vs.
  poultry feed use, Finding 6).
- **Chain (confectionery type):** achene harvested → dried → dehulled
  (hull removed efficiently to preserve whole kernels) → roasted in-
  shell or as kernel → eaten as a snack, used in baking, or sold as bird
  feed.

## Open research questions

- No confirmed world-average yield figure was found; both sourced yield
  figures are managed-trial or breeder-catalog numbers, not a population
  average.
- The soil pH conflict (6.5-7.5 vs. 6.0-7.0) is kept open rather than
  resolved.
- No crop-water-use curve was opened directly from a primary reference
  table this pass; the cited figures all come from secondhand citations
  or individual regional studies with differing results.
- Heliotropism (Finding 3) is a genuinely distinctive, mechanistically
  well-sourced trait without an obvious place in a simple crop-data
  framework built around ordinary flowering/bolting triggers.
- Bee-hive placement/management in isolated hybrid-seed-production
  fields (Finding 2) is a real, specific labor input without a clean
  place in a simple crop-data framework distinct from ordinary field
  pollination.
- No sourced isolation distance, minimum population, or seed-yield-per-
  hectare figure was found for either the open-pollinated seed-
  production path or the F1 hybrid seed-production field.
- No named commercial hybrid cultivar list (5-10 specific named lines)
  was found this pass, beyond the real, well-sourced confectionery/
  oilseed/high-oleic type distinctions already captured.
- No raw-seed or raw-kernel composition table (kcal/protein) was found
  this pass, unlike the pressed-oil composition, which is sourced.

## Estimates and derivations

The `phOptimal` midpoint of 7.0 is the midpoint of the two sourced-but-
conflicting ranges (6.5-7.5 and 6.0-7.0); both ranges overlap at
6.5-7.0, so 7.0 sits inside both, but the conflict itself is kept visible
above rather than silently resolved by this midpoint alone. No other
DERIVED values are restated here as reusable formulas; the drought/
waterlogging-tolerance ratings and generic C3 photosynthesis placeholder
are judgement calls flagged as such in Research findings and Agronomic
data above, rather than formula-based derivations.
