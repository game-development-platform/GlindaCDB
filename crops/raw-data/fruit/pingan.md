## Naming and scope

*Artocarpus sarawakensis* F.M.Jarrett (Moraceae): **mountain tarap**; also
called **pingan** in trade and some references [2][4]. It is a rare,
Sarawak-endemic evergreen tree in the same genus as marang, jackfruit,
champedak, breadfruit and peluntan [2][5]. This doc covers the cropID `pingan`,
which was proposed from `marang.md` for this species [5].

**Naming conflict — resolved here (the reason this row was flagged).** The name
"pingan" is contested across **three** *Artocarpus*, and resolving it was an
explicit open item for this row [5]:
- The **current molecular authority, Gardner et al. (2022)**, assigns the Iban
  name **pingan** to ***A. mutabilis*** — which this project already researched
  as the `artocarpus_mutabilis` row (the *wild sister of marang*) [3][5].
- **Older PROSEA material** gives "pingan" as an Iban name for the lumped
  **marang** (*A. odoratissimus*) [1][5].
- **Wikipedia and horticultural/trade sources** apply "pingan" (and "mountain
  tarap") to ***A. sarawakensis*** — this row [2][4].

So the same vernacular is used for three species by different authorities. To
avoid a collision with the `artocarpus_mutabilis` row (which, following Gardner
2022, is the better holder of "pingan"), **this doc uses "mountain tarap" as the
preferred common name for *A. sarawakensis***, and recommends the cropID itself
be renamed (e.g. `mountain_tarap` or `artocarpus_sarawakensis`); that rename is
left to the maintainer (see Open questions) [3][5]. Classification/naming is a
sourcing matter, not a crop trait, so it is noted here rather than logged as a
schema exception.

**Distinctiveness (gate check, 2026-11-27): passes.** *A. sarawakensis* is a
**validly distinct species**, not a form of another row, and clears the check
much as its sibling species did (and unlike the rejected infraspecific *D.
longan* var. *echinatus*) [2][5]:
- **Taxonomy:** a separate *Artocarpus* species [2].
- **Range:** **endemic to Sarawak and rather rare** (PROSEA), a narrow range of
  its own, against marang's wide Borneo-plus-Philippines spread [1][2][5].
- **Farming system:** a **wild forest tree, gathered not grown** ("not grown at
  all"), distinct from marang's cultivation and from market-traded peluntan
  [1][4][5].
- **Fruit / uses:** orange, marang-shaped but with **smaller kernel sections**,
  eaten fresh locally and said to be of **good flavour** [1][2].

**Relatives in this project** [5]: `marang` (*A. odoratissimus*, the proposing
doc and nearest look-alike), `artocarpus_mutabilis` (the wild sister of marang,
and the taxon that properly holds "pingan"), `peluntan` (*A. sericicarpus*),
and the other *Artocarpus* rows `jackfruit`, `champedak`, `breadfruit`,
`lakoocha`.

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — a rare Sarawak-endemic forest fruit, gathered from the wild
rather than cultivated.** PROSEA describes *A. sarawakensis* as **endemic to
Sarawak and rather rare**, with **edible fruits of good flavour** [1]. Grower
and reference sources agree the fruit is **harvested from the wild** and eaten
locally, and state plainly that it is **"not grown at all"** as a planted crop
[4]. This places it with the project's other wild/minor Bornean *Artocarpus*
(the uncultivated `artocarpus_mutabilis`), rather than with cultivated marang or
market-traded `peluntan` [5]. Its distribution is given as Sarawak (type from
Bintulu District, in lowland dipterocarp forest), with a disputed additional
record from Riau, Sumatra that conflicts with the "endemic to Borneo/Sarawak"
accounts (see Open questions) [2].

**Finding 2 — a marang look-alike, which is exactly the source of the naming
confusion.** The fruit is **orange and shaped like marang**, but with **smaller
kernel sections** [2]. Sources repeatedly note it is **"very similar to, and
often confused with," *A. odoratissimus*** [2]. This close resemblance — not
any true synonymy — is why "pingan" and marang data get mixed, and why
delimiting what is genuinely *A. sarawakensis* needs care. One Flora Malesiana
figure of an "ellipsoid fruit c. 1.3 cm" is best read as an individual
drupe/achene rather than the whole syncarp, and is treated as dubious at face
value here [2].

**Finding 3 — biological and agronomic data are very thin, reflecting a
little-studied rare species.** A 2025 Bornean functional-foods review notes that
**reports on its biological activity are very rare** [1]. No bearing age, yield,
composition, propagation, storage or season data specific to *A. sarawakensis*
was found this pass; relatives (marang, peluntan) supply the only analogues, and
are not borrowed as figures here [1][2][5].

## Agronomic data

Little is documented for this species specifically; most lines are honestly
"Not sourced," with marang/peluntan as the nearest (uncited-for-figures)
analogues [5].

- **Life form / season:** Perennial evergreen tree, to about **25 m** [2].
  Fruiting season: Not sourced.
- **Harvest pattern:** Perennial bearing; fruit **gathered from the wild** when
  ripe (orange) [1][4]. Window: Not sourced.
- **Days/years to first bearing:** Not sourced.
- **Climate zones:** Wet equatorial lowland tropics — **lowland dipterocarp
  forest** in Sarawak [2]. Frost-tender; no chill requirement.
- **Composition:** Not sourced (a 2025 review notes biological-activity data are
  "very rare") [1].
- **Edibility:** Pulp eaten **fresh**, of **good flavour** [1][2]. Seed
  edibility: Not sourced for this species (edible in relatives) [5].
- **Pollination:** Not sourced. (*Artocarpus* are monoecious with separate
  male/female heads; no species-specific detail found.)
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced; not expected to apply.
- **Yield:** Not sourced.
- **Water use:** Not sourced.
- **Propagation:** Not sourced for this species; presumably by seed as in the
  genus, but no germination or vegetative-propagation data found [4][5].
- **Labor:** Not sourced.
- **Soil:** Lowland forest soils; pH, texture and salinity: Not sourced [2].
- **Storage (postharvest):** Not sourced. By analogy to marang the ripe fruit is
  likely very perishable, but nothing species-specific was found [5].
- **Establishment:** Not planted as a crop in the sources found (wild-gathered)
  [4].
- **Cultivars:** None — a wild species, not under selection [1][4].
- **Weather response:** Frost-tender wet-tropics tree; tolerances not quantified
  [2].
- **Water regime:** Ordinary tropical rainfall; no paddy requirement [2].
- **Harvest stages / windows:** Gathered on ripe (orange) colour; seasonal
  windows not sourced [1][2].
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** Sarawak-endemic and **rare** — the biosecurity
  concern, if any, runs the other way (a restricted-range native of conservation
  interest rather than a weed); no invasive listing found, and no
  cultivation/movement restriction specific to it found [1][2].
- **Controlled environment:** Not sourced.

## Hazards

- No specific human-toxicity hazard was found; the fresh fruit is eaten locally
  [1][2]. Like other *Artocarpus*, the tree exudes **sticky latex** when cut, a
  handling nuisance rather than a food hazard [5]. **No hazards found in this
  pass** beyond the latex note.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain (fruit):** wild-gathered ripe fruit → opened → **pulp eaten fresh**,
  good flavour [1][2]. Marang-style fritters/ice-cream uses are reported but
  appear to describe marang preparation applied by analogy, not documented
  *A. sarawakensis* practice, so they are not claimed here [1].
- **Not otherwise detailed here:** seed use (undocumented for this species);
  composition; any processed products; timber (undocumented for this species,
  though common in the genus) [5].

## Open research questions

- **Should the cropID be renamed?** Following Gardner et al. (2022), "pingan"
  belongs to *A. mutabilis* (the `artocarpus_mutabilis` row). Keeping this row's
  id as `pingan` perpetuates the collision; renaming it to `mountain_tarap` (or
  `artocarpus_sarawakensis`) and updating the cross-links in `marang.md` and
  `artocarpus-mutabilis.md` is recommended but left to the maintainer [3][5].
- **Range conflict:** endemic to Sarawak (PROSEA) vs an additional Riau,
  Sumatra record (Flora Malesiana) — unresolved [1][2].
- **Essentially all agronomy** (bearing age, yield, season, composition,
  propagation, storage, seed edibility) is unsourced; this is a little-studied
  rare species [1][2].
- **How much "marang"/"pingan" data is really *A. sarawakensis*,** given the
  century of confusion and the shared names — the same delimitation problem
  flagged under `artocarpus_mutabilis` [2][5].
- **Deeper sources not read this pass:** the full PROSEA *A. sarawakensis*
  entry, Jarrett's *Artocarpus* revision (Blumea, 1975), Flora Malesiana, and
  any Lim compendium entry [1][2].
