## Naming and scope

*Citrus sphaerocarpa* Hort. ex Tanaka (Rutaceae): kabosu (かぼす, カボス),
a Japanese sour citrus (*kōsan kankitsu*) [1][3]. Like sudachi it is sold
green and unripe, for its juice and aroma [5].

**Parentage is disputed:**
- **Genetic survey:** a summary of a NARO survey of 67 Japanese sour
  citrus says kabosu came from a cross of yuzu, kunenbo and a parent of
  Unshū mikan [4].
- **Mutation:** a US germplasm note says it arose as a spontaneous
  mutation of yuzu in Ōita. RAPD markers separated it from other yuzu-like
  fruit but did not clarify its origin [3].
- **Chance seedling:** an older account calls it a chance seedling in Ōita
  [3].
- **Papeda × sour orange:** the same germplasm page also says "believed
  to be a hybrid of papeda and sour orange" [3]. An encyclopedia gives
  ichang papeda × bitter orange, unsourced [1].
- **Resemblance:** a 1967 reference found it "virtually indistinguishable"
  from Zadaidai except for its normal calyx [3].

**Name clash:** kabosu is not the *kabusu* of the daidai group (see
`daidai.md`).

**Gate check (2026-11-19): passed.** Kabosu was proposed from
`hetsuka-daidai.md` and checked against its nearest researched relatives,
`daidai` and `hetsuka_daidai`:
- **Taxonomy:** a separately named taxon with its own disputed but
  yuzu-linked hybrid origin. Daidai is a bitter orange type [1][3][4].
- **Range:** over 90% of national output is from Ōita, at 541 ha in 2023,
  the largest area of any prefecture [2][11].
- **Farming system:** open-field, greenhouse and long cold storage for
  year-round green fruit. Shipping dates are set by a juice-content
  standard of at least 20%. It has its own breeding programme and a
  registered seedless triploid [2][6][7][11].
- **Harvest:** green, from August, for juice and aroma; never ripe [1][11].
- **Uses:** a condiment and vinegar substitute, plus drinks and
  processing, and a feed additive for branded farmed fish [1][12].

It has a geographical indication (GI) and a regional trademark [11], and
more agronomic data than hetsuka daidai.

**Separate entries:**
- **Yuzu** (`yuzu`) and **sudachi** (`sudachi`): proposed rows, named in
  the parentage claims [4]. **Kunenbo** (`kunenbo`), also named there,
  passed its gate check on 2026-11-19 and is researched in `kunenbo.md`.
- **Ichang papeda** (`ichang_papeda`): proposed here because of the
  papeda-parent claim [1][3]; rejected at its gate check (below).

**Gate check for `ichang_papeda` (2026-11-19): failed.** *Citrus
cavaleriei* H.Lév. ex Cavalerie (syn. *C. ichangensis* Swingle; 宜昌橙)
was checked as a candidate crop row:
- **Taxonomy:** a distinct wild papeda species, so it would qualify on
  taxonomy alone [13][14].
- **Range:** wild in the mountains, hills and valleys of west and
  south-west China, below 2,500 m [14]. A wild population of about 20
  plants was first recorded in Guangdong in April 2026 [14]. A news report
  says it has been a nationally protected wild plant (class II) since
  1999; that was not confirmed against the official list [14].
- **Farming system:** none found. It is "relatively rare in
  cultivation," and occasionally grown as an ornamental [13]. One Chinese
  summary says it is used as a rootstock for edible citrus, without
  detail [14].
- **Harvest and uses:** the fruit is small and mandarin-like, with many
  large monoembryonic seeds and little bitter or sour juice. Some fruit
  has no juice at all [13]. It is valued mainly for its fragrance and as a
  breeding parent. It is the hardiest citrus after trifoliate orange
  [13][14].

Its real role is as a cold-hardy parent of crops this project tracks.
Yuzu is given as Ichang papeda × mandarin and the Ichang lemon
(shangjuan) as Ichang papeda × pomelo; it is also a claimed parent of
kabosu [1][13]. That genetic role belongs in those crops' parentage
notes, not in a crop row of its own. No useful agronomic data for this
entry came out of the check.

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — an Ōita crop built up from garden trees since 1970.**
- **Old trees:** Usuki, Ōita, has a "mother tree" estimated at 300 years
  old, a prefectural protected tree still bearing over 100 kg a year [5].
  Many trees there are about 200 years old [1].
- **Legend:** a doctor named Sōgen brought seed or a sapling from Kyoto in
  the Edo period [1].
- **Growth:** output was only 360 t in 1970. Prefectural promotion from
  1971 raised it to 4,600 t on 572 ha by 1984. Kabosu was planted to
  replace surplus Unshū mikan [5].
- **Recent output:** 5,185 t nationally in 2007, of which Ōita grew 5,019
  t, Aichi 144 t and Miyazaki 17 t. Ōita grew about 6,587 t in 2009, 3,623
  t in 2010 and 5,273 t in 2011 [1].
- **2023 and target:** in 2023 Ōita grew 6,500 t on 541 ha, worth ¥2.7
  billion. The 2030 target is 6,000 t on 600 ha [2].
- **Districts:** Usuki, Taketa, Bungo-Ōno, Kunisaki and Bungotakada [2].
- **Protection:** "Ōita kabosu" was GI-registered in May 2017 and became
  a regional trademark in February 2026 [11].
- **Trends:** corporate entrants are increasing and farms are getting
  larger. Harvest labour is the main burden. Falling shipments have left
  processors short of fruit [2].

**Finding 2 — a year-round green-fruit calendar from three systems.**
- **Greenhouse:** heated and unheated houses ship from March [2][7], or
  from June under vinyl cover in the 1980s [5].
- **Open field:** harvest starts green in late August and peaks in
  September. Fruit turns yellow around October-November [2][11].
- **Storage:** fruit picked before colour break is stored and shipped
  until about February [2][11].
- **Shipping standard:** Ōita sets a "season-opening" date each year,
  predicted from its own data as the day juice content reaches about 20%
  across the prefecture [11].
- **Cultivars by system:** 'Ōita 1' and 'Kaminokawa' for greenhouses;
  'Ōita 1' and 'Toyo-no-midori' for open-field and storage fruit [7].

**Finding 3 — storage is a trade-off between degreening and rind
injury.**
- **Problem:** at room temperature after November, rind degreening and
  acid loss limit shelf life [5].
- **Earlier work:** fruit stored at 1-30 °C showed 5 °C to be the safe
  lower limit. Lower temperatures slow degreening but raise rind injury
  and rot [5].
- **Polyethylene-bagged fruit (picked 1 October):**
  - at 4 °C, irregular sunken brown rind lesions began around day 30 in
    all dip and vacuum treatments, and rot followed after removal;
  - at 8 and 12 °C there was no such injury;
  - chlorophyll broke down at 12 °C, was retained at 8 °C, and rose at
    4 °C [5].
- **Hormone treatment:** NAA 100 ppm + GA₃ 10 ppm + BA 50 ppm by vacuum
  infiltration gave the best overall result [5].
- **Modern cultivar trial at 3 °C (fruit picked 1 September):** by 14
  January, calyx browning scored 79.6 in 'Ōita Kaken 6', 45.6 in 'Ōita 1'
  and 11.4 in 'Toyo-no-midori'. Puffiness scored 4.4, 17.8 and 11.1 [6].
  'Ōita Kaken 6' keeps its green colour better than 'Ōita 1' [6].
- **Juice loss:** in a 2017 trial, juice content fell from 30.7-33.2% at
  harvest (30 August) to 22.3-28.2% by 15 November [9].

**Finding 4 — breeding for seedlessness in a polyembryonic citrus.**
- **Seed counts:** 'Ōita 1' had 27 full seeds per fruit and
  'Toyo-no-midori' 32 [6][7]. Low-seeded 'Sobo-no-kaori' and 'Kaminokawa'
  are small and store poorly [6].
- **'Kaminokawa':** 70-80% of the weight of 'Ōita 1', with a higher juice
  yield, a slightly lower Brix and more peel oil [8].
- **'Ōita Kaken 6':** a triploid from a native tetraploid × 'Toyo-no-
  midori', made by embryo culture because kabosu is polyembryonic [6]:
  - registered on 17 January 2022 (no. 28847);
  - 92.5 g fruit, 28.9% juice, Brix 8.0 and 5.3 g citric acid per 100 ml;
  - almost seedless, but 1-2 seeds if cross-pollinated by other citrus,
    so isolated orchards are advised [6];
  - can ship from early August [7].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Evergreen thorny tree [1][3]. Twenty-year-old
  trees on citrange rootstocks were about 3 m tall at Riverside, with a
  dense, spreading habit [3]. Thorns up to 5 mm [3]. Fruit-bearing shoots
  of 'Ōita Kaken 6' droop and lose thorns once cropping [6].
- **Harvest pattern:** Perennial. The open-field crop is picked green
  from late August to October, and stored fruit is sold to February
  (Finding 2) [2][11]. Labour at harvest is the main constraint [2]. The
  number of passes is not sourced.
- **Days/years to first bearing:** Not sourced for grafted trees. Seedlings
  take about 10 years, so breeders top-work selections [7]. Productive
  life: the old Usuki trees still bear after about 300 years [5].
- **Climate zones:**
  - **Ōita siting criteria:** annual mean of at least 14 °C, and a winter
    absolute minimum no lower than -6 °C. Yuzu's are 13 °C and -7 °C, and
    lemon's 15.5 °C and -3 °C [2].
  - **Wind:** avoid windy sites, to prevent blemished and diseased fruit
    [2].
  - **Elevation:** not sourced. Ōita districts include the Kyushu
    mountain zone, with an annual mean of 16.8 °C and an extreme minimum of
    -6.2 °C at Hita [2].
  - **Chill:** not applicable; it is evergreen. A chill requirement is not
    listed [2].
- **Composition:**
  - **Juice, per 100 g:** water 90.7 g; energy 36 kcal (154 kJ); protein
    0.4 g; fat 0.1 g; carbohydrate 8.5 g; fibre 0.1 g; potassium 140 mg;
    calcium 7 mg; vitamin C 42 mg [10].
  - **Fruit:**
    - weight 71.2 g at Riverside [3]; 59-159 g in Ōita trials [6];
    - juice content 28.2% at Riverside [3], and 18-33% in Ōita by date and
      cultivar [6][9];
    - citric acid 3.2% at Riverside [3], and 4.5-6.3 g/100 ml in Ōita
      [6][9];
    - Brix 7.8-11.2 [6][9];
    - peel 4.2 mm [3] to 6.9 mm [6] thick.
- **Edibility:** Not eaten raw as a fruit. Juice and zest are condiments
  [1].
- **Pollination:**
  - **Seeds:** seeded cultivars carry 17-32 seeds [3][6].
  - **Seedless cultivar:** the triploid 'Ōita Kaken 6' sets fruit without
    seeds, but other citrus pollen adds seeds, so isolation is advised
    [6].
  - **Pollinators and pollinizers:** whether pollinizers or bees are
    needed for set is not sourced.
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced. Not applicable to storage
  organs.
- **Yield:**
  - **Ōita average:** about 12 t/ha in 2023 (see Estimates, below) [2].
  - **Management targets:** 25 t/ha [2]; 28 t/ha in a family model and 20
    t/ha in a 10 ha corporate model [2].
  - **1984:** about 8 t/ha [5].
- **Water use:** Not sourced. Irrigation to relieve tree water stress is
  listed among general citrus measures in the plan [2].
- **Propagation:**
  - **Polyembryony:** kabosu is polyembryonic [6], so seedlings are
    largely nucellar (true to type); that is an inference.
  - **Grafting:** selections are top-worked to speed flowering [7]. The
    plan calls for secure supplies of nursery trees and good budwood [2].
    Rootstocks for commercial Japanese orchards were not sourced.
  - **Clonal/viral risk:** the original US import carried citrus
    tristeza virus, removed by thermotherapy [3]. Grafting from healthy
    budwood is therefore the safeguard; that is an inference.
- **Labor:**
  - **Management target:** 296 h per 10 a, or about 2,960 h/ha [2].
  - **Model farms:** about 2,090 h/ha for a 50 a family farm and 800 h/ha
    for a 10 ha corporate farm (see Estimates, below) [2].
  - **Mechanisation:** the plan pushes mechanised orchard layouts to cut
    harvest labour [2].
- **Soil:** Not sourced specifically.
  - **Salinity:** not sourced.
  - **Fertiliser:** appropriate fertiliser and soil improvement are listed
    as general measures [2].
- **Storage (postharvest):**
  - **Temperature:** 5 °C was the safe lower limit in early work. At 4 °C,
    rind pitting appeared, while 8 °C in polyethylene bags gave good
    colour without injury (Finding 3) [5].
  - **3 °C trial:** storage to January ran with calyx browning and
    puffiness by cultivar [6].
  - **Shelf life:** stored fruit sells until about February [2][11].
- **Establishment:**
  - **Spacing:** not sourced.
  - **Isolation:** windbreak nets are needed against canker [6], and
    seedless cultivars should be kept away from other citrus [6].
  - **Reflective sheets:** laid under the canopy from early July, they
    deepen the green of lower fruit and improve colour retention in
    storage [9].
- **Cultivars:**
  - **'Ōita 1':** the main cultivar; seeded [6][7][8].
  - **'Toyo-no-midori':** seeded; best storage [6][7].
  - **'Sobo-no-kaori':** low-seeded; small [6][7].
  - **'Kaminokawa':** seedless type; smaller; higher juice [7][8].
  - **'Ōita Kaken 6':** triploid, nearly seedless, early (2022) [6][7].
  - **Native tetraploid:** a breeding parent [6].
- **Weather response:**
  - **Wind:** wind and canker are linked; sites exposed to strong wind
    are avoided [2][6].
  - **Cold:** winter minima below -6 °C are unsuitable [2].
  - **Light:** sunlight on fruit deepens green colour [9].
- **Water regime:** Rainfed or irrigated orchard; no flooded requirement
  [2].
- **Harvest stages / windows:**
  - **Green stage:** the sales stage, shipped from when juice reaches
    about 20% [11].
  - **Yellow stage:** yellowing from October-November lowers acid and
    flavour [5][11].
  - **Early-picked fruit:** fruit picked around 1 September tops 30% juice
    [9].
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** No listing found. Tristeza was found in
  imported material [3]. Canker susceptibility is noted for all cultivars
  [6].
- **Controlled environment:** Heated and unheated greenhouse production
  for spring and early-summer fruit [2][5][7].

## Hazards

- **Citrus canker:** all cultivars are prone, so windbreaks and thorough
  control on young trees are needed [6].
- **Citrus tristeza virus:** found in the original US import [3].
- **Storage disorders:** rind pitting at 4 °C, plus calyx browning, puffy
  fruit and rot after storage [5][6].
- **Thorns:** thorns in the leaf axils [3].
- **Fungal contamination of the product:** rot after cold storage [5].
  No mycotoxin finding.
- **Chemical:** no plant toxin found. Peel oil is high in seedless
  'Kaminokawa' [8]. Phototoxicity was not sourced.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain 1 (fresh green fruit):** picked green → graded by colour, shape
  and blemish under the shipping manual → shipped fresh, or
  polyethylene-bagged and cold-stored → sold to February [5][11].
- **Chain 2 (condiment):** juice squeezed over sashimi and grilled fish;
  used in *ponzu*, as a vinegar substitute, and in miso soup, noodles and
  *shōchū*. Fruit is cut in radial quarters and squeezed peel-down to keep
  seeds out [1].
- **Chain 3 (processing):**
  - fruit for processing is collected alongside fresh fruit [2];
  - products are juice and drinks, condiments, frozen desserts, snacks,
    *wagashi* and pastries [1];
  - in model farms, processing fruit is paid about ¥60/kg against ¥330/kg
    for fresh fruit [2];
  - Ōita research covers frozen green peel and powders made from juicing
    residue, of which over 900 t a year is produced [12].
- **Chain 4 (aquaculture feed):** kabosu added to fish feed slows
  colour loss in the dark muscle. It is used for "Kabosu Buri" (amberjack)
  and "Kabosu Hirame" (flounder), and also for yellowtail amberjack and
  pufferfish [1][12]. Kabosu Buri ships October-March and is tested lot by
  lot [12].
- **Other:** dried peel has been burned as a mosquito repellent; the tree
  is an ornamental [3].
- **Cultural:** the mascot "Kabotan" dates from the 2003 National Greening
  Fair in Ōita [1].

No religious or ritual use was found in the sources consulted.

## Open research questions

- **Parentage:** a three-way hybrid with yuzu [4], a yuzu mutation [3],
  or papeda × sour orange [1][3]. The primary NARO data were not opened.
- **Storage temperature:** 5 °C safe limit [5], 4 °C injurious [5], and a
  3 °C cultivar trial [6]. These need reconciling; cultivar, harvest date
  and bagging may explain the differences.
- **Acidity:** Riverside-grown fruit measured 3.2% citric acid [3],
  against 4.5-6.3 g/100 ml in Ōita [6][9]. This may reflect climate or
  sampling date.
- **Labour table:** the plan's extracted table pairs kabosu with 2,500
  kg/10 a and 296 h/10 a; the column alignment should be confirmed [2].
- **Not sourced:** rootstocks, spacing, years to first crop for grafted
  trees, water use, soil and salinity, photoperiod, soil biology, pests
  other than canker, pollination needs for set.

## Estimates and derivations

**Ōita average yield.**
- 2023: 6,500 t ÷ 541 ha [2] = 12.0 t/ha.
- 2030 target: 6,000 t ÷ 600 ha [2] = 10.0 t/ha.
- 1984: 4,600 t ÷ 572 ha [5] = 8.0 t/ha.

**Targets per hectare** (1 ha = 10 × 10 a) [2]:
- 2,500 kg/10 a = 25 t/ha;
- 2,800 kg/10 a = 28 t/ha;
- 2,000 kg/10 a = 20 t/ha.

**Labour per hectare** [2]:
- target: 296 h/10 a = 2,960 h/ha;
- family model: 1,044 h ÷ 0.5 ha = 2,088 h/ha;
- corporate model: 7,960 h ÷ 10 ha = 796 h/ha.

**Model farm revenue check** [2]:
- **Family model:** 0.5 ha × 28 t = 14.0 t. Fresh share: 70% × 14 t × ¥330
  = ¥3.234 million. Processing share: 30% × 14 t × ¥60 = ¥0.252 million.
  Total ¥3.486 million, matching the plan.
- **Corporate model:** 10 ha × 20 t = 200 t. Fresh share: 30% × ¥330 =
  ¥19.8 million. Processing share: 70% × ¥60 = ¥8.4 million. Total ¥28.2
  million, matching the plan.

**Average value.** ¥2.7 billion ÷ 6,500 t [2] = about ¥415/kg.

**Ōita share in 2007.** 5,019 ÷ 5,185 t [1] = 96.8%.

**Juice per tonne.** 18-33% juice [6][9] = 180-330 kg of juice per tonne
of fruit. The Riverside figure checks out: 20.2 ÷ 71.2 g [3] = 28.4%.
