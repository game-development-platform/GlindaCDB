## Naming and scope

*Garcinia prainiana* King (Clusiaceae, also called Guttiferae), a small
fruit-bearing *Garcinia* of the Malay Peninsula [2][3]. It is the very example
`mangosteen.md` gives for the "mangosteen = whole genus" name clash: the common
name **button mangosteen** applies the mangosteen name to a different, orange,
thin-skinned *Garcinia* [2][6].

- **Names:** button mangosteen; **cherapu** / **cerapu**, and **mencupu** in Malay
  [1][2][3]. The fruit is often sold and described as a small, orange "mangosteen
  alternative" [1][5].
- **Range:** native to Peninsular Malaysia and Peninsular (southern) Thailand,
  growing in lowland and hill forest and on ridges up to about 900 m [2][3][4].
  IUCN Red List: Least Concern [3].
- **Name clash — "mangosteen" for the genus.** As `mangosteen.md` notes, the
  mangosteen name is loosely used across *Garcinia*; "button mangosteen" is that
  usage, not a form of true mangosteen (*G. mangostana*) [2][6].

**Gate check (2026-11-25): passes.** Proposed from `mangosteen.md` as "an edible
*Garcinia* fruit... no farming data were read this pass" [6]. It is distinct
enough for its own row on every count, and sharply so against its parent:
- **Taxonomy:** a validly distinct species, *G. prainiana*, not a variety of true
  mangosteen [2][3].
- **Range:** Peninsular Malaysia and Peninsular Thailand [2][3], a narrower
  Malesian range than true mangosteen's pantropical cultivation [6].
- **Reproduction — the sharpest distinction:** button mangosteen is **dioecious**
  and sexually reproducing, so **both a male and a female tree are needed to set
  fruit** (growers hand-pollinate) [3][4]. True mangosteen is the opposite:
  apomictic, with **no known male trees**, setting seed without fertilisation
  (`mangosteen.md` Finding 1) [6].
- **Farming system / harvest / uses:** a cultivated minor fruit that bears
  **early** (it can fruit while only a few feet tall) [1][5] — versus mangosteen's
  famous 10-20-year wait [6] — with a bright-orange, thin, hand-peelable rind
  rather than mangosteen's thick purple one; eaten fresh (Finding 1). It is also
  itself used as a **rootstock for true mangosteen** [1][3].

**Relatives in this project:** `mangosteen.md` (the proposing doc and the species
button mangosteen is grafted under), and the other *Garcinia* rows it lists —
`kokum.md` (*G. indica*) and `malabar-tamarind.md` (*G. gummi-gutta*), filed as
souring spices, both dioecious like button mangosteen [6]. The sibling `PROPOSED`
rows `charichuelo` (*G. madruno*) and `garcinia_dulcis` (*G. dulcis*) remain
unresearched. No new relatives were surfaced for proposing.

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — a small orange "mangosteen alternative" that bears young.** The
fruit is bright orange, button- or tangerine-shaped, about 4-5 cm across, with a
**thin skin that peels easily by hand** and segments whose seeds separate cleanly
[1][2]. The pulp is sweet to sour, its flavour likened to purple mangosteen but
more acidic [2][5]. It is eaten raw — in Malaysia sometimes with *sambal belacan*
or added to dishes [1]. The tree is small (to ~10 m), very slow-growing but
long-lived, and notable for fruiting **while still only a few feet tall** [1][3],
with fruiting variously reported from about 4-5 years (nursery claim) to ~6-7
years [1][3] — far earlier than true mangosteen's 10-20 years [6].

**Finding 2 — dioecious and insect- or hand-pollinated, the reverse of true
mangosteen.** Male and female flowers are borne on **separate trees**, so an
orchard needs both sexes to crop; hand-pollination is reported to give good fruit
set [3][4]. This is the defining physiological contrast with true mangosteen,
which has no male trees and sets fruit apomictically (`mangosteen.md` Finding 1)
[6]. No cultivar names, sex-ratio practice or flowering-trigger (e.g. a dry-spell
requirement like mangosteen's) were sourced for button mangosteen specifically.

**Finding 3 — propagated by seed, and a rootstock for its famous cousin.** The
standard propagation route is seed, which germinates slowly [4][5]. Young
*G. prainiana* plants are used as **rootstock for true mangosteen** — a direct
horticultural link between the two rows [1][3]. No grafting protocol specific to
*G. prainiana* as the scion was found (other *Garcinia* species have published
wedge- and approach-grafting methods, but those are not this species). Whether
button mangosteen inherits any of true mangosteen's recalcitrant-seed handling was
not sourced.

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Small evergreen tree to about 10 m; very slow-growing,
  long-lived [1][3]. Like all *Garcinia* it carries yellow latex (by genus habit;
  not separately sourced for this species).
- **Harvest pattern:** Perennial bearing; fruit eaten fresh. Number of seasons a
  year and months not sourced for this species.
- **Days/years to first bearing:** early for the genus — can fruit while only a
  few feet tall; roughly 4-7 years is variously reported [1][3]. Much earlier than
  true mangosteen [6].
- **Climate zones:** humid tropical Malesia; lowland and hill forest and ridges up
  to about 900 m [3][4]. Frost-intolerant; one older Florida grower account calls
  it among the more cold-sensitive edible *Garcinia*, but this is unconfirmed
  [5]. Chill requirement: none (equatorial). Mean temperatures / rainfall band:
  Not sourced to figures.
- **Composition:** Not sourced. No fruit-chemistry or nutrition analysis specific
  to *G. prainiana* was found (nursery "xanthone / vitamin C" claims are marketing
  and are not relied on) [1].
- **Edibility:** the orange pulp is raw-edible, sweet to sour [1][2]. Seed
  edibility not sourced.
- **Pollination:** required — dioecious, both sexes needed; hand-pollination gives
  good set (Finding 2) [3][4]. Pollinator species not sourced.
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced. Not applicable to any storage organ.
- **Yield:** Not sourced. No per-tree or per-hectare figures were found.
- **Water use:** Not sourced. A wet-tropics forest-edge tree.
- **Propagation:** by seed (slow to germinate); young trees serve as mangosteen
  rootstock (Finding 3) [1][3][4][5]. Clonal/viral degeneration: Not sourced.
- **Labor:** Not sourced. Hand-pollination (Finding 2) is an added labour step
  that true mangosteen, being apomictic, does not need [3][6].
- **Soil:** Not sourced to specifics; a lowland/hill-forest tree. pH, texture and
  **salinity tolerance: Not sourced** (true mangosteen is salt-sensitive, but that
  is not confirmed here) [6].
- **Storage (postharvest):** Not sourced. The thin rind (Finding 1) suggests a
  shorter, more perishable shelf life than thick-rinded mangosteen, but this is an
  inference, not sourced.
- **Establishment:** Not sourced (planting method, spacing). Both a male and a
  female tree must be established to crop (Finding 2) [4].
- **Cultivars:** None sourced; no named selections were found.
- **Weather response:** Not sourced to figures. Frost-intolerant; possibly
  relatively cold-sensitive within the genus [5]. Frost-as-a-quality-event: not
  applicable.
- **Water regime:** rain-fed wet-tropics tree; no flooded/paddy requirement known.
- **Harvest stages / windows:** picked when the rind colours bright orange and the
  thin skin yields; window and successiveness not sourced [1][2].
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** native to Peninsular Malaysia and Peninsular
  Thailand [2][3]; IUCN Least Concern [3]. No invasive listing was found. As a
  slow-growing, seed-propagated forest tree it has no weedy reputation. Movement /
  quarantine rules (true mangosteen is a regulated fruit-fly host): not sourced
  for this species.
- **Controlled environment:** Not sourced; grown outdoors in the tropics and as a
  rare-fruit specimen in warmer collections [5].

## Hazards

- **Ordinary fruit:** no toxicity sourced for the pulp, which is eaten raw [1][2].
- **Latex staining:** *Garcinia* yellow latex stains skin and fabric (a genus
  trait, as recorded for true mangosteen `mangosteen.md`); not separately measured
  here [6].
- **Ergot / mycotoxin, allergy:** none sourced.
- **TO BE ADDRESSED** — nothing specific for this crop beyond the genus latex-stain
  note; no supplement/extract hazard applies here as it does for mangosteen peel.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain (fresh):** bright-orange fruit hand-picked → thin rind peeled by hand →
  segments eaten raw, sweet-sour, sometimes with *sambal belacan* [1][2].
- **Culinary:** added to local dishes in Malaysia [1].
- **Rootstock:** young *G. prainiana* trees are grown as rootstock for true
  mangosteen (Finding 3) [1][3].
- **Ornamental / landscape:** planted as an ornamental fruit tree [4].
- **Not otherwise detailed here:** any preserve, juice or processed use was not
  found; composition and processing literature is lacking for this species.
