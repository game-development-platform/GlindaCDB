## Naming and scope

*Citrus junos* Siebold ex Tanaka (also written *C.* × *junos*; Rutaceae):
**yuzu** (柚子), Japan's premier aromatic sour citrus; Korean **yuja** (유자)
[1][3]. This doc covers the `yuzu` row, proposed from `hetsuka-daidai.md`,
`kabosu.md` and `ichang_papeda.md` [4].

- **Taxonomy (a cold-hardy natural hybrid):** yuzu is a **natural hybrid of
  Ichang papeda (*C. cavaleriei* / *C. ichangensis*) and a mandarin** — sources
  variously name the mangshanye mandarin, sour mandarin (*C. reticulata*) or
  satsuma as the mandarin parent, and place its origin on the **upper Yangtze**;
  the parentage remains debated [1]. Its defining **cold-hardiness comes from the
  Ichang-papeda parent** (Finding 1) [1]. It is thus the aromatic, cold-hardy
  counterpart to its sibling sour citrus and the cultivated expression of the
  wild `ichang_papeda` that this project rejected as a standalone crop [1][4].

**Distinctiveness (gate check, 2026-11-27): passes, strongly.** The flagship of
the Japanese sour-citrus group, clearly distinct from every relative [1][2][3]:
- **vs the sour-citrus siblings** (`kabosu`, `sudachi`, `shiikuwasha`,
  `hetsuka_daidai`): a different species (*C. junos*) and by far the **largest
  and most commercial** of the group (~23,000-27,000 t/yr in Japan), with its own
  GIs, booming exports, and uses the others lack (the **winter-solstice bath**,
  broad cosmetics) [2][3].
- **vs its parent `ichang_papeda`** (rejected as a non-crop): yuzu is the
  intensely cultivated, edible, economically major hybrid, not the wild papeda
  [1][4].
- **Range/hardiness:** uniquely among the group, grown well **outside the
  subtropics** — the hardiest common edible citrus (Finding 1) [1].

**Relatives in this project:** `hetsuka-daidai.md`, `kabosu.md`, `sudachi.md`,
`shiikuwasha.md`, `kunenbo.md` (the Japanese sour-citrus cluster), `ichang_papeda`
(its hardy wild parent), and `mandarin.md` [4].

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — the cold-hardy citrus: it grows where others can't.** Yuzu
inherits strong **frost tolerance from its Ichang-papeda parent** and is among
the **hardiest widely grown edible citrus** (after trifoliate orange / *Poncirus*
and Ichang papeda itself), tolerating winters to about **−7 to −9 °C** [1]. This
is why, beyond its East Asian heartland (Japan, Korea), it has recently been
taken up in **New Zealand, Australia, Spain, Italy and France** — a cold-tolerant
aromatic citrus for temperate growers [1].

**Finding 2 — Japan's premier aromatic citrus crop: concentrated, thorny,
slow-to-bear, and booming for export.** Japan produces roughly **23,000-27,000 t
a year** (sources differ), with **Kochi Prefecture by far the largest** (~51-52%
and the biggest growing area), **Tokushima second** (~13%) and **Ehime** ~11%
[2]. Several regions hold **geographical indications** (e.g. **Kito yuzu**, Naka
Town, Tokushima; **Monobe yuzu**, Kami City, Kochi) [2]. The trees are **covered
in sharp thorns that scratch the peel**, so fresh fruit must be carefully hand-
harvested and precooled (3-5 °C) to hold fresh-market grade (Finding; schema
Entry 72) [2]. Yuzu is **notoriously slow to bear from seed** (a proverb gives it
~18 years), so it is grown from **grafted seedlings** (as in Kami City since 1960)
[2]. **Exports are surging** — Kochi's reached ¥744 million in 2022, up 74.5% on
the year, to Europe, the US and Australia — and villages such as **Umaji** built a
rural economy on the yuzu boom [2].

**Finding 3 — a zest-first citrus with food, bath and cosmetic uses.** Yuzu is
prized above all for its **highly aromatic zest/peel and juice**, not its flesh
[1][3]. In the kitchen it is the base of **ponzu** (with soy, mirin, kombu,
bonito) and **yuzu kosho** (a paste of pounded yuzu zest, chili and salt), and
flavours sauces, sweets, drinks and confectionery [3]. Beyond food, it has two
distinctive non-food lives: the **yuzuyu winter-solstice (Tōji) bath**, in which
whole fruits are floated in hot water (often in a cloth bag) for their aroma — a
custom dating to at least the early 18th century, believed to ward off colds and
bad luck (schema Entry 65) — and wide use in **perfumes, soaps, skin care and
hair oil**, including **yuzu seed oil** [3].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Perennial evergreen citrus tree; **green yuzu** used in
  summer-autumn, **yellow ripe fruit** in late autumn-winter [1][3].
- **Harvest pattern:** Perennial bearing; **thorny trees**, careful **hand
  harvest** to protect the peel (Finding 2) [2].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** **Very slow from seed** (proverbially ~18
  years), so grown on **grafts**, which bear far sooner (Finding 2) [2].
- **Climate zones:** The **hardiest common edible citrus** (~−7 to −9 °C), grown
  from subtropical East Asia into temperate NZ/Europe (Finding 1) [1].
- **Composition:** Very aromatic, sour, **seedy**; zest/peel carries the prized
  aroma oil; quantitative composition not fully sourced here [1][3].
- **Edibility:** Not eaten as sweet flesh — used for **zest and juice** as an
  aromatic acidulant (Finding 3) [1][3].
- **Pollination:** A **parentage contributor** to other Japanese citrus (e.g.
  kabosu); detailed floral biology not sourced [4].
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced; not expected to apply.
- **Yield:** National output ~23,000-27,000 t/yr; per-tree yield not sourced
  (Finding 2) [2].
- **Water use:** Not sourced.
- **Propagation:** **Grafted seedlings** (seed is too slow to bear); nursery
  stock long established (Kami City since 1960) (Finding 2) [2].
- **Labor:** **Careful hand harvest** forced by the thorny canopy and scratch-
  prone peel (Finding 2; Entry 72) [2].
- **Soil:** Not specifically sourced; grown on Shikoku hill land (Kochi,
  Tokushima, Ehime) [2].
- **Storage (postharvest):** **Precooling to 3-5 °C** for harvested fresh fruit
  (Finding 2) [2].
- **Establishment:** Planted as grafted orchard trees; rural revival crop (Umaji
  and others) (Finding 2) [2].
- **Cultivars:** Regional GI yuzu (Kito, Monobe) and local selections; a mostly
  seed-propagated-then-grafted landrace rather than a broad named-cultivar set
  [2].
- **Weather response:** **Frost-tolerant** (its signature trait); other responses
  not quantified (Finding 1) [1].
- **Water regime:** Ordinary rainfall/irrigation; no paddy requirement [1].
- **Harvest stages / windows:** **Green (aoyuzu)** earlier for sharper aroma, then
  **yellow ripe** fruit in winter [1][3].
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** A cultivated citrus; no invasive listing found.
  Export growth brings standard **citrus phytosanitary** handling, not detailed
  here [2].
- **Controlled environment:** Mostly open-field; precooling/cold-chain for fresh
  export (Finding 2) [2].

## Hazards

- As with all citrus, **peel-oil furanocoumarins** are a minor contact-
  photosensitiser note; yuzu is a common food and bath additive [3]. The tree's
  **sharp thorns** are a handling hazard to pickers and to the fruit (Finding 2)
  [2]. **No distinctive ingestion hazard found in this pass.**

## Uses and processing

- **Chain (zest/juice — the core use):** fruit → **zest and juice** as an
  aromatic acidulant → **ponzu**, **yuzu kosho** (zest + chili + salt), sauces,
  sweets, drinks, confectionery, marmalade, *yuja-cha* (Korea) [3].
- **Chain (winter-solstice bath):** whole fruit → **yuzuyu** Tōji bath (floated,
  often bagged) for aroma and custom (schema Entry 65) [3].
- **Chain (cosmetics/fragrance):** peel/seed → **perfume, soap, skin care, hair
  oil, yuzu seed oil** [3].
- **Chain (breeding):** a **parent/contributor** to other Japanese citrus (e.g.
  kabosu) [4].
- **Not otherwise detailed here:** essential-oil extraction specifics; quantitative
  juice/oil composition; seed-oil processing [1][3].
