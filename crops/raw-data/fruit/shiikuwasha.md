## Naming and scope

*Citrus depressa* Hayata (Rutaceae): **shiikuwasha** (also *shikuwasa*,
*shikwasa*, *shequasar*); English **hirami lemon** / **flat lemon**, **Taiwan
tangerine**; a small sour citrus of the Ryukyu Islands and mountainous Taiwan
[1]. This doc covers the `shiikuwasha` row, proposed from `kunenbo.md` and
`hetsuka-daidai.md` [5].

- **Taxonomy (a near-wild Ryukyu mandarin):** *C. depressa* is a genetically
  diverse, semi-wild East Asian mandarin-type, treated as one of the Ryukyus'
  own citrus (grouped with tachibana as a "typical Japanese citrus"), and is a
  **parent — with Kunenbo — of the Ryukyu hybrids 'Kabuchi', 'Oto' and
  'Tarogayo'** [4][5]. Its many named landraces are a diversity, not separate
  rows (classification/landrace variation is out of this project's schema
  scope) [4].

**Distinctiveness (gate check, 2026-11-27): passes.** A validly distinct
species with its own range, chemistry and use-pattern, clearly separate from the
project's other East Asian sour citrus [1][5]:
- **vs the sibling sour citrus** (`yuzu`, `sudachi`, `kabosu`, `hetsuka_daidai`
  — the group it is grouped with as an acidulant "control" in
  `hetsuka-daidai.md`): a different species (*C. depressa*), a **Ryukyu/Taiwan**
  range rather than mainland-Japan prefectures, and a signature **peel chemistry
  (highest polymethoxyflavones of commercial Japanese citrus)** none of the
  others is noted for (Finding 2) [1][3][5].
- **vs `kunenbo`** (a hybridisation partner): shiikuwasha is the native Ryukyu
  parent, Kunenbo the introduced mandarin; their crosses are the named Ryukyu
  hybrids above [5].

**Relatives in this project:** `kunenbo.md` and `hetsuka-daidai.md` (the
proposing docs), and the other East Asian sour-citrus candidates `yuzu`,
`sudachi`, `kabosu`, plus `mandarin`/`tachibana` as the mandarin backbone [5].

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — one fruit, three products, chosen by how ripe it is picked.**
Shiikuwasha is harvested across a long window (about August to February) at
**different maturities for different products**, and taking it at one stage
precludes the others on that fruit [2]:
- **~August-September — picked green and most sour:** used as an **acidulant /
  vinegar substitute** (squeezed over dishes, into *awamori*, as a lime/lemon
  stand-in) [1][2];
- **~September-December — part-ripe:** pressed for **juice**, the main
  commercial product [2];
- **~December-January — ripe and orange:** **eaten raw**, by which point sugars
  have risen and acidity fallen [2].
Sugar and acid shift steadily over the season [2]. This per-fruit,
maturity-staged product trade-off is the project's schema pattern of
**mutually-exclusive harvests on one plant** (Entry 32) [2]. The fruit is very
acidic — an internal cross-reference records about **4.53% citric and 0.15%
malic acid** for one shiikuwasha sample [5].

**Finding 2 — the citrus with the highest polymethoxyflavones, and the health
story built on it.** Among commercially harvested Japanese citrus, shiikuwasha
**peel has the highest concentration of polymethoxyflavones (PMFs)**, with
**nobiletin predominating over tangeretin** [3]. These PMFs drive a large
functional-food/nutraceutical interest: nobiletin has shown **liver-protective**
effects (against CCl₄/acetaminophen injury in rats), **anti-diabetic** effects
(slowing diabetes and preserving β-cell mass in db/db mice), and **cognitive**
benefits in experimental models, but **human clinical evidence remains limited**
[3]. The popular association with **Okinawan longevity** (shiikuwasha is a
staple of the northern "blue zone") is cultural and **not established** by the
clinical sources read here [1][3].

**Finding 3 — a signature Okinawan crop centred on Ōgimi, with high landrace
diversity and processing that tames the bitterness.** Commercial production
centres on **northern Okinawa, above all Ōgimi village** (which holds ~29
domesticated accessions and runs harvest festivals), with lesser Japanese
production in Kagoshima and Wakayama and the only other meaningful production in
**mountainous Taiwan** [1][4]. Seven superior landraces (Nakamoto-seedless,
Ishi-kunibu, Izumi-kugani, Katsuyama-kugani, Asahikawa-Shiikuwasha, Fusubuta,
Ogimi-kugani) are kept as reference material at the Okinawa Agricultural
Research Center, and PMF content varies by landrace and origin (Japan vs Taiwan)
[4]. On processing, the raw juice is sour and often **over-sweetened**;
**fermentation significantly cuts the bitterness and astringency**, and a
**ponzu** sauce is commercialised in Okinawa from the **vinegar extract of
'Kugani' juice-pressing waste** [2].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Perennial evergreen citrus tree/shrub; a long autumn-
  to-winter harvest season (~Aug-Feb) taken in stages (Finding 1) [1][2].
- **Harvest pattern:** Perennial bearing; **multi-stage harvest by maturity**
  for distinct products (green acidulant → juice → ripe raw) (Finding 1) [2].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** Not sourced for *C. depressa* specifically.
- **Climate zones:** Subtropical (Ryukyu/Okinawa, Taiwan mountains, warm
  southern Japan); frost-tender citrus [1]. No chill requirement.
- **Composition:** Very acidic juice (~4.53% citric, 0.15% malic in one sample,
  internal cross-ref) [5]; **peel exceptionally rich in polymethoxyflavones —
  nobiletin (dominant) and tangeretin, the highest among commercial Japanese
  citrus** [3]. Sugar rises and acid falls with ripening [2].
- **Edibility:** Green fruit too sour to eat — used as an **acidulant/juice**;
  **ripe fruit eaten raw** (sweeter) (Finding 1) [1][2].
- **Pollination:** Not detailed; many landraces, including a **seedless**
  selection ('Nakamoto-seedless'), exist [4].
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced; not expected to apply.
- **Yield:** Not sourced (no per-tree or regional tonnage figure found).
- **Water use:** Not sourced.
- **Propagation:** Citrus-standard (seed/graft); the crop is a diverse set of
  **landrace selections** maintained clonally/at research stations (Finding 3);
  seedless and superior landraces are propagated for planting [4]. No specific
  rootstock/virus detail sourced.
- **Labor:** Not sourced; green fruit is hand-picked across several passes by
  maturity (Finding 1) [2].
- **Soil:** Not specifically sourced; grown on Okinawan and Taiwanese hill
  land. pH/salinity: Not sourced [1].
- **Storage (postharvest):** Not sourced for the fresh fruit; most of the crop
  is processed to **juice**, which is the keeping form [2].
- **Establishment:** Planted as grafted/seedling trees in orchards and
  dooryards in Okinawa/Taiwan [1].
- **Cultivars:** Many named **landraces** — Nakamoto-seedless, Ishi-kunibu,
  Izumi-kugani, Katsuyama-kugani, Asahikawa-Shiikuwasha, Fusubuta, Ogimi-kugani
  (Okinawa) and Nantou (Taiwan), differing in seediness, acidity and PMF content
  (Finding 3) [4].
- **Weather response:** Frost-tender; other tolerances not quantified [1].
- **Water regime:** Ordinary rainfall/irrigation; no paddy requirement [1].
- **Harvest stages / windows:** **The defining feature** — green/sour (~Aug-Sep,
  acidulant), part-ripe (~Sep-Dec, juice), ripe/orange (~Dec-Jan, eaten raw)
  (Finding 1) [2].
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** Native Ryukyu/Taiwan citrus; no invasive
  listing found. As a near-wild, highly diverse species it is of **germplasm/
  conservation** interest (reference landrace collections) rather than a weed
  risk [4]. No cultivation/movement restriction specific to it found.
- **Controlled environment:** Not sourced; a field/orchard crop [1].

## Hazards

- As with all citrus, **peel oil furanocoumarins** are a minor contact-
  photosensitiser note, not a distinctive hazard; the juice is a common food
  [1]. Citrus PMFs (nobiletin) can in principle affect drug-metabolising
  enzymes — relevant to concentrated **nutraceutical extracts** rather than the
  fruit, and not quantified here [3]. **No distinctive hazard found in this
  pass.**

## Uses and processing

- **Chain (acidulant):** green sour fruit → **squeezed** → lime/lemon/vinegar
  substitute over dishes, seafood and *awamori*; marinades and dressings
  [1][2].
- **Chain (juice — the main product):** part-ripe fruit → **pressed → juice**
  (often sweetened; fermentation used to cut bitterness/astringency); juice-
  pressing **waste → vinegar extract → commercial ponzu** ('Kugani') [2].
- **Chain (fresh):** ripe orange fruit → **eaten raw** [1][2].
- **Chain (nutraceutical):** peel/fruit → **nobiletin/PMF extracts** for
  functional-food and supplement use (Finding 2) [3].
- **Not otherwise detailed here:** jam, sweets, confectionery and flavourings;
  essential-oil extraction; per-landrace processing differences [1][4].

## Open research questions

- **Bearing age, yield and per-tree productivity** for *C. depressa* were not
  sourced [1][4].
- **Harvest-window dates vary between sources** (e.g. a vinegar study sampled
  June-August; others give Aug-Feb) — likely landrace/location-dependent and not
  reconciled here [2].
- **The Okinawan-longevity link** is popular but clinically unestablished in the
  sources read; the human evidence for nobiletin benefits is still limited
  [1][3].
- **Composition** beyond the single acid measurement and the qualitative PMF
  ranking (Brix, juice yield, vitamin C, per-landrace PMF figures) is only
  partly sourced [3][4][5].
- **Taiwan's production scale and harvest calendar** were not found in detail
  [1].
