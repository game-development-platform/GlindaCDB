## Naming and scope

*Citrus × clementina* Hort. ex Tanaka (Rutaceae) [1][4]. A tangor:
genomic analysis shows a cross of sweet orange (*C. × sinensis*) and the
Mediterranean willowleaf mandarin (*C. × deliciosa*) [1].
- **Names:** clementine; *clémentine* (French), *clementina* (Spanish,
  Italian) [1][6]. Sold in Britain as "easy-peelers", a term also used
  for other small citrus [1].
- **Name clashes:**
  - **Trade statistics:** FAOSTAT groups clementines with tangerines,
    mandarins and satsumas [1]; Spain's ministry reports them as a
    separate share of "small citrus" [2].
  - **"Sweetclems", "Clemengold", "Clemcott":** brand names that
    Wikipedia files under clementine; some are hybrids, not
    clementines [1].
- **Origin:** a spontaneous hybrid found in the late 19th century in the
  orphanage garden of the missionary Brother Clément Rodier at
  Misserghin, Algeria, and named after him in 1902 [1]. Similar fruit
  from Guangxi and Guangdong is likely a distinct mandarin hybrid [1].
- **Range:** the principal mandarin group of the Mediterranean basin [4]:
  Spain, Morocco, Algeria, Italy and Corsica [1][2][8][10]; California
  since 1914 [1].
- **Cultivar types** [1]:
  - **Common (seedless):** self-incompatible, with few or no seeds.
  - **Clementine:** up to 10 seeds.
  - **Monreal:** self-compatible, more than 10 seeds, larger, sweeter,
    with a heavier bloom. A natural mutant of the common type that has
    lost expression of the S7-RNase gene [4].
  - **Bud sports:** many varieties are bud sports of the original
    'Comune' [4]. 'Clemenules' (Nules), a mutation of 'Fina', is the
    most planted in Spain [1].

**Gate check (2026-11-22): passes.** The row was proposed from
`mandarin.md`, for its distinct pollination and water data [11]. It is
distinct enough for its own row:
- **Taxonomy:** a tangor of known parentage [1], not the *C. reticulata*
  of the `mandarin` row. It was chosen for the international citrus
  reference genome, via a haploid 'Clemenules' [5].
- **Farming system:** fruit set depends on parthenocarpy, and seedless
  crops need blocks kept away from compatible pollen (Finding 2) [3][4].
  Gibberellic acid sprays are the standard fruit-set treatment [7].
- **Range:** a Mediterranean-basin crop of North African origin [1][4],
  where the mandarin row centres on East Asia [11].
- **Market:** over half of Spain's small-citrus crop in 2025/26 [2], and
  two EU-protected origins with their own harvest rules (Finding 3)
  [8][9].
- **Against `mandarin.md`:** that doc kept clementine as a cultivar
  until it was researched [11]. This doc now covers it.

**Relatives in this project:**
- mandarin (`mandarin`, `mandarin.md`): the wider mandarin group [11].
- orange (`orange`, `orange.md`): sweet orange is one parent [1].
- satsuma (`satsuma`, `satsuma.md`): the other early small citrus in the
  Spanish statistics [2]; `satsuma.md` excludes clementine [11].
- tangelo (`tangelo`, proposed): another mandarin hybrid split from
  `mandarin.md` [11].

**Proposed:** none new.

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — Spain's main small citrus.**
- **Spain, 2025/26:** small citrus were forecast at 1.73 million t, 32% of
  all citrus; clementines were 52.2% of that group, satsumas 5.4% and
  other mandarins and hybrids 42.3% [2].
- **Spain, 2026/27:** small citrus 1.8 million t, with clementines 47.3%
  and mandarins and hybrids 47.1%. Summer heat caused heavy fruit drop
  in clementines, and "for the first time" the two groups are forecast
  level [2].
- **Exports:** over 60% of Spain's small citrus is exported [2].
- **Morocco:** clementine exports of about 436,000 t from July 2024 to
  February 2025, and a 2024/25 production forecast of 1.1 million t
  [10] (snippets).
- **See Estimates** for clementine tonnage.

**Finding 2 — seedless only when kept from pollen.**
- **Self-incompatible:** pollen tubes stop in the upper style; fruit set
  without seeds relies on parthenocarpy [3][4]. Self-pollination does
  not trigger it: unpollinated and self-pollinated ovaries set fruit
  alike [3].
- **Cultivar differences:** 'Marisol' sets 44% more unpollinated fruit
  than 'Clemenules', with 50% heavier fruit, matching higher ovary
  gibberellin (GA1) [3].
- **Cross-pollination:** compatible pollen (such as 'Fortune') makes
  'Clemenules' seedy. Gibberellic acid at 10 mg/L around flowering
  impairs or prevents fertilization [3].
- **Bees:** California growers threatened in 2006 to sue beekeepers to
  keep bees away [1].
- **Poor set:** 'Nules' has poor fruit set, giving too many small fruit.
  GA3 spraying is "the only available practice"; GA3 raised yield by
  17.4% and a methyl-xanthine alternative by 25.7% [7].

**Finding 3 — Corsica: leaves on, no degreening.**
- **IGP "Clémentine de Corse" (2007):** hand picking, at least 42%
  juice, colour and size rules, and one or two leaves attached to the
  stalk [8]. The specification bans degreening and requires coloured
  fruit to be picked with leaves in at least two passes [9].
- **Scale:** 37,350 t in 2021/22, the third year in four above 37,000 t;
  172 approved producers in 2020, with certified area up 400 ha on
  the 1,140 ha of 2010; 284 ha
  organic, one hectare in six [8]. Corsica grows 99.8% of France's
  clementines [9].
- **Season:** from the second half of October to about 15 January [8].
- **History:** in 1993 the European Commission let Spain sell clementines
  with leaves, ending Corsica's distinction and starting a crisis that
  led to the IGP [9].
- **Italy:** two more EU-protected origins, Clementine del Golfo di
  Taranto and Clementine di Calabria [1].

**Finding 4 — sensitive to stress at flowering.**
- **Heat:** lower heat requirements than other citrus; it bears early in
  hot regions [1]. Summer heat in 2026 caused heavy fruit drop in Spain
  [2].
- **Water:** see Agronomic data; flowering and fruit set are the most
  stress-sensitive stages, and clementine may be more sensitive to water
  deficits than navel orange [11].
- **Rind disorders:** shaded fruit from inside the canopy had the most
  rind breakdown in 'Nules' after nine weeks at 8 °C (score 0.88 against
  0.12 for sun-exposed fruit) [6]. Water spot is another rind problem;
  chitosan cut it by 65% [6]. Corsican fruit suffered keeping problems in
  a wet, warm November [8].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** evergreen grafted citrus tree. Not sourced as
  tree size here.
- **Harvest pattern:** one crop a year. 'Clemenules' matures mid-November
  to mid- or late January in Spain, and October-December in California
  [1]; Corsica mid-October to mid-January [8].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** Not sourced for clementine; see
  `mandarin.md` [11].
- **Climate zones:** Mediterranean subtropical [1][2]; lower heat
  requirements than other citrus [1]. Chill requirement: Not sourced.
- **Composition (raw, per 100 g)** [12]: water 86.6 g, energy 47 kcal
  (198 kJ), protein 0.85 g, fat 0.15 g, carbohydrate 12.0 g, fibre
  1.7 g, vitamin C 48.8 mg, potassium 177 mg, calcium 30 mg, folate
  24 µg. Typically sweet and less acid than oranges, with 7-14
  segments [1].
- **Edibility:** raw-edible, easy to peel [1].
- **Pollination:** none needed; parthenocarpic (Finding 2) [3][4].
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced. Not applicable to storage
  organs.
- **Yield:** Not sourced per hectare as a measured figure; see Estimates
  for Corsica.
- **Water use:** single crop coefficient 0.44 for clementines at Mazagón,
  Spain; for drip clementines, Kc = 0.006 × ground cover (%) + 0.272.
  Summer stress has no yield effect down to -1.3 MPa predawn leaf water
  potential; late-summer irrigation at 25-50% of ETc cut fruit size by
  11-25% and increased peel creasing; 55% of water needs season-long cut
  yield by 17% [11].
- **Propagation:** grafted. 'Fina' was originally grown on bitter orange,
  and now on other rootstocks after disease [1].
  - **Clonal risk:** most commercial types are bud sports of one original
    tree [4]. Not sourced as a disease consequence.
- **Labor:** Not sourced. Corsican IGP fruit is hand-picked in at least
  two passes [8][9].
- **Soil:** Not sourced. Salinity: Not sourced.
- **Storage (postharvest):** 'Nules' trials stored fruit 9 weeks at 8 °C
  [6]. The Corsican sector is adding cold stores because the fruit is
  fragile in heat and humidity [8].
- **Establishment:** Not sourced.
- **Cultivars:** 'Fina', 'Clemenules', 'Marisol', 'Comune', 'Monreal';
  Taranto and Calabria types [1][3][4].
- **Weather response:** summer heat → fruit drop [2]; spring rain, heat
  and hail cut Spain's 2025/26 citrus crop to a 16-year low [2]; warm,
  wet autumns → poor keeping [8].
- **Water regime:** irrigated (Spain, Corsica) [9][11].
- **Harvest stages / windows:** gibberellic acid at colour break reduces
  peel blemish and allows a late harvest in Spain [11].
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** Not sourced for clementine.
- **Controlled environment:** Not sourced.

## Hazards

- **Drug interaction:** a 2017 in-vitro study suggested clementine juice
  may interact with drugs like grapefruit; a 2019 follow-up in two
  people found no effect on midazolam clearance [1].
- **Ergot / mycotoxin:** none sourced for clementine.
- **TO BE ADDRESSED** — the drug-interaction question (shared with
  `mandarin.md`) has no field in the hazard model yet [11].

## Uses and processing

- **Chain (fresh, Spain and Morocco):** orchard → picked → packed →
  mostly exported fresh (over 60% of Spanish small citrus) [2][10].
- **Chain (Corsica IGP):** orchard → hand-picked with one or two leaves,
  no degreening → graded for colour, size and juice → French mainland
  market [8][9]. Fruit unfit for fresh sale goes to new local processing
  units [8].
- **Chain (peel):** peel → essential oil, mostly limonene with myrcene,
  linalool and α-pinene [1]. Peel by-products are also studied for
  carotenoids and phenolics [6].
- **Research use:** a haploid 'Clemenules' plant is the source of the
  citrus reference genome [5].
- **Ritual and religious:** Not sourced.

## Estimates and derivations

**Spanish clementine crop, about 0.85-0.90 million t.** 2025/26: 1.73
million t × 52.2% [2] = 903,000 t. 2026/27: 1.80 million t × 47.3% [2] =
851,000 t. Both are forecasts.

**Corsican yield, about 20-24 t/ha.**
- 2021/22: 37,350 t [8] / about 1,700 ha. The area is 284 ha organic ×
  6, from "one hectare in six" [8] = 1,704 ha; 37,350 / 1,704 = 21.9
  t/ha. Against the certified area, 1,140 + 400 = 1,540 ha [8]: 24.3 t/ha.
- 2016: 26,417 t / 1,331 ha [9] = 19.8 t/ha.

**Energy check.** Atwater: 0.85 × 4 + 0.15 × 9 + 12.0 × 4 = 52.8 kcal,
against 47 kcal listed [12]; the gap is fibre and organic acids in the
carbohydrate figure.
