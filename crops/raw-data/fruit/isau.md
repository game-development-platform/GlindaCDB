## Naming and scope

**Isau** is a named local race of *Dimocarpus longan* subsp. *malesianus* var.
*malesianus* Leenh. (Sapindaceae) from Sarawak, Borneo — the subspecies this
project covers as mata kucing (`mata_kucing`, `mata-kucing.md`) [5]. This doc
covers the **isau race specifically**, which was pulled out as its own row
because it is the one being actively selected and brought toward cultivation,
with a quality, development and propagation story distinct from the wild/village
mata kucing [1][4][5].

- **Name:** *isau* (Sarawak); also written *sau* for a closely related race —
  *isau* and *sau* are distinct races, both staying green when ripe [1][5].
  The subspecies as a whole carries many local names (*mata kucing*, *kakus*,
  *buku*, *ihau*, *medaru*) catalogued under `mata_kucing` [5].
- **Rank:** a **race/landrace selection within var. *malesianus***, not a
  separate botanical taxon; the 1988 UPM Bintulu work identified the Sarawak
  material as *D. longan* subsp. *malesianus* var. *malesianus* and compared
  three groups within it — **isau, sau and kakus** [1].

**Gate check (2026-11-26): passes** against both `mata_kucing.md` (the parent
subspecies) and `longan.md`. Unlike the sibling candidate var. *echinatus*
(rejected the same week as a wild spiny form with no distinct crop story), isau
clears the distinctiveness check on multiple axes:
- **Product / quality:** isau is the **best-eating race** — a **thick,
  worth-eating aril** against true mata kucing's aril, which the parent doc
  records as "hardly worth eating" — so it is qualitatively a different eating
  fruit, not just a colour or size variant [1][4][5].
- **Farming system / trajectory:** it is a **semi-wild fruit under active
  domestication**, the subject of a dedicated University programme since 1986
  and of repeated propagation research — a cultivation trajectory the
  undifferentiated subspecies lacks [1][3][4].
- **Harvest / range:** a restricted riverbank range (Rajang river, Bario
  valley) and a distinct local-market presence in specific Sarawak districts
  [2][5].
This is the crop-development case the project splits out, parallel to how
`mata_kucing` itself was split from commercial `longan` [5].

**Relatives in this project:** `mata-kucing.md` (the parent subspecies, and the
source of the shared species-level agronomy below); `longan.md` (the commercial
longan it is benchmarked against); and the other project Sapindaceae fruits
`lychee`, `rambutan`, `pulasan` [5].

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — selected from among Sarawak's wild longan races as the best
eater, on measured quality.** The 1988 UPM Bintulu study compared the Sarawak
*malesianus* races and found **isau had the highest Brix** and comparatively
**higher vitamin C, calcium and iron** than the sau and kakus groups, which is
why the programme focused on it [1]. Isau is distinguished by a **pyramidal
tree form, small leaflets, green fruit when ripe, and a thick aril** [1]. Local
and press accounts agree it is "the best in eating quality" for its thick flesh
and that it **tastes better than imported (Thai) longan** [1][4]. The actual
Brix and mineral numbers were only read at snippet/abstract depth; the full
1988 paper (open-access but not retrievable this pass — see Open questions)
holds the values [1].

**Finding 2 — a semi-wild fruit in active domestication, not a wild curiosity.**
Work on isau began at Universiti Pertanian Malaysia's **Bintulu campus in
1986**, treating it as "a potential tropical longan" worth developing [1]. The
wider subspecies is described by FAO as "mainly unexploited genetic materials
which may have great potential," with the **superior Sarawak forms having
greater aril recovery** [2]. Press reporting describes the wild *malesianus*
as having been **domesticated in Sarawak**, with isau the prized selection
[4]. This active-selection-and-improvement trajectory — rather than mere wild
gathering — is what separates isau from the undifferentiated subspecies and
from the rejected wild var. *echinatus* [1][4][5].

**Finding 3 — expansion is bottlenecked by a shortage of true-to-type planting
material, which drives the propagation research.** Isau grows naturally on
river banks, and **"there is not much planting outside its natural habitat …
partly due to scarcity of true-to-type planting material"** [3]. Seed is a poor
route: longan/mata-kucing seedlings take long to bear (FAO gives ~7-8 years for
the species) and **do not come true to type**, so vegetative propagation is
needed to fix the selected race [2][3]. A 2006 UNIMAS cuttings study found
**cuttings from young seedlings rooted more readily** than softwood or young
shoots of mature trees, with **peat + IBA (≈2 g/L)** most effective, and tested
tissue culture (25% Clorox for clean shoot-tip explants); a 2007 UNIMAS project
added conventional and in-vitro work including **inarching** [3]. For the
species generally, FAO notes **air-layering (marcotting) is the most used
method**, though marcotted trees are more wind-susceptible than grafted ones
[2]. The through-line: isau's domestication hinges on solving true-to-type
multiplication [2][3].

**Finding 4 — a short local season and a documented district-level presence.**
In Sarawak the subspecies flowers in a single short window and fruits roughly
late October to mid-February, one crop a year — against commercial longan's two
[5]. Isau/sau are concentrated on the **Rajang river banks and in the Bario
(Bareo) valley** [2][5]; Malaysian fruit statistics record mata-kucing/isau
production in specific Sarawak districts (Sebauh and Bukit Mabong by one reading
of the extracted table), though no isau-specific area or tonnage was isolable
[5]. Snippet-level weights for two isau accessions were about **8.00 g and
4.52 g per fruit with 3.79 g and 2.68 g of aril** (≈47% and 59% aril), but the
OCR was garbled and these need checking against the original (see Open
questions) [1].

## Agronomic data

Species-level agronomy (climate, flowering physiology, hazards, general
composition) is shared with the parent subspecies and documented in
`mata-kucing.md`; only race-specific or especially relevant points are repeated
here [5].

- **Life form / season:** Perennial evergreen tree; **pyramidal** in the isau
  race, with small leaflets [1]. One short flowering window and one annual crop
  in Sarawak (Finding 4) [5].
- **Harvest pattern:** Perennial bearing, **a single annual picking** in
  Sarawak (vs two for commercial longan) [5].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** Not sourced for isau specifically; the
  species from seed is slow (~7-8 years), a reason vegetative propagation is
  preferred (Finding 3) [2].
- **Climate zones:** Equatorial Bornean lowlands — "fully adapted" to constant
  heat, humidity and daylength, flowering without the cool trigger commercial
  longan needs (parent-doc trait) [5].
- **Composition:** **Highest Brix** of the Sarawak races, with comparatively
  **higher vitamin C, calcium and iron** [1]; broadly "not very different" from
  longan but with lower carbohydrate/energy and much higher minerals at the
  subspecies level [5]. Exact figures: only snippet-level (Finding 1) [1].
- **Edibility:** Eaten **fresh**; a thick, sweet, worth-eating aril — the race's
  defining advantage over thin-arilled true mata kucing [1][4][5].
- **Pollination:** Not sourced for isau; no race-specific pollination
  requirement found [5].
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** The subspecies flowers without a cool/short-day
  trigger (day-length-neutral in effect); not sourced as a yield lever for isau
  specifically [5].
- **Yield:** Not sourced (no isau-specific yield figure; aril-recovery share
  only, Finding 4) [1][2].
- **Water use:** Not sourced.
- **Propagation:** Vegetative, to hold the race true: **cuttings** (young-
  seedling cuttings root best; peat + IBA), **tissue culture**, **inarching**
  and, for the species, **air-layering/marcotting** (most common, but wind-
  susceptible); **seed is not true-to-type** and slow, and true-to-type
  material is scarce — the main bottleneck on wider planting (Finding 3)
  [2][3].
- **Labor:** Not sourced.
- **Soil:** Grows on **alluvial river-bank soils** in Sarawak, with a wider
  tolerance at the subspecies level [5].
- **Storage (postharvest):** Not sourced for isau; no race-specific shelf-life
  found [5].
- **Establishment:** Planted from vegetative material near its riverbank
  habitat; expansion elsewhere limited by planting-material scarcity (Finding
  3) [3].
- **Cultivars:** No formally named/registered clones; isau is itself a
  **selected race** (with sau and kakus the other Sarawak races) rather than a
  cultivar lineage [1][5].
- **Weather response:** Not sourced for isau beyond the species' equatorial
  adaptation; marcotted trees are noted as more **wind-susceptible** [2][5].
- **Water regime:** Ordinary tropical rainfall on riverbank sites; no paddy
  requirement [5].
- **Harvest stages / windows:** One Sarawak window, ~late October to
  mid-February; isau **stays green when ripe**, so colour is not the ripeness
  cue it is for brown-ripening races (Finding 4) [1][5].
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** Native Bornean race; no invasive listing. The
  parent subspecies carries the longan group's fruit-fly quarantine and a
  Hawaii weed-risk context (see `mata-kucing.md`); nothing isau-specific found
  [5].
- **Controlled environment:** Tissue culture has been used experimentally for
  multiplication (Finding 3); no commercial protected-production system [3].

## Hazards

- No isau-specific hazard was found. The longan group's seed contains
  **hypoglycin A** (the ackee toxin), which the project tracks at the genus
  level — the **aril (the eaten part) carries only traces** while the seed can
  hold much more; this is documented and cited in `mata-kucing.md`/`longan.md`
  and is carried here by reference rather than re-sourced [5]. **No hazards
  found in this pass** beyond that inherited genus-level seed note.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain (food):** fresh fruit → hand-peeled → **thick sweet aril eaten
  fresh**; the race's selling point is eating quality rather than a processed
  product [1][4]. No distinct drying/canning chain was sourced for isau (the
  commercial dried-longan trade is a `longan` matter) [5].
- **Development / planting material:** vegetatively propagated **true-to-type
  planting stock** is itself a sought output, because its scarcity limits
  spread (Finding 3) [3].
- **Not otherwise detailed here:** any processed products; detailed composition
  tables; the shared species-level uses in `mata-kucing.md` [5].

## Open research questions

- **The 1988 UPM Bintulu paper's actual numbers** (Brix, vitamin C, calcium,
  iron, fruit and aril weights) were not retrievable this pass — the
  open-access PDF returned HTTP 403. Those values would replace every
  snippet-level figure in Findings 1 and 4 [1].
- **Isau-specific yield, bearing age, storage life and labour** are all
  unsourced; only subspecies-level or species-level analogues exist [1][2][5].
- **Current domestication status:** how far isau has actually moved into
  planted cultivation since the 1986-2007 programme, and whether the
  true-to-type bottleneck has been solved, is not established by recent sources
  [3][4].
- **Race boundaries:** isau vs sau vs kakus are distinguished mainly on fruit
  colour, shape and aril thickness; a firmer delimitation (and whether "isau"
  is used loosely for several green-ripening forms) was not pinned down [1][5].
- **Snippet weights need verification** against the original 1988 tables (the
  OCR was garbled) [1].
