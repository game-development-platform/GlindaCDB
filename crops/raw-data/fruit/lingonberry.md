## Naming and scope

*Vaccinium vitis-idaea* L. (Ericaceae): lingonberry, cowberry,
partridgeberry, mountain cranberry, lowbush cranberry, foxberry,
*puolukka* (Finnish), *Preiselbeere* (German), *kokemomo* (Japanese);
more than 25 European and North American names are in use [1][2]. The
name comes from Swedish, and lingonberry is Sweden's national fruit [2].
- **Two types** [1]:
  - **wild** (var. *minus*): under 18 cm tall, blooms and fruits once;
  - **European, or cultivated:** 5-40 cm tall, with two bloom periods
    and two crops a year in the Pacific Northwest.
- **This entry covers** the cultivated system (raised beds, pollinizer
  rows, two crops). The large Nordic and Russian wild harvest is
  described as context [2][4].
- **Look-alike:** kinnikinnick (*Arctostaphylos uva-ursi*) has similar
  leaves and berries but is a different plant [1].
- **Separate entries:**
  - highbush blueberry (`blueberry`), `blueberry.md`;
  - cranberry (`cranberry`), `cranberry.md`;
  - lowbush blueberry (`lowbush_blueberry`), `lowbush-blueberry.md`,
    which shares the rhizomatous, rake-harvested habit [1].
- **Proposed:** bilberry (`bilberry`, *V. myrtillus*), the other main
  Nordic wild *Vaccinium* berry [4].

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — mostly a wild crop, and only a small share is picked.**
- **Wild yield, 2014-2019** [4]:
  - Finland: 246 million kg a year on productive forest land;
  - Sweden: 382 million kg a year.
- **Share picked:**
  - Finland picked almost 10% of its lingonberries in 1997-1999 [4];
  - perhaps 10% of available wild fruit is harvested generally,
    though some districts take as much as 40% [2].
- **Harvest swings:** Sweden's harvest runs from as little as 2,000 t to
  over 20,000 t, and Finland's from under 2,000 t to over 10,000 t,
  depending on weather [2].
- **Trade:** Sweden exported 6 million kg of wild lingonberries in 2015,
  worth about €20 million [4]. Finland exported an average of 0.4
  million kg a year in 2016-2020 [4].
- **Pickers:** 5,000-8,000 migrant pickers work in northern Sweden, and
  about 4,000 in Finland [4].
- **Cultivated area:** only 71 acres (29 ha) worldwide when the guide
  was written, with more than 17 acres in Oregon, Washington and
  British Columbia [1].

**Finding 2 — two blooms and two crops.**
- **Bloom:** on 1-year-old wood, in March-April and July-August [1].
- **Ripening:** mid-August and mid-October respectively [1]. Fruit
  ripens about 78-84 days after full bloom [3].
- **The fall crop is the commercial one:** the late-October to
  early-November crop is larger and is the one usually sold [2].
- **Summer crop drop:** summer berries fall off by the fall harvest and
  are overripe and store poorly if picked then [1].
- **Wild type:** fruits once, in August-September [2].

**Finding 3 — needs a pollinizer row and insects.**
- **Pollinizers:** plant 10% of the field to pollinizer cultivars;
  'Red Pearl' and 'Sussi' are common [1].
- **Pollinators:** bees, bumblebees, flies and butterflies; wind does
  little [1].
- **Frost at bloom:** as mild as -2 °C can damage flowers and prevent
  fruit set [1].
- **Shade:** plants in shade rarely flower or fruit [3].

**Finding 4 — very sensitive to salt, fertiliser and wet roots.**
- **Soil:** light, well-drained sandy loam to silty loam with 2-6%
  organic matter and pH 4.3-5.5 [1]. Wild plants grow at pH 2.7-8.2,
  best at 4.0-4.9 [3].
- **Salt:** very sensitive to sodium and chloride; high calcium can be
  toxic; use only chloride-free fertilisers [1].
- **Nitrogen:** 20-40 lb N per acre a year in years 1-5 and 40 lb from
  year 6; over 60 lb causes excess growth and lower yield [1].
- **Raised beds:** 4-8 inches (10-20 cm) high, to cut the risk of root
  rot [1].
- **Water:** about 0.5-0.75 inch (13-19 mm) a week by drip; never let the
  soil dry out, because that concentrates salts [1].

**Finding 5 — high yields in small trials, unproven at scale.**
- **Oregon commercial fields (5-8-year-old plants):** 2-15 US tons per
  acre, against 0.5-6 in Germany and 1-3.6 in Sweden [1].
- **Fall Creek, Oregon trials (1999-2003):** average 6.6 tons per acre,
  from 2 tons for 'Red Pearl' to 15 tons for 'Erntesegen' [1].
- **Plot yield series** [2]:
  - medium-yielding cultivars: 997 lb per acre in year 3, rising to
    33,942 lb in year 9;
  - high-yielding: 66,700 lb in year 9;
  - the authors warn these "are not guaranteed in a commercial
    production setting".
- **Wild against cultivated:** wild stands yield from 17.4 kg/ha in
  Swedish peatlands to 500 kg/ha in some Finnish forests; cultivated
  stands up to 8,150 kg/ha [3].
- **Life:** a healthy planting is productive for 20 years or more [1];
  the budget assumed 7 production years and called longevity unknown
  [2].

**Finding 6 — keeps well, thanks to benzoic acid.**
- **Fresh storage:** high natural benzoic acid gives a long shelf life;
  cleaned berries keep 3-5 weeks at 0-7 °C before shipping [1].
- **Storage trial:** 'Sanna', 'Sussi' and wild berries showed no
  noticeable deterioration for 4 weeks at 1.5 °C and 2 weeks at 5.5 °C;
  soluble solids and anthocyanins fell late in storage, and acids fell
  throughout [6].
- **Frozen:** berries keep for many months frozen; IQF fruit goes into
  jams, jellies and juice concentrate [1].
- **Ripening after picking:** berries picked light red darken in
  refrigerated storage over several weeks [1].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Low, evergreen, rhizomatous dwarf shrub, 5-40
  cm tall (cultivated type) [1]. Leaves persist up to 3 years [3].
  Wild rooting depth 5-28 cm [3]; rhizomes 10-20 cm deep in Britain
  [3].
- **Harvest pattern:** Perennial; two crops a year in cultivated
  European types, with the fall crop sold (Finding 2) [1][2].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** First fruit in year 2-3 of the budget
  for high-yielding cultivars (year 1 is land preparation) [2]; seedlings
  first fruit at 3-4 years [3]; beds fill in by year 4-5 [1].
- **Climate zones:**
  - **Climate:** cool and maritime; does well where blueberries and
    cranberries do [1].
  - **Cold:** survived -17 °C in Oregon and Washington; protect plants
    below -12 °C without snow cover [1].
  - **Frost:** ripe fruit has survived -2 °C and green fruit -3 °C;
    frost at bloom or fruiting can cost up to 50% of the crop [1].
  - **Elevation:** sea level to 1,290 m in eastern Canada; to 2,250 m in
    Alberta; 2,100-2,400 m in the Yukon [3].
  - **Chill:** not sourced.
- **Composition:**
  - **Raw lingonberries, per 100 g (Norway):** water 88.0 g; energy 44
    kcal (183 kJ); protein 0.7 g; fat 0.6 g; carbohydrate 7.0 g; fibre
    4.0 g; calcium 25 mg; potassium 118 mg; magnesium 11 mg; iron 0.3 mg;
    vitamin C 8.0 mg; vitamin E 1.5 mg [5]. The table lists sugars as
    0.0 g, which is implausible (see Open research questions).
  - **Fresh-fruit juice quality:** 12.96-14.57 °Brix; pH 2.95-3.18;
    organic acids 1.13-1.73 g/100 g as citric acid; anthocyanins
    49.39-68.74 mg/100 g [6].
  - **Brix by cultivar:** 13.9 ('Erntesegen') to 10.2 ('Erntekrone')
    [1]; mature fruit 9-16 [2].
  - **Berry size:** 0.17-0.45 g; 7-11 mm [1].
  - **Seeds:** 3-15 per berry in North America [3].
  - **Water content:** about 30% less water than cranberries, giving a
    more concentrated juice [1].
- **Edibility:** Raw-edible when ripe, but tart; mostly processed [1][2].
  Unripe berries are bitter [1].
- **Pollination:** Insect, with a 10% pollinizer planting (Finding 3)
  [1].
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced. Not applicable to storage
  organs.
- **Yield:**
  - **Oregon fields:** 2-15 US tons per acre, about 4.5-34 t/ha (see
    Estimates, below) [1].
  - **Trial average:** 6.6 tons per acre, about 14.8 t/ha [1].
  - **Germany and Sweden:** 1.2-14.8 and 2.5-8.9 t/ha [1].
  - **Wild:** 17.4-500 kg/ha [3].
- **Water use:**
  - **Irrigation:** 0.5-0.75 inch a week (13-19 mm) by drip, plus
    irrigation through the first summer to cut sunburn [1].
  - **Crop coefficient:** not sourced.
- **Propagation:**
  - **Methods:** micropropagation (tissue culture) and stem cuttings are
    most common; rhizome divisions and seedlings are also used [1].
  - **Trade-offs:** cuttings may spread poorly; rhizome divisions can
    spread pathogens; seedlings are not true to type [1].
  - **Plant cost (2006):** $1-3 for 1-2-year-old plants and $3-7 for
    2-3-year-old plants [1]. Tissue culture could halve plant cost to
    $1.50 [2].
  - **Clonal/viral risk:** start with disease-free plants [1]; blueberry
    scorch and shock viruses have not been found in lingonberry, but
    growers are advised to watch for them [1].
- **Labor:**
  - **Hand raking:** one person picks 22-45 lb (10-20 kg) an hour with a
    berry rake [2].
  - **Hours per hectare:** not sourced directly (see Estimates, below).
  - **Budget wage assumptions (2003):** general labour $10 an hour and
    harvest labour $15 an hour [2].
  - **Weeding:** critical in the first 3 years; hand weeding and shallow
    cultivation [1].
- **Soil:**
  - **Texture and pH:** see Finding 4 [1][3].
  - **Salinity:** very sensitive to sodium and chloride [1]. No
    threshold value was sourced.
  - **Mulch:** about 10 tons of organic matter per acre incorporated
    before planting, then 4-6 inches of mulch, replenished every 3-6
    years [1].
- **Storage (postharvest):** 3-5 weeks at 0-7 °C (Finding 6) [1][6].
- **Establishment:**
  - **Spacing:** beds 24-36 inches (61-91 cm) apart on centre; plants
    8-18 inches (20-46 cm) apart in the row [1].
  - **Density:** 5,800-10,900 plants per acre, average 8,700 [1]; the
    budget used 5,438 per acre [2].
  - **Cost (2003):** total establishment costs at the end of year 2 were
    almost $30,000 per acre, annualised at just under $5,800 per acre
    over 7 production years [2].
  - **Renewal:** no pruning for 4-5 years; then mow alternate rows to 5
    cm every 3-6 years [1].
- **Cultivars** [1]:
  - **High-yielding:** 'Erntesegen' (0.41 g berries), 'Ida' (0.45 g),
    'Erntekrone', 'Erntedank', 'Ammerland', 'Masovia';
  - **Others:** 'Koralle', 'Linnea', 'Sanna', 'Scarlet', 'Splendor',
    'European Red', 'Regal' (ornamental);
  - **Pollinizers:** 'Red Pearl', 'Sussi';
  - **Origins:** Germany, the Netherlands, Sweden (Balsgård), Poland,
    Norway and the US.
- **Weather response:**
  - **Frost** at bloom (Finding 3) [1];
  - **Rain and wind** hamper pollination [1];
  - **Prolonged freezing** before harvest causes crop loss, though a
    light frost does not [1].
- **Water regime:** Irrigated by drip or overhead sprinkler in
  cultivation [1]; rainfed in the wild [3].
- **Harvest stages / windows:** Pick light to dark red [1]; wait 1-2
  weeks after the first mature fruit so all fruit reaches full weight
  [2]. 'Ida', 'Linnea' and 'Sanna' are overripe by November [1].
- **Soil biology:**
  - **Root rot:** *Phytophthora cinnamomi* and *P. citricola* [1].
  - **Damping off:** *Pythium* on over-watered heavy soils [1].
  - **Allelopathy:** lingonberry produces a compound that reduces annual
    weed germination in the rows [1].
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** Sudden oak death (*Phytophthora
  ramorum*) tested positive on lingonberry, but has not been found in
  growers' fields [1]. No invasiveness listing was found.
- **Controlled environment:** Not sourced.

## Hazards

- **Fungal diseases** [1]:
  - black rot (*Allantophomopsis lycopodina*);
  - upright dieback and twig blight (*Phomopsis columnaris*), first
    identified in Oregon in 2000, which can kill plants;
  - ripe rot (*Coleophoma empetri*);
  - *Rhizoctonia*.
- **Insects:** cucumber beetles and obliquebanded leafroller feed
  lightly; lingonberry fruitworm (*Grapholita libertina*) in
  Newfoundland [1].
- **Vertebrates:** voles and mice eat ripe berries; robins and finches
  attack mainly the fall crop; deer, bears and moose browse new
  plantings [1].
- **Human health:** leaves and stems contain arbutin [1]. No hazard
  from the fruit was found. No mycotoxin finding.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain 1 (fresh):** hand-raked [2] → cleaned and destemmed with a
  blueberry stemmer [1] → stored at 0-7 °C for 3-5 weeks [1] → sold in
  baskets at $2.50-7.00 per pound retail (2006) [1].
- **Chain 2 (frozen and processed):** IQF frozen → jams, jellies,
  syrups, sauces, juice concentrate (10% of the world crop) [1].
- **Chain 3 (dairy and baking):** ready-to-mix preparations for yogurt,
  sherbet and ice cream; muffins, pies and pancakes [1][2].
- **Chain 4 (drinks):** wine and liqueurs [1][2].
- **Chain 5 (leaves):** arbutin extraction for skin-care products, and
  herbal teas [1][2].
- **Colorant:** anthocyanins as natural food colour [1].
- **Consumption:** just over 2 lb per person a year in Sweden and 2.6 lb
  in Finland; Swedish jam makers use 5,500-6,600 tons a year [2].

No religious or ritual use was found in the sources consulted.

## Open research questions

- **Plot against field yields:** the 66,700 lb/acre (about 75 t/ha)
  year-9 plot figure [2] is far above any field yield in [1]; the
  authors say it is not guaranteed commercially.
- **Unit errors in the guide's yield table:** it converts 2.3, 6.6 and
  15 US tons per acre to 5.8, 16.4 and 35.7 t/ha; the correct values are
  5.2, 14.8 and 33.6 t/ha (see Estimates, below) [1]. Its nitrogen line
  pairs 40 lb per acre with "20 kg", which is 18 kg per acre or 45 kg
  per hectare [1].
- **Sugar value:** the Norwegian table's 0.0 g sugars conflicts with
  7.0 g carbohydrate and 13-15 °Brix juice [5][6].
- **Vitamin C:** the guide says "very high in vitamin C" [1], but the
  Norwegian table gives only 8.0 mg/100 g [5].
- **Not sourced:** chill requirement, crop coefficient, labour hours
  per hectare, salt threshold values and controlled-environment use.

## Estimates and derivations

**Yield conversions.** 1 US ton per acre = 0.9072 t ÷ 0.4047 ha = 2.2417
t/ha [1]:
- 2 tons per acre = 4.5 t/ha; 15 tons = 33.6 t/ha;
- 6.6 tons = 14.8 t/ha; 2.3 tons = 5.2 t/ha.

**Plot yields** [2]: 33,942 lb × 0.4536 = 15,396 kg per acre ÷ 0.4047 =
38.0 t/ha (medium, year 9); 66,700 lb = 74.8 t/ha (high, year 9).

**Planting density:**
- 8,700 plants per acre ÷ 0.4047 = 21,500 per ha [1];
- range 5,800-10,900 = 14,300-26,900 per ha [1];
- 5,438 per acre = 13,400 per ha [2].

**Irrigation** [1]: 0.5-0.75 inch × 25.4 = 12.7-19.1 mm a week.

**Hand-harvest labour** (an estimate, not a sourced figure). At the
14.8 t/ha trial average [1] and 10-20 kg an hour raking [2]: 14,800 ÷
20 = 740 hours to 14,800 ÷ 10 = 1,480 hours per hectare per year.

**Rake rate** [2]: 22-45 lb × 0.4536 = 10.0-20.4 kg an hour.

**Wild harvest picked, Finland** (an estimate combining different years).
Almost 10% picked in 1997-1999 [4] × 246 million kg yield (2014-2018)
[4] = about 25 million kg. This is far above the 2,000-10,000 t harvest
range in [2]; the gap suggests the 10% figure includes household
picking not counted in commercial statistics.

**Mulch** [1]: 10 short tons per acre × 2.2417 = 22.4 t/ha.

**Establishment cost per hectare (2003)** [2]: $30,000 ÷ 0.4047 =
$74,100 per ha.
