## Naming and scope

*Rubus idaeus* L. (Rosaceae), red raspberry [1][2]. Two subspecies:
*R. idaeus* subsp. *vulgatus* (European) and subsp. *strigosus* (American),
which "interbreed freely"; most modern commercial cultivars come from
hybrids between them [1][2][3].
- **Yellow raspberries** are colour forms (recessive genes block
  anthocyanin), kept here as a cultivar note [3].
- **Excluded:** black raspberry (*R. occidentalis*, its own row), purple
  raspberry (*R. neglectus*, black × red), *R. glaucus* (South American
  black raspberry) [1].
- **Scope:** one cropID, with two cropping systems (Finding 1).
- **Gate check (2026-09-29, re-confirmed 2026-11-23): passes,** against
  black raspberry and blackberry:
  - the fruit leaves the receptacle behind and is hollow; blackberry's
    torus "picks with" the fruit [3];
  - red raspberry is "relatively cold hardy compared to black raspberries
    and trailing blackberries" [2];
  - it spreads by root suckers, giving hedgerows [2].

**Relatives in this project:**
- black raspberry (`black_raspberry`, `black-raspberry.md`);
- blackberry (`blackberry`, `blackberry.md`).

**Proposed:** none.

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — biennial canes on perennial roots, cropped in two ways.**
- "Although the canes are biennial and live for only 2 years, the roots
  are perennial" [2].
- **Summer-bearing (floricane):** canes 6-13 ft; vegetative in year 1,
  fruit along the whole floricane in year 2; flower initiation in late
  summer as days shorten and cool [2]. Fruit ripens over about 30 days,
  up to 55 in some cultivars [2].
- **Primocane-fruiting (fall-bearing):** canes 4-6 ft; initiation "does
  not depend on day length and temperature, but rather on the
  physiological age of the cane". In 'Heritage' it begins at about 50
  nodes and proceeds down 10-12 nodes [2]. Crops late summer and fall
  [2].
- **Alternate-year cropping** (mowing all canes every other year): yields
  fell 60% over 6 years at WSU Vancouver, "generally" uneconomical [2].
- Fruit matures 30-35 days after pollination, gaining most size in the
  last 4-5 days [2].

**Finding 2 — machine-picked for processing, hand-picked for fresh.**
- "More than 90 percent" of Washington and Oregon raspberries are machine
  harvested; Idaho's are almost all hand harvested [2].
- One harvester with 1 operator and 4 field graders "can do the work of 80
  to 85 hand pickers"; machines run at night [2].
- "Hand harvesting can account for up to two-thirds of the total labor
  cost" [2].
- **Hand picking:** 5-6 pickers/acre at first and last pickings, 10-12 for
  the main crop, 15-20 at a warm peak; beginners pick 40 lb per 6-hour
  day, experienced pickers 60 lb, the best over 100 lb [2].
- **Prices:** processing about $0.60/lb vs. fresh $1.50/lb (PNW, 2000s)
  [2]. 2024: California $3.78/lb, Washington $1.16/lb [4].

**Finding 3 — one of the most perishable fruits.**
- Force-air cool to 1 °C within 12 h; hold 2-5 days at -0.5 to 0 °C, >90%
  RH [1].
- CA: 10-20% CO2 with 5-10% O2; above 20% CO2 causes discolouration and
  off flavours [1].
- "As little as 1 day at 20 °C (68 °F) can result in growth of gray mold";
  not chilling sensitive [1].
- Ethylene 1-12 µL/kg/h at 20 °C; ethylene darkens red fruit and promotes
  *Botrytis* [1].
- Respiration: 16-18 mg CO2/kg/h at 2 °C, 74-175 at 20-21 °C [1].
- "One of the most fragile and perishable of all fruits" [1].

**Finding 4 — Phytophthora root rot and viruses set the site and the stand
life.**
- Wet, heavy soils or excess irrigation bring Phytophthora root rot, "one
  of the most serious pest problems" [3]. PNW 598 measures: raised beds,
  drip instead of overhead irrigation, gypsum at 6 tons/acre before
  planting, sloped and subsoiled alleys [2].
- **Roots:** to 6 ft in well-drained soil, but "70 percent of the roots
  generally are in the upper foot of soil, with 20 percent lying 1 to 2
  feet deep"; 64% of water used came from the top 2 ft [2].
- **Stand life:** "typically about 15 years" for a healthy planting;
  fields with much raspberry bushy dwarf virus (RBDV) and crumbly fruit
  are replanted every 7-8 years, some after the fifth harvest season [2].
- Avoid land that grew tree fruits, strawberries, tomatoes, potatoes,
  eggplants, peppers or caneberries (Verticillium wilt) [2].

**Finding 5 — US yields of about 7-18 t/ha.**
- **US 2024 [4]:** 16,800 acres harvested, 10,800 lb/acre, 180.96 million
  lb. California 15,800 lb/acre; Washington 8,300 (6,400 in 2022);
  Oregon 3,640.
- **US fresh vs. processed 2024:** 96.1 vs. 84.5 million lb utilized [4].
- **History [2]:** US 7,249 lb/acre (2000); Poland 2,809; Germany 5,084;
  France 5,326 (FAO 2005 data). Washington 1980-2005: 4,500-7,500 lb/acre.
- **World (FAOSTAT 2023, via Wikipedia):** 940,979 t; Russia 219,338,
  Mexico 190,412, Serbia 98,674, Poland 96,100, US 62,640 [3]. No area
  figure, so no world yield.

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** perennial crown and roots, biennial canes
  (Finding 1) [2].
- **Harvest pattern:** summer crop over about 30 days (up to 55) on
  floricanes; late-summer and fall crop on primocane types; picking every
  few days (5-6 days between the first and second pickings) [2].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** primocane types fruit on first-year
  canes; summer-bearers fruit on second-year canes [2]. Planting-year
  yields: Not sourced.
- **Climate zones:**
  - hardiness zones 3-9 [3];
  - chill 800-1,400 hours at or below 45 °F (PNW cultivars); 'Meeker'
    needs more than 'Willamette' [2];
  - canes injured at 0 to -10 °F with drying winds; cane tissue 3-27 °F
    hardier than buds; flowers and growing tissue killed near 28 °F [2];
  - some cultivars fruited commercially in Idaho after -24 to -30 °F [2];
  - long-cane production moves chilled canes from Scotland or the PNW to
    places like Spain for early crops [3]; high tunnels extend the season
    [3].
- **Composition (raw, USDA via Wikipedia) [3]:** 53 kcal, water 85.8 g,
  carbohydrate 11.94 g (sugars 4.42), fibre 6.5 g, fat 0.65 g, protein
  1.2 g, vitamin C 26.2 mg, manganese 0.67 mg per 100 g. Main sugars
  sucrose, glucose, fructose; citric acid dominant [1].
- **Edibility:** fresh, IQF, purée, juice, jam, pies, liqueurs [3].
- **Pollination:** flowers have many stamens around many pistils [2]; at
  least two hives per acre, placed in the open facing south [2];
  colony failure rates "reported to be high" [2]. Self-fertility: Not
  sourced this pass.
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** summer-bearers initiate flowers as days
  shorten in late summer; primocane types do not depend on day length [2].
- **Yield:** Finding 5. Components: canes per acre, nodes, bud break,
  laterals, fruit per lateral, drupelet number and size; plants
  compensate when one falls [2].
- **Water use:**
  - FAO-56 "Berries (bushes)": Kc ini 0.30, mid 1.05, end 0.50; height
    1.5 m; rooting depth 0.6-1.2 m; p = 0.50 [5]. FAO-56 gives no stage
    lengths for berries.
  - PNW 598 worked example: 4 ft effective root depth, 2 in available
    water per ft, irrigate at 65% depletion (5.2 in); "Raspberries use 1
    inch of irrigation water for each inch of water lost from the
    evaporation pan" [2].
  - Water stress cuts drupelet size and berry weight [2].
- **Propagation:** dormant canes (at least 1/4 inch diameter) with roots,
  root cuttings, or tissue-culture plugs from certified, virus-tested
  stock [2][3]. Suckers spread plants [3].
- **Labor:** harvest only (Finding 2). Other operations: Not sourced.
- **Soil:** pH 6.2-6.8, lower limit about 5.6 (lime below); above 7.0
  causes iron chlorosis (sulfur to lower) [2]. Wikipedia: pH 6-7 [3].
  Avoid shallow, eroded soils [2].
- **Nutrients [2]:**
  - summer-bearing: 30-50 lb N/acre in the establishment year, 50-80 lb
    after;
  - primocane-fruiting: 30-50 then 70-100 lb N/acre;
  - senescing floricanes return 1-2 g N per plant (4-8 lb/acre) to the
    crown.
  - Nutrient removal per tonne: Not sourced.
- **Storage (postharvest):** Finding 3 [1].
- **Establishment [2]:**
  - rows 10-12 ft apart (8 ft possible with small tractors);
  - hedgerows: plants about 24 inches apart; hills about 30 inches;
  - trellis posts 25-30 ft apart; one wire 12-24 in up for the drip line.
  - Wikipedia: 2-6 plants per metre, raised beds where root rot is a risk
    [3].
- **Cultivars:**
  - 'Meeker' is about 70% of PNW plant sales [2];
  - HB66: 'Meeker', 'Heritage', 'Tulameen', 'Willamette', 'Chilliwack',
    'Munger' [1];
  - primocane: 'Heritage', 'Autumn Bliss', 'Joan J', 'Polka' [2][3];
  - UK summer: 'Glen Ample', 'Glen Moy', 'Malling Jewel', 'Tulameen' [3];
  - 'Cascade Bounty' very tolerant of root rot; 'Cascade Delight' for hand
    harvest only [2].
- **Weather response:** cold, wind (Climate); rain promotes grey mould
  [3]; dust raises spider-mite risk [2].
- **Water regime:** irrigated in the PNW, by overhead sprinklers or drip
  (drip preferred against root rot) [2].
- **Soil biology:** Phytophthora and Verticillium (Finding 4) [2][3].
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** "can be locally invasive"; escaped
  plants spread by bird-dispersed seed [3]. Clean stock is required
  against viruses [2].
- **Controlled environment:** high tunnels, at close spacing [3].

## Hazards

- **Diseases:**
  - Phytophthora root rot (Finding 4) [2][3];
  - Verticillium wilt [2][3];
  - RBDV, alone or with other viruses, causes crumbly fruit; a British
    Columbia trial on 'Meeker' found fruit yield cut by 72% after
    inoculation [2][3];
  - raspberry mosaic complex; tomato ringspot virus [3];
  - grey mould (*Botrytis cinerea*) and Rhizopus rot after harvest [1].
- **Pests:** Japanese beetle, raspberry beetle (Europe), raspberry
  fruitworm (North America), large raspberry aphids [3]; spider mites [2].
- **Postharvest disorders:** shrivelling, leakers, UV-whitened drupelets
  [1].
- **Ergot / mycotoxin:** none reported this pass.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain 1 (fresh):** hand-picked → forced-air cooled to 1 °C within 12 h
  → 125 g clamshells, 12 per tray → held 2-5 days at -0.5 to 0 °C [1].
- **Chain 2 (processing):** machine-harvested at night → bins →
  processors → IQF, purée, juice, dried fruit [2][3].
- **Chain 3 (preserves):** jam, pies, ice cream, liqueurs [3].
- **Grades:** U.S. No. 1 and No. 2 (1% mould limit for No. 1) [1].
- **Not detailed here:** IQF line practice, juice concentrate, long-cane
  nursery practice.

- **Rooting depth:** FAO-56 0.6-1.2 m [5] vs. PNW's 4 ft example and roots

## Estimates and derivations

**Yield conversions (1 lb/acre = 1.121 kg/ha) [2][4]:**
- US 2024: 10,800 × 1.121 = 12.1 t/ha;
- California 2024: 15,800 × 1.121 = 17.7 t/ha;
- Washington 2024: 8,300 × 1.121 = 9.3 t/ha; 2022: 6,400 → 7.2 t/ha;
- Oregon 2024: 3,640 → 4.1 t/ha;
- Poland 2000: 2,809 → 3.1 t/ha; US 2000: 7,249 → 8.1 t/ha.

**Hand-picking rate [2]:** 60 lb = 27.2 kg per 6-hour day = 4.5 kg/h;
range 40-100+ lb = 18-45+ kg/day (3.0-7.6 kg/h).

**Hives [2]:** 2 per acre × 2.471 = 4.9 per ha.

**Plant density [2]:** rows 10-12 ft (3.05-3.66 m) × 24 in (0.61 m) in-row:
10,000 / (3.05 × 0.61) = 5,375 and 10,000 / (3.66 × 0.61) = 4,479
plants/ha.

**Nitrogen [2]:** 50-80 lb/acre × 1.121 = 56-90 kg N/ha; 70-100 lb =
78-112 kg; 30-50 lb = 34-56 kg.

**Temperatures [2]:** 45 °F = 7.2 °C; 28 °F = -2.2 °C; 0 to -10 °F = -17.8
to -23.3 °C; -24 to -30 °F = -31.1 to -34.4 °C; 3-27 °F difference =
1.7-15 °C.

**Water example [2]:** 4 ft = 1.22 m root depth; 8 in = 203 mm available;
65% = 5.2 in = 132 mm per irrigation. The 1:1 pan ratio is a crop factor
of about 1.0 on pan evaporation, not an FAO-56 Kc.

No other estimates are made in this pass.
