## Naming and scope

*Ananas comosus* (L.) Merr. (Bromeliaceae), pineapple [1][2][6].
- **Plant:** terrestrial herb "2 1/2 to 5 ft (.75-1.5 m) high with a
  spread of 3 to 4 ft (.9-1.2 m)", leaves 50-180 cm long [1]. Five
  botanical varieties, formerly separate species [6].
- **Origin:** the Paraná-Paraguay river drainages [1][6].
- **Production:** 29.4 million t in 2024, led by Costa Rica (3.1), the
  Philippines (2.9) and Indonesia (2.7) (FAOSTAT, via Wikipedia) [6].
- **Scope:** one cropID. Cultivar groups: 'Smooth Cayenne', 'Red
  Spanish', 'Queen', 'Abacaxi' (Pernambuco), 'Sugarloaf', 'Perolera', and
  the modern 'MD-2' hybrid [1][2][6][9].
- **Gate check (2026-09-29, re-confirmed 2026-11-23): passes.** A major
  world fruit crop.

**Relatives in this project:** none in the Bromeliaceae. It is the only
herbaceous, CAM-pathway fruit crop in the fruit list.

**Proposed:** none.

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — a CAM plant whose water use is mostly soil evaporation.**
- Pineapple "carries out CAM photosynthesis", fixing CO2 at night and
  storing it as malate [6].
- FAO-56: "The pineapple plant has very low transpiration because it
  closes its stomates during the day... Therefore, the majority of ETc
  from pineapple is evaporation from the soil." Kc mid is lower than
  Kc ini because full ground cover reduces soil evaporation [3].
- Rainfall: ideal "about 45 in (1,143 mm)"; tolerates 650-3,800 mm a year
  [1]. Cannot tolerate waterlogging [1].

**Finding 2 — flowering is forced with ethylene.**
- 1874, Azores: smoke brought plants into bloom in 6 weeks; ethylene was
  later found to be the active agent [1].
- From 1936: acetylene, or calcium carbide placed in the crown [1].
- BOH: "Treatment is given when the plants are 6 months old, 3 months
  before natural flowering time", at the 30-leaf stage [1].
- Ghana 'Sugarloaf': carbide and BOH equally effective at 42-46 weeks;
  Ethrel best at 35-38 weeks [1].
- Ethrel at the first sign of ripening makes all fruit ripen together and
  saves harvest passes [1]. HB66: postharvest ethephon degreens the shell
  but slightly shortens shelf-life and "is not approved for postharvest
  use" [2].

**Finding 3 — vegetative planting, a plant crop, then ratoons.**
- Planting material: crowns, slips, suckers, ratoons and stumps; seed is
  for breeding only [1].
- Vegetative plants fruit in 15-22 months; seedlings are planted out at
  15-18 months and fruit 16-30 months later [1].
- After the first harvest, "all but 2 ratoons" are trimmed off; they bear
  in 15-18 months. A second or third ratoon may follow before clearing
  [1]. Some 'Red Spanish' plantings lasted 25-26 years [1].
- Costa Rica (snippet): 360 days for the first crop and 668 days for the
  second [11].
- 'P.R. 1-67' at 51,265 plants/ha: 86 t/ha main crop, 45.43 t/ha ratoon
  [1].
- Hummingbirds are the main pollinators and "are prohibited in Hawaii to
  avoid the development of undesired seeds" [1][6].

**Finding 4 — non-climacteric fruit with delayed chilling injury.**
- "Fruit do not continue to ripen or sweeten after harvest"; minimum 12%
  SSC for fresh fruit in Hawaii [2].
- Storage: 7-12 °C, 85-95% RH, 14-20 days at colour break; ripe fruit
  7-10 days at 7.2 °C; maximum about 4 weeks at 7 °C [2].
- Chilling injury (internal browning, endogenous brown spot, blackheart)
  appears after return to 15-30 °C, "within 2 to 3 days" [2].
- Morton gives chilling injury at 4.44 °C and lower, normal ripening at
  7-8 °C and above with 80-90% RH, and storage at best 4-6 weeks [1].
- Blackheart is treated with a one-day heat treatment at 32-38 °C [1].

**Finding 5 — the whole plant is used.**
- **Bromelain:** "Present in all parts of the pineapple plant" [6]; now
  extracted from mature stems salvaged at field clearing [1]. 167 kg of
  stem juice yields 3.6 kg of bromelain [1]. The European Medicines
  Agency authorised a bromelain debridement product (NexoBrid) in 2012
  [7].
- **Fibre (piña):** a Philippine leaf fibre; 'Red Spanish' was grown for
  textiles from the 17th century [8]; Morton dates Filipino extraction to
  before 1591 [1]. 22,000 leaves (1 ton) yield 22-27 kg of fibre [1].
  Aklan piña weaving was inscribed by UNESCO in 2023 [8].
- **Feed:** factory waste is dried to bran for cattle, pigs and chickens
  [1]. In 1982 in Hawaii, heptachlor reached milk from cows fed green chop
  of sprayed leaves; a "one year lapse" is advised [1].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** perennial herb; one fruit per shoot per cycle,
  then ratoons (Finding 3) [1].
- **Harvest pattern:** plant crop, then 1-3 ratoon crops; forced
  flowering and Ethrel allow scheduled, single-pass harvests [1].
- **Days to first harvest:** 15-22 months from vegetative planting [1];
  360 days (Costa Rica, snippet) [11]. FAO-56 stage lengths for Hawaii:
  60 / 120 / 600 / 10 = 790 days [3]. 'Red Spanish' reaches shipping-green
  150 days after natural bloom; 'Ripley' takes 22 weeks from flowering to
  maturity [1].
- **Climate zones:**
  - **Latitude:** "low elevations between 30°N and 25°S" outside
    greenhouses [1].
  - **Temperature:** favourable 65-95 °F, printed as 18.33-45 °C [1]
    (95 °F is 35 °C, see Estimates). Prolonged cold "retards growth,
    delays maturity and causes the fruit to be more acid" [1].
  - **Altitude:** Hawaii 'Smooth Cayenne' sea level to 600 m; Kenya
    1,371-1,738 m best for canning; Ecuador to 2,300 m [1].
  - **Heat:** sunburn is common above 35 °C [2].
  - **Frost:** no tolerance figure found.
  - **Chill:** none required (tropical); Not sourced as a figure.
- **Composition (per 100 g raw):** water 86.0 g, energy 50 kcal (209 kJ),
  protein 0.54 g, fat 0.12 g, carbohydrate 13.12 g, fibre 1.4 g,
  potassium 109 mg, vitamin C 47.8 mg (FDC 169124) [5]. Sugars 9.85 g
  [6]. Central American fruit: moisture 81.3-91.2 g, ascorbic acid
  27.0-165.2 mg [1].
- **Edibility:** raw when ripe; unripe fruit is poisonous (Hazards) [1].
- **Pollination:** not required; the fruit sets without seed, and seed
  lowers quality [1][6].
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced as a figure. Winter-flowered
  fruit are more malformed [2], and the winter crop matures about 30 days
  later [1].
- **Yield:**
  - World: about 27 t/ha (snippets give 27 and 28 t/ha for 2023) [11];
    derived 26.4 t/ha (Estimates).
  - Trials: 69.12 t/ha at 43,200 plants/ha; 'P.R. 1-67' 86 t/ha main
    crop and 45.43 t/ha ratoon; 75 t/ha at 32,167 plants/ha (all as
    printed) [1].
  - Costa Rica (snippet): average 67 t/ha, range 2.8-113.4 t/ha [11].
  - Venezuela: 15,000 fruits/ha is "a very good crop" [1].
  - India model budget: 25 and 20 t/acre in years 2 and 3 (as printed)
    [10].
- **Water use:**
  - **Kc (FAO-56):** bare soil 0.50 / 0.30 / 0.30; with grass cover 0.50
    / 0.50 / 0.50. Assumes 50% black plastic mulch and sprinklers; reduce
    by 0.10 for drip under plastic [3].
  - **Maximum crop height:** 0.6-1.2 m [3].
  - **Rooting depth:** 0.3-0.6 m; depletion fraction p = 0.50 [3].
  - **Irrigation:** only in dry seasons, and it "should not exceed 1 in
    (2.5 cm) semi-monthly" [1].
- **Propagation:**
  - Slips, crowns and suckers are planted on raised, mulched beds [1].
  - Planting depth: crowns 5 cm; slips and suckers 9-10 cm [1].
  - **Butts:** sprout in 6-8 weeks and give an average of 6 suckers, up to
    25. A one-acre nursery of 25,000 butts yields 100,000-200,000 suckers
    [1].
  - **Stem slices:** 3-4 shoots per slice, transplanted at 6-8 weeks [1].
  - **Clean stock:** dip propagules in fungicide, since base rot enters
    through wounds [1][6].
- **Labor:**
  - Not sourced as hours.
  - Machine planting: 3 men set 7,000 plants an hour, and advanced
    machines up to 50,000 a day [1].
  - Hand weeding is "difficult and expensive" [1].
  - India model budget: 68 man-days of intercultural work in year 1; 30
    man-days intercultural plus 30 man-days of harvest, grading and
    transport in each later year [10].
  - Kerala (snippet): labour is 42.5% of cultivation cost [11].
- **Soil:**
  - **Texture:** "well-drained, sandy loam with a high content of organic
    matter", friable to at least 60 cm [1].
  - **pH:** 4.5-6.5, with sulphur where the soil is not acid enough [1];
    5-6.5 (Ghana) [9].
  - **Salinity:** moderately tolerant (MT), from shoot dry weight; no
    threshold given [4]. FAO-56 also rates it MT [3].
  - **Nutrients:**
    - Kenya: 471.7 kg N/ha in 4 applications, with no gain from added P
      and K [1].
    - Puerto Rico: urea sprays supplying 151 kg N/ha gave maximum yield
      [1].
    - Mg: 327 kg/ha of magnesium sulphate on acid sandy clay raised yield
      (printed figures inconsistent; see Estimates) [1].
    - Zinc deficiency causes crookneck at 12-15 months [1].
    - Potassium uptake is minimal below 20 °C soil temperature [1].
- **Storage (postharvest):** Finding 4 [1][2]. CA: tentative 2-5% O2 with
  5-10% CO2 [2]. Ethylene 0.1-1.0 µL/kg·h [2]. Respiration 2 (5 °C), 4-7
  (10 °C), 10-16 (15 °C) and 19-29 (20 °C) mg CO2/kg·h [2]. Fresh-cut: at
  least 7 days [2].
- **Establishment:**
  - **Double rows:** 25-30 cm apart in the row, 60 cm between rows. Alleys
    of 0.9, 1.6 or 1.8 m give 42,700, 37,920 or 33,800 plants/ha [1].
  - **Ghana:** 30 × 25 × 90 cm [9].
  - **Costa Rica (snippet):** about 54,000 plants/ha [11].
  - **Density effects:** close spacing gives the highest total weight but
    smaller, elongated fruit and fewer slips; very wide spacing causes
    multiple crowns [1].
- **Cultivars:**
  - **'Smooth Cayenne':** 1.8-4.5 kg, low fibre; mainly for canning;
    disease-prone and ships poorly [1].
  - **'Red Spanish':** 1.36-2.7 kg, highly resistant to fruit rot [1], and
    more resistant to black rot than 'Smooth Cayenne' [2].
  - **'Queen':** 0.45-1.13 kg, more cold- and disease-resistant [1].
  - **'Abacaxi':** 1-5 kg, "rated by many as the most delicious", too
    tender to ship [1].
  - **'Sugarloaf':** 0.68-1.36 kg [1].
  - **'Cabezona':** natural tetraploid [1].
  - **'P.R. 1-67':** resists gummosis and mealybug wilt [1].
  - **'MD-2' (73-114):** launched by Del Monte in Costa Rica in 1996; it
    replaced 'Smooth Cayenne' in Hawaii and US stores [6]. Fruit 1.3-2.5
    kg, Brix 15-17, low acid; highly susceptible to Phytophthora [9].
- **Weather response:** cold raises acidity [1]; sunburn above 35 °C [2];
  Phytophthora top and root rot in prolonged wet weather [1].
- **Water regime:** upland, raised beds; no waterlogging [1].
- **Soil biology:**
  - **Nematodes:** they stunt plants unless the soil is fumigated, and
    nematicides raised yields 22-40% in Queensland [1].
  - **Rotation:** three years of pangola grass or green foxtail suppresses
    nematodes [1].
  - **Replant:** clearing means 12-16 weeks of drying before burning;
    paraquat cuts this to 3 weeks, but stumps take 3-5 months to rot [1].
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** 'Smooth Cayenne'-majority fruit is not
  a tephritid fruit-fly host; pineapple caterpillars (*Thecla basilides*
  and others) are exotic quarantine pests outside the Americas [2].
- **Controlled environment:** historic European glasshouse cultivation
  [1][6]; potted ethylene-treated plants are shipped from Florida as
  ornamentals [1].

## Hazards

- **Pests:** mealybugs (*Pseudococcus brevipes*, *P. neobrevipes*) causing
  wilt, spread by ants [1]; nematodes [1]; mites and pineapple scale [2];
  *Thecla basilides* [1][2].
- **Diseases:** Phytophthora top and root rot [1]; base or black rot
  (*Ceratocystis*/*Thielaviopsis*, *Chalara paradoxa*), which infects
  within 8-12 h of harvest [1][2]; fruitlet core rot, worst in low-acid
  cultivars [2]; Erwinia heart rot; yellow spot virus [1].
- **Spines:** 'Perolera' has smooth leaves without a tip spine [1];
  'MD-2' is spineless, and 'Smooth Cayenne' is spineless except at the
  leaf base and tip [9]. Spininess of other groups: Not sourced this pass.
- **Ergot / mycotoxin:** none reported this pass.

**TO BE ADDRESSED** — unripe fruit is "not only inedible but poisonous,
irritating the throat and acting as a drastic purgative"; eating too many
cores caused bezoars; very young fruit was a folk abortifacient [1].
Bromelain is a proteolytic enzyme with drug uses [7]. Heptachlor carryover
to milk via fed leaves (1982) [1]. Deferred until this project has a place
for health-and-safety data.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain 1 (fresh):** harvested by hand crews or conveyor booms → cut
  stem treated against black rot → graded by colour and size → 7-12 °C
  storage → fresh or fresh-cut [1][2].
- **Chain 2 (canning and juice):** fruit skinned, cored and sliced →
  canned in syrup; trimmings to juice and concentrate [1].
- **Chain 3 (bromelain):** stems salvaged at clearing → juice → bromelain
  [1].
- **Chain 4 (fibre):** long outer leaves → decorticated and retted → piña
  fibre and cloth [1][8].
- **Chain 5 (feed):** factory waste → dried bran; old plants → silage
  with urea and molasses [1].
- **Other foods:** nata de piña (fermented), young shoots and hearts as
  vegetables [1].
- **Not detailed here:** pineapple vinegar and alcoholic drinks.

## Estimates and derivations

**World yield (derived):** 29 million t ÷ 1.1 million ha (2023 snippets)
[11] = 26.4 t/ha. This is consistent with the snippet's 27 t/ha.

**Temperature [1]:** 65 °F = 18.3 °C and 95 °F = 35 °C. Morton's printed
"18.33-45 °C" has a wrong upper bound; this doc uses 35 °C.

**FAO-56 cycle [3]:** 60 + 120 + 600 + 10 = 790 days.

**Morton's Mg figure [1]:** 2.7 tons/acre is about 6 t/ha, not the
printed 64.8 t/ha, which looks like a total yield. The 3 tons/acre (7
t/ha) figure is used.

**India model budget [10]:** at 1 acre = 0.405 ha, 25 and 20 t/acre are
62 and 49 t/ha, if "Mt/Acre" means metric tonnes per acre. The budget does
not state the area its man-days refer to.

**Ratoon fraction [1]:** 45.43 ÷ 86 = 0.53 of the main crop ('P.R. 1-67').

No other estimates are made in this pass.
