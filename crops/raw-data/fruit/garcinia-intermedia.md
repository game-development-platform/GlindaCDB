## Naming and scope

*Garcinia intermedia* (Pittier) Hammel (Clusiaceae / Guttiferae): lemon drop
mangosteen, monkey fruit, cherry mangosteen, *mameyito*, *berba*, wild-lemon
rheedia [1][2]. Older literature places it in the segregate genus *Rheedia*,
so the synonyms **_Rheedia intermedia_ Pittier** and **_Rheedia edulis_**
(Seem.) Planch. & Triana refer to this same species — the latter a sourcing
hazard, since "edulis" reads like a different plant [1][3]. It is a small
evergreen tree of the warm American tropics [1][2].

This row was flagged for proposal in `charichuelo.md` ("another New World
mangosteen, not proposed this pass") and is researched here on its own gate
check [5].

**Distinctiveness within the project's *Garcinia* set.** The project now holds
true mangosteen (`mangosteen`, *G. mangostana*), button mangosteen
(`button_mangosteen`, *G. prainiana*), charichuelo (`charichuelo`, *G.
madruno*), *G. dulcis* (`garcinia_dulcis`), and the souring spices
`kokum`/`malabar_tamarind`. *G. intermedia* is distinct from all of them [5]:
- **vs charichuelo** (the nearest — also a Neotropical "New World mangosteen"):
  charichuelo's fruit is **warty/bumpy and yellow** with whitish subacid pulp,
  slow to bear (~5-7 yr); *G. intermedia*'s is a **small (~4 cm), smooth**
  globe ripening **orange to red**, eaten out of hand, and reportedly
  **precocious** (Finding 2) [1][5]. Two different species, though trade notes
  call the pulp flavour "madruno-like" [2].
- **vs the Malesian mangosteens:** wholly separate hemisphere (American vs
  Malesian) and species [5].

No sub-rows are proposed. This passes the distinctiveness check on taxonomy,
range, fruit morphology, bearing speed and farming system.

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — a small, smooth "lemon drop" fruit eaten out of hand, with
antioxidant levels reported above true mangosteen.** The fruit is a globose
berry about 4 cm (an inch) across, with a thin rind ripening from yellow
through orange to red and a translucent-to-white, juicy pulp of sweet-and-sour
("subacid") flavour; it holds few (1-5) seeds and exudes yellow latex when cut
— the group's hallmark [1][2]. It is eaten mostly fresh, and also made into
drinks, jams and jellies [1]. A 2022 review reports the fruit has **higher
antioxidant capacity than true mangosteen** (*G. mangostana*), while noting
how little is known of the species' horticulture — "including basics such as
asexual propagation" [4]. One Costa Rican botanical source instead calls
mature fruit "orange and bitter," conflicting with the sweet-subacid
descriptions and probably reflecting over-mature or wild material (see Open
questions) [2].

**Finding 2 — unusually precocious for a *Garcinia*, which is its main appeal
to growers.** Multiple sources report it can **bear in as little as 2-3 years
from seed** [1][2]. If reliable, that is dramatically faster than true
mangosteen (10-20 yr) and faster than charichuelo (~5-7 yr) or achachairú
(*G. humilis*, 7-8 yr) — making precocity the trait that distinguishes it
agronomically within the genus [1][5]. The claim leans on nursery/hobbyist
sources and conflicts with one "very slow growing" description, so it is
reported but not fully corroborated (see Open questions) [1][2].

**Finding 3 — the sexual system is reported inconsistently, so orchard sex
management can't be settled from this pass.** *Garcinia* is typically
dioecious (as recorded here for `button_mangosteen` and `charichuelo`), and a
Hawaii-Pacific weed-risk-assessment keyword list tags *G. intermedia*
"dioecious" [3]. But Singapore's NParks flora entry describes its flowers as
**bisexual**, and the broader *Rheedia*/*Garcinia* dioecy note does not list
this species [1][2]. Because the sources disagree, this doc does **not** assert
a sex system — unlike its confirmed-dioecious siblings it cannot yet join the
"plant sex managed at planting" pattern (see Open questions) [1][3].

**Finding 4 — a minor, widely-introduced collector/dooryard fruit rather than
a commodity.** It is native to the warm tropics of Mexico and Central America
(the 2022 review gives 300-1,000 m elevation), with range statements extending
variously toward Ecuador; it is grown on a **small scale** across Belize,
Costa Rica, Ecuador, El Salvador, Mexico and Panama, and widely planted
elsewhere in the tropics (e.g. Hawaii's Lyon Arboretum) as a rare-fruit and
ornamental tree [1][2][4]. No production tonnage, yield, water-use or labour
data specific to the species was found — consistent with minor-crop /
collector status [1][4].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Perennial evergreen tree, ~5-12 m (height reports
  conflict, from ~5 m to much taller) [1][2].
- **Harvest pattern:** Perennial bearing; a fleshy berry harvested when the
  rind colours orange to red. Harvest window/season: Not sourced for a specific
  region.
- **Days/years to first bearing:** Reported as **~2-3 years from seed** —
  exceptionally early for the genus, but on weak/hobbyist sourcing (Finding 2)
  [1][2].
- **Climate zones:** Warm lowland-to-mid-elevation tropics; the review gives
  **300-1,000 m** in Mexico/Central America [4]. Frost-tender tropical tree;
  no chill requirement. Hardiness-zone figure not firmly sourced.
- **Composition:** Fruit reported to have **higher antioxidant capacity than
  *G. mangostana*** [4]; per-100 g nutrition figures: Not sourced.
- **Edibility:** Eaten raw/fresh out of hand; sweet-subacid pulp (one source
  reports bitterness in over-mature fruit) [1][2].
- **Pollination:** Not resolved — sources disagree on whether the species is
  dioecious or bisexual-flowered (Finding 3) [1][3].
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced; not expected to apply to a tropical
  tree fruit.
- **Yield:** Not sourced.
- **Water use:** Not sourced. Tolerant of a range of tropical sites; no
  crop-coefficient data found.
- **Propagation:** Generally **by seed**; grafting is possible but **asexual
  propagation is poorly documented**, which the 2022 review calls out as a
  basic knowledge gap [1][4]. Whether its seed is apomictic/nucellar (as in
  true mangosteen and some charichuelo) was **not sourced**, and bears on
  whether seedlings come true — an open question given the unresolved sex
  system [1][5].
- **Labor:** Not sourced.
- **Soil:** Not sourced in detail; reported to tolerate a range of tropical
  soils. pH, texture and **salinity tolerance:** Not sourced.
- **Storage (postharvest):** Not sourced. Thin-skinned small *Garcinia* fruit
  are generally perishable, but no regime or shelf-life figure was found.
- **Establishment:** Planted as seedlings (or occasionally grafted); spacing
  and nursery practice: Not sourced [1].
- **Cultivars:** No named cultivars found; propagated as seedling selections by
  rare-fruit growers. Fruit colour/size vary between trees [1][2].
- **Weather response:** Frost-tender tropical tree; drought/waterlogging
  tolerance not quantified [1].
- **Water regime:** Ordinary tropical rainfall/irrigation; no paddy
  requirement [1].
- **Harvest stages / windows:** Picked on rind colour (orange→red); specific
  seasonal windows not sourced [1][2].
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** Native to Mexico/Central America; **subject
  to a Hawaii-Pacific weed-risk assessment** (Plant Pono), i.e. formally
  screened where introduced, though no confirmed invasive listing or
  prohibition was found in this pass [3]. Widely introduced across the tropics
  as a cultivated fruit [1][2].
- **Controlled environment:** Not sourced; grown in botanical-garden and
  collector plantings but no closed-system production data found.

## Hazards

- No specific human-toxicity hazard was found for the fruit, which is a
  common edible; the plant exudes **yellow latex** when cut (typical of
  *Garcinia*), relevant to handling rather than a food hazard [2]. The
  *Garcinia* genus more broadly includes the hydroxycitric-acid souring
  spices (`kokum`, `malabar_tamarind`), but no equivalent processed-extract
  safety issue was sourced for *G. intermedia* [5]. **No hazards found in this
  pass** beyond the latex note.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain (food):** small smooth fruit → eaten **fresh, out of hand** (the
  dominant use); or → pulped → drinks, jams and jellies [1][2].
- **Ornamental / collector:** planted as a rare-fruit and landscape tree in
  botanical collections and tropical gardens, prized partly for how **young it
  fruits** (Finding 2) [1][2].
- **Not otherwise detailed here:** any phenolic/antioxidant extract use implied
  by Finding 1; the madruno-like-flavour comparison; processing yields and
  spoilage deadlines (none sourced).

## Open research questions

- **Sexual system (dioecious vs bisexual-flowered)** is contradicted between
  sources (Finding 3); resolving it decides whether the crop needs both sexes
  planted and whether it would join schema Entry 3 ("plant sex managed at
  planting") alongside `button_mangosteen` and `charichuelo` [1][3].
- **Bearing age (~2-3 yr)** rests on weak sourcing and conflicts with a
  "slow-growing" description; a horticultural source (the 2022 review's full
  text) would confirm or correct the precocity claim that defines the crop
  [1][2][4].
- **Apomixis / seed fidelity:** whether seed is nucellar (coming true) as in
  some of its cousins — not sourced, and tied to the sex-system question [1][5].
- **Flavour conflict:** sweet-subacid vs "orange and bitter" when mature —
  likely a maturity/wild-vs-selected effect, unconfirmed [2].
- **No quantitative agronomy** (yield, water, labour, storage regime,
  production, soils, cultivars) was found; the 2022 review itself notes the
  horticulture is barely documented [4].
- **Native-range extent** (Mexico/Central America only, or south to Ecuador)
  is stated inconsistently across sources [1][2].
