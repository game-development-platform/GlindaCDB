## Naming and scope

*Garcinia dulcis* (Roxb.) Kurz (Clusiaceae, also Guttiferae), an Indo-Malesian
fruit tree [1][5]. It is the third of the edible *Garcinia* proposed from
`mangosteen.md` — the one "introduced across the Pacific as a fruit suited to
village production" [6]. Like button mangosteen and charichuelo it falls under the
loose "mangosteen = whole genus" naming noted there, though it is not usually
called a "mangosteen" in English [6].

- **Names:** **mundu** is the usual trade and Indonesian/Thai name [1][2][4].
  Other local names exist across its wide range but were not pinned down this pass.
- **Range:** very wide across tropical Asia to Australasia. Kew's POWO gives the
  native range as **Assam to northern Queensland**, with records in the Andaman and
  Nicobar Islands, Cambodia, Laos, Peninsular Malaysia, Borneo, Java, the
  Philippines and the Bismarck Archipelago [5]. It was domesticated early and
  spread inland into mainland Asia; Java is listed as both native and cultivated
  [1][5].
- **Gamboge cross-link.** `mangosteen.md` uses "gamboge" for true mangosteen's
  yellow latex and its latex disorder; *G. dulcis* is a literal source of the
  gamboge **dye**, extracted from its unripe fruit, with the bark also used to dye
  mats (Uses) [1][6].

**Gate check (2026-11-26): passes.** Proposed from `mangosteen.md` with no farming
data read that pass [6]. It is distinct enough for its own row on every count:
- **Taxonomy:** a validly distinct species, *G. dulcis*, not a form of true
  mangosteen, button mangosteen or charichuelo [1][5].
- **Range:** an exceptionally wide Indo-Malesian-to-N-Queensland distribution [5] —
  broader than, and different from, the other three *Garcinia* rows.
- **Farming system — the distinctive point:** it is **not grown commercially**;
  it is mostly **wild-harvested**, with occasional **dooryard cultivation** near
  village homes and fruit sold locally [1][3]. That semi-wild, village-production
  system sets it apart from commercially-orcharded true mangosteen [6].
- **Harvest / uses:** a globose light-yellow-to-orange fruit with soft, butter-like
  sweet-acid (often sour) flesh, eaten fresh and, above all, made into **jam** and
  sherbet, plus a **dye** use (Finding 1) [1][2][5].

**Relatives in this project:** `mangosteen.md` (the proposing doc), and the other
edible/souring *Garcinia* now in the project — `button-mangosteen.md`,
`charichuelo.md`, `kokum.md` (*G. indica*) and `malabar-tamarind.md` (*G.
gummi-gutta*) [6]. With this row the three *Garcinia* fruits proposed from
`mangosteen.md` are all researched. No new relatives were surfaced for proposing.

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — a sour dooryard fruit grown mainly for jam, and for dye.** The fruit
is globose, light yellow to orange or golden, with soft, yellow, butter-like flesh
that is sweet-acid but often distinctly **sour**; it can be eaten fresh but is
valued above all for **jam** (PROSEA calls it "excellent"), and also sherbet
[1][2][5]. A separate, long-standing use is as a **dye**: a yellow gamboge dye is
extracted from the unripe fruit, and the bark dyes mats [1]. The tree is evergreen,
with horizontal branches and a dense pyramidal crown, to about 15 m [3].

**Finding 2 — wild-harvested and dooryard-grown, not a commercial orchard crop.**
*G. dulcis* is **not grown commercially**: it is mostly gathered from the wild as a
local source of food, medicine and dye, and is **sometimes cultivated near village
homes**, with fruit occasionally sold in local markets [1][3]. This "suited to
village production" character is exactly how `mangosteen.md` flagged it [6]. It
therefore sits between a true wild resource and an orchard crop — a semi-domesticated
dooryard fruit — but there was no sign of a *managed* wild-harvest system
(quotas, rights), so it is treated as an ordinary (if minor) crop here, not a
`researchNotes/wild-managed/` resource.

**Finding 3 — flowers in the dry season; grown from seed or air-layer.** NParks
reports flowering in the **hot, dry period**, echoing the dry-spell flowering cue
of true mangosteen (though not described as a deliberate stress treatment here)
[4][6]. Propagation is by **seed**, and **air-layering** is also listed as a method
[4] — a contrast with charichuelo, for which air-layering is reported ineffective
(`charichuelo.md`) [6]. No grafting practice was found for this species, and its
**sexual system (dioecious or monoecious) could not be confirmed** — a notable gap
given how much the sex system matters across this *Garcinia* cluster.

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Evergreen tree to ~15 m, horizontal branches, dense
  pyramidal crown [3]. Carries *Garcinia* yellow latex/gamboge (Uses) [1].
- **Harvest pattern:** Perennial bearing; fruit eaten fresh or processed. Harvest
  calendar not sourced; flowering is in the hot dry period (Finding 3) [4].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** Not sourced.
- **Climate zones:** humid tropical; grows best with daytime temperatures about
  **22-30 °C**, and is well adapted to shade and humidity [3]. Spans lowland
  tropical Asia to northern Queensland [5]. Frost-intolerant; chill: none.
  Elevation band: Not sourced to a figure.
- **Composition:** Not sourced. No fruit-chemistry, organic-acid or
  hydroxycitric-acid data specific to *G. dulcis* fruit were found (an HCA figure
  that surfaced belongs to a different *Garcinia*); nursery health claims are
  marketing and are not relied on [1]. The fruit and its extracts appear in
  phytochemical/cytotoxicity literature, but no edible-fruit composition was
  sourced this pass.
- **Edibility:** the soft yellow flesh is raw-edible but sour; mostly processed to
  jam or sherbet (Finding 1) [1][2].
- **Pollination:** Not sourced — the sexual system (dioecious vs monoecious) could
  not be confirmed for this species (Finding 3) [4].
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced. Flowering tracks a dry period, not day
  length (Finding 3) [4]. Not applicable to any storage organ.
- **Yield:** Not sourced. No per-tree or per-hectare figures; no commercial
  production exists [1].
- **Water use:** Not sourced. A humid-adapted, shade-tolerant tree [3].
- **Propagation:** by seed; air-layering is also used (Finding 3) [4].
  Clonal/viral degeneration: Not sourced.
- **Labor:** Not sourced. Wild gathering and dooryard picking rather than orchard
  operations (Finding 2) [1][3].
- **Soil:** Not sourced to specifics. pH, texture and **salinity tolerance: Not
  sourced.**
- **Storage (postharvest):** Not sourced.
- **Establishment:** Not sourced (planting method, spacing). Typically a few
  dooryard trees rather than a stand (Finding 2) [1][3].
- **Cultivars:** None sourced; a semi-wild crop with no recorded named selections.
- **Weather response:** Not sourced to figures. Shade- and humidity-tolerant [3];
  frost-intolerant. Frost-as-a-quality-event: not applicable.
- **Water regime:** rain-fed; no flooded/paddy requirement known.
- **Harvest stages / windows:** picked when the fruit colours yellow to orange;
  window not sourced [4][5].
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** native across a very wide Indo-Malesian-to-N-
  Queensland range [5]; no invasive listing was found. As a slow dooryard/forest
  tree it has no weedy reputation. IUCN status not sourced. Movement/quarantine
  rules: not sourced.
- **Controlled environment:** Not sourced; grown outdoors in the tropics and as a
  rare-fruit specimen elsewhere [5].

## Hazards

- **Ordinary fruit:** no toxicity sourced for the flesh, which is eaten raw/sour or
  as jam [1][2].
- **Latex / gamboge staining:** the yellow gamboge latex and dye stain skin and
  fabric (a genus trait; here also a deliberate dye, Uses) [1][6].
- **Medicinal claims:** traditional uses (e.g. for wounds, scurvy) appear in
  several sources but are unverified; nursery health claims are not relied on [1][3].
- **Ergot / mycotoxin, allergy:** none sourced.
- **TO BE ADDRESSED** — nothing specific beyond the genus latex/dye-staining note.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain (fresh):** ripe yellow-orange fruit picked → soft flesh eaten fresh
  (sour) [1][2].
- **Chain (preserves):** fruit → **jam** ("excellent," PROSEA) and sherbet [1][2].
- **Chain (dye):** **unripe fruit → yellow gamboge dye**; bark → mat dye [1].
- **Medicinal:** traditional remedies from fruit, bark and other parts (unverified)
  [1][3].
- **Not otherwise detailed here:** timber, latex-chemistry and any oil use were not
  chased down; edible-fruit composition and processing detail are lacking.

## Open research questions

- **Sexual system:** is *G. dulcis* dioecious, monoecious or sub-dioecious, and
  does it (like other *Garcinia*) set apomictic seed? Not confirmed (Finding 3) [4].
- **Composition:** no organic-acid, sugar or nutrition data were found for the
  edible fruit.
- **Bearing age, yield, harvest calendar, seed-handling, storage, labour, soil
  and salinity:** all Not sourced.
- **Village-production detail:** no yields, home-garden densities or local-market
  volumes were found behind the "suited to village production" framing [6].

## Estimates and derivations

No quantitative estimates are derived: no yield, spacing, composition or
bearing-age figure is available to build on, and there is no commercial production
dataset. The only firm climate figure is the 22-30 °C daytime optimum, reported
directly [3].
