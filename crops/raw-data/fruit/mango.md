## Naming and scope

*Mangifera indica* L. (Anacardiaceae) [1].
- **Names:** mango in English and Spanish; *mangue* (French), *manga*
  (Portuguese), *manja* (Dutch); *mangou* or *mangoro* in parts of Africa
  [1].
- **Origin:** between north-eastern India and Myanmar [7]. Morton gives
  eastern India, Burma and the Andaman Islands [1].
- **Two groups:** an "Indian" type, monoembryonic, and a "Southeast
  Asian" type, polyembryonic [7]. Polyembryonic seed carries up to 4
  vegetative embryos that "faithfully reproduce" the parent, so it comes
  true from seed. Monoembryonic seed gives a hybrid, so selections must
  be grafted [1]. The distinction "is not absolute" [1]. A 2019 study
  "would at least indicate multiple domestication events" [7].
- **Cultivars:** "Over 500 named varieties (some say 1,000)" in India,
  at least 350 of them in nurseries [1]; "over 500" [7].
- **Wild ancestry:** natural hybridization with *M. sylvatica* is
  thought to have occurred; selection has gone on for 4,000-6,000 years
  and grafting for 400 [1].
- **Green mango:** an early harvest stage of the same fruit, not a
  separate crop (Finding 4).

**Relatives in this project:**
- cashew (`cashew`, nut category): same family [1];
- pistachio (`pistachio`, nut category): same family.

**Proposed** (wet-tropics *Mangifera*; the genus has 64 species, more
than 27 with edible fruit, centred on Sumatra, Borneo and the Malay
Peninsula [8]):
- kuini (`kuini`, *M. odorata*) [8];
- bachang (`bachang`, *M. foetida*), whose astringent fruit "can be eaten
  pickled" [8];
- binjai (`binjai`, *M. caesia*) [8];
- bambangan (`bambangan`, *M. pajang*) [8].

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — dryness makes flowers; rain at bloom ruins them.**
- **Ideal climate:** 75-250 cm of rain in the four summer months, then 8
  dry months. "The amount of rainfall is not as critical as when it
  occurs" [1].
- **Bloom weather:** rain, heavy dew or fog at bloom stimulate growth,
  cut flowering and encourage disease. Heavy rain washes pollen off [1].
- **Indian practice:** irrigation of bearing trees is withheld for 2-3
  months before flowering, then a heavy watering at bloom and monthly
  until the rains [1].
- **Brazilian practice ('Kent', São Francisco valley):** production
  pruning, paclobutrazol 45-60 days later, then five sprays of 2.5%
  potassium nitrate. Irrigation drops to about 2.8 mm a day during floral
  induction, against a 4.5 mm mean [4].
- **Induction history:** potassium nitrate and ethrel were developed in
  1974 in the Philippines, from a traditional smoke method. Before that
  trees "only flowered every 16 to 18 months" [7].
- **Folk methods (Philippines, West Indies):** smoking, exposing roots,
  pruning, girdling, withholding nitrogen and water, even salt; slashing
  the trunk with a machete [1].
- **Alternate bearing:** trees under 10 years may bear every year; most
  then alternate [1]. Removing half the panicles in an "on" year gives at
  least a small crop the next year [1].
- **Multiple crops:** Thai 'Sam Ru Du' bears three crops a year; Indian
  "Baramasi" types flower irregularly all year [1]. Some cultivars give a
  double crop [7].

**Finding 2 — a tender tree, damaged by light frost.**
- **Range:** tropical lowlands between 25° N and 25° S, up to 915 m. At
  cooler altitudes it "is apt to suffer cold damage" [1].
- **Florida history:** most plantings north of Ft. Myers were killed in
  the January 1886 freeze [1]. Florida groves use overhead sprinklers for
  frost protection [1].
- **India:** early blooms of 'Dasheri' "are often damaged by frost" [1].
- **Encyclopedia:** grown in "most frost-free tropical and warmer
  subtropical climates" [7].
- **Thresholds:** not yet sourced. The UF/IFAS home-landscape guide that
  supplied them in the old doc is identified, not yet read.

**Finding 3 — picked mature-green, ripened off the tree,
chilling-sensitive.**
- **Maturity:** 4-5 months from flowering [1]. Fruit is ready when the
  "shoulders" rise above the stem end and the first fruit show slight
  colour break; two weeks later all full-sized fruit can be picked [2].
  The stem "will snap easily with a slight pull" when mature [1].
- **Storage:** 10-13 °C at 85-90% RH for 14-28 days for mature-green
  fruit. Ripe fruit stores at 7-8 °C. "Diseases are the principal factor
  limiting storage life" [2].
- **Chilling:** most cultivars are injured below 10 °C; 'Haden' and
  'Keitt' are particularly susceptible, and tolerance rises during
  ripening [2]. Morton gives 13 °C as damaging to 'Kent', but 'Alphonso'
  keeping 6-7 weeks at 7.2 °C in Bombay [1].
- **Ripening:** 20-23 °C [2]. Ethylene (1-2 µL/kg/h produced) gives faster,
  more even ripening [2]: full colour in 7-10 days with ethylene, against
  10-15 days without. Fruit needing over 48 hours of ethylene was picked
  too early [1].
- **CA:** 3-5% O2 and 5-10% CO2 at 7-9 °C [2].
- **Quarantine:** a fruit-fly host. US entry needs hot water at 47 °C
  for 65-90 minutes, vapour heat to a 46-48 °C core, or 300 Gy
  irradiation [2]. Hawaiian mangoes are barred from the US mainland,
  Australia and Japan because of the seed weevil [1].

**Finding 4 — a large green-fruit economy.**
- **Uses:** green fruit is pickled; made into chutney; salted and
  sun-dried; or sliced thin with turmeric, dried and powdered for a tart
  seasoning [1]. Thai "keo" fruit is soaked 15 days in brine [1].
- **Storage:** green seedling mangoes for chutney and pickle keep up to
  40 days at 5.6-7.2 °C and 85-99% RH [1].
- **Export:** Kenya sends immature fruit to the UK for chutney [1].
- **Wind-fall:** immature fruit blown down by spring winds goes to pie,
  jelly and sauce [1].

**Finding 5 — hazards from sap, skin, flowers, smoke and leaves.**
- **Sap and peel:** stem sap, trunk sap and unripe skin are "a potent
  skin irritant", with a delayed, poison-ivy-like reaction [1]. People
  with urushiol dermatitis "may be most at risk" [7].
- **Sap burn:** stem sap burns the fruit skin, causing black lesions and
  rot, so fruit is washed straight after harvest [1][2].
- **Flowers:** blooming trees cause itching eyes, facial swelling and
  breathing difficulty, probably from vaporized flower oil, not pollen
  [1].
- **Smoke:** mango wood "should never be used in fireplaces or for
  cooking fuel" [1].
- **Leaves:** cows fed mango leaves for euxanthic-acid dye from their
  urine; "continuous intake of the leaves may be fatal," and the practice
  was outlawed [1].

**Finding 6 — yields span 9 to over 50 t/ha.**
- **Colombia (Tommy Atkins/Fairchild, Cundinamarca, 2017):** 236 plants
  per hectare at 7 × 7 m; first crop in year 4; 20 t/ha in years 7-18
  (67% first grade); 281 t/ha over a 20-year cycle [3].
- **Florida:** "about 30,000 lbs/acre" average; one grower 22,000-27,500
  lb/acre; a hedged grove at 100 trees per acre 14,000-19,000 lb/acre
  [1].
- **Puerto Rico (lb/acre):** 'Lippens' 67,079, 'Keitt' 45,608,
  'Earlygold' 42,310, 'Parvin' 38,369, 'Haden' 32,732, 'Palmer' 28,868
  [1].
- **India (1980s):** 9 million t on 1 million ha, 65% of world output
  [1].
- **Mexico, Campeche (2018):** 13.2 t/ha for Tommy Atkins (snippet) [10].
- **Per tree:** 200-300 fruit at 10-20 years, doubling at twice that
  age; Indian trees 800-3,000 fruit in "on" years [1].
- **See Estimates** for t/ha.

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Large evergreen tree, 10-30 m, with a taproot
  to about 6 m in deep soil. Specimens 300 years old are still fruiting
  [1].
- **Harvest pattern:** Perennial, usually one crop a year per cultivar,
  picked over several passes. Some cultivars bear two or three crops, and
  induced flowering shifts the season (Finding 1) [1][7].
- **Days/years to first bearing:**
  - **Grafted:** may set fruit within 1-2 years of planting, but should
    be thinned [1]. First commercial crop in year 4 (Colombia) [3].
  - **Top-worked trees:** bear in 2-3 years [1].
  - **Seedlings:** about 6 years to fruit and 15 years to optimum yield
    [1].
- **Climate zones:** Tropical and warm subtropical, frost-free [1][7].
  - **Latitude and elevation:** 25° N-25° S, to 915 m [1]. The Colombian
    reference zone is 0-1,600 m, 24-32 °C mean and 1,500-2,000 mm of rain
    [3].
  - **Dry season:** Finding 1. Queensland favours 100 cm areas with 75%
    of rain in January-March [1].
  - **Cold:** Finding 2.
  - **Chill:** none required.
- **Composition (per 100 g):**
  - **USDA SR Legacy 169910, raw:** 60 kcal (250 kJ); water 83.5 g;
    protein 0.82 g; fat 0.38 g; carbohydrate 15.0 g; fibre 1.6 g; vitamin
    C 36.4 mg; potassium 168 mg; calcium 11 mg; phosphorus 14 mg;
    magnesium 10 mg; iron 0.16 mg; copper 0.111 mg; folate 43 µg; vitamin
    A 54 µg RAE; β-carotene 640 µg [6].
  - **Morton:** 62.1-63.7 calories; ascorbic acid 7.8-172.0 mg. In
    Puerto Rico ascorbic acid ranged from 3.43 mg ('Keitt') to 62.96 mg
    ('Julie') [1].
  - **Fruit parts:** kernel 13% of fruit weight (55-65% of the stone);
    peel 20-25% [1].
  - **Kernel:** starch 40-72%, fat 6-15%, protein 4.76-8.5%, tannin 0.12-
    0.18% or more [1].
- **Edibility:** Raw-edible ripe flesh; green fruit eaten raw with salt
  and cooked (Finding 4). Kernels are eaten roasted or boiled in famine,
  or as flour after soaking out tannins. Young leaves are cooked in
  Indonesia and the Philippines [1].
- **Pollination:** Insect-assisted.
  - **Flowers:** 3,000-4,000 per panicle, 25-98% male, the rest
    hermaphrodite [1]. 'Neelum' has 16% hermaphrodite flowers and bears
    every year; 'Alphonso' has 10% [1].
  - **Visitors:** bats, flies, wasps, wild bees, butterflies, moths,
    beetles, ants and bugs, plus some self-pollination [1].
  - **Honeybees:** "do not especially favor mango flowers"; effective
    pollination would need 3-6 colonies per acre [1].
  - **Self-incompatibility:** 'Dasheri' is self-incompatible [1].
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced. Not applicable to storage
  organs.
- **Yield:** Finding 6 and Estimates.
- **Water use:**
  - **'Kent', Petrolina (2017-18):** ETc 4.40 mm/day in vegetative
    growth, 4.29 in floral induction, 3.48 in induction plus flowering,
    3.63 in fruit drop and 4.46 in fruit formation and maturation;
    recommended Kc 0.80, 0.97, 0.85, 0.80 and 0.74 for those phases. ETc
    over the cycle was 1,503 mm [4].
  - **'Tommy Atkins', Petrolina (1998-99):** 4.6 and 4.1 mm a day, with
    effective rooting depth about 1.80 m, on 7-year drip-irrigated trees
    at 5 × 8 m [5].
  - **Other Tommy Atkins means:** Kc 0.71 and 0.91, from two earlier
    studies cited in [4]. FAO-56 gives 0.55, 0.90 and 0.65 for fruit
    trees under 3 m, which the 'Kent' study found low for the semi-arid
    climate [4]. Snippet-level work gives Kc 0.87 (3.5 mm/day) at
    Petrolina and a Kc curve rising and falling with days after flowering
    [10].
  - **Deficit at fruit fill:** water restriction during fruit formation
    can cut fruit weight by up to 20% (cited in [4]).
  - **India:** young trees are irrigated only for 3-4 years, until the
    taproot reaches the water table [1].
  - **Waterlogging:** good drainage is the one soil requirement [1].
    Waterlogging tolerance itself was not sourced.
- **Propagation:**
  - **Seed:** sow fresh, husked kernels; sprouting takes 8-14 days in the
    tropics. Kernels kept in charcoal dust at 50% RH were 80% viable
    after 70 days [1].
  - **Grafting:** veneer grafting adopted in Florida in the mid-1950s;
    inarching, approach and stone grafting in India; shield and patch
    budding up to 70% success [1].
  - **Cuttings and layers:** cuttings only 40% successful, and neither
    cuttings nor air layers form good roots [1].
  - **Rootstocks:** polyembryonic 'Olour', 'Vellai Colamban' and 'Sabre'
    dwarf; 'No. 13-1' tolerates salty, limy soil in Israel [1].
  - **Clonal/viral risk:** none sourced. Whether malformation (Hazards)
    spreads in scionwood was not sourced.
- **Labor:**
  - **Colombia:** year 7-18 labour costs COP 6.52 million per hectare at
    COP 40,000 per 8-hour day. Irrigation and harvest are "55% of the
    total" labour cost [3]. See Estimates for hours.
  - **Harvest:** long-poled picking bags holding no more than 4 fruit;
    clipped fruit keeps a 10 cm stem to avoid sap spurt, trimmed to 6 mm
    before packing [1].
  - **Spraying:** Florida growers apply "up to 20 sprayings" for
    anthracnose before harvest [1].
- **Soil:**
  - **Texture:** "not too particular as to soil type, providing it has
    good drainage." Grows in sand, gravel and oolitic limestone [1].
    Colombian reference: deeper than 1.20 m, medium loam to sandy loam
    [3].
  - **pH:** 5.5-7.0 [3]. Iron chelates are added where lime is over 30%
    [1].
  - **Fertility:** soil that is "too rich and moist and too well
    fertilized" gives growth but poor flowering. High nitrogen while
    young, then more phosphate and potash; a 5-8-10 mix at 1 lb per year
    of age per dressing [1].
  - **Disorder:** excess nitrogen on sandy acid soil causes "soft nose,"
    countered with calcium [1].
  - **Salinity:** 'No. 13-1' rootstock did well in sand with 15% lime
    and irrigation water over 600 ppm salts [1]. No salt-tolerance
    threshold was sourced.
- **Storage (postharvest):** Finding 3.
  - **Respiration (mg CO2/kg/h):** 10-22 at 4.5 °C, 23-46 at 10 °C,
    45-90 at 15 °C, 75-151 at 20 °C [2].
  - **Packing:** 16 kg, 6 kg and 4.5 kg cartons; no US or international
    grades [2]. Florida packs 8-20 fruit in 6.35 kg lugs [1].
  - **Precooling:** within 24 hours of harvest [2].
- **Establishment:**
  - **Spacing:** 10.5-18 m: 10.5 × 10.5 m gives 86 trees/ha and 15.2 ×
    15.2 m gives 44.5 [1]. Colombia uses 7 × 7 m, 236 per hectare [3];
    Petrolina 4 × 6 m [4] and 5 × 8 m [5].
  - **Hedged groves:** one Florida grower keeps 100 trees per acre on
    limestone by hedging and topping [1]. Topping at 4.25 m eases
    spraying and harvest [1].
  - **Transplanting:** taproot cut back to 30 cm 6 weeks before
    planting [1].
- **Cultivars:**
  - **US:** 'Tommy Atkins', 'Kent', 'Keitt' and 'Haden' [2]. 'Haden' came
    from 'Mulgoba' seed in Miami [1].
  - **India:** 'Alphonso', 'Bangalora' (best keeping, but most
    susceptible to seed weevil), 'Dusehri', 'Langra', 'Neelum' and
    others; hybrids 'Mallika' (1972) and 'Amrapali' (dwarf, regular
    bearing, 2-3 times the carotene) [1].
  - **Dwarf:** 'Julie' [1].
- **Weather response:**
  - **Rain, dew and fog at bloom:** Finding 1.
  - **Wind:** strong winds in fruiting season drop fruit [1].
  - **Unfavourable set weather:** small, seedless fruit in some
    cultivars [1].
- **Water regime:** Rainfed with a dry spell in monsoon climates;
  irrigated with induction stress in semi-arid Brazil and Egypt [1][4].
- **Harvest stages / windows:**
  - **Green:** for pickle, chutney and powder (Finding 4).
  - **Mature-green:** for shipping and ripening (Finding 3).
  - **Ethephon (Philippines):** 200 ppm at 54 days after full bloom allows
    harvest 2 weeks later, with larger fruit [1].
  - **Season:** Florida bloom runs late November to March [1].
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:**
  - **Hawaii WRA:** score 1, low risk (screened 2006), though
    "naturalized in Hawaii, Fiji, and Tonga" and hybridizes naturally
    [9].
  - **Quarantine pests:** fruit flies and seed weevils (Finding 3,
    Hazards).
  - **Invasive listings:** not yet sourced (CABI datasheet identified, not
    yet read).
- **Controlled environment:** Not sourced.

## Hazards

- **Dermatitis and respiratory irritation:** Finding 5. Anaphylaxis is
  also possible, and mango allergens may cross-react with urushiol [7].
- **Peeling hygiene:** use one knife to peel and another to slice, so the
  flesh doesn't pick up resin from the peel [1].
- **Leaf toxicity to cattle:** Finding 5.
- **Kernel tannins:** feeding value is low unless tannins are removed by
  soaking [1].
- **Pests:**
  - **Fruit flies:** several *Dacus* (*Bactrocera*) species; *Anastrepha
    suspensa* in Florida [1].
  - **Seed weevils** (*Sternochetus mangiferae*, *S. gravis*): hidden in
    the stone until larvae tunnel out [1].
  - **Hoppers** (*Idiocerus* spp.): the main tree pest in India, with
    sooty mould on their honeydew [1].
  - Scales, thrips, mealybugs, stem borers, mites and mistletoe [1].
- **Diseases:**
  - **Anthracnose** (*Colletotrichum gloeosporioides*): preharvest
    infection showing as fruit ripens [1][2].
  - **Stem-end rots:** postharvest, often wound invaders [2].
  - **Powdery mildew** (*Oidium mangiferae*): attacks flowers; up to 20%
    of the crop lost [1].
  - **Malformation:** *Fusarium moniliforme* with eriophyid mites,
    across South Asia, Africa and Latin America; reduced by NAA in
    October and deblooming in January [1].
  - **Tommy Atkins dry stem-end rot (Mexico):** 10-80% losses from 1973
    [1].
  - **Disorders:** jelly seed (Tommy Atkins very susceptible), soft nose
    and spongy tissue [2].
- **Ergot / mycotoxin:** none reported. *Alternaria*, *Aspergillus* and
  *Rhizopus* rots are listed as decay only [2].

## Uses and processing

- **Chain 1 (fresh export):** picked mature-green with stem → stem sap
  washed off → hot water or other quarantine treatment → graded and packed
  → precooled within 24 h → 10-13 °C → ethylene-ripened at 20-23 °C
  [1][2].
- **Chain 2 (green fruit):** chutney (peeled, parboiled, cooked with
  sugar and spices); salted and sun-dried for pickle; turmeric-dried
  slices powdered as a souring agent; brined (Thailand) [1].
- **Chain 3 (ripe processing):** canned slices in syrup; jam,
  marmalade, jelly and nectar; mango leather and halva from fibrous pulp;
  spray-dried juice powder; frozen slices and purée [1].
- **Chain 4 (kernel):** soaked to remove tannin → dried → flour (mixed
  with wheat or rice); kernel fat, solid like cocoa butter, is proposed
  for chocolate; high-stearic fat for soap; residue for cattle feed [1].
- **Chain 5 (peel):** pectin, about 13% on a dry-weight basis [1].
- **Other products:** timber (rafters, boats, plywood, crates), charcoal,
  bark tannin (16-20%) and dyes, and gum used in place of gum arabic [1].
- **Medicinal:** dried flowers (15% tannin) and kernel as astringents;
  bark against rheumatism; Caribbean leaf decoctions [1].
- **Ritual and religious:**
  - **Hinduism:** flowers and leaves in the worship of Saraswati at
    Vasant Panchami, and leaves at Ganesh Chaturthi [7].
  - **Jainism:** the goddess Ambika is shown seated under a mango tree
    [7].
  - **Buddhism:** monks are believed to have carried mango to Malaya and
    eastern Asia in the 4th-5th centuries BC [1].
  - **National symbols:** national fruit of India, Pakistan and the
    Philippines; national tree of Bangladesh [7].

- **Cold thresholds:** identified, not yet read: Crane, Balerdi and

## Estimates and derivations

**Yields per hectare** [1][3] (lb/acre × 1.12085 = kg/ha):
- Florida average 30,000 lb/acre = 33.6 t/ha. Grower 22,000-27,500 =
  24.7-30.8 t/ha. Hedged grove 14,000-19,000 = 15.7-21.3 t/ha.
- Puerto Rico: 'Lippens' 75.2, 'Keitt' 51.1, 'Earlygold' 47.4, 'Parvin'
  43.0, 'Haden' 36.7, 'Palmer' 32.4 t/ha. Morton's "lbs per acre is
  roughly the equivalent of kg per hectare" understates kg/ha by 12%.
- India (1980s): 9,000,000 t ÷ 1,000,000 ha = 9.0 t/ha.
- Colombia years 7-18: 13,400 + 6,600 = 20,000 kg/ha = 20 t/ha; over the
  cycle 281 ÷ 20 years = 14.1 t/ha a year.

**Yield tiers** (per hectare):
- Poor: 9.0 t/ha (India national average, 1980s) [1].
- Typical: 20 t/ha (Colombia mature bearing) [3].
- Good: 33.6 t/ha (Florida average) [1].
- Exceptional: 51.1 t/ha (Puerto Rico 'Keitt'); 'Lippens' at 75.2 t/ha
  is a single-cultivar outlier [1].

**Labour** [3]: COP 6,520,000 ÷ 40,000 per day = 163 days × 8 h = 1,304
h/ha/year in years 7-18. Year 1: 3,680,000 ÷ 40,000 = 92 days = 736 h.
Irrigation plus harvest at 55% of labour cost = 717 h/ha a year, if the
cost share equals the time share.

**Harvest hours, alternative:** not derivable from Morton's pole-picking
description; no picking rate was sourced.

**Spacing** [1][3][4][5]:
- 7 × 7 m square: 10,000 ÷ 49 = 204/ha. Triangular: 10,000 ÷ (49 ×
  0.866) = 236/ha, matching the stated density.
- 10.5 × 10.5 m: 10,000 ÷ 110.25 = 90.7/ha (Morton gives 86).
- 15.2 × 15.2 m: 10,000 ÷ 231 = 43.3/ha (Morton gives 44.5).
- 100/acre × 2.471 = 247/ha. Morton's yield section prints "41/ha".
- 4 × 6 m = 417/ha; 5 × 8 m = 250/ha.

**Water** [4][5]:
- 'Tommy Atkins': 4.6 × 365 = 1,679 mm (1998); 4.1 × 365 = 1,497 mm
  (1999).
- 'Kent': 1,503 mm over the cycle (stated).
- Induction cut: 2.8 ÷ 4.5 = 62% of the mean daily irrigation depth.
- Days of reserve: assuming 100 mm of plant-available water per metre
  over 1.8 m roots = 180 mm; 180 ÷ 4.4 mm/day = about 41 days without
  rain or irrigation before stress (assumed water-holding capacity;
  range 25-60 days for 60-150 mm/m).

**Storage** [2]: 21 days is the midpoint of 14-28 days.

**Bees** [1]: 3-6 colonies/acre × 2.471 = 7.4-14.8 per hectare.

**Morton conversions** [1]:
- "20 ft (6 in)" taproot: 20 ft = 6.1 m.
- "65° to 75° F (18.33° -23.09° C)": 75 °F = 23.9 °C.
- Minas Gerais "243,018 tons (22,000 MT)": 243,018 short tons = 220,460
  t, so 220,000 t, not 22,000.
- 'Kent' "55.4° F (13° C)" is correct.

**Kernel and pulp fractions** [1]: kernel 13% and peel 20-25%. With the stone at 13 ÷ 0.60 = 21.7% of the fruit
(kernel at 55-65% of the stone), flesh is about 100 - 22.5 - 21.7 = 56%
(range 52-60%).

**World share** [7]: India 26.2 ÷ 61.1 = 43% of the combined mango,
mangosteen and guava total (2023).
