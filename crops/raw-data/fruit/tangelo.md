## Naming and scope

*Citrus* × *tangelo* J.W.Ingram & H.E.Moore (Rutaceae): **tangelo**, the
**mandarin (tangerine) × grapefruit/pomelo** hybrid group [1][2]. This doc covers
the `tangelo` row, proposed from `mandarin.md` and `clementine.md` [3]. The main
crops are **'Minneola' (Honeybell)** and **'Orlando'** — sibling USDA hybrids of
**Duncan grapefruit × Dancy tangerine**, released from Orlando, Florida in 1931 —
plus the natural **'Ugli' / Jamaican tangelo** [1][2].

**Distinctiveness (gate check, 2026-11-27): passes — on pollination biology,
exactly as `clementine` did.** `mandarin.md` lists tangelos ('Minneola',
'Orlando') within its broad scope, so the test is whether tangelo carries
**distinct data** beyond the mandarin profile; it does [1][3]:
- **Pollination (the decisive distinguisher):** the main tangelos are
  **self-incompatible / not self-fruitful** and set poorly in solid blocks; they
  **require interplanted pollenizer cultivars** for a commercial crop, and
  cross-pollination then governs **seediness, fruit size and yield** — a planting-
  design trade-off mandarin.md does not detail (Finding 2; schema Entry 18)
  [1]. This is the same basis on which `clementine` was split out, and the two
  are mirror cases (Finding 2) [3].
- **Distinct cultivars/products:** the **Honeybell** premium gift fruit and the
  necked 'Minneola' shape are a distinct commercial identity (Finding 3) [2].
- **Taxonomy:** a named grapefruit/pomelo-ward hybrid, distinct from pure
  mandarins, from `clementine`, and from the tangors (mandarin × sweet orange)
  [1][2].

**Relatives in this project:** `mandarin.md` (the parent doc), `clementine.md`
(the parallel pollination-split precedent and parthenocarpic mirror), and the
`grapefruit`/`pomelo` side of the cross [3].

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — a mandarin × grapefruit hybrid, built around two USDA siblings and
a Jamaican natural.** The tangelo group crosses a **mandarin/tangerine with a
grapefruit or pomelo** [1][2]. **'Minneola'** and **'Orlando'** are **siblings
from the same cross** — **Duncan grapefruit × Dancy tangerine**, released by the
USDA station at Orlando, Florida in **1931** [1]. 'Minneola' is sold as the
premium **"Honeybell,"** named for its distinctive **necked/knobbed "bell"
shape**; **'Ugli' (Jamaican tangelo)** is a rugged natural tangelo [2].

**Finding 2 — the pollination story: isolate for seedlessness and little fruit,
or interplant pollenizers for a crop full of seeds.** The main tangelos are
**not strongly self-fruitful** and **produce poorly in solid-set blocks**, so
growers **interplant pollenizer cultivars** — UF/IFAS recommends **Temple**
(most consistently), with Sunburst, Osceola, Robinson or possibly Fallglo; UC
Riverside adds **Dancy, Clementine and Kinnow** mandarins [1]. Several
constraints make this a real design problem (schema Entry 18) [1]:
- **Cross-incompatibility:** **'Orlando' and 'Minneola' cannot pollinate each
  other**, so a *third* variety is needed; 'Minneola' is itself a poor pollenizer
  (low flower production some years) [1].
- **Seedlessness vs yield trade-off:** fruit in **solid 'Minneola' plantings is
  seedless or nearly so**, but yield is low; in **mixed plantings** it carries
  **7-12 seeds** ('Minneola') or **0-45 seeds** ('Orlando') from cross-pollination
  [1]. Seed number tracks **fruit size** — each extra seed steps the fruit up in
  size — so the seedy, cross-pollinated crop is also the larger, more productive
  one [1].
- **Over-pollination:** too strong a pollenizer backfires — alternating rows of
  Robinson and Orlando can cause **excessive set and limb breakage** [1].
- **Chemical substitute:** **gibberellic acid** at full bloom raises set and
  yield where pollination is poor [1].
This is the **mirror of `clementine`**: clementine is **parthenocarpic** (sets
seedless fruit *without* pollination, and cross-pollen only *adds* unwanted
seeds), whereas tangelo **needs** cross-pollination for a crop at all — but pays
for the yield in seeds (Finding 2) [1][3].

**Finding 3 — premium, short-season Florida fruit.** 'Minneola'/**Honeybell** is
a prized fresh and **gift/mail-order** citrus with a short Florida window
(**~December-February**, often only a few peak weeks) [1][2]. **'Orlando'** is
**earlier** (**~November-January**), on a large, **cold-hardy tree**, and is also
valued as an **excellent pollenizer** for other hybrids (e.g. Robinson) [1].
Tangelos are eaten fresh (easy-peel, tart-sweet, zipper skin) and juiced [2].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Perennial evergreen citrus tree; **'Orlando' early**
  (~Nov-Jan), **'Minneola' mid/late** (~Dec-Feb) (Finding 3) [1][2].
- **Harvest pattern:** Perennial bearing, strongly dependent on **cross-
  pollination** for commercial set (Finding 2) [1].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** Not specifically sourced (citrus-standard).
- **Climate zones:** Subtropical citrus (Florida, etc.); **'Orlando' has a large,
  cold-hardy tree** (Finding 3) [1].
- **Composition:** Tart-sweet, aromatic; **seed count varies with cross-
  pollination** (0-45 in 'Orlando'); seedier fruit is larger (Finding 2) [1].
- **Edibility:** Fresh (easy-peel) and juice [2].
- **Pollination:** **The defining trait — self-incompatible, requiring
  interplanted pollenizers** (Temple, Sunburst, Robinson, Dancy, Clementine,
  Kinnow); **'Orlando'×'Minneola' cross-incompatible**; GA₃ substitutes where set
  is poor (Finding 2; Entry 18) [1].
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced; not expected to apply.
- **Yield:** **Poor in solid blocks, good with pollenizers**; no per-tree figure
  sourced (Finding 2) [1].
- **Water use:** Not sourced (mandarin-like; see `clementine.md` for distinct
  citrus water data) [3].
- **Propagation:** Grafted citrus; grown with a **pollenizer-interplanting
  layout** (Finding 2) [1].
- **Labor:** Not sourced; grove **design** (pollenizer placement) is the key
  management input (Finding 2) [1].
- **Soil:** Not specifically sourced.
- **Storage (postharvest):** Not sourced; premium fruit shipped fresh/gift
  (Finding 3) [2].
- **Establishment:** Orchard planting **must include compatible pollenizer
  cultivars** in the layout (Finding 2) [1].
- **Cultivars:** **'Minneola' (Honeybell)**, **'Orlando'**, **'Ugli'/Jamaican
  tangelo**; others (e.g. 'Seminole', 'Nova', 'Lee' hybrids) (Finding 1) [1][2].
- **Weather response:** **'Orlando' cold-hardy**; other responses not quantified
  [1].
- **Water regime:** Ordinary irrigation; no special regime sourced.
- **Harvest stages / windows:** **'Orlando' Nov-Jan, 'Minneola' Dec-Feb**; short
  premium window (Finding 3) [1][2].
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** A cultivated citrus; no invasive listing found;
  standard citrus phytosanitary handling [1].
- **Controlled environment:** Not sourced; a field orchard crop [1].

## Hazards

- As with all citrus, **peel-oil furanocoumarins**; and, from the **grapefruit
  parent**, tangelos may carry **furanocoumarin "grapefruit-drug" interaction**
  potential — not quantified in this pass and worth confirming [2]. **No other
  distinctive hazard found.**

## Uses and processing

- **Chain (fresh/premium):** fruit → **fresh eating** (easy-peel, tart-sweet) and
  **gift/mail-order "Honeybell"** fruit (Finding 3) [2].
- **Chain (juice):** fruit → **juice** [2].
- **Chain (pollination service):** **'Orlando' as a pollenizer** for other citrus
  hybrids (Finding 3) [1].
- **Not otherwise detailed here:** processing/segments, quantitative composition
  and cold-hardiness figures [1][2].
