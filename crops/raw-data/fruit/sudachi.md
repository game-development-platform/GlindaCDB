## Naming and scope

*Citrus sudachi* Hort. ex Shirai (Rutaceae): **sudachi** (すだち), a small green
Japanese sour citrus (*kōsan kankitsu*) and the signature crop of **Tokushima
Prefecture** [1][2]. Like kabosu, it is **sold green and unripe for its juice and
aroma** [1][4]. This doc covers the `sudachi` row, proposed from
`hetsuka-daidai.md` and `kabosu.md` [4].

- **Parentage (disputed; a classification matter, out of schema scope):** sources
  agree on a **yuzu-linked hybrid origin** but differ on the second parent —
  Wikipedia gives yuzu × a citrus "akin to koji and tachibana"; a NARO survey of
  67 Japanese sour citrus names sudachi among the yuzu-related group; and an
  outlier record gives *C. ichangensis* × *C. reticulata* var. *austera*, which
  conflicts with the yuzu account [1][4].
- **Name:** said to come from *su* (vinegar) + *tachibana*, reflecting its use as
  an acidulant [1].

**Gate check (2026-11-27): passes.** Checked against its nearest researched
relative `kabosu` (the Ōita green sour citrus) and the sour-citrus group it is
grouped with in `hetsuka-daidai.md` [4]:
- **Taxonomy:** a separately named, yuzu-linked taxon — not kabosu (*C.
  sphaerocarpa*), not yuzu itself, not *C. depressa* shiikuwasha [1][4].
- **Range / farming system:** a **prefecture-signature crop** — Tokushima has
  held **≥95% of national output since 1964** (commonly cited ~98%), with its own
  **GI** and a greenhouse + open-field + cold-storage system for year-round green
  fruit (Findings 1-2) [1][2].
- **Harvest / uses:** picked **green**, used as an acidulant/garnish; its own
  signature peel flavonoid **sudachitin** (Finding 3) [2][3].
It is a direct Tokushima counterpart to Ōita's `kabosu`, but a distinct taxon,
place and chemistry — distinct enough for its own row.

**Relatives in this project:** `kabosu.md` (the Ōita sibling and model),
`hetsuka-daidai.md` (the sour-citrus "control" group and a proposing doc),
`yuzu` (its putative parent), `shiikuwasha.md` (the Okinawan sour citrus, the
other distinctive-flavonoid case), and `mandarin`/`tachibana` [4].

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — a green, never-ripe acidulant, used for juice, garnish and
aroma.** Sudachi is harvested **dark green and unripe** and valued for its sharp,
aromatic juice [1][2]. It is squeezed as a **lime/lemon/vinegar substitute** into
ponzu, sauces, marinades and dressings, sliced thin as a **garnish** (classically
over matsutake mushrooms, grilled fish, sashimi and soba), and increasingly used
by manufacturers in **confectionery, desserts, juices and alcoholic drinks**
[1][2]. If left on the tree it ripens yellow/orange, but ripe fruit is not the
product [1].

**Finding 2 — an intensely place-bound crop: Tokushima, a GI, and a year-round
green supply built from three systems.** Tokushima Prefecture has supplied
**≥95% of Japan's sudachi since 1964** (several sources say ~98%) [1][2].
Planted area rose to about **660 ha in 1992** and has fallen to roughly **400 ha
(397 ha in 2016/17)**, and the national harvest is reported to have dropped to
**less than half its level a decade earlier** [2]. **Tokushima Sudachi** holds a
registered **geographical indication** (Japan GI, registered 31 March 2023),
requiring cultivation by the prefectural promotion council's methods [2]. Green
fruit is kept available almost year-round by combining three systems:
**greenhouse** fruit (~mid-March to mid-August), the traditional **open-field**
harvest (~August to October), and **cold-stored** fruit (~November to March)
[2]. A newer cultivar, **'Katsuura No. 1'**, was bred to **stay green later** in
the season [2]. (This greenhouse/field/cold-store year-round-green pattern is the
same one documented for `kabosu`, and like kabosu is treated as template
agronomy, not a schema exception.)

**Finding 3 — its own namesake flavonoid, sudachitin.** Sudachi peel is the main
natural source of **sudachitin** (a polymethoxyflavone, 5,7,4'-trihydroxy-
6,8,3'-trimethoxyflavone) and its relative demethoxysudachitin [3]. These have
shown bioactivity in experimental models — vasorelaxation of rat aorta, effects
on rat atria — and sudachitin has been **reported to improve hyperlipidemia in
humans** [3]. The juice also carries **eriocitrin** at concentrations comparable
to lemon and lime, and the aroma is built on farnesenes, limonene, linalool and
related terpenes [1][3]. This is the sudachi parallel to shiikuwasha's
nobiletin — a distinctive peel flavonoid chemistry, recorded here under
Composition/Uses rather than as a schema trait [3][4].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Perennial evergreen citrus tree/shrub; flowers in late
  spring, fruit harvested green from late summer (Finding 1) [1][2].
- **Harvest pattern:** Perennial bearing; **green harvest** extended to near
  year-round by greenhouse, field and cold storage (Finding 2) [2].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** Not sourced for *C. sudachi* specifically.
- **Climate zones:** Warm-temperate southern Japan (Tokushima/Shikoku);
  frost-tender citrus [1][2].
- **Composition:** Sharp acidity (high citric/malic acid; a quantitative figure
  was **not reliably sourced**); peel rich in the namesake PMF **sudachitin**;
  juice **eriocitrin** comparable to lemon/lime (Finding 3) [1][3].
- **Edibility:** Green juice used as an **acidulant**; fruit not eaten as a sweet
  dessert fruit [1].
- **Pollination:** Not detailed; seedy and seedless selections exist in the group
  (not confirmed for sudachi here) [2].
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced; not expected to apply.
- **Yield:** Not sourced as a per-tree figure; national output is **declining**
  (Finding 2) [2].
- **Water use:** Not sourced.
- **Propagation:** Citrus-standard (graft/seed); cultivation follows the GI
  promotion council's recommended methods, and **'Katsuura No. 1'** is a bred
  stay-green selection (Finding 2) [2].
- **Labor:** Not sourced; green fruit is hand-picked [2].
- **Soil:** Not specifically sourced; grown on Tokushima hill and orchard land
  [2].
- **Storage (postharvest):** **Cold storage** holds green fruit roughly November
  to March, one leg of the year-round supply (Finding 2) [2].
- **Establishment:** Planted as grafted trees in open field and under greenhouse
  cover (Finding 2) [2].
- **Cultivars:** The traditional sudachi plus **'Katsuura No. 1'** (stays green
  later); seedless/selected lines exist in the group [2].
- **Weather response:** Frost-tender; other tolerances not quantified [1].
- **Water regime:** Ordinary irrigation/rainfall; no paddy requirement [1].
- **Harvest stages / windows:** Picked **green** only; the "stages" here are the
  three supply systems (greenhouse / field / cold store) rather than fruit
  maturities (Finding 2) [2].
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** A cultivated Japanese citrus; no invasive
  listing found. Its "biosecurity"-adjacent feature is the **GI/origin control**
  (Tokushima-only production), an economic/labelling protection rather than a weed
  concern [2].
- **Controlled environment:** **Greenhouse production** is a routine part of the
  supply (early-season green fruit, ~mid-March to mid-August) (Finding 2) [2].

## Hazards

- As with all citrus, **peel-oil furanocoumarins** are a minor contact-
  photosensitiser note, not a distinctive hazard; the juice is a common food [1].
  Concentrated **sudachitin extracts** are bioactive (a nutraceutical, not a
  food-level, consideration) [3]. **No distinctive hazard found in this pass.**

## Uses and processing

- **Chain (acidulant/garnish — the main use):** green fruit → **squeezed/sliced**
  → juice or thin slices over matsutake, grilled fish, sashimi, soba; into ponzu,
  sauces, marinades, dressings [1][2].
- **Chain (processed):** fruit → **confectionery, desserts, juices, alcoholic
  drinks** (sudachi sour, liqueur) and vinegar [1][2].
- **Chain (nutraceutical):** peel → **sudachitin / PMF extracts** for functional-
  food interest (Finding 3) [3].
- **Not otherwise detailed here:** essential-oil extraction; precise juice
  composition; per-cultivar differences [1][3].

## Open research questions

- **Bearing age, per-tree yield** and quantitative **juice acidity/composition**
  for *C. sudachi* were not reliably sourced [1][3].
- **The production decline** (national harvest "less than half" a decade ago) and
  its causes (aging growers, climate) were noted but not quantified in detail
  [2].
- **Parentage** (yuzu × which second parent) remains disputed — a classification
  matter, not a crop trait [1][4].
- **Whether a juice-content shipping standard** exists (as for kabosu) was not
  found for sudachi this pass [4].
