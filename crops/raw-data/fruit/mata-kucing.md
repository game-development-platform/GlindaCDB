## Naming and scope

*Dimocarpus longan* subsp. *malesianus* Leenh. (Sapindaceae) [1].
- **Synonyms:** *Nephelium malaiense* Griff., *Euphoria malaiensis*
  Radlk. and *E. cinerea* Radlk. (both illegitimate names) [1][6]. The
  Darwin fact sheet still uses *Euphoria malaiense* [5].
- **Species rank proposed:** a 2017 multi-locus phylogeny found *D.
  longan* polyphyletic. It raised var. *malesianus* to species rank as
  *Dimocarpus malesianus* (Leenh.) Lithanatudom & Chaowasku [3]. It
  rested on three herbarium samples of var. *malesianus* [3]. The one
  var. *echinatus* sample grouped with commercial longan, not with
  *malesianus* [3]. The encyclopedia uses the new name [8]; PROSEA,
  the Singapore flora and the USDA germplasm list keep the subspecies
  [1][6][9].
- **Local names:** *mata kucing* ("cat's eye", Peninsular Malaysia and
  Sabah); *isau*, *sau* and *kakus* (Sarawak); *buku* and *ihau*
  (Kalimantan); *medaru* (Sumatra) [1]. The encyclopedia calls it
  *alupag* [8], a name not in PROSEA's list; that article is tagged as
  resting on one source.
- **Gate check (2026-10-03, re-confirmed 2026-11-23): passes** against
  longan (`longan.md`):
  - **Climate:** commercial longan needs a cool winter and "will not
    flower" in the true tropics. This subspecies is "fully adapted" to
    the equatorial zone's unchanging heat, humidity and day length [2].
  - **Farming system:** mostly forest, village and backyard trees, sold
    only in local markets in season [1]. Husbandry is "largely limited
    to harvesting" [1].
  - **Harvest:** one single picking, against two for longan [1].
  - **Taxonomy:** proposed as a separate species [3].
- **Two varieties** [1]:
  - **var. *malesianus*:** across Indo-China and Malesia, with the
    greatest variation in Borneo, where 30-40 local races might be
    distinguished [1][2]. This is the main subject of this doc.
  - **var. *echinatus*:** fruits with spines up to about 1 cm, like
    rambutan; Borneo (Sabah) and the Philippines [1][2]. Gate-checked as a
    candidate row and **rejected** (2026-11-26); folded here as a variety —
    see the gate-check note below.
- **Local races of var. *malesianus*** [1][2][4]:
  - **True *mata kucing*:** smooth, globose fruit that turns brown when
    ripe. It is the common form in Peninsular Malaysia and also grows in
    Borneo and Sumatra. Its aril is very thin, "hardly worth eating"
    [1][2].
  - **The Sarawak warty races** [1][2][4]:
    - ***isau*:** round fruit that stays green when ripe;
    - ***sau*:** slightly oblong fruit that also stays green;
    - ***kakus*:** round fruit that turns brown; the most widespread in
      Sarawak.

    *Isau* and *sau* are mainly confined to the banks of the Rajang
    river and the Bario valley [1][2]. All three have thicker arils
    than the true *mata kucing* [1][4].
  - **The best race:** *isau* has higher Brix, more vitamin C, calcium
    and iron, a thicker and less watery aril and a smaller seed than
    *sau* or *kakus* [4].

**Relatives in this project:**
- longan (`longan`, `longan.md`): the commercial subspecies and the
  source of every analogy flagged below;
- rambutan (`rambutan`, `rambutan.md`): var. *echinatus* has
  rambutan-like spines, and powdery mildew damages mata kucing "the
  same" way [1];
- lychee (`lychee`) and pulasan (`pulasan`), other Sapindaceae fruits.

**Proposed:**
- **Isau** (`isau`, the Sarawak *isau* race): the race singled out for
  development. It has the thickest aril and is green when ripe [4]. It
  was the subject of a 1988 UPM Bintulu programme on taxonomy, botany
  and propagation [11], and of later cuttings and tissue-culture work
  [11]. Planting away from its river-bank habitat is said to be limited
  by scarce true-to-type material [11].

**Gate check — rejected (2026-11-26): spiny longan (`longan_echinatus`, *D.
longan* var. *echinatus*).** Flagged above as a candidate for its own row, it
was re-examined against the distinctiveness check and **does not clear it**.
Its only distinguishing trait is fruit morphology — rambutan-like spines up to
about 1 cm [1][2] — for which Leenhouts gave it varietal rank. On every other
axis the check weighs, it belongs with existing rows rather than a new one:
- **Taxonomy (weak as a separate lineage):** the 2017 multi-locus phylogeny's
  single var. *echinatus* sample grouped with **commercial longan**, not with
  *D. malesianus* [3], so it is at most a spiny form of *D. longan* — and rank
  assigned on one fruit trait is a classification matter, not a crop trait.
- **Range:** Borneo (Sabah) and the Philippines [1][2] — overlapping, not
  separate from, the *malesianus* complex this doc already covers.
- **Farming system / harvest / uses:** none found as distinct. No source
  describes var. *echinatus* being cultivated, gathered, marketed or eaten as
  anything apart from longan / mata kucing; its edibility is only inferred from
  the species, so it reads as a **wild spiny-fruited form**, not a crop. A
  dedicated chapter exists in Lim's compendium [11], but the compendium is
  arranged one taxon per chapter and that chapter was not readable in full this
  pass.
The spine trait is folded into this doc and `longan.md` (where it is already
noted) at the variety level — the template's home for "a minor difference that
failed the distinctiveness check" — and the status row is set to `REJECTED`.
The Lim compendium chapter [11] and a 1988 "Edible Sapindaceae in Sabah"
survey (Van Welzen, Lamb & Wong; not read this pass) remain the places to look
if a later pass turns up a distinct gathered-fruit use that would justify
reopening it.

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — a longan that flowers on the equator.**
- **No cool trigger:** commercial longan needs a short, cool winter
  (mean 15-22 °C) to flower [1]. It has "not been successfully grown" in
  the humid lowlands of Malaysia [4]. Mata kucing grows "within about 10°
  from the equator" near sea level [1].
- **Its climate:** rainfall 2,500 to over 4,000 mm a year, mean
  temperature 25-30 °C and relative humidity 65-95% [1].
- **Its flowering:** short, 1-2 weeks, at some point between late June
  and mid-October in Sarawak [1]. Fruit ripens about 4 months after
  bloom [1] (see Estimates for the harvest window).
- **Darwin:** in tropical Australia it fruits "several times a year"
  [5]. This conflicts with Sarawak's single short bloom (Open research
  questions).
- **Breeding value:** the subspecies is called mainly "unexploited" and a
  possible new crop, or a breeding source for commercial longan [2].
  Its superior Sarawak races "may offer an attractive alternative" to
  longan in the humid lowland tropics [1][2].

**Finding 2 — an understorey forest tree, barely farmed.**
- **Habitat:** the trees grow mainly in the substage or understorey of
  primary, or sometimes secondary, forest [1]. In Sarawak they grow on
  alluvial soil, often on river banks [1].
- **Husbandry:** "largely limited" to harvesting and cutting back the
  fruiting twigs [1].
- **Markets:** the fruit is "found in their season in some local markets
  only" [1].
- **Census, reference year 2023:** Malaysia's 2024 agriculture census
  uses one combined "Mata Kucing/Longan" category. It is among the top
  three fruits by production in two Sarawak districts [7]. Reading the
  extracted table's column order, these are Sebauh and Bukit Mabong. No
  separate area or production figure is given (Open research questions).
- **Germplasm:** Universiti Pertanian Malaysia's Bintulu campus, Sarawak,
  was the largest collector [1]. The USDA Hilo station lists the
  subspecies among its *Dimocarpus* holdings [9].

**Finding 3 — the name covers a thin-fleshed fruit and a prized one.**
- **Size:** fruit 1-3 cm across for the species [1], and up to about 2
  cm (some larger) in Darwin [5].
- **Thin form:** the true smooth *mata kucing* has a very thin aril
  [1][2].
- **Thick forms:** the warty Sarawak races have "greater aril recovery"
  [2]. *Isau* is said to taste better than imported Thai longan [11].
- **Snippet-level weights:** two *isau* accessions had fruits of about
  8.00 g and 4.52 g, with arils of 3.79 g and 2.68 g [11]. That is
  roughly 47% and 59% aril (see Estimates). The OCR of that source was
  garbled, so these need checking against the original.
- **Flavour:** "sweet" [5][6].
- **Composition:** "not very different" from longan, but with much lower
  carbohydrate and energy and much higher minerals [1]. PROSEA gives no
  figures, and USDA FoodData Central has no entry [10].

**Finding 4 — short-lived seed, slow seedlings, hard-to-clone trees.**
- **Seed:** short-lived and best sown fresh; germination takes 7-10 days
  [1]. Desiccation is fatal. Viability can be extended for a while with
  a fungicide and seed moisture above 30% [1].
- **Juvenile phase:** about 7 years for longan seedlings, and PROSEA says
  this "also" applies to mata kucing [1]. Darwin reports seedlings
  fruiting in 2-3 years [5]. This is a large conflict (Open research
  questions).
- **Cloning:** air layering "is not difficult" [1]. Budding by the
  modified Forkert method works, but budbreak is slow and uneven [1].
  Darwin says it "can be grafted" [5].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Evergreen tree. The species reaches 40 m [1];
  Singapore gives 20 m for this subspecies [6]. Backyard trees in Darwin
  are 2-3 m, with a drooping habit [5].
- **Harvest pattern:** Perennial bearing. In Sarawak there is one crop a
  year, picked in a single harvest by cutting whole panicles [1]. In
  Darwin it fruits several times a year [5].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** 2-3 years from seed in Darwin [5],
  against a juvenile phase of about 7 years in PROSEA [1]. Air layers:
  not sourced for this subspecies (longan air layers bear in years 3-4
  [1]).
- **Climate zones:** humid equatorial lowlands near sea level, within
  about 10° of the equator [1]. Rainfall 2,500 to over 4,000 mm, mean
  25-30 °C, RH 65-95% [1]. No cool-season or chill requirement [2]. It is
  grown outdoors in northern Australia (Darwin, about 12° S) [5]. Frost
  tolerance: not sourced.
- **Composition:** no figures. PROSEA says it is like longan, with much
  lower carbohydrate and energy and much higher minerals [1]. *Isau* has
  higher Brix, vitamin C, calcium and iron than *sau* and *kakus* [4].
  There is no USDA entry [10]. Longan's own figures are in `longan.md`
  [12].
- **Edibility:** the aril is eaten raw [1][6]. The seed is not eaten
  (see Hazards).
- **Pollination:** the panicles have male and hermaphrodite flowers [1].
  Insects pollinate it, mainly ants, flies and honey bees (*Apis cerana*,
  *A. florea* and *A. dorsata*) [1]. Flowering is often prolific, but
  fruit set is low, and many fruitlets drop about 2 weeks after
  flowering, at about 1 cm [1].
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** adapted to the equator's unchanging day
  length [2]. No day-length response was sourced.
- **Yield:** Not sourced. No per-tree or per-hectare figure was found for
  this subspecies. The national statistics cited in the earlier version
  of this doc could not be re-opened (Open research questions).
- **Water use:** Not sourced. Its native rainfall of 2,500 to over 4,000
  mm [1] is well above longan's 1,500-2,000 mm [1]. Singapore classes it
  as needing moderate water [6].
- **Propagation:**
  - **Seed:** the usual route; the seed is recalcitrant (see Finding 4)
    [1].
  - **Air layering:** "not difficult" [1].
  - **Budding:** modified Forkert, with slow, uneven budbreak [1].
  - **Cuttings and tissue culture:** studied for *isau* [11].
  - **Clonal or viral risk:** none sourced. Longan's witches' broom is
    seed- and budwood-borne (`longan.md`) [12]; whether it infects this
    subspecies is not sourced.
- **Labor:** Not sourced. Husbandry is minimal: harvesting and cutting
  back fruiting twigs [1].
- **Soil:** alluvial river-bank soils in Sarawak, and a wider range
  elsewhere [1]. PROSEA notes that pH 4.5-6.5 "is common in this region"
  [1]; this describes the region's soils, not a tested tolerance range.
  Darwin advises a rich, moist soil in a protected spot [5]. Salinity:
  not sourced.
- **Storage (postharvest):** Not sourced for this subspecies. The fruit is
  non-climacteric and must be picked ripe [1]. Longan keeps only a few
  days at 25-31 °C, or 40-45 days at 5 °C after precooling [1]. Applying
  that to mata kucing is an analogy only.
- **Establishment:** 10 × 10 m in a square or hexagonal pattern for
  orchards (see Estimates) [1]. Darwin advises a protected site [5].
- **Cultivars:** no named clones [5]. The local races are listed in
  Naming and scope: true *mata kucing*, *isau*, *sau* and *kakus*
  [1][2][4]. In Darwin, fruit and tree size differ somewhat from the
  wild stock [5].
- **Weather response:** Not sourced. Drought, flood, wind and heat
  thresholds have not been measured for this subspecies. River-bank
  sites [1] suggest it tolerates seasonal flooding, but this is
  unconfirmed.
- **Water regime:** rainfed; no irrigation practice sourced.
- **Harvest stages / windows:**
  - **Bloom to ripe:** about 4 months [1].
  - **Sarawak season:** roughly late October to mid-February (see
    Estimates) [1].
  - **Ripeness signs:**
    - fruit shape, skin colour and taste [1];
    - immature fruit is tasteless [1];
    - Darwin says to pick "when fruit is fully brown" [5];
    - *isau* and *sau* stay green when ripe [1][2].
  - **Picking:** the whole crop can be taken in one picking [1].
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:**
  - **Status:** native from Indo-China through Malesia [1], including
    Singapore, where its conservation status is Data Deficient [6].
  - **Weed listing:** none found in this pass. Longan itself scores low
    risk in Hawaii (`longan.md`) [12].
  - **Quarantine:** it hosts fruit flies [1][5], so the fruit-fly
    rules for longan in `longan.md` may apply [12].
  - **Germplasm:** held at USDA Hilo [9].
- **Controlled environment:** Not sourced. The small backyard trees in
  Darwin (2-3 m) [5] and the understorey habit [1] suggest it might suit
  a large tropical glasshouse. This is an inference only.

## Hazards

- **Seed toxin (by analogy):** longan seed contains hypoglycin A at
  45.3-3,540 µg/g dried seed, while its aril carries only traces
  (`longan.md`) [12]. Mata kucing seed has not been tested.
- **Diseases:** powdery mildew on inflorescences and young fruit, with
  damage like that in rambutan; thread blight on branches and leaves [1].
- **Pests:** fruit-piercing moths and fruit flies [1][5]; fruit bats [1].
- **Ergot / mycotoxin:** none reported.

**TO BE ADDRESSED** — the presumed seed-toxin hazard, inferred from a
close relative's measured values, is deferred until this project has a
place for health and safety data and the seed has been tested directly.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain 1 (fresh):** panicles cut with knife or scissors in a single
  picking → sold in local markets in season → aril eaten raw [1][6]; peel
  and seed discarded. Longan practice is to sort and bunch the panicles
  and not to drop them [1].
- **Chain 2 (timber):** fairly hard, light brown to yellow wood, useful
  but "rarely available" [1]; used for construction [6]; used for combs
  [8].
- **Breeding material:** a gene source for adapting commercial longan to
  the tropics [2].
- **Not otherwise detailed here:** no drying, canning or other
  processing was found for this subspecies. Longan's processing chains
  are in `longan.md` [12].
- **Ritual and religious:** Not sourced.

## Open research questions

- **Years to bearing:** about 7 years from seed [1] against 2-3 years in
  Darwin [5].
- **Fruiting frequency:** one 1-2-week bloom a year in Sarawak [1]
  against "several times a year" in Darwin [5]. This may be the drier,
  monsoonal Darwin climate or the backyard selections.
- **Ripe colour:** "fully brown" [5] against *isau* and *sau*, which stay
  green [1][2]. This is probably differences between races, not an error.
- **Darwin flesh thickness:** the sheet says "about 50mm thick" in a fruit
  of about 2 cm, which is impossible (see Estimates).
- **Aril share:** only snippet-level *isau* figures [11]. The true *mata
  kucing* aril is "very thin", with no number given [1].
- **Area and production:** the census merges mata kucing with longan [7].
  The earlier version of this doc cited Malaysian Department of
  Agriculture statistics (a planted area under 100 ha, mostly in one
  state) that could not be re-opened this pass. They are requested.
- **Taxonomy:** the species split rests on three herbarium samples [3].
  Where var. *echinatus* belongs is unresolved [1][3].
- **Not sourced:**
  - yield, water use, labour and salinity;
  - weather thresholds and storage life;
  - seed hypoglycin A;
  - soil biology and controlled-environment use.
- **Works identified, not yet read:**
  - **Wong, Ibrahim Yusof, Pearce & Alau Tayan (1988), *isau* paper:**
    full aril and fruit data and propagation results; read only at

  - **Wong (2001), Acta Hortic 558:** the full fruit and vegetative data
    for the four groups; read as an abstract only [4].
  - **Wong & Gan (1992), Acta Hortic 292:29-39:** the Sarawak diversity

  - **Lim (2013), the compendium chapters** on var. *malesianus* and var.

## Estimates and derivations

**Sarawak harvest window** [1]: flowering falls somewhere between late
June and mid-October, and fruit ripens about 4 months later. Late June +
4 months = late October; mid-October + 4 months = mid-February. So the
harvest falls somewhere between late October and mid-February, with any
one tree's crop taken in a single picking. Range: about 120 days ± 2-3
weeks from bloom. A dated Sarawak market record would replace this.

**Orchard density** [1]: a 10 × 10 m square = 100 m² per tree, so
10,000 ÷ 100 = 100 trees/ha. A hexagonal layout at the same 10 m
spacing gives 10,000 ÷ (10² × 0.866) = about 115 trees/ha.

**Isau aril share** (snippet-level inputs) [11]: 3.79 ÷ 8.00 = 47.4%;
2.68 ÷ 4.52 = 59.3%. Range about 47-59%. For comparison, export-grade
longan is 67-78% edible [1]. The original paper would replace this.

**Darwin flesh thickness check** [5]: an aril 50 mm thick cannot fit in
a fruit about 20 mm across, since the whole fruit radius is about 10 mm.
The figure is probably a typo for about 5 mm or less. It is not used
anywhere in this doc.

**Seed toxin by analogy** [12]: longan seed carries 45.3-3,540 µg/g
dried seed. If mata kucing seed fell anywhere in that range, the top
value would be 35 times the US ackee import limit of 100 µg/g that
`longan.md` uses as a yardstick. This is an analogy only; a direct
measurement would replace it.
