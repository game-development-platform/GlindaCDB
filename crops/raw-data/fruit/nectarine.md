## Naming and scope

*Prunus persica* (L.) Batsch (Rosaceae) carrying the recessive glabrous
allele at the G locus: a peach without fuzz [1][4][5].
- **Genetics:** monogenic, with nectarine (g/g) recessive to fuzzy
  fruit (G/–) [4]. Breeding work showed this as early as 1906 [5].
- **Same tree:** tree, leaves and flowers are mostly indistinguishable
  from peach [1]. Commercially the two are treated as different fruits
  [5].
- **Names:** nectarine. A formal varietal name was not sourced this pass.
- **History:** known in China for over 2,000 years; described by
  European botanists since the Renaissance; white Sicilian and Calabrian
  nectarines grown since the 16th century [4]. Most modern US and
  European nectarines descend from three donors: 'Quetta' (found near
  Quetta in 1906) and 'Goldmine' and 'Lippiatt' (found in New Zealand in
  1900 and 1916) [4].
- **Scope:** one cropID covering both high-chill (temperate) and low-chill
  (subtropical) nectarine cultivars. Low-chill nectarines belong here,
  not in `peach_low_chill` (that doc's own scope note says so).
- **Gate check (2026-10-18, re-confirmed 2026-11-23): passes** against
  peach (`peach.md`):
  - **Product:** a separate market fruit [5], about 20% of US and 30% of
    Italian *P. persica* output (2013) [4].
  - **Yield:** lower than freestone peach in both California statistics
    and the Mississippi trial (Finding 3) [1][6].
  - **Skin:** no fuzz, so more thrips scarring, cuticle microcracks and
    rain cracking (Finding 4) [8][11][12].
  - **Postharvest:** less chilling injury than current peach cultivars,
    but visible shrivel at 6-8% weight loss (Finding 2) [2].
  - **Protected culture:** about 80% of China's covered peach/nectarine
    area is nectarine (Finding 5) [8].

**Relatives in this project:**
- peach (`peach`, `peach.md`): the fuzzy form of the same species;
- low-chill peach (`peach_low_chill`, `peach-low-chill.md`).

**Proposed:** none. Flat nectarines (for example 'Zhongyoupanweimei' from
the Zhengzhou Fruit Research Institute [8]) are a fruit-shape cultivar
group, noted under Cultivars. Interspecific "nectaplum"-type hybrids were
not sourced this pass.

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — one retroelement insertion makes the nectarine.**
- **Locus:** the G locus is distal on linkage group 5, narrowed to a
  1.1 cM (635 kb) interval in a 'Contender' (peach) × 'Ambra'
  (nectarine) F2 of 305 seedlings [4].
- **Cause:** an LTR retroelement inserted in exon 3 of the R2R3-MYB gene
  *PpeMYB25* [4].
- **Marker:** the indelG marker (941 bp peach allele, 197 bp nectarine
  allele) cosegregated with the trait. On a 95-accession panel (46 peach,
  49 nectarine), every nectarine was 197/197, and several peaches,
  including 'O'Henry' and 'Fairtime', were carriers [4]. The authors read
  this as a single mutational origin [4].
- **Second gene:** a 2022 study (snippet only) reports *PpMYB25* and
  *PpMYB26* jointly controlling fuzz and cuticular wax [20]. Not read.
- **Breeding consequence:** two nectarine parents breed true for smooth
  skin; a carrier peach × nectarine cross segregates about 1:1 (see
  Estimates) [4].

**Finding 2 — stores better than peach, but shrivels and still has a
narrow safe band.**
- **Regime:** −0.5 to 0 °C, 90-95% RH, 4-6 weeks by cultivar; highest
  freezing point −0.9 °C [2]. A 1996 UC page (snippet) gives −1 to 0 °C
  and a freezing point of −3 to −2.5 °C [21] (see Open research
  questions).
- **Versus peach:** current nectarine cultivars are less prone to
  internal breakdown (mealiness, flesh browning) than current peach
  cultivars [2].
- **Danger band:** injury develops faster and harder at 2.2-7.8 °C for
  nectarines (2.2-7.6 °C for peaches) than at 0 °C or below [3].
  - Modern cultivars keep at least 6 weeks at 0 °C; tolerant cultivars
    keep at least 3 weeks at 5 °C; susceptible ones go mealy or brown in
    1-2 weeks at 5 °C [2].
  - Moving fruit from 0 °C to 5 °C after 1-2 weeks injures it as badly
    as holding at 5 °C throughout [2].
  - No injury at 10 °C or above, but fruit softens, loses water and rots
    [2]. Symptoms appear only on ripening at 18.3 °C or above [2].
- **Who is at risk:** melting-flesh cultivars more than non-melting;
  mealiness is generally a melting-flesh symptom; bleeding is commoner
  in white-fleshed cultivars [3]. Early-picked fruit, shaded fruit and
  low crop loads raise risk [3].
- **Shrivel:** visible at 6-8% weight loss; reduced by picking earlier in
  the day, fast cooling, waxing, and tight temperature, airflow and
  humidity control [2].
- **Controlled atmosphere:** 1-2% O2 + 5% CO2 keeps firmness and colour
  with limited CI control; 10% O2 + 10% CO2 is used against CI. Avoid
  O2 below 1% and CO2 above 15% (off-flavours, skin browning) [2]. CA
  results are inconsistent across cultivars [3].
- **Delayed cooling:** holding firm-ripe fruit 24-48 h at 20 °C and high
  RH before forced-air cooling extended minimum market life by up to 2
  weeks in the cultivars tested; it is used commercially in California
  [3].

**Finding 3 — California yields about a third below freestone peach.**
- **NASS 2022-2024 (California, the only state reported):** 16,300,
  16,100 and 17,000 bearing acres; 8.40, 9.05 and 7.55 short tons/acre;
  137,000, 145,500 and 128,500 tons total [6]. That is about 16.9-20.3
  t/ha (see Estimates).
- **Same years, California freestone peach:** 11.80, 10.80 and 11.90
  tons/acre [6]. Nectarine yielded 63-84% of freestone peach (see
  Estimates).
- **UC cost-study orchard (2004):** a mature July/August-harvest orchard
  is budgeted at 1,200 25-lb boxes/acre from year 7 [7], about 33.6 t/ha
  of packed fruit (see Estimates). Fresno-Tulare county averages for all
  fresh nectarines were 6.47-8.38 tons/acre in 1999-2003 [7].
- **Mississippi trial (1984-1995):** nectarines averaged 4.8 kg per tree
  a year against 8.4 kg for peaches in Mississippi, "almost twice" as
  productive [1]. The peach figure comes from a different study cited by
  the bulletin, not an adjacent planting [1].
- **Price and value:** $1,290, $1,690 and $1,920 per ton in 2022-2024
  [6]. Harvested-not-sold tonnage fell from 5,480 (2022) to 260 (2024)
  [6].
- **Dropped claim:** the old doc's "below 100,000 tons in 2022/23" does
  not match NASS's 137,000-145,500 tons for 2022-2023 [6].

**Finding 4 — the bare skin is a pest, crack and rot target.**
- **Thrips:** "Nectarine is more susceptible than peach to thrips
  damage" [11]. Western flower thrips feeding on tiny fruit leaves tan or
  silvery scars that enlarge as the fruit grows, often visible after the
  thrips have left; internal quality is not much affected [11]. Peach
  fuzz is the protection named in one extension source (snippet) [11].
  Insecticidal soap is not recommended on yellow-skinned nectarines [11].
- **Cuticle and brown rot:** nectarines were highly resistant to
  *Monilinia laxa* during endocarp lignification (about 63 to 90-108 days
  after bloom), with medium to high susceptibility before and again in
  the final growth stage [12]. Cuticular microcracks forming near
  maturity are proposed as entry points (summary-level only) [12].
- **Rain cracking:** protected cultivation in China eliminates
  rain-induced fruit cracking, "especially nectarines" [8].
- **Disease pressure, unresolved:** the claim that the lack of fuzz makes
  nectarines more prone to brown rot and insects was not borne out in
  the Mississippi trial, where pressure "did not seem any greater" than
  in an adjacent peach orchard, though neither was measured [1].

**Finding 5 — China grows most of its early nectarines under cover.**
- **Area:** nearly 16,000 ha of protected peach and nectarine (2012),
  about 80% of it nectarine [8], so roughly 12,800 ha (see Estimates).
  The largest provinces are Shandong (5,300 ha), Liaoning (3,400 ha) and
  Hebei (2,800 ha) [8].
- **Structures:** unheated solar lean-to houses at 32-47° N, and
  north-south high tunnels [8].
- **Forcing:** after leaf fall, lean-to houses are covered by day and
  opened at night from about late November for about a month, to satisfy
  chilling early; bud break follows 30-40 days after heat conservation
  starts [8]. High tunnels wait for natural chill [8].
- **Gain:** harvest advanced up to 90 days (lean-to) or 30 days (tunnel),
  15 days without insulation blankets [8]. Mid-March nectarines fetched
  nearly five times the price of May fruit [8].
- **Yield:** 15-23 t/ha [8].
- **Practice:** trees at 1.0 × 1.5 m to 1.5 × 2.0 m on wild peach
  seedling rootstock, central leader; first crop in the second year after
  paclobutrazol (0.075%) and drought from late July; productive 10 or
  more years [8]. Pollination is by introduced bees or by hand [8]. Light
  is 30-40% below outside [8].
- **Breeding:** low-chill nectarines for greenhouse use are a breeding
  target at the Zhengzhou Fruit Research Institute [8].

**Finding 6 — chill requirement sets where each cultivar can fruit.**
- **High-chill cultivars:** most Mississippi trial cultivars needed over
  800 chill-hours [1]. At the southern site (31.8° N), yields averaged
  9.4 lb (4.3 kg) per tree against 42 lb (19.1 kg) in the north, blamed
  on "insufficient and erratic flowering" from too little chill [1].
- **Lower-chill cultivars:** 'Karla Rosa' (700 h) and 'Sunfre' (525 h)
  were the top southern producers [1].
- **Florida:** UF/IFAS bands are 100-250 chill units for low-chill and
  300-525 for moderate-chill peaches and nectarines (snippet) [15].
  'UFRoyal', 'Sunraycer', 'Sunmist' and 'UFQueen' appear at 100-200
  chill units in one UF table and 200-300 in a later one. The 'UFRoyal'
  release paper gives 225-250 units and an 88-day fruit development
  period, against 78 days for 'Sunraycer' (snippets) [15].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** deciduous perennial tree. UC budgets a
  15-year orchard life [7]; Chinese greenhouse plantings are productive
  for 10 or more years [8].
- **Harvest pattern:** perennial bearing, picked several times per tree
  as skin ground colour changes, never allowed to over-ripen [1]. In
  Mississippi the harvest ran from May 22 to July 23 by cultivar and year
  [1]; the UC study picks in July and August [7].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** an economic crop in year 3, full yield
  from year 7 (California) [7]; first production year 1987 from a 1984
  planting (Mississippi) [1]; second year under cover in China [8].
- **Climate zones:**
  - **Chill:** about 100-250 units (Florida low-chill) to over 800 h
    (Mississippi high-chill) (Finding 6) [1][15].
  - **Spring frost:** −4.5 °C after bloom destroyed the whole crop in
    two of the six Mississippi seasons (1987, 1989) [1].
  - **Elevation:** Not sourced.
- **Composition (per 100 g raw; USDA SR Legacy via an aggregator, per-cup
  values converted, see Estimates) [18]:**
  - energy 44 kcal; water 87.6 g; protein 1.06 g; fat 0.32 g;
  - carbohydrate 10.55 g; fibre 1.7 g; total sugars 7.89 g;
  - vitamin C 5.4 mg; vitamin A 17 µg RAE; β-carotene 150 µg;
    potassium 201 mg.
  - **Storage manual proximate [2]:** moisture 86.28%, protein 0.94%, fat
    0.46%, carbohydrate 11.78%, fibre 1.60%, ash 0.54%. That implies
    about 49-55 kcal (see Estimates).
- **Edibility:** raw-edible fresh fruit; the kernel is cyanogenic (see
  Hazards).
- **Pollination:** Not sourced as a self-fertility statement this pass.
  Under cover, bees are introduced or flowers hand-pollinated because
  there are "no insects" and no wind [8].
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** flowering is not reported as
  photoperiodic. In peach generally, short days (with falling
  temperature) start growth cessation and dormancy, through the DAM
  genes of the *evergrowing* locus (snippets) [19]. Not tested on
  nectarines specifically.
- **Yield:** 16.9-20.3 t/ha California average 2022-2024 [6]; about 33.6
  t/ha budgeted for a mature July/August orchard [7]; 15-23 t/ha under
  Chinese protected culture [8]; about 4.8 kg per tree a year in
  Mississippi [1] (see Estimates).
- **Water use:**
  - **FAO-56 stone fruit Kc [9]:** ini 0.45-0.55, mid 0.90, end 0.65
    with no ground cover; ini 0.50-0.80, mid 1.15, end 0.85-0.90 with
    active ground cover. Kc end after leaf drop is about 0.20 on bare soil
    [9]. No stone-fruit stage lengths are given; a California deciduous
    orchard row has 30/50/130/30 days (240 days) [9].
  - **California budget:** 36 acre-inches seasonal consumptive use, 44
    applied by furrow at about 82% efficiency, late March to early
    October, peaking in June (9.76 acre-inches) [7]. That is about 914 mm
    used and 1,118 mm applied (see Estimates).
- **Propagation:** budded nursery trees. Rootstocks named are 'Halford'
  and 'Lovell' (Mississippi) [1], 'Nemaguard', 'Nemared' and 'Lovell'
  (California) [7], and wild peach seedlings (China) [8]. Clean stock:
  plum pox virus spreads long distances in infected nursery stock and
  by aphids; APHIS relies on certified nursery stock and import rules on
  trees and propagative material [14]. Other graft-transmitted viruses
  were not sourced this pass.
- **Labor (UC 2004, mature orchard, per acre) [7]:**
  - 302.59 h non-machine plus 22.77 h machine labour, about 804 h/ha
    (see Estimates);
  - hand thinning 103 h (April), winter pruning 64 h, hand picking 73 h
    (July-August), leaf removal 30 h, summer pruning 9.3 h, irrigation
    11.3 h.
- **Soil:**
  - **Trial soils:** a pH 6.0 silt loam and a pH 6.5 clay loam
    (Mississippi) [1]; deep, well-drained class I-II soil, ripped 4-5 ft
    and fumigated before planting (California) [7].
  - **Nitrogen:** 151 lb N/acre a year (about 169 kg/ha), split April and
    September [7].
  - **Salinity:** peach is rated sensitive, with an ECe threshold of 1.7
    dS/m and a 21%/dS/m slope, for shoot growth and fruit yield [10]. The
    data assume rootstocks that do not accumulate Na⁺ or Cl⁻ fast [10];
    rootstocks absorb these ions at different rates [10]. Yield halves at
    about 4.1 dS/m (see Estimates).
- **Storage (postharvest):** −0.5 to 0 °C, 90-95% RH, 4-6 weeks; precool
  below 2.2 °C by forced air or hydrocooling (Finding 2) [2].
- **Establishment:**
  - **California:** 16 × 18 ft, 151 trees/acre (about 373/ha); trees
    topped at planting; 2% replanted in year 2 [7].
  - **Mississippi:** 4.3 × 3.7 m, hedgerow or palmette [1].
  - **China (greenhouse):** 1.0 × 1.5 m to 1.5 × 2.0 m [8].
  - **Fruit thinning:** to about 15 cm apart at about 15 mm fruit
    diameter, about 24 days after bloom [1].
  - **Support:** limbs roped and propped in heavy-crop years [7].
- **Cultivars:**
  - **High-chill (Mississippi):** 'Harko', 'Redgold', 'Stark Sunglo'
    and 'Hardired' gave over 90 kg per tree cumulative in the north;
    'Early King' and 'Stark Early Bird' were very low producers [1].
    Flesh was yellow except 'Redchief', 'Lafayette' and 'Karla Rosa'
    [1].
  - **California cost-study examples:** 'Summer Bright', 'Summer Fire',
    'August Red' [7].
  - **Low-chill (Florida):** 'UFRoyal' (a replacement for 'Sunraycer'),
    'Sunraycer', 'Sunmist', 'UFQueen', 'Sunbest' [15].
  - **Flat nectarine:** 'Zhongyoupanweimei', a small mid-season flat
    nectarine (snippet) [8].
  - **Old European:** 'Lord Napier', 'Précoce de Croncels', 'Galopin';
    Italian white 'Sbergie' and 'Merendelle' [4].
- **Weather response:**
  - **Frost:** −4.5 °C post-bloom killed whole crops [1]; earlier bloom
    in the south raised freeze exposure [1]. Frost is not a quality
    improver for this crop in any source found.
  - **Rain:** cracks fruit (Finding 4) [8].
  - **Hail and hot wind:** named as reasons to grow under cover [8].
- **Water regime:** ordinary irrigation (furrow, drip, or flood under
  cover) [1][7][8]. No flooded requirement. Waterlogging is a named
  problem for sunken greenhouses once the plastic comes off in summer
  [8].
- **Harvest stages / windows:** one fresh stage, picked when the skin
  ground colour changes [1]. Firm-ripe harvest plus delayed cooling is a
  commercial option (Finding 2) [3]. Ripening is successive on each tree,
  so the crop is picked several times [1].
- **Soil biology:** pre-plant soil fumigation is budgeted on replant
  ground [7], but replant disease itself was not sourced this pass.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:**
  - **Native range:** eastern Asia (northern China) [13].
  - **Australia:** *P. persica* var. *persica* is widely naturalised in
    southern and eastern Australia and is an environmental weed in New
    South Wales and a minor one in Victoria and South Australia [13].
    The fact sheet does not name nectarine [13].
  - **Hawaii:** naturalised on four islands; the Hawaii-Pacific weed risk
    assessment places it in "Evaluate" (snippet) [13].
  - **Plum pox:** nectarines are named hosts. PPV was found in North
    America in 1999 and declared eradicated from the US in 2019. Under a
    quarantine, trees, rootstock, budwood and leaves of host *Prunus* are
    regulated, but not seed or fruit [14].
- **Controlled environment:** yes, at scale (Finding 5): about 12,800 ha
  of unheated solar greenhouses and high tunnels in China, 15-23 t/ha,
  bee or hand pollination, light 30-40% below outside, ammonia build-up
  and high humidity as problems [8].

## Hazards

- **Kernel cyanogens:** a review table gives cyanogenic potential of 196
  mg HCN/kg for nectarine kernel against 710 for peach kernel (snippet
  only) [17]. The figures are HCN released, not amygdalin mass [17].
- **Allergen (Pru p 3):** the peach lipid-transfer protein. In 103
  Chinese varieties (23 nectarines), Pru p 3 was usually higher in peach
  than nectarine; the nectarine 'Hu You 278' measured 4.02 µg/g
  (snippet) [16]. It is concentrated in the skin, about seven times the
  pulp in peach, and is heat-stable (snippet) [16].
- **Brown rot (*Monilinia*):** the main postharvest disease; Rhizopus rot
  is second and does not grow below 7 °C [2]. Blossom-blight fungicides
  at 20-40% and 80-100% bloom, and a preharvest fruit-rot spray, are
  budgeted in California [7].
- **Plum pox virus:** no cure; managed by resistant varieties, aphid
  control, removing infected trees and quarantine [14].
- **Other pests and diseases budgeted in California:** peach leaf curl,
  peach twig borer, oriental fruit moth, San Jose scale, katydids, mites,
  thrips and aphids [7].
- **Ergot / mycotoxin:** none reported in this pass.

**TO BE ADDRESSED** — kernel cyanide, the Pru p 3 allergy, and livestock
poisoning from wilted *Prunus* foliage are deferred until this project
has a place for health-and-safety data.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain 1 (fresh):** hand-picked into field bins → hauled to a packing
  shed → packed, palletised and cooled (precool below 2.2 °C) → stored
  at −0.5 to 0 °C for up to 4-6 weeks → sold [2][7]. Field packouts are
  typically 60-80% [7].
- **Chain 2 (early greenhouse fruit, China):** forced fruit sold from
  March, often in gift packs, at up to five times the May price [8].
- **Processed:** NASS withholds the fresh/processed split for
  confidentiality [6]. No processed product was sourced this pass.
- **Not detailed here:** dried, canned and frozen nectarine products;
  culls.

## Estimates and derivations

**California yield in t/ha** [6]: 1 short ton/acre = 0.907185 t ÷
0.404686 ha = 2.2417 t/ha. 8.40 × 2.2417 = 18.8; 9.05 × 2.2417 = 20.3;
7.55 × 2.2417 = 16.9 t/ha. The 2022-2024 mean is (8.40 + 9.05 + 7.55) ÷ 3
= 8.33 tons/acre = 18.7 t/ha.

**Nectarine : freestone peach yield** [6]: 8.40 ÷ 11.80 = 0.71 (2022);
9.05 ÷ 10.80 = 0.84 (2023); 7.55 ÷ 11.90 = 0.63 (2024). Range 63-84%.

**Mississippi ratio** [1]: 4.8 ÷ 8.4 = 0.57.

**Mature UC orchard yield** [7]: 1,200 boxes × 25 lb = 30,000 lb ×
0.4536 = 13,608 kg/acre ÷ 0.404686 = 33.6 t/ha (marketable, packed).
County averages 6.47-8.38 tons/acre × 2.2417 = 14.5-18.8 t/ha.

**Mississippi 4-year average** [1]: 'Harko' >200 lb cumulative ÷ 4
harvest years = >50 lb a year, against a stated 99.5 lb "4-year
average". So the "average" figure is not a per-year mean as printed. The
4.8 kg per tree a year comparison figure is used as given.

**Chinese protected nectarine area** [8]: 16,000 ha × 0.80 = 12,800 ha.
For scale, California's 17,000 bearing acres (2024) [6] × 0.404686 =
6,880 ha.

**Water depths** [7]: 1 acre-inch = 25.4 mm over the acre. 36 × 25.4 =
914 mm used; 44 × 25.4 = 1,118 mm applied; 36 ÷ 44 = 82%.

**Labour per hectare** [7]: (302.59 + 22.77) h/acre = 325.36 h/acre ÷
0.404686 = 804 h/ha. Thinning 103 ÷ 0.404686 = 255 h/ha; picking 73 ÷
0.404686 = 180 h/ha; winter pruning 64 ÷ 0.404686 = 158 h/ha.

**Nitrogen** [7]: 151 lb/acre × 0.4536 ÷ 0.404686 = 169 kg/ha.

**Tree densities** [1][7][8]: 151 ÷ 0.404686 = 373 trees/ha (California);
10,000 ÷ (4.3 × 3.7) = 629 trees/ha (Mississippi); 10,000 ÷ (1.0 × 1.5) =
6,667 and 10,000 ÷ (1.5 × 2.0) = 3,333 trees/ha (Chinese greenhouses).

**Composition per 100 g** [18]: the aggregator prints values per 143 g
cup; each is divided by 1.43. For example, 63 kcal ÷ 1.43 = 44 kcal;
125.25 g water ÷ 1.43 = 87.6 g; 11.28 g sugars ÷ 1.43 = 7.89 g; 287 mg K
÷ 1.43 = 201 mg. Replace with a direct FoodData Central read.

**Energy from the storage-manual proximate** [2]: 0.94 × 4 + 0.46 × 9 +
11.78 × 4 = 3.8 + 4.1 + 47.1 = 55 kcal/100 g, if all carbohydrate counts
at 4 kcal/g. With fibre (1.60 g) at 0 kcal: 55 − 6.4 = 48.6 kcal. The
USDA figure is 44 kcal [18], so the manual's sample was higher in
carbohydrate and fat.

**Salinity points** [10]: relative yield = 100 − 21 × (ECe − 1.7).
50% at ECe = 1.7 + 50 ÷ 21 = 4.1 dS/m; zero at 1.7 + 100 ÷ 21 = 6.5 dS/m.

**One-gene segregation** [4]: g/g × g/g gives all nectarines; G/g ×
g/g gives about 1:1; G/G × any gives all fuzzy; G/g × G/g gives about
3 fuzzy : 1 nectarine.
