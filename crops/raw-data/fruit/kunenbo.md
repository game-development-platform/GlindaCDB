## Naming and scope

*Citrus nobilis* Lour. (Rutaceae), the Kunenbo or King mandarin group:
Kunenbo / Kunembo / *kunebu* (九年母) in Japan and the Ryukyus, King
mandarin, King orange or King tangor in English, and *cam sành* in
Vietnam [1][2][4][5][6].
- **Name status:** the name *C. nobilis* is regarded as invalid, because
  the original description was based on an interspecific hybrid, a
  tangor (mandarin × sweet orange) [5]. Tanaka's system keeps it as a
  species with the Japanese Kunenbo as var. *kunep* [1][2].
- **A mixed group:** genomic work describes Kunenbo as a heterogeneous
  group of at least four mandarin-pomelo hybrids, with King as a
  Kunenbo with high pomelo admixture [12]. A Japanese DNA survey placed
  'Kunenbo' and 'King' on separate branches [3], and two genetically
  different trees both carry the name "Kunenbo" [2].
- **Two cultivar groups are covered here:**
  - **Japanese/Ryukyu Kunenbo:** introduced from Southeast Asia through
    the Ryukyu Islands [1], probably by the Muromachi period [2]. It was
    once a leading fruit of western Japan and is now kept mainly in
    genebanks and at a shrine (Findings 1, 6) [2].
  - **King mandarin / cam sành:** a major commercial crop of the Mekong
    Delta and northern Vietnam (Findings 3-5) [6][7][10][11].
- **Naming conflict for cam sành:** Vietnamese sources label cam sành
  as *C. nobilis* var. *typica* [8] or "King" *C. nobilis* [7], but also
  as *C. reticulata* × *sinensis* [6][9]. One 2025 paper gives *C.
  nobilis* to a different fruit, *cam xoàn* [9]. This entry follows the
  older usage and keeps the conflict visible.

**Gate check (2026-11-19): promoted.** Proposed in `hassaku.md` and
`kabosu.md` as a parent of both. It passes because:
- **Taxonomy:** it is a tangor or mandarin-pomelo hybrid group, not the
  *C. reticulata* of the `mandarin` row [5][12].
- **Range and farming system:** it is a tropical wet-lowland crop
  ("produce prolifically in wet lowlands") [5], grown at very high
  density on Mekong Delta raised beds with water-stress flower induction
  [8], and on northern Vietnamese hills [13].
- **Scale:** about 18,000 ha in Vĩnh Long province alone [10][11].
- **Fruit:** a thick, lumpy peel that often stays green when ripe [6].
- **Uses and history:** a 600-year ritual-offering use in Japan [2], and
  a parent of satsuma, hassaku and many Ryukyu citrus [1][3].

**Separate entries:**
- mandarin (`mandarin`, *C. reticulata*), `mandarin.md`;
- satsuma (`satsuma`), `satsuma.md`, for which Kunenbo is the inferred
  pollen parent [1];
- hassaku (`hassaku`), `hassaku.md`, and kabosu (`kabosu`),
  `kabosu.md`, both inferred Kunenbo hybrids;
- natsudaidai (`natsudaidai`), which replaced Kunenbo in old Yamaguchi
  gardens [2].

**Proposed:**
- **Cam xoàn** (`cam_xoan`): a Mekong Delta orange labelled *C.
  nobilis* in one 2025 paper [9]. Gate-checked 2026-11-22 and not
  promoted: other sources name it a sweet-orange cultivar; see
  `orange.md`, Naming and scope;
- **Shiikuwasha** (`shiikuwasha`, *C. depressa*): the native Ryukyu
  mandarin, a parent with Kunenbo of 'Kabuchi', 'Oto' and 'Tarogayo'
  [3].

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — a founding parent of Japanese citrus.**
- **Satsuma:** a 206-SNP parentage study found satsuma probably arose
  from a 'Kishuu mikan'-type seed parent and a 'Kunenbo'-type pollen
  parent [1]. A 2025 genome study proposes two Chinese mandarins
  instead ('Ruju' × 'Bendiguang'), and 'Bendiguangju' has the same
  genotype as 'Kunenbo' [1][12].
- **Others:** the same study inferred Kunenbo as a parent of 'Kabuchi'
  and 'Keraji', and four of its five newly suggested parent-offspring
  pairs involved Kunenbo [1]. A Ryukyu survey adds 'Oto' and 'Tarogayo'
  (with shiikuwasha) [3].
- **Hassaku and kabosu:** see those entries.
- **Arrival:** reached Okinawa from Indochina about 400-500 years ago by
  maritime trade [12]; then spread north through Kyushu to the Kanto
  region [2].

**Finding 2 — lost in Japan to seedless satsuma.**
- **Former importance:** in early Meiji, Kunenbo was grown widely in
  western Japan, and Yamaguchi accounted for 46% of national production
  value [2].
- **Fruit then:** more acid than today's satsuma and large, but sweet
  for its time and eaten fresh [2].
- **Why it disappeared:** it is seeded and its peel has a peculiar odour,
  so growers switched to seedless, sweeter satsuma [2].
- **Today:** not grown in Yamaguchi, and in Fukuoka only at Munakata
  Taisha [2]. A 2022 DNA search of old Yamaguchi garden trees found no
  Kunenbo; most unknown trees were small natsudaidai [2].

**Finding 3 — cam sành: a mass-planted Mekong Delta crop.**
- **Vĩnh Long province:** about 18,000 ha at the end of 2023 and over
  18,000 ha at the end of 2024 [10][11]. The 2020 plan had targeted
  9,000 ha [10].
- **Output:** over 900,000 t a year [11] or over 1 million t [10] in
  Vĩnh Long; about 2 million t across the Mekong Delta [10].
- **Other provinces:** Trà Vinh over 3,400 ha, including over 2,600 ha
  in Cầu Kè [10]; also Bến Tre, Cần Thơ, Tiền Giang and Hậu Giang
  [6][12].
- **Northern Vietnam:** Hà Giang had 5,881 ha of oranges in 2023-2024
  (5,375 ha bearing, about 54,400 t), down from about 7,067 ha of cam
  sành and 60,759 t in 2019 [13].

**Finding 4 — boom and bust.**
- **Prices:** 15,000 to over 25,000 VND/kg in the boom years [10]; in
  late 2023, 1,000-2,000 VND/kg for non-contract growers [11].
- **Costs:** production cost almost 8,000 VND/kg; investment over 350
  million VND/ha [11].
- **Causes:** unplanned expansion, sharply higher yields from intensive
  methods, competition from northern oranges, and no official export
  channel or processing industry [10][11].

**Finding 5 — huanglongbing (HLB) is the limiting disease.**
- **Southern Vietnam:** HLB is "the most devastating disease" in
  citrus, where 'King' mandarin is commonly grown [7].
- **JICA-SOFRI project** [7]:
  - wider spacing (4.0 m against a traditional under 1.5 m), spherical
    canopy training and HLB management;
  - first harvest 26 months after planting, averaging 25.2 kg per tree
    against 10 kg traditionally;
  - HLB infection 4.3% of trees against 56% on traditional farms.
- **Northern losses:** greening wiped out cam sành at Bố Hạ, Bắc Giang
  [6].
- **Mandarin generally:** in Southeast Asia, orchards often have to be
  abandoned 8-15 years after planting because of the greening complex
  [5].

**Finding 6 — a 600-year ritual fruit.**
- **Munakata Taisha (Fukuoka):** Kunenbo is offered as a special
  sacred food (*shinsen*) at the shrine's ancient festival, recorded in
  the *Kuji Kongen* of 1422; the shrine tree matches the national
  genebank Kunenbo at all 12 DNA markers [2].
- **Diplomatic dessert:** the Chōshū lord Mōri Takachika served Kunenbo
  to a visiting British naval commander as a dessert [2].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Evergreen tree, about 3-3.5 m tall with a
  3-4 m canopy (cam sành) [6]; upright, spiny or spineless [5].
- **Harvest pattern:** Perennial; one main crop a year, with off-season
  crops forced in the Mekong Delta (see Harvest stages, below) [8][11].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** First harvest 26 months after planting
  in the JICA project [7]. Trial orchards at 4 years old had one year of
  economic harvest [8].
- **Climate zones:**
  - **Climate:** tropical wet lowlands (King) [5]; also hill districts
    of northern Vietnam [6][13].
  - **Elevation:** "does well at comparatively high altitudes" [6]; no
    figure was sourced.
  - **Cold:** King mandarins are "less resistant to cold than many other
    mandarins" [5], but the Japanese Kunembo is "as hardy as Satsuma"
    [4] (see Open research questions).
  - **Chill:** not applicable to an evergreen citrus.
- **Composition:**
  - **Cam sành flesh, per 100 g (Vietnam, 2025):** moisture 91.14 g;
    protein 0.73 g; lipid 0.32 g; ash 0.38 g; carbohydrate 7.44 g;
    vitamin C 32.31 mg [9].
  - **Cam sành fruit:** 118 g at 58-65 mm, 143 g at 64-73 mm and 328 g
    at 87-100 mm [9]; elsewhere 150-250 g and 4-12 cm [6]. Peel about 5
    mm thick, green or part green, lumpy [6].
  - **Japanese Kunembo:** about 120 g; rind 3-5 mm or "very thick";
    15-20 seeds [4]; seeds average 12.8 (4.3-21.5) per fruit in another
    count [2]. Tanaka described it as almost acidless and not juicy [4].
  - **King (general):** large fruit, thick peel, few to many seeds,
    12-14 segments [5].
- **Edibility:** Raw-edible; eaten fresh [2][6].
- **Pollination:** Kunenbo is self-incompatible, but polyembryonic
  seed gives true-to-type nucellar seedlings [2][4]. Boron sprays raised
  pollen germination and fruit set in cam sành [8].
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced. Not applicable to storage
  organs.
- **Yield:**
  - **Vĩnh Long, official-looking series:** 36.6 t/ha in 2019 to 44.1
    t/ha in 2023 [11].
  - **Vĩnh Long, press claims:** an average of about 70 t/ha [11], or
    70-90 t/ha [10], with some gardens near 100 t/ha [11].
  - **Boron trial (Trà Ôn, 2004-2005):** 19.47 t/ha untreated, 25.25
    t/ha at 100 ppm and 27.43 t/ha at 250 ppm [8].
  - **Per tree:** 25.2 kg at first harvest (JICA) against 10 kg
    traditionally [7].
  - **Hà Giang:** about 10 t/ha on bearing area (see Estimates, below)
    [13].
  - **Southeast Asian mandarins (general):** about 5 t/ha now, 25 t/ha
    attainable [5].
- **Water use:** Not sourced for this crop. For Southeast Asian
  mandarins generally, tree rows are watered regularly in the dry
  season [5]. In the Mekong Delta, irrigation is withheld (*xiết nước*)
  and then restored to induce flowering [8].
- **Propagation:**
  - **Methods:** grafting and marcotting (air layering) [6].
  - **Rootstocks:** various [6]; Vietnamese citrus generally uses
    local or sour pomelo rootstocks, which are susceptible to gummosis
    [12].
  - **Clonal/viral risk:** HLB is spread by the psyllid *Diaphorina
    citri*; micro-grafting is used to produce clean plants [6].
- **Labor:** Not sourced. Canopy training and pruning are part of the
  improved system [7].
- **Soil:** Prefers alluvial soil [6]. Salinity tolerance was not
  sourced; the 2019-2020 Mekong drought and salinity crisis damaged
  about 6,650 ha of fruit orchards in six provinces including Vĩnh Long,
  but no cam sành figure was found [13].
- **Storage (postharvest):** Not sourced for this crop. Mandarins
  generally keep 4-5 weeks at 10 °C and 85-90% RH [5]. Unsold fruit
  ripens and drops on the tree [11][13].
- **Establishment:**
  - **Traditional Mekong spacing:** 2 m × 1.7 m (about 2,940 trees/ha)
    in the boron-trial orchard [8]; under 1.5 m traditionally [7].
  - **Improved spacing:** 4.0 m [7].
- **Cultivars:**
  - **Japan:** 'Kunenbo', 'Kunenbo-Kagoshima' and
    'Kunenbo-Kamikoshikijima' (one genotype); a second, different
    "Kunenbo" (Kunenbo-B) [1][2].
  - **China:** 'Bendiguangju' (same genotype as Kunenbo) [1].
  - **Vietnam:** cam sành, including Hà Giang's protected geographical
    indication in Bắc Quang, Quang Bình and Vị Xuyên [6][13].
- **Weather response:** Cold-weather demand affects price, not the crop
  itself [11]. Mass fruit drop has been reported in Hà Giang [13].
  Salinity intrusion threatens Mekong orchards [13].
- **Water regime:** Irrigated, with deliberate water stress for flower
  induction in the Mekong Delta [8].
- **Harvest stages / windows:**
  - **Mekong Delta:** 8 months from flowering to harvest [9]; off-season
    (*mùa nghịch*) fruit fetched 13,000-18,000 VND/kg [11].
  - **Hà Giang:** December to March [13].
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced for this crop; *Phytophthora* root rot
  is a major mandarin problem in Thailand [5].
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** HLB and its psyllid vector (Finding 5)
  [6][7]. No invasiveness listing was found.
- **Controlled environment:** Not sourced.

## Hazards

- **HLB (citrus greening):** kills trees and destroys orchards (Finding
  5) [5][6][7].
- **Pests:** citrus leaf miner, green shoot borer, brown stem borer,
  citrus rust mite, fruit fly and mealybugs [6].
- **Human health:** no hazard specific to this fruit was found. The
  Japanese Kunenbo peel has a peculiar odour [2]; no toxicity was
  reported. No mycotoxin finding.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain 1 (fresh, Vietnam):** hand-picked → sold domestically through
  traders; the south supplies much of the north [6][10][11].
- **Chain 2 (juice):** its vigour and sweetness suit it to orange juice
  [6], but there is no established processing industry [10].
- **Chain 3 (fresh, historical Japan):** a fresh dessert fruit of
  Edo-Meiji western Japan [2].
- **Chain 4 (breeding):** a parent of satsuma and other Japanese and
  Ryukyu citrus (Finding 1) [1][3].
- **Ritual and religious:** a sacred food offering at Munakata Taisha
  since at least 1422 (Finding 6) [2].

## Open research questions

- **What *C. nobilis* means:** the Japanese Kunenbo, King and cam sành
  may be different genotypes under one name [2][3][6][9][12].
- **Satsuma parentage:** Kishuu × Kunenbo [1] against Ruju ×
  Bendiguang [12]; the two may be compatible, since Bendiguangju shares
  Kunenbo's genotype [1].
- **Yield:** 36.6-44.1 t/ha [11] against 70-90 t/ha [10][11] in Vĩnh
  Long; the higher numbers are press claims.
- **Cold hardiness:** "less resistant to cold" for King [5] against "as
  hardy as Satsuma" for the Japanese accession [4].
- **Fruit acidity:** almost acidless [4] against more acid than satsuma
  [2].
- **Not sourced:** water use, salinity threshold, storage life for this
  crop, labour hours, elevation figures and controlled-environment use.

## Estimates and derivations

**Vĩnh Long yield from totals** [10][11]:
- 900,000 t ÷ 18,000 ha = 50 t/ha;
- 1,000,000 t ÷ 18,000 ha = 55.6 t/ha.

This lies between the 44.1 t/ha series and the 70-90 t/ha claims.

**Hà Giang yield** [13]:
- 2023-2024: 54,400 t ÷ 5,375 ha bearing = 10.1 t/ha;
- 2019: 60,759 t ÷ 7,067 ha (all area) = 8.6 t/ha.

**Traditional density** [8]: 2 m × 1.7 m = 3.4 m² per tree; 10,000 ÷ 3.4
= 2,941 trees/ha, matching the source's 980 three-tree plots per ha
(2,940 trees).

**Improved system yield** (an estimate). The source gives only 4.0 m
spacing [7]. If square (4 × 4 m = 625 trees/ha): 25.2 kg × 625 = 15.8
t/ha at first harvest. At 4 × 5 m (500/ha), 12.6 t/ha.

**Boron response** [8]: (27.43 − 19.47) ÷ 19.47 = 41% gain at 250 ppm.

**Margin, late 2023** [11]: price 1,000-2,000 VND/kg against cost
almost 8,000 VND/kg, a loss of about 6,000-7,000 VND per kg.

**Ritual age** [2]: 2026 − 1422 = 604 years since the earliest record.
