## Naming and scope

*Actinidia arguta* (Siebold & Zucc.) Planch. ex Miq. (Actinidiaceae):
kiwiberry, hardy kiwi, baby kiwi, tara vine or bowerberry; Korean *darae*
[1][2][6].
- **Varieties:** three are recognised (var. *arguta*, var. *giraldii* and
  var. *hypoleuca*) [2].
- **Hybrids:** 'Issai' is *A. arguta* × *A. polygama* [2], and 'Ken's Red'
  is a hybrid with *A. melanandra* [1][2].

**Arctic kiwi** (*A. kolomikta*) is treated here as a cultivar group:
- **Hardiness:** hardier than *A. arguta*, to -37 to -40 °C [3][4];
- **Vigour:** less vigorous, to about 6 m against 12 m for *A. arguta* [3];
- **Fruit:** smaller [3], and very high in vitamin C [9];
- **Season:** ripens earlier, around early August in Minnesota [4];
- **Status:** "considered more of an ornamental vine" in the Pacific
  Northwest [1];
- **Name clash:** the Russian 'Ananasnaya' is *A. kolomikta*, while the
  'Ananasnaya' grown in Oregon is *A. arguta* [1].

**Separate entries:** fuzzy kiwifruit (`kiwifruit`, *A. deliciosa*) is its
own entry, `kiwifruit.md`. It shares the trellis, pruning and storage
systems, but needs 225-240 frost-free days and tolerates only about -12 °C
[1].

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — fully dormant vines are very cold-hardy; spring frost is
the real limit.**
- **Dormant hardiness of *A. arguta*:**
  - -23 to -32 °C [1];
  - about -32 °C [3];
  - -29 to -34 °C [4];
  - about -30 °C, "sometimes below" [6];
  - -45 °C in the native range [2].
- **Other types:** 'Issai' is less hardy, at -18 to -23 °C [1].
  *A. kolomikta* tolerates -37 to -40 °C [3][4].
- **Spring frost:** buds break early. Shoots are severely damaged by 30
  minutes at -1 °C or less [1][3]. Oregon growers have seen total crop
  loss without frost protection [1].
- **Protection:** in "essentially all" Pacific Northwest regions, the
  crop needs overhead-sprinkler frost protection [1]. Over-canopy
  sprinklers give 3-4 °C of protection [1].
- **Partial recovery:** only 25-57% of buds break, so buds held back can
  replace a frosted first flush [1].
- **Males:** male vines are more frost-susceptible than females [1].
  *A. deliciosa* pollen can then be bought and applied instead [1].

**Finding 2 — eaten whole, picked firm in a single pass.**
- **Fruit:** 6-14 g in 'Ananasnaya' and 4-9 g in 'Issai' [1]. The smooth
  skin is eaten [1][2].
- **Uneven ripening:** fruit ripens unevenly. Even at the earliest
  harvest, 1-3% is already soft and overripe, and multiple harvests are
  "not practical or economical" [1].
- **Harvest standard:** pick at 8-14 °Brix, though some growers pick at
  6.5 [1]. Fruit left on the vine reaches 18-25 °Brix and tears when
  picked [1][2]. Alternative indices are black seeds and 17-20% dry
  matter [1].
- **Timing:** harvest falls in early to mid-September in Oregon, at
  2,400-2,600 growing degree days (°F, base 50) [1]. *A. kolomikta*
  ripens earlier [4].
- **Handling:** fruit shrivels after 3-4% water loss and must be cooled
  quickly [1].

**Finding 3 — storage is short and trades firmness against pitting.**
- **General:** store near 0 °C at 90-95% RH with ethylene removed, for
  less than two months, against 3-6 months for 'Hayward' fuzzy kiwifruit
  [1]. Softening is three times faster at 5 °C than at 0 °C [1].
- **'Autumn Sense' (Korea, picked 105-110 days after full bloom at 7-8%
  soluble solids):**
  - at 1 °C, fruit kept firmness and lost less weight than at 5 °C;
  - but pitting was significantly worse at 1 °C after 2 weeks, and
    became more severe in mid-to-late storage [5].
- **Other cultivars:** pitting, browning and shrivel rose over 8 weeks in
  'Cheongsan' and 'Daebo' [10]. For 'Ken's Red', 0 and 2 °C differed only
  in slightly higher firmness at 0 °C [10].
- **Controlled atmosphere:** improves firmness in some cultivars but can
  increase skin blemishes [1].

**Finding 4 — a vigorous dioecious vine on a permanent trellis, slow to
reach full crop.**
- **Planting:** commonly 15 × 15 ft (194 vines per acre), with one male
  per eight females [1]. Ratios of 1:6-10 [1], 1:6 [2] and 1:4 [3] are
  also given.
- **Vigour:** canes grow 1.8-3.7 m a year, occasionally 6 m [1].
- **Trellis:** T-bar or pergola, with the canopy 1.8-2.1 m above the
  ground [1].
- **Time to crop:** a commercial crop takes three or more years, and
  mature production up to five [1]. Flowering starts in year three [2].
- **Yield per vine:**
  - 'Ananasnaya' 27-68 kg; 'Issai' 23-41 kg [1];
  - an average of about 23 kg and up to 45 kg [2];
  - 23-45 kg per mature plant [12];
  - 9-35 kg per plant in a European study [9].
- **Pollination:** flowers carry no nectar, so 3-4 honeybee hives per
  acre are needed [1]. A well-pruned female vine can carry 10,000
  flowers, and 'Ananasnaya' fruit hold up to 240 seeds [1]. Poor
  pollination gives grooved, small fruit [1].
- **No cost study:** none exists for the Pacific Northwest. Plantings are
  expensive because of plant and trellis costs and the years to full
  production, and the crop is labour-intensive relative to other berries
  [1].

**Finding 5 — a small, scattered world industry.**
- **Area estimates (conflicting):**
  - about 1,640 ha in 2015, of which 1,260 ha in China, with about
    2,300 ha more added in China by 2019 [2];
  - a 2022 New Zealand industry report puts world commercial plantings
    "at under 200 ha" [8], probably excluding China.
- **Suppliers:** the main international suppliers are New Zealand, the
  United States, Canada and Chile [8].
- **South Korea:** 45.4 t in 2023 [2].
- **Oregon:** commercial plantings began in the late 1990s [2], and Oregon
  is a leading US producer [1].
- **Market limits:** acreage is limited by marketing, uneven fruit size, a
  short ripening window and short shelf life [1].

**Finding 6 — an escaped ornamental in the northeastern United States.**
- **Introduction:** arrived in 1877 as an ornamental [6].
- **Spread:** it now smothers woodland and pulls down trees at at least 20
  sites in Berkshire County, Massachusetts, and at sites in Connecticut,
  New York and New Jersey [6]. Raccoons and possibly birds spread the
  seed [6].
- **Status:** the Massachusetts advisory group voted it "likely invasive"
  in 2015 [6]. The state decided in 2017 not to add it to the prohibited
  list [7].
- **Wild plants:** wild populations show no significant disease [6].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Deciduous woody climbing vine [2][6]:
  - *A. arguta* to about 12 m in cultivation and 30 m in the wild [3];
  - female flowers have 5 petals and male flowers 6; bloom in June-July
    [6], or mid to late June in Oregon [1].
- **Harvest pattern:** Perennial, with one picking a year (Finding 2)
  [1]. Productive life: not sourced.
- **Days/years to first bearing:** A light crop in year 2 is possible.
  A commercial crop comes in year 3+, and mature production by about year
  5 [1]. Seeds germinate in about a month after 1-2 months of cold
  stratification [2].
- **Climate zones:**
  - **Season length:** needs about 150 frost-free days [2][3].
  - **Elevation:** native to China, the Russian Far East, Korea, Taiwan
    and Japan, at 150-1,500 m, up to 1,900 m in southern Japan [2].
  - **Heat:** tolerates summer heat above 37 °C [2].
  - **Chill:** buds break after the chilling requirement is met [1]; the
    amount is not sourced.
  - **Wind:** winds of 16-24 km/h break developing shoots [3]. Wind scars
    fruit, making it fit only for processing [1].
- **Composition:**
  - **Raw fruit, per 100 g:** energy 77 kcal (322 kJ); carbohydrate 18 g
    (sugars 7.6 g); fibre 3 g; fat 0.6 g; protein 1.2 g; vitamin C 93 mg;
    potassium 288 mg [2]. The encyclopedia does not name the analysis
    behind these figures; no national food-composition entry exists.
  - **Vitamin C:** *Actinidia* fruit can reach 430 mg per 100 g [1].
    *A. kolomikta* has measured about 1,008 mg and up to 1,164 mg per 100 g
    [9].
  - **Acid:** 1-3% of fresh weight for the genus [1].
- **Edibility:** Raw-edible whole fruit, skin included [1][2]. Young
  leaves are cooked as a Korean vegetable (*darae-sun*) [2].
- **Pollination:**
  - **Dioecious:** one male per 6-10 females [1]; female flowers are
    receptive for 7-9 days, and male pollen is viable for 2-3 days [1][3].
  - **Pollinators:** honeybees, at 3-4 hives per acre [1].
  - **Hand pollination:** with *A. deliciosa* pollen, 10 g per acre per
    day from 10% bloom [1].
  - **Self-fertile:** 'Issai', though fruit is larger when
    cross-pollinated [1].
  - **Arctic kiwi:** needs an *A. kolomikta* male [12].
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced. Not applicable to storage
  organs.
- **Yield:**
  - **Per vine:** see Finding 4 [1][2][9][12].
  - **Per hectare:** about 12-29 t/ha for 'Ananasnaya' at standard
    spacing (see Estimates, below) [1]. A New Zealand grower estimated
    15-20 t/ha in 2017 [8].
- **Water use:**
  - **Measured:** 25-35 US gallons (95-132 L) per mature vine per day at
    full canopy in late summer, at 21-27 °C, in British Columbia [1].
  - **Crop coefficient:** 0.9 recommended; full canopy from mid-July to
    mid-October [1].
  - **Converted:** about 4.5-6.3 mm a day at standard density (see
    Estimates, below).
  - **Stress:** drought causes leaf drop and premature, uneven,
    poor-flavoured ripening, and delays wood maturity, raising cold risk.
    Excess water promotes root rot [1].
- **Propagation:**
  - **Planting stock:** 2-year-old bare-root or container stock [1].
  - **Cuttings:** hardwood cuttings 5-6 nodes long, taken in early January
    [1].
  - **Rootstock:** self-rooted vines are preferred over grafted ones,
    since cold can kill a grafted vine above the union [1].
  - **Clonal/viral risk:** not sourced. Propagation is clonal for named
    cultivars [1].
- **Labor:**
  - **Hours:** not sourced. No cost-of-production study exists for the
    region [1].
  - **Character:** labour-intensive relative to other berries [1]. Males
    are pruned in summer and females in winter [1].
  - **Harvest:** a single hand harvest. Pickers must wear gloves because
    the fruit bruises more easily than fuzzy kiwifruit [1].
- **Soil:**
  - **pH and texture:** pH 5.6-6.0 [1], or 5-6.5 [3]; deep, well-drained
    silt or sandy loams [1].
  - **Drainage:** raised beds at least 30 cm high; three or more days of
    waterlogging can kill roots [1].
  - **Salinity:** roots are very sensitive to fertiliser burn and high
    salts. Chloride is toxic, so avoid muriate of potash [1]. Irrigation
    water should have chloride under 70 ppm, sodium under 50 ppm and boron
    under 0.25 ppm [1].
  - **Nitrogen:** 100-190 lb N per acre a year on mature vines [1].
- **Storage (postharvest):** About 0 °C, 90-95% RH, ethylene-free, for
  under 2 months [1]. The 1 °C versus 5 °C trial is in Finding 3 [5].
  Ethylene at 10 ppb for a week hastens softening [1].
- **Establishment:**
  - **Spacing:** 15 × 15 ft (4.6 × 4.6 m), or pergola rows 15-20 ft apart
    [1].
  - **Planting design:** males in every third row between pairs of
    females [1].
  - **Frost protection:** irrigation is installed before planting [1].
  - **Trunk protection:** trunks are painted or wrapped against sunburn
    [1].
  - **Windbreaks:** protect 200-250 ft downwind [1].
- **Cultivars:**
  - **'Ananasnaya'** ('Anna'): the main Oregon cultivar, ripening mid to
    late September [1].
  - **'Issai':** self-fertile, early September, more prone to root rot
    [1].
  - **'Ken's Red':** a red-fleshed hybrid [1].
  - **'74-49':** a USDA selection [1].
  - **'HortGem Tahi':** early [1][2].
  - **'Jumbo':** late and bland [1].
  - **'Geneva 3':** ripens 1-2 weeks earlier than 'Ananasnaya' and stores
    well [1].
  - **Males:** 'Meader' and '74-52' [1].
  - **Regional cultivars:** 'Weiki' and 'Geneva' (Europe), and 'Long Cheng
    No. 2' (China) [2]; Korean 'Autumn Sense', 'Cheongsan' and 'Daebo'
    [5][10].
  - **Arctic kiwi:** 'Arctic Beauty' [3].
- **Weather response:**
  - **Frost and wind:** spring frost and wind (Findings 1-2).
  - **Hail:** hail scars fruit [1].
  - **Sun:** sudden sun exposure sunburns fruit [1]; fruit shaded in the
    canopy stays green [1].
- **Water regime:** Irrigated (drip or under-vine microsprinklers),
  plus overhead sprinklers for frost [1]. Flood-intolerant [1].
- **Harvest stages / windows:** Physiologically ripe and firm at 8-14
  °Brix (sometimes 6.5), with black seeds and 17-20% dry matter [1].
  Vine-ripe fruit at 18-25 °Brix is unstorable [1].
- **Soil biology:** Phytophthora root rot, Verticillium wilt, Armillaria
  and root-knot nematodes are soil-borne threats [1]. Avoid sites after
  mint or black raspberry, which host Verticillium [1].
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** Escaped and naturalised in the US
  Northeast (Finding 6) [6][7]. The encyclopedia notes the invasion risk
  is debated [2].
- **Controlled environment:** Not sourced.

## Hazards

- **Allergen:** actinidin (Act d 1) is the major kiwifruit allergen. One
  overview says kiwiberry may also cause reactions, but no study testing
  *A. arguta* directly was found [11].
- **Ecological:** forest-smothering escapes in the US Northeast [6].
- **Diseases:** Phytophthora root rot (the main killer of vines) and gray
  mould in storage [1].
- **Pests:** thrips scar fruit in Oregon [1]. Spotted-wing drosophila,
  sooty blotch and flyspeck are concerns in Minnesota [4]. Deer, rodents
  and voles damage vines, and cats rubbing on trunks damage young shoots
  [1][3].
- **Fungal contamination of the product:** gray mould after harvest [1].
  No mycotoxin finding.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain 1 (fresh):** picked firm into lugs → cooled quickly →
  stored at 0 °C, ethylene-free → ethylene-triggered ripening before
  shipping → sold in clamshells [1].
- **Chain 2 (soft and scarred fruit):** fruit soft at harvest is frozen
  or sold immediately [1]. Scarred fruit goes to processing [1].
- **Chain 3 (processed):** jams, preserves, juice, wine, fruit vinegar and
  nutritional powders [2].
- **Chain 4 (leaves):** young leaves are cooked as Korean *namul* [2].
- **Other:** vines of the genus are used in the Iya *kazura* suspension
  bridge in Tokushima, Japan, which is 45 m long and replaced every three
  years [2]. The vines are also grown as ornamentals [6].

No religious or ritual use was found in the sources consulted.

## Open research questions

- **Dormant hardiness:** figures range from -23 °C to -45 °C. The coldest
  figure is from the native range [2] and the others are from cultivated
  vines [1][3][4][6].
- **Storage temperature:** colder storage keeps firmness, but warmer
  storage limits pitting. Results differ by cultivar [5][10].
- **World area:** 1,640 ha in 2015 [2] against "under 200 ha" in 2022 [8].
  The difference is probably China, but this is not confirmed.
- **Composition:** the encyclopedia figures have no named analysis [2].
- **Not sourced:**
  - labour hours, productive lifespan and chill hours;
  - virus risk in cuttings;
  - controlled-environment use;
  - an allergen study specific to *A. arguta*.

## Estimates and derivations

**Vine density** [1]:
- 194 vines per acre ÷ 0.4047 = 479 vines/ha;
- 174 females per acre ÷ 0.4047 = 430 female vines/ha.

**Per-hectare yield**, using 430 females/ha and 1 lb = 0.4536 kg [1]:
- 'Ananasnaya': 60-150 lb = 27.2-68.0 kg per vine → 11.7-29.3 t/ha;
- encyclopedia figures [2]: an average of 23 kg/vine → 9.9 t/ha, and a
  maximum of 45 kg → 19.4 t/ha;
- 'Issai': self-fertile, so all 479 vines/ha could be female. Then 50-90
  lb = 22.7-40.8 kg → 10.9-19.5 t/ha.

These assume every female reaches the stated mature yield, so they are
upper estimates for a mature block.

**Water use** [1]:
- 25-35 US gal × 3.785 = 94.6-132.5 L per vine per day;
- × 479 vines/ha = 45.3-63.5 m³/ha/day = 4.5-6.3 mm/day at peak.

**Other unit conversions** [1][3]:
- **Hardiness:** -10 to -25 °F = -23 to -32 °C; -35 to -40 °F = -37 to
  -40 °C; 0 to -10 °F = -18 to -23 °C.
- **Frost:** 30 °F = -1.1 °C. A protection of 6-8 °F (a temperature
  difference) = 3.3-4.4 °C.
- **Heat units:** 2,400-2,600 growing degree days (°F, base 50) × 5/9 =
  1,333-1,444 degree days (°C, base 10).
- **Nitrogen:** 100-190 lb N per acre × 1.121 = 112-213 kg N/ha.
- **Hives:** 3-4 hives per acre ÷ 0.4047 = 7.4-9.9 hives/ha.
- **Hand pollen:** 10 g per acre per day = 24.7 g/ha/day.
