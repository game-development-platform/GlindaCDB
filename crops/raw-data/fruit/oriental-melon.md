## Naming and scope

This entry covers the Makuwa group of *Cucumis melo* (Cucurbitaceae), the
East Asian melons [1]:
- **Korea:** chamoe (참외). The name joins "true" and "cucumber".
- **Japan:** makuwa uri (真桑瓜), after the village of Makuwa in Mino
  province.
- **China:** xiāng guā (香瓜, "fragrant melon").

Makino described it in 1928 as *C. melo* var. *makuwa*. That name is now
a synonym of the cultivar group [1]. Genetic studies suggest it came from
eastern India and reached China along the Silk Road, then Korea and Japan
[1]. Korean chamoe and Japanese makuwa share one entry here.

**Gate check on candidates (none proposed):**
- **Korean landraces.** Sunghwan or "frog" chamoe is sometimes placed in
  the Chinensis group, and gotgam chamoe smells of dried persimmon [1].
  Both are grown like chamoe and differ mainly in fruit, so they are
  cultivar notes, not rows.
- **Prince melon.** A 1962 Japanese cross of a makuwa type with
  Charentais [1]. It is a hybrid cultivar grown like other dessert
  melons, so it is a cultivar note here.
- **Balengcui.** A Chinese oriental-melon cultivar, known from the
  bitter-fruit study (Finding 1). It is a cultivar note.

**Relatives in this project:**
- `cantaloupe`: the netted and European cantaloupes.
- `honeydew`: the Inodorus group.
- `pickling_melon` and `snake_melon` (vegetable category): the Conomon and
  Flexuosus groups.

Material that applies to the whole species stays in `cantaloupe.md`: sex
expression, wild weedy melon, seed rules and vine decline.

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — a grafted plastic-house crop, with the rootstock trading
disease control against fruit quality.** The Jeju guide calls grafting
essential [2]. It gives five reasons:
- to prevent Fusarium wilt;
- to avoid continuous-cropping injury;
- to improve growth at low temperature;
- to strengthen nutrient uptake;
- to bring harvest forward.

Squash rootstocks do not get Fusarium wilt [2]. The guide compares three
types of rootstock:
- **Hybrid squash:** vigorous, but the vine takes up too much nitrogen and
  the fruit is prone to internal rot.
- ***Cucurbita maxima*-type squash:** grows well but is short-lived.
- ***C. pepo* types:** compatibility varies.

It recommends the rootstock 'Shintosa' as the safe choice [2]. The same
rootstocks cost fruit quality in two ways:
- **Fermented fruit.** This watery, alcoholic flesh disorder is worse on
  squash rootstock than on the melon's own roots, which the guide puts
  down to the stronger feeding of the squash roots [2].
- **Bitter fruit.** In a 2018 Chinese summer greenhouse trial (35 °C day,
  22 °C night), all six *C. maxima* rootstocks gave bitter fruit of
  'Balengcui': 28-72% of fruit, highest on 'Ribenxuesong'. Plants on two
  muskmelon rootstocks and ungrafted plants gave none [3]. Cucurbitacins
  O, C and S rose sharply in the bitter fruit [3].

**Finding 2 — five staggered growing systems give a market from February
to October.** The Jeju guide lists them [2]:

| System | Sow | Transplant | Harvest from | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Forcing | late Oct-mid Nov | early-mid Dec | early Apr | south |
| Semi-forcing | late Dec-mid Jan | late Jan-mid Feb | late May | south and centre |
| Tunnel early | late Jan-late Feb | late Mar-mid Apr | late Jun | nationwide |
| Early | early-late Mar | late Apr-mid May | mid Jul | nationwide |
| Open field | late Apr-early May | late May-early Jun | early Aug | nationwide |

How each system is run [2]:
- **Forcing:** a straw mat outside, double plastic and an inner tunnel
  keep the house above 15 °C at night, and the house should stay below
  30 °C by day.
- **Tunnel crop:** covered at night until mid-May against late frost.
- **Open field:** this area is shrinking, because the crop runs through
  the summer heat and rains.

The guide's table is older than the current calendar. In the 2026 crop,
27.6% of area was planted in November 2025 and 71.7% in December [4].
Some farms put planting off from November to December for lack of labour
[4]. Large Seongju farms moved grafting and planting about a week earlier
to harvest sooner [5]. Wholesale arrivals run from February to October
[5].

A cold-storage study gives a different picture. It says the crop is
commonly sown directly in the open field in mid-April, or transplanted at
the end of May, and harvested in May-August [6]. That conflicts with the
KREI planting data (Open research questions).

**Finding 3 — Korean output is concentrated in one province and one
county.** KREI figures:

| 2024 | 4,504 | 4,725 | 212.8 |
| 2025 (est.) | 4,664 | 4,663 | 217.5 |
| Normal year | 4,579 | 4,367 | 199.9 |
| 2026 (outlook) | 4,636 | 4,498 | 208.5 |
| 2035 (outlook) | 4,153 | 4,580 | 190.2 |

All rows are from KREI [4]. The yield of 4,663
kg/10a is 46.6 t/ha (see Estimates).

**Province and county share:**
- Gyeongsangbuk-do had 4,293 ha in 2025, about 92% of the area [4].
- It also grew 94.7% of the melons shipped to wholesale markets [4].
- Of that wholesale volume, Seongju County grew 71.5%, Chilgok 10.7% and
  Goryeong 7.1% [4].
- Seongju carries 90.3% of the weight in KREI's area survey [5].
- Wikipedia says Seongju grows 70% of national output [1].

**Long-term trend.** Area fell by an average of 1.9% a year from 2010 to
2025, as farmers aged [4]. Output has risen by 2.3% a year since 2021, as
yields rose [4]. In 2000 the crop covered 10,203 ha at 3,262 kg/10a [4].

**2025 season.** Cool, dull weather in June cut fruit set, and June
arrivals fell 30.1% from the year before [4].

**Consumption and trade:**
- Koreans eat about 4.2 kg per person per year [4].
- Exports were 405 t in 2025, of which 276 t went to Japan and 66 t to
  Vietnam. The Vietnam trade opened in 2025 [4].

**Finding 4 — hand pollination or hormones, inside a narrow temperature
window.** Most flowers are bisexual, with some male flowers [1][2].
Pollen from the bisexual flowers is weak, so growers do one of two things
[2]:
- brush pollen from vigorous male flowers onto the stigma by hand;
- spray a fruit-set hormone.

The timing rules [2]:
- Pollen is best between about 07:00 and 10:00, at around 20 °C.
- Fertilization takes about 24 hours, but rain more than 3 hours after
  pollination does little harm.
- Fruit sets well above 20 °C and needs at least 15 °C.
- Fruit set is very poor above 32 °C.

The guide lists six fruit-set or fruit-swelling products: a
chlorophenoxy-acid tomato product, 2,4-D, benzyladenine, NAA, gibberellin
and 2,4,5-TP [2]. It advises using the lowest effective dose in hot
weather. It says good temperature management without fruit-swelling
agents gives sweeter fruit [2].

Fruit sets on "granddaughter" (second-order) laterals at nodes 7-12 [2]:
- Two to four vines are kept from nodes 3-6.
- The primary laterals are stopped at nodes 15-20.
- Two or three leaves are left on each fruiting lateral.

**Finding 5 — harvest is timed exactly, and storage has to stay above the
chilling range.** Harvest timing [2]:
- Fruit is ready 35-38 days after pollination in cool weather, or 27-30
  days in hot weather.
- Unripe fruit does not develop colour, aroma or sweetness after picking.
- It is picked in the cool of the morning or late afternoon. Hot fruit
  blotches and softens in transit.

Shelf life and storage [6]:
- About 10 days at room temperature, given its climacteric ripening and
  thin rind.
- Cited best storage: 7-10 °C at 90-95% RH.
- At 4 °C the white sutures and the peel turn brown. Water-soaking and
  *Alternaria* rot are other cold-storage injuries.
- A suggested controlled atmosphere is 2-3% O₂ with 5-10% CO₂, and a
  38 °C, 48-hour heat treatment before storage reduces chilling injury.
  Both come from earlier studies the authors cite.

In their own trial ('Smart', from Seongju, August 2018), fruit was held 14
days at 4 or 10 °C and then 5 days at 20 °C [6]:
- Unwrapped fruit at 4 °C lost 3.67% of its weight in 14 days.
- Polyethylene bags held the loss to 0.2% (4 °C) and 0.07% (10 °C).
- Both film types reduced browning at 4 °C.

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** A warm-season, trailing annual vine [1]. Most
  of it is grown under plastic from winter or spring plantings [2].
- **Harvest pattern:** Repeated picking from successive fruit sets on
  granddaughter laterals [2]. The number of flushes per crop is not
  sourced.
- **Days to first bearing:** About 100-160 days from sowing (see
  Estimates). The nursery takes 40-50 days of that [2].
- **Climate zones:**
  - Temperatures [2]:
    - Best growth at 26-30 °C by day and 17-20 °C at night.
    - Soil at 18 °C or more, ideally 20-25 °C.
    - Germination best at 25-30 °C; growth checked below 15 °C.
  - Wikipedia gives 24-28 °C by day and 16-24 °C at night, and calls it
    a "cool sub-temperate crop" [1].
  - It needs plenty of sun. Long cloudy spells give leafy vines, poor
    fruit set and less sweetness [2].
  - Chill requirement: not applicable.
  - Elevation: not sourced.
- **Composition:** No entry in the US food-composition database (searched
  this pass). An RDA magazine article gives these values per 100 g [7]:
  - water about 90%;
  - potassium 450 mg;
  - β-carotene 90 µg;
  - folate 132 µg.

  The folate figure is about seven times honeydew's 19 µg
  (`honeydew.md`), and the article doesn't name its database. Energy is
  not sourced (see Estimates for 30-36 kcal per 100 g). Wikipedia calls
  it noticeably less sweet than Western melons [1].
- **Edibility:** Eaten raw. The thin rind and small seeds can be eaten
  with the flesh [1]. The RDA article recommends eating the washed peel
  for its β-carotene [7].
- **Pollination:** Andromonoecious, with bisexual and male flowers on the
  same plant [1]. Growers pollinate by hand or use hormones, in a narrow
  time and temperature window (Finding 4) [2]. Bees are not mentioned.
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced.
- **Yield:**
  - National average: 46.6 t/ha in 2025, 47.3 t/ha in 2024, 43.7 t/ha in
    a normal year, and 45.5 t/ha in 2023 [4].
  - Past and projected: 32.6 t/ha in 2000, and a projected 45.8 t/ha by
    2035 [4].
  - Yields by system, protected vs. open field: not sourced.
  - A trailing hydroponic system on trial in Gyeongbuk is reported to
    give 1.7 times the output. KREI cites a provincial press release for
    this [4].
- **Water use:** FAO-56 has no oriental-melon row. Its "sweet melons" row
  gives [8]:
  - Kc 0.5 initial, 1.05 mid-season and 0.75 late;
  - height 0.4 m;
  - rooting depth 0.8-1.5 m;
  - depletion fraction p 0.40.

  Stage lengths (initial/development/mid/late, in days) [8]:
  - 25/35/40/20 for a May Mediterranean crop;
  - 30/30/50/30 for March in California;
  - 15/40/65/15 for August in the California desert;
  - 30/45/65/20 for a December-January planting in arid regions.

  The Jeju guide puts most roots in the top 15-25 cm [2]. That is root
  concentration, not FAO's maximum effective depth. Irrigation [2]:
  - The most water is needed while fruit swells. Too little gives small
    or misshapen fruit.
  - Watering is cut from about 25 days after flowering.
  - It is stopped completely 10 days before harvest.
- **Propagation:** From seed. Seedlings are grafted onto squash rootstock
  [2]:
  - The scion is sown first for an approach graft. The rootstock is sown
    first for a cleft or insertion graft.
  - Grafting is done about 17 days after sowing.
  - For an approach graft, the scion's own root is cut 15-18 days after
    grafting.
  - Grafts heal at 25-28 °C by day and 15-18 °C at night, shaded for 2-3
    days.
  - The seedling is stopped at 4-6 true leaves and hardened for 7-10 days
    before planting.

  KREI mentions grafting robots as a coming labour saver [4]. Whether
  commercial cultivars are F1 hybrids is not sourced.
- **Labor:** 219 hours per 10a per crop for protected oriental melon,
  from RDA's 2024 farm-income data as cited by KREI [4]. That is 2,190
  h/ha (see Estimates). The same source gives 530 h for protected
  cucumber and 124 h for protected watermelon [4]. The breakdown by task
  is not sourced. Ageing farmers and labour shortages are the main reason
  area is falling [4][5].
- **Soil:** The Jeju guide's soil advice [2]:
  - Well-drained, organic sandy loam at pH 6.0-6.8.
  - Sandy soil warms fast and suits early crops, but vines age quickly
    and disease is worse.
  - Clay loam keeps vines going longer and gives denser, sweeter fruit,
    so it suits late summer crops.

  Fertiliser per 10a [2]: 3,000 kg compost, 100 kg lime, and nutrients of
  25 kg N, 7.7 kg P₂O₅ and 16 kg K₂O. Per hectare, that is 250 kg N
  (see Estimates). The nitrogen is split between a base dressing and
  three top-dressings.

  Salinity: FAO-56 rates melons moderately sensitive, with no numbers
  [8]. FAO-61 gives muskmelon (Reticulatus group) a threshold of 1.0 dS/m
  with an 8.4% yield loss per dS/m, also moderately sensitive [9]. No
  figure specific to oriental melon was found. Fermented-fruit fields
  have higher EC, nitrogen, calcium and magnesium than clean fields [2].
- **Storage (postharvest):** Best at 7-10 °C and 90-95% RH. Chilling
  injury at 4 °C. About 10 days at room temperature. MAP bags cut weight
  loss and browning (Finding 5) [6].
- **Establishment:** Planting [2]:
  - Transplant into high, plastic-mulched beds when the soil is at least
    15 °C.
  - Finish planting before 14:00, and plant shallow, because deep planting
    increases gummy stem blight.

  Spacing [2]:
  - Beds 1.5-2 m wide in houses.
  - With two vines trained both ways, plants are 35-40 cm apart; with two
    vines trained one way, 60-75 cm.
  - With three vines, 60-75 cm trained both ways or 75-90 cm trained one
    way.
  - This works out at 700-1,100 plants per 10a, or 7,000-11,000 per ha.
- **Cultivars:**
  - **Ginsen makuwa / Euncheon chamoe:** developed in Toyama, Japan, and
    introduced to Korea in 1957, where it quickly became dominant [1].
    'Sin-euncheon' followed in the 1970s and 'Geumssaragi-euncheon' in
    1984; the latter now dominates [1].
  - **Typical modern chamoe:** yellow with white stripes, about 15 cm long
    and 450 g, with white flesh [1].
  - **Fermented-fruit susceptibility:** 'Geumssaragi Euncheon' gets more
    fermented fruit than 'Chammat Euncheon' [2].
  - **Landraces:** Sunghwan (frog) chamoe and gotgam chamoe [1].
  - **Japan:** Golden Makuwa (about 300 g) is a Nara "Yamato vegetable",
    and was 85.6% of melons sold at the Osaka central market in 1955
    [1]. New Melon weighs 300-400 g, and Prince melon (1962, crossed with
    Charentais) went on to displace makuwa in Japan [1].
  - **China:** 'Balengcui', a crisp Chinese cultivar [3].
  - **Seongju trials:** the 2018 storage trial used 'Smart' [6].
- **Weather response:**
  - Frost: tunnel crops are covered at night until mid-May against late
    frost [2]. Frost is not a quality event.
  - Heat: fruit set fails above 32 °C [2].
  - Cool, dull weather: cuts fruit set, as in June 2025 [4], and causes
    fruit to drop [2].
  - Drought: Wikipedia calls the plant drought tolerant [1]. The Jeju
    guide says water shortage while fruit swells gives small, misshapen
    fruit [2].
  - Waterlogging: a waterlogged field at maturity fills the seed cavity
    with water and leads to cracking [2]. Phytophthora is worse in wet
    soil [2].
- **Water regime:** Ordinary irrigation; not a flooded crop [2].
- **Harvest stages / windows:**
  - One maturity stage per fruit, judged by the fruit stalk yellowing and,
    in Euncheon types, by yellow skin and aroma [2].
  - Fruit sets in successive flushes on different laterals, so a planting
    ripens over a period [2].
  - The national calendar covers February to October through the five
    systems [2][5].
- **Soil biology:**
  - Continuous-cropping injury and soil-borne Fusarium wilt are the main
    reasons for grafting [2].
  - Fusarium wilt is worse on acid soil and with excess nitrogen, and
    rarer on slightly alkaline soil [2].
  - The guide advises 200 kg/10a of slaked lime, mulching, and avoiding
    replanting the same field [2].
  - Symbionts and allelopathy: not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:**
  - No invasive listing was found for oriental melon in this pass.
  - Western Australia has a 2022 weed risk assessment of *C. melo*,
    written for rockmelon. Its findings were not readable, so it is listed
    as not yet read.
  - Australia has a plant import risk analysis for melons from Korea,
    which could not be fetched (not yet read).
  - Fresh-fruit export protocols exist: trade to Vietnam opened in 2025
    after quarantine negotiation [4]. Their pest conditions are not
    sourced.
  - See `cantaloupe.md` for the species-wide weedy-melon material.
- **Controlled environment:** Almost all of the crop is grown in plastic
  houses or tunnels [2][4]. A trailing hydroponic system and a top-down
  vertical system are being introduced in Gyeongbuk [4]. Whitefly
  smart-traps are also being trialled [4].

## Hazards

- **Bitter fruit (cucurbitacins) on squash rootstocks:** 28-72% of fruit
  on *C. maxima* rootstocks in a summer greenhouse trial, and none on
  muskmelon rootstocks or ungrafted plants [3]. **TO BE ADDRESSED** —
  cucurbitacins are toxic in quantity, and this project has no home yet
  for health and safety data.
- **Fusarium wilt:** soil-borne. Controlled by grafting onto squash, which
  does not get the disease [2].
- **Gummy stem blight:** the most damaging disease under plastic [2].
  - It attacks the stem near the ground, and plants can die within 2-3
    days.
  - It is favoured by 20-24 °C, rain, poor drainage and poor ventilation.
  - Control: high beds, mulch, shallow planting and pruning on sunny days.
- **Phytophthora:** in waterlogged soil [2].
- **Viruses:** cucumber mosaic virus and melon mosaic virus, worst in late
  summer crops. Aphid screens and disinfected pruning tools control them
  [2].
- **Foliar diseases and pests:** powdery mildew, downy mildew and a
  blight; aphids and mites [2].
- **Physiological disorders** [2]:
  - fermented fruit (above);
  - cracking of young fruit, from large day-night temperature swings;
  - cracking at harvest, from sudden soil-moisture rises;
  - gourd-shaped fruit, from low light, rank growth or too many fruit;
  - protruding-navel fruit, from temperature swings, strong hormone doses
    or early drought;
  - acute late-season wilt, from weak roots and too little leaf for the
    fruit load.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain, fresh:**
  - Grafted seedlings are planted out.
  - Fruit is hand-pollinated or set with hormone.
  - Fruit is picked in the cool of the day.
  - It is sold through the Seongju auction and wholesale markets [2][4].
  - Consumers prefer 1.5 kg bags (39.6%) and packs of 1 kg or less
    (34.2%) [4].
  - Exports need cold storage to reach market [6]. In trials, fruit
    bagged in film kept for 14 days at 10 °C, then 5 days at 20 °C [6].
- **Chain, pickled (Korea):** whole fruit is pickled with spices as
  chamoe-jangajji [1].
- **Chain, pickled (Japan):** unripe fruit is made into tsukemono [1].
- **Ritual:** in Japan it is an offering at the Bon festival, which is
  also its peak season [1].
- **Not otherwise detailed here:**
  - hormone application rates in detail;
  - house heating and covering systems;
  - fresh-cut processing;
  - the seed trade.

## Estimates and derivations

**National yield, 46.6 t/ha in 2025.** 4,663 kg/10a [4]. 10a = 0.1 ha, so
4,663 kg / 0.1 ha = 46,630 kg/ha = 46.6 t/ha. Check against output and
area: 217.5 kt / 4,664 ha = 46.6 t/ha. Using the same arithmetic:
- 2024: 4,725 kg/10a = 47.3 t/ha;
- normal year: 4,367 kg/10a = 43.7 t/ha;
- 2023: 4,553 kg/10a = 45.5 t/ha (208.5 kt / 4,580 ha = 45.5);
- 2000: 3,262 kg/10a = 32.6 t/ha;
- 2035: 4,580 kg/10a = 45.8 t/ha.

**Gyeongbuk area share, about 92%.** 4,293 / 4,664 = 0.920 [4].

**Labour, 2,190 h/ha per crop.** 219 h/10a [4] × 10 = 2,190 h/ha. The
plausible range is about 2,000-2,400 h/ha, since this is one survey year
and protected crops only. A task-level budget would replace it.

**Fertiliser per hectare.** Per 10a [2]: N 25 kg, P₂O₅ 7.7 kg, K₂O 16 kg.
Multiplying by 10 gives 250 kg N, 77 kg P₂O₅ and 160 kg K₂O per ha.

The table lists fertilisers by product. Treating its nutrient column as
oxides is our reading, checked against those products:
- urea: 54 kg × 46% N = 24.8 kg N;
- fused phosphate: 39 kg × about 20% P₂O₅ = 7.8 kg;
- potassium chloride: 27 kg × 60% K₂O = 16.2 kg.

Converted to elements, that is about 34 kg P (77 × 0.436) and 133 kg K
(160 × 0.830) per ha.

**Plant density, 7,000-11,000 per ha.** 700-1,100 per 10a [2] × 10.

**Days from sowing to first harvest, about 100-160.** From the Jeju
calendar [2], taking mid-points of each part of a month (late = 25th,
early = 5th):
- open field: late April (25 April) to early August (5 August) = 102 days;
- forcing: late October (25 October) to early April (5 April) = 162 days;
- forcing sown in mid-November (15 November) = 141 days.

The 35-38 or 27-30 days from pollination to harvest [2] fit within these
spans. Grower records by system would replace this.

**Energy, about 30-36 kcal per 100 g (weak).** Honeydew has 36 kcal at
89.8% water (`honeydew.md`). Oriental melon is about 90% water [1][7] and
less sweet [1], so it should be at or a little below honeydew. The range
is 30-36 kcal (300-360 kcal/kg). The Korean national composition table
would replace this.
