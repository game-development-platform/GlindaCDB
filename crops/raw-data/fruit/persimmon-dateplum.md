## Naming and scope

*Diospyros lotus* L. (Ebenaceae), date-plum [1][2]. Synonyms include
*Dactylus trapezuntinus* Forssk. and *Diospyros mediterranea* Oken [2].
- **Range:** Turkey, the Caucasus, Iran, Tajikistan, Afghanistan,
  Pakistan, northern India, Nepal, China and Korea [2]. PFAF gives NE and
  S-central Türkiye to Korea, introduced in dry Mediterranean regions
  (Albania, the Balearic Islands, Greece, Italy, Spain) [1].
- **Habitat:** mixed mountain forests, up to 1,500 m in China [1][2].
- **Fruit:** about the size of a large cherry, about 20 mm across, yellow
  turning blue-black when fully ripe [1].
- **Status:** IUCN Least Concern; harvested from the wild for local use
  [2]. "Sometimes cultivated for its edible fruit in Italy and E. Asia,
  there are some named varieties" [1][2].
- **Scope:** one cropID; wild, seedling-rootstock and fruiting forms are
  cultivar groups.
- **Gate check (2026-10-12, re-confirmed 2026-11-23): passes.** A
  separate species with its own fruit, used fresh, dried and as rootstock.

**Relatives in this project:**
- Asian persimmon (`persimmon`, `persimmon.md`): this species is one of
  its rootstocks [3][4][5];
- American persimmon (`persimmon_american`, `persimmon-american.md`);
- Texas persimmon (`persimmon_texas`, `persimmon-texas.md`).

**Proposed:** none.

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — a kaki rootstock with compatibility costs.**
- **Use:** in East Asia, kaki is grafted onto wild kaki stock or onto
  *D. lotus* [3]. In California, *D. lotus* and *D. kaki* are the common
  rootstocks [4][5]. Morton reports "much lower yields" on it than on
  *D. kaki* in California [3].
- **Incompatibility:** some incompatibility with 'Fuyu', 'Jiro' and 'Izu',
  and it may raise fruit drop in 'Hachiya' [4].
- **Turkish trial (snippets):** graft retention was 44.4% for 'Hana Fuyu'
  and 45.0% for 'MKÜ Harbiye' on *D. lotus*, against 70.0% for 'MKÜ
  Harbiye' on *D. kaki* [6]. *D. lotus* rootstocks were thinner and
  shorter [6].
- **Conflicting vigour (snippet):** a Korean comparison found *D. lotus*
  seedlings grew "much faster" than *D. kaki* in stem length, diameter
  and leaf number [6].
- **Other:** 'Nightingale' is semi-dwarf and precocious on *D. lotus* in
  Queensland [3].

**Finding 2 — resistant to persimmon wilt, but prone to root rot and
crown gall in Florida.**
- *D. kaki* and *D. lotus* are "immune" to *Cephalosporium diospyri* wilt;
  *D. virginiana* rootstock is susceptible [4]. Morton calls *D. lotus*
  wilt-resistant [3].
- In Florida it suffers root rot and crown gall [3].
- Texas: trees have been planted on 'Lotus' rootstock, "but their
  long-term performance is unknown" [5].

**Finding 3 — seed needs 60-70 days of cold stratification (snippets).**
- A 2018 Shiraz University study found 70 days at 4-7 °C gave the highest
  germination; GA3 at 250 mg/L gave the fastest germination [6].
- Another Iranian study on two genotypes found 60 days best, confirmed in
  a follow-up trial [6].
- A Turkish study measured 67.2% germination for *D. lotus* and 66.5% for
  *D. kaki* [6].

**Finding 4 — dioecious, but females can set seedless fruit.**
- Dioecious; "the female tree can produce seedless fruits in the absence
  of a pollinator" [1][2]. PFAF suggests unfertilised fruit are likely more
  astringent, by analogy with kaki [1].
- Seed needs both sexes [1].

**Finding 5 — the fruit is eaten when nearly overripe, frosted or dried.**
- "Exquisitely rich flavour when it is fully ripe (almost at the point of
  going bad)", "very harsh and astringent before then" [1].
- It may not ripen in a cool summer, but frost normally improves the
  flavour [1]. It can be picked in autumn, preferably after frost, and
  bletted [1].
- Dried fruit "acquires a date-like flavour" [1].
- **Composition (Turkey, snippets):** ascorbic acid 13.9 mg/100 g; total
  phenolics 130.3 and flavonoids 12.7 mg/100 g; fructose the main sugar
  (371 mg/g); no sucrose [7]. Korean fruit powder: more acid and phenols
  in immature fruit, more sugar in mature fruit [7].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** deciduous tree to 30 m with a trunk up to 130 cm
  [2]; 9 × 6 m at a medium growth rate (PFAF) [1]. A snippet of Flora of
  China text gives 2-10(-20) m [8].
- **Harvest pattern:** one crop a year; flowers in July, seeds ripen
  October-November (UK) [1].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** Not sourced.
- **Climate zones:**
  - **Hardiness:** USDA zones 7-9; "hardy to -15°C (5°F)" [1]. UK zone 5
    [1].
  - **Spring frost:** dormant plants are very hardy, but young spring
    growth is frost-tender, even on mature trees; shelter from early
    morning sun [1][2].
  - **Summer heat:** needs a warm, sheltered, sunny spot for fruit; may
    not ripen in cool summers [1].
  - **Chill:** Not sourced.
- **Composition:** Finding 5 [7]. PFAF: about 1.9% protein, 0.2% fat,
  47.7% carbohydrate and 1% ash [1]; the basis (fresh or dried) is not
  stated. Energy: Not sourced.
- **Edibility:** raw when fully ripe, cooked, dried, or made into wine and
  vinegar [1][2].
- **Pollination:** Finding 4 [1][2]. Pollinator species: Not sourced;
  flowers are rich in nectar and pollen [1].
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced.
- **Yield:** Not sourced (no orchard or per-tree figure found).
- **Water use:** Not sourced. Prefers moist soil [1]; FAO-56 has no row,
  and FAO-61 lists only *D. virginiana* (checked).
- **Propagation:** seed after cold stratification (Finding 3) [6]. Graft
  take on *D. lotus* depends on method: one Afghan study reported at least
  92% success with grafting tape, but at most 10% with mud and other
  treatments [6]. Clean stock: Not sourced.
- **Labor:** Not sourced.
- **Soil:**
  - **Texture:** light, medium and heavy soils; prefers well-drained soil
    [1]; needs "a good deep loamy soil" [1][2].
  - **pH:** mildly acid to mildly alkaline [1]; "dislikes very acid or wet
    and poorly drained soils" [1][2].
  - **Salinity:** Not sourced.
  - **Nutrients:** Not sourced.
- **Storage (postharvest):** Not sourced; bletting (Finding 5) [1].
- **Establishment:** long taproot and hard to transplant; plant out early
  and protect for the first winter or two [1][2]. Spacing: Not sourced.
- **Cultivars:** "some named varieties" exist [1][2]; none named in the
  sources read.
- **Weather response:** late spring frost on new growth (Climate zones)
  [1]; poor ripening in cool summers [1].
- **Water regime:** upland; avoids wet, poorly drained soils [1].
- **Harvest stages / windows:** fully ripe and soft, after frost or
  bletted; or picked for drying [1].
- **Soil biology:** root rot and crown gall on Florida rootstocks [3].
  Replant: Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** PFAF weed potential "No" [1];
  introduced in parts of the Mediterranean [1].
- **Controlled environment:** Not sourced.

## Hazards

- **Known hazards:** "None known" [1][2].
- **Diseases:** root rot and crown gall (Florida) [3]; immune to persimmon
  wilt [4].
- **Pests:** Not sourced.
- **Ergot / mycotoxin:** none reported this pass.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain 1 (fresh):** picked fully ripe or after frost → bletted → eaten
  raw or cooked [1].
- **Chain 2 (dried):** fruit dried, with a date-like flavour [1].
- **Chain 3 (fermented):** wine and vinegar [1][2].
- **Chain 4 (rootstock):** seed cleaned → stratified 60-70 days → sown →
  grafted with kaki [3][6].
- **Non-food:** wood "durable, pliable, resists rot", for construction and
  carpentry [1]. Fruit febrifuge; seed regarded as sedative in China [1].
- **Not detailed here:** the Japanese tannin (kakishibu) use from the old
  doc; South Asian dried-fruit trade.
