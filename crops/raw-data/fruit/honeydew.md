## Naming and scope

The non-netted, non-musky sweet melons of the *Cucumis melo* Inodorus
group, called "winter melons" even though they are grown mainly in spring
and summer [1]. Honeydew is the dominant type grown and shipped [1]. This
entry covers honeydew as the baseline, plus the other Inodorus cultivar
groups grown the same way: casaba, crenshaw (a casaba × Persian cross),
canary (Juan Canary), the Spanish piel de sapo / Santa Claus melon, and
the Chinese Hami melons [1][10][13][14]. "Honeydew" is the American name
for the French cultivar White Antibes, long grown in southern France and
Algeria [2]. In China honeydew is called Bailan melon, grown famously
around Lanzhou, Gansu, from seed said to have been given by US Vice
President Henry Wallace in the 1940s; it is sometimes called "Wallace"
melon there [2].

**Hami melon spans groups.** "Hami melon" is an umbrella term for
Xinjiang sweet melons that includes Ameri and Chandalak types as well as
Inodorus [14]. Only its Inodorus members properly belong here; the rest
are not covered by any row in this project. Not proposed as its own row,
because the sources found do not separate Hami melon's agronomy from
other melons beyond its region and its film-mulch drip system (Finding 6).
Worth revisiting if a Xinjiang production source is found.

The netted and European cantaloupes (`cantaloupe`), oriental melon
(`oriental_melon`), and the vegetable melons are separate entries. See
`cantaloupe.md` for the shared species-wide material referred to below
(sex expression, weedy wild melon, seed-health rules, Monosporascus vine
decline).

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — cut, not slipped; low-ethylene, and stored warm.**
Non-hybrid honeydews, casabas, crenshaws, and canaries do not slip from
the vine. Crews cut them with knives [1][4]. They are picked by rind
colour and feel: slightly waxy, mostly whitish with a light-green tinge,
well filled out [1]. They produce very little ethylene [1]. Cut fruit
needs no precooling and holds 3 weeks at 10 °C and 90-95% RH [1]. Chilling
injury occurs below 7 °C, with pitting and dark patchy lesions, and riper
fruit tolerates it better [1]. The handbook's chilling table gives 7-10 °C
as the lowest safe range, with reddish-tan discoloration and failure to
ripen as symptoms [8]. The sources do not agree on how warm or cold
honeydew can go:
- A county guideline says not to store below 5 °C (41 °F), that 7 °C
  (45 °F) is best for honeydew and 10 °C (50 °F) for casaba, and that
  honeydew keeps 2-3 weeks even at 18 °C (65 °F) [13].
- A classic trial found chilling symptoms after 2½ weeks at 0 or 2.5 °C
  but not at 5 °C [8].
- The Spanish odourless types, piel de sapo among them, keep 2-6 weeks at
  7-10 °C depending on cultivar [9][17].
- One Hami cultivar is reported holding 18 days at 3 °C, colder than the
  US handbook allows [14].

Its netted sibling `cantaloupe` is picked at slip, is climacteric, and is
held colder for a shorter time (see `cantaloupe.md`).

**Finding 2 — hybrid honeydews slip, and that makes a second product.**
Hybrid honeydews abscise (slip) when mature, and consumers rate full-slip
fruit higher for flavour, texture, and sweetness [1]. Full-slip fruit is
highly perishable. It must be precooled to 10-15 °C soon after harvest,
held at 7 °C and 95% RH for only 7-10 days, and never gassed with
ethylene [1]. Hydrocooling can take a 35 °C melon to 15 °C at the centre
within 20 minutes [1]. An amino-acid-chelated calcium rinse (80 mM)
extends full-slip life [1]. About half of California honeydew was grown
from hybrid seed in 2002. Open-pollinated fruit was still preferred for
export by ship, because some hybrids soften from their higher sugar
during the several weeks in transit [4].

**Finding 3 — ripened on demand with ethylene, at doses that conflict.**
Cut fruit benefits at shipping from 100 µL/L ethylene at 12.5-25 °C for
up to 24 hours [1]. A county production guideline gives 5,000 ppm for
18-24 hours, and says immature fruit will not ripen with the treatment
[13]. That is a 50-fold difference in concentration; both are kept here.
Full-slip fruit must never be gassed [1].

**Finding 4 — two picks, mostly shed-packed, and export-driven pest
control.** Honeydews are harvested in two picks. A knife crew walks ahead
of the packing machine and lays the cut fruit beside the bed [4]. Because
honeydew is heavier than cantaloupe, field-pack crews cover only nine
rows a pass instead of eleven [4]. About 40% of cut fruit is field-packed
and 60% goes by truck or gondola to a shed [4]. Shed fruit is washed in
chlorinated dump tanks a little warmer than the fruit, to stop the fruit
taking up water and decay organisms. Export fruit is then waxed with food-grade
wax [4]. Cantaloupe fields, by contrast, are picked once a day over 10-14
days [4]. Top-quality fruit is exported to Japan and Hong Kong, with
volumes depending on the yen exchange rate [4]. Export fruit must be free
of leafhopper frass, so insecticide use peaks in July with peak harvest
[4]. Fruit must never be dropped more than 60 cm. Stackable field boxes
(1.2 × 1.2 × 0.6 m) cut crushing compared with 1.8 m deep truck beds [1].

**Finding 5 — yields have risen while area has halved.** California
grew 22,000 acres in 2000, 77% of US honeydew, averaging 190 cwt per
acre [4]. In 2013 it grew 10,500 acres, 73% of the US crop, averaging
260 cwt per acre against a US average of 249 [3]. Growers call 1,000
30-lb cartons per acre an excellent yield, and one field reached 1,250
[3][4]. In metric terms that is about 21 t/ha in 2000, about 29 t/ha in
2013, and 34-42 t/ha for an excellent crop (see Estimates, below). Desert
Imperial County averaged far less, 368-777 40-lb cartons per acre in
1997-2001 [13], about 16-35 t/ha (see Estimates, below). Its budget
assumes 600 30-lb cartons [13]. Value per harvested acre rose from $2,709
(2000) to $5,764 (2013) [3][4]. In 2013 honeydew was 17% of California's
melon tonnage [3].

**Finding 6 — Hami melon: an ancient tribute crop grown under film.** Hami
melons were sent as tribute from the Western Regions to Emperor Ming of
Han in 58-76 AD, and more than 100 forms and hybrids are grown in China
[14]. Xinjiang's altitude, wide day-night temperature swing, and sunshine
are credited for their quality [14]. Hami's melon fields were about 4,000
ha in a 2018 report, and a separate paper gives about 6,333 ha. The years
differ [14]. Melons there moved fully to drip irrigation under plastic
film in 2001. Smartphone-scheduled drip is reported to have cut water
costs by 30% [14]. One town alone expects 100,000 t in a season [14].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Annual, tender, running herbaceous vine of the
  Cucurbitaceae [1]; warm-season, frost-tender. California's honeydew
  season runs August to October [2]. Imperial County plants in January
  for a May-July harvest, and in July-early August for a harvest from
  October until frost [13]. The desert fall crop runs October to late
  December [3].
- **Harvest pattern:** Single crop per planting, harvested in two picks (Finding 4) [4]. Growers stagger plantings over
  many weeks to spread harvest and market timing [4].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** Not sourced directly for honeydew.
  Honeydew and mixed melons take several days longer to develop than
  cantaloupe [3], whose phenology table totals about 112-125 days from
  planting to the end of harvest [3]. FAO-56's sweet-melon stage lengths
  total 120-160 days [6]. So about 115-160 days in total (see Estimates,
  below).
- **Climate zones:** Best in semi-arid climates [2]. Grown in California's
  Sacramento and San Joaquin valleys and its desert valleys, in Arizona
  and Texas [1][3], in Spain, and in Xinjiang and Gansu in China [2][14].
  Near the Salton Sea, early crops are planted where the sea reduces frost
  risk [13]. Chill requirement: none (annual). Elevation: Xinjiang
  production is credited partly to altitude [14]; no range found.
- **Composition:** Raw honeydew, per 100 g, from the US national food
  composition database (opened directly): water 89.8 g, energy 36 kcal,
  protein 0.54 g, fat 0.14 g, carbohydrate 9.09 g, fibre 0.8 g, potassium
  228 mg, calcium 6 mg, vitamin C 18 mg, folate 19 µg, β-carotene 30 µg
  [5]. Sugars are 8.12 g [2]. Seeds are high in polyunsaturated fatty acids
  [2]. Minimum quality is 8% soluble solids (10% in California) [1].
  Federal grades set an 8% minimum only from 1 May to 20 June [1].
- **Edibility:** Raw-edible; eaten fresh as dessert [2]. Wash and sanitise
  the rind before cutting (Hazards) [1].
- **Pollination:** Required, by honeybees. Imperial County recommends at
  least one colony per acre and 1½ as better, placed on at least two
  sides of the field [13]. The budget assumes 1.5 hives per acre [13].
  California growers use a half to one hive per acre by melon type and
  region, held about 30 days [3]. Fruit-bearing flowers open for one day.
  A well-pollinated flower gets at least 15 bee visits, and an
  under-pollinated one aborts [13]. Sex expression: see `cantaloupe.md`.
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced for honeydew; see
  `cantaloupe.md` for the species-level sex-expression evidence. Not
  applicable to tuberization (no storage organ).
- **Yield:** About 21 t/ha (California 2000) to 29 t/ha (California
  2013); an "excellent" crop is about 34 t/ha and the best field about
  42 t/ha; desert Imperial County averaged about 16-35 t/ha in 1997-2001
  (Finding 5; see Estimates, below) [3][4][13]. Imperial County says a
  field can potentially yield 1,200-1,300 cartons per acre [13], with no
  carton size stated.
- **Water use:** FAO-56 sweet melons: Kc 0.5 initial, 1.05 mid-season,
  0.75 late; height 0.4 m; rooting depth 0.8-1.5 m; depletion fraction
  0.40 [6]. Sweet-melon stage lengths (initial/development/mid/late days)
  are 25/35/40/20 for a May Mediterranean planting, 30/30/50/30 for a March
  California planting, 15/40/65/15 for an August desert planting, and
  30/45/65/20 for a December-January arid-region planting [6]. Imperial
  County's budget applies 1 acre-foot to pre-irrigate plus 3¾ acre-feet in
  five irrigations, about 1,450 mm in total (see Estimates, below) [13].
  In the Sacramento Valley, growers flood pre-irrigate and then dry-farm on
  the high water table [3][4]. Drip is used on some honeydews [4].
- **Propagation:** Seed only. Almost all honeydews are direct-seeded;
  greenhouse transplants for the early market are rare because of frost
  risk and cost [4]. Open-pollinated plantings used about 3 lb of seed per
  acre [4]. Hybrid seed is precision-planted, about 20,000 seeds per acre
  in the Imperial County budget [13] (see Estimates, below). Melons
  germinate best above about 16 °C (60 °F) soil, and in 3 days in warm
  July soil [3]. Hybrids develop faster and more vigorously than
  open-pollinated types [3]. Clonal or viral degeneration: not applicable
  (seed-grown); seed-borne virus rules are covered in `cantaloupe.md`.
- **Labor:** Imperial County's honeydew budget lists 20 hand-labour hours
  per acre before harvest: 1 for pre-irrigation, 7 for thinning, 4 for
  hand weeding, and 8 for five irrigations. That is about 49 h/ha (see
  Estimates, below) [13]. Harvest is contracted at $3.50 per 30-lb carton
  to pick, pack, haul, cool, and sell [13]; no hours are given. One
  tractor driver plants 80-100 acres a day, and a cultivator covers
  90-100 acres in a 10-hour shift [4].
- **Soil:** Heavier soils are preferred for their water-holding capacity,
  which slows vine collapse; sandy or silt loams are used for the
  earliest crops [13]. Beds are left cloddy to keep fruit off the soil
  [13]. Honeydew receives somewhat less nitrogen than cantaloupe in
  regional guidance [7]. The Imperial County budget applies 500 lb of
  11-52-0 preplant plus 80 lb N sidedressed and 40 lb N water-run per
  acre, about 196 kg N/ha in total (see Estimates, below) [13]. pH: Not
  sourced for honeydew. *Salinity:* FAO-56 rates "melons" moderately
  sensitive without a threshold [6]. A Mediterranean field trial on the
  Spanish Inodorus cultivar Tendral found thresholds of 1.73 dS/m for
  total and 1.54 dS/m for marketable yield, with total yield falling 14%
  per dS/m above it [15]. 'Honey Dew' germinated poorly or not at all in
  15,000 mg/L NaCl, but tolerated salt as a seedling, and its yield was
  unaffected by saline field conditions in one study [15]. The netted
  muskmelon figure (1.0 dS/m, 8.4% per dS/m) is in `cantaloupe.md`.
- **Storage (postharvest):** Cut fruit 10 °C and 90-95% RH for 3 weeks;
  gassed or full-slip fruit 7 °C and 95% RH for 7-10 days (Findings 1-3)
  [1]. Chilling below 7 °C [1][8], with conflicting thresholds kept
  visible in Finding 1 [8][13][14]. Respiration in mg CO₂ per kg per hour
  is 8 at 5 °C, 14 at 10 °C, 24 at 15 °C, 30 at 20 °C, and 33 at 25 °C
  [1][8]. Controlled atmosphere has limited commercial use for cut fruit
  and is unknown for full-slip fruit [1]. Relative humidity should be at
  least 90% to prevent shrivel [13].
- **Establishment:** One row per 80-inch bed [4][13]. Spring desert crops
  are planted in a trench in the bed centre under a plastic "hoopless
  tunnel." Furrow irrigation wets the seed lines, and no further water is
  needed for 40-50 days, because condensation drips back inside the
  tunnel [13]. Holes are then punched to vent it [13]. An alternative
  slant-bed ("Yuma-bed") system plants midway up the slope and thins to
  about 12 inches (30 cm) in-row [13]. The average honeydew field is about
  75 acres, so two plantings fit on a quarter-section [4].
- **Cultivars:**
  - **Green-flesh honeydews used in Imperial County:** Silver World, Honey
    Ace, Honeycomb, Saturno, Santa Fe, Morning Ice, Honeybrew [13].
  - **Specialty flesh colours:** gold, orange or pink [1].
  - **Casaba:** furrowed or wrinkled yellow rind; near-white flesh; no
    aroma beyond a hint of cucumber; about 110 days [1][11].
  - **Crenshaw (casaba × Persian):** ripe when half the dark-green rind
    yellows; salmon flesh; spicy aroma; must be packed blossom end up or
    it breaks down quickly; about 110-120 days [1][11][13].
  - **Canary:** bright yellow, oval; crisp white flesh; about 80-95 days
    [1][11].
  - **Piel de sapo / Santa Claus:** about 110 days; stores longer than
    the other types (Finding 1) [9][10][11][17].
  - **Hami melon** (Xinjiang): Yellow Honey, Red Honey, Gold Queen
    (Finding 6) [14].

  Fruit is typically 15-22 cm long and 1.8-3.6 kg [2]. US packs are 4, 5,
  6, 8 or 9 fruit to a 13.6 kg (30 lb) box [1], about 1.5-3.4 kg each (see
  Estimates, below).
- **Weather response:** Frost: tender; early crops avoid it by site
  choice near the Salton Sea and by tunnels [13]. Heat and sun: solar
  injury (sunscald, sunburn) is a grading defect, as are hail damage and
  growth cracks [1]. Wet soil near harvest raises ground spotting and
  fruit rot, so the last irrigation is a week before harvest [13]. Frost
  as a quality event: not found. Drought: no honeydew response figure
  found; see `cantaloupe.md` for melon deficit-irrigation results.
- **Water regime:** Ordinary furrow, drip, or pre-irrigation followed by
  dry-farming on a water table; no flooded requirement [3][4][13].
- **Harvest stages / windows:** Two products from one crop type: cut
  fruit at the colour stage (non-hybrid), and full-slip fruit (hybrid),
  with different handling (Findings 1-2) [1]. Two picks per planting [4].
  Spring and fall windows in the desert [3][13].
- **Soil biology:** Soil-borne diseases dominate: Pythium sudden wilt
  after fruit set, and Monosporascus vine decline, which can collapse
  fields near harvest [13]. See `cantaloupe.md` for vine decline and
  grafting. Mycorrhizae: Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** Cultivated honeydew: no invasive listing
  found in this pass. Weedy wild forms of the same species are covered in
  `cantaloupe.md`. Honeydews entering the United States must be fumigated
  with methyl bromide for external feeders (noctuid moths, thrips, and
  *Copitarsia*) [1]. Seed-health import rules for *C. melo* seed are in
  `cantaloupe.md`.
- **Controlled environment:** Plastic tunnels and film mulch are standard
  in parts of production [13][14]. No glasshouse or hydroponic honeydew
  data found.

## Hazards

- **Salmonella on pre-cut melon:** a 2018 *Salmonella* Adelaide outbreak
  from pre-cut melon, including honeydew, caused 77 illnesses in 9 states
  and 36 hospitalisations [12][16]. A 2019 *Salmonella* Carrau outbreak
  from pre-cut watermelon, honeydew, cantaloupe, and mixes from the same
  processor caused at least 93 illnesses in 9 states [16]. Mitigation:
  sanitised shed washing, rind sanitising before cutting (200 µL/L of 5.25%
  sodium hypochlorite at 5 °C, pH 6.5-7.0, for 5 minutes), a second rinse
  of the cut pieces, and cold holding [1][4]. TO BE ADDRESSED, with
  cantaloupe's equivalent material.
- **Postharvest rots:** bacterial brown spot (*Pantoea ananatis*) in the
  western US; *Alternaria alternata* and *Cladosporium cucumerinum* rots
  after chilling, cuts, or overlong storage [1]. Mould contamination of the
  edible product: none found beyond these rots.
- **Field pests and diseases:** leafhoppers (frass blocks export) [4];
  silverleaf whitefly on late-season melons [13]; mosaic viruses
  (ZYMV, WMV) [13]; Pythium sudden wilt and Monosporascus vine decline
  [13].
- **Quarantine fumigant:** methyl bromide for import fumigation is an
  ozone-depleting substance, phased out except for quarantine uses [1].
- No plant toxin or livestock hazard found.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain 1 (cut fruit → whole fresh):** knife-cut at colour stage → field
  pack (40%) or haul to shed (60%) → chlorinated warm-water dump tank →
  sort, size into 4-9 count 13.6 kg cartons → wax for export → optional
  ethylene gassing (Finding 3) → 10 °C store up to 3 weeks [1][4][13].
- **Chain 2 (full-slip hybrid → whole fresh):** slip harvest → hydrocool or
  forced-air to 10-15 °C → optional calcium rinse → 7 °C, 7-10 days → prompt
  retail display [1].
- **Chain 3 (fruit → fresh-cut):** cut fruit, or hybrid fruit at half-slip,
  with 11-12% soluble solids → rind sanitised → cubed with very sharp
  blades → rinse at 150 µL/L hypochlorite, 30 seconds → 5 °C, shelf life
  6-10 days; 5% O₂ + 5% CO₂ modified atmosphere helps [1]. The handbook's
  fresh-cut table lists 0-5 °C with 2% O₂ and 10% CO₂ as giving good results [8].
  Defect areas (ground spot, sunburn) do not shorten shelf life but lower
  eating quality [1].
- **Chain 4 (Hami → dried):** sun-dried Hami melon, noted as early as the
  Kangxi emperor's reign, eaten rehydrated [14]. No process data.
- **Not otherwise detailed here:** seed saving and F1 seed production
  follow cantaloupe practice (see `cantaloupe.md`). No canned or frozen
  product was identified.

## Open research questions

- **Ethylene dose** for ripening cut fruit: 100 µL/L [1] vs. 5,000 ppm
  [13].
- **Storage temperature floor:** chilling below 7 °C [1], 7-10 °C lowest
  safe [8], no injury at 5 °C in one trial [8], "not below 5 °C" [13], and
  18 days at 3 °C for a Hami cultivar [14]. Cultivar and maturity probably
  explain much of this, unconfirmed.
- **Days to harvest** is not sourced directly for honeydew; derived from
  cantaloupe phenology plus "several days longer" [3] and FAO-56 sweet
  melon [6].
- **Current yield:** the latest figures are 2013 [3]. National statistics
  were not opened.
- **Hami melon** production and agronomy are thin and mostly at snippet
  depth; whether it deserves a row (Naming and scope) needs a decision
  once better sources are found.
- **Labor** at harvest is contracted per carton; no hours found.
- **pH, mycorrhizae, frost-as-quality, controlled environment:** not
  sourced.

## Estimates and derivations

Conversions: 1 lb/acre = 1.1209 kg/ha; 1 acre = 0.4047 ha; 1 acre-foot
per acre = 304.8 mm.

**California yields.** 190 cwt/acre (2000) [4] = 19,000 lb × 1.1209 =
21.3 t/ha. 260 cwt/acre (2013) [3] = 29.1 t/ha; US 2013, 249 cwt [3] =
27.9 t/ha. "Excellent" 1,000 × 30 lb cartons [3][4] = 30,000 lb = 33.6
t/ha; best field 1,250 cartons [4] = 37,500 lb = 42.0 t/ha. Budget, 600 ×
30 lb [13] = 18,000 lb = 20.2 t/ha. Imperial 1997-2001, 368-777 × 40 lb
[13] = 14,720-31,080 lb = 16.5-34.8 t/ha. Overall range about 16-42 t/ha.
A current national statistics table would update it.

**California area.** 22,000 acres (2000) [4] = 8,900 ha; 10,500 acres
(2013) [3] = 4,250 ha; a 52% fall.

**Season length, about 115-160 days.** Cantaloupe phenology averages
7 + 30 + 30 + 35 + 10 = 112 days (Sacramento and San Joaquin valleys) and
7 + 45 + 30 + 35 + 8 = 125 days (desert) [3]. Honeydew takes "several
days longer" [3], so about 115-130 days. FAO-56 sweet-melon totals are
120-160 days [6]. Seed-catalogue maturity figures (80-120 days to first
harvest) [11] are consistent. A honeydew planting-date trial would
replace this.

**Applied water, about 1,450 mm.** 1 + 3.75 = 4.75 acre-feet per acre [13]
× 304.8 = 1,448 mm. This is gross furrow application including
pre-irrigation, not crop evapotranspiration.

**Seed rate.** Hybrid: 20,000 seeds/acre [13] × 2.471 = 49,400 seeds/ha.
Open-pollinated: 3 lb/acre [4] × 1.1209 = 3.4 kg/ha.

**Pre-harvest hand labour, about 49 h/ha.** 1 + 7 + 4 + 8 = 20 h/acre [13]
× 2.471 = 49.4 h/ha. Excludes tractor work and contracted harvest.

**Nitrogen, about 196 kg/ha.** 500 lb 11-52-0 = 55 lb N, plus 80 + 40 lb
N [13] = 175 lb N/acre × 1.1209 = 196 kg N/ha; P₂O₅ 260 lb/acre = 291
kg/ha. One county budget, not a recommendation.

**Fruit weight by pack size.** 13.6 kg box ÷ 9 = 1.5 kg; ÷ 4 = 3.4 kg [1].
Consistent with 1.8-3.6 kg [2].
