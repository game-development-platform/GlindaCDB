## Naming and scope

*Artocarpus sericicarpus* Jarrett (Moraceae): **peluntan**, **pedalai**,
**gumihan**, **terap/tarap bulu** ("hairy tarap") [1][2][3]. It is a large
evergreen tree of Borneo and the nearby islands, in the same genus as
marang, jackfruit, champedak and breadfruit [1][2].

This row was flagged for proposal in `marang.md` as "a hairy, bright-orange
fruit from the same areas, often confused with marang," with no farming data
read that pass [5]. It is researched here on its own gate check.

**Distinctiveness within the project's *Artocarpus* set** [5]:
- **vs marang** (`marang`, *A. odoratissimus*, the nearest look-alike and the
  proposing doc): marang's fruit is greenish-brown with soft spines and a
  strong odour; peluntan's is **bright orange and densely hairy/bristly** —
  "like a giant rambutan" — ~15 cm across, with **little of marang's smell**
  and flesh several sources call firmer, larger-segmented and better-flavoured
  [1][2][3].
- **vs the wild sister of marang** (`artocarpus_mutabilis`, *A. mutabilis*,
  pingan/tonggom-tonggom): that one is an uncultivated wild tree with smaller,
  less-sweet fruit; peluntan is, by contrast, a **market-traded** fruit [1][5].
- **vs jackfruit, champedak, breadfruit, lakoocha:** separate *Artocarpus*
  species, each its own row [5].

**Name-clash note (sourcing hazard, not a trait):** "tarap"/"terap" and
"pingan" are shared across several Bornean *Artocarpus* (marang, *A.
elasticus*, *A. sarawakensis*, *A. mutabilis*), catalogued in `marang.md`; the
qualifier **"bulu" (hairy)** is what pins "terap bulu" to this species [2][5].
Sources also warn that *A. sericicarpus*, *A. odoratissimus* and *A.
sarawakensis* are easily confused, so some records may be mixed [3].

**Gate check (2026-11-27): passes.** A validly distinct species with a distinct
fruit, a distinct vernacular identity and a documented (if local) market — it
clears the distinctiveness check on taxonomy, range, fruit morphology, farming
system and uses, unlike the sibling candidate *D. longan* var. *echinatus*
rejected earlier as a mere wild morphological form.

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — a hairy, bright-orange "giant rambutan" relative of marang, often
rated the better eater.** The ripe fruit is globular, about **15 cm across**,
with skin that turns **bright orange and is covered in soft hairs/bristles** —
the trait behind the name *tarap bulu* and the "giant rambutan" comparison
[2][3]. Inside is **white, sweet, creamy pulp** around many seeds [1][2].
Several sources rate it above marang — firmer flesh, slightly larger segments,
and **much less of marang's strong smell** — and it is repeatedly called the
tastiest of the *Artocarpus*, though that superlative is flagged
"citation needed" and should be read as popular opinion, not a measured result
[1][2]. One Malaysian source gives the countervailing note that its **pulp is
thinner than marang's**, so the "better" claim is about flavour/aroma, not
necessarily flesh quantity (see Open questions) [3].

**Finding 2 — a multi-use tree: fresh fruit, edible seeds, and timber.** The
pulp is eaten **fresh**; the **seeds are edible boiled or roasted** (eaten "like
peanuts"), as with marang [1][2]. The **wood is used for furniture, house and
boat building**, making it a timber tree as well as a fruit tree [1]. Peel and
other residues of the genus are of some processing interest, but no
peluntan-specific processing chain was sourced beyond fresh fruit and roasted
seed [2][5].

**Finding 3 — cultivated and wild-gathered, and the minor Bornean *Artocarpus*
most seen in markets — but only ever at local scale.** It is native to the wet
tropical forests of **Borneo (Sabah, Sarawak, Kalimantan), the Philippines,
Sulawesi and the Maluku islands**, at roughly **500-1,000 m** elevation
[1][2]. In Sarawak it is reportedly **the *Artocarpus* found most frequently in
the markets** of the minor species — yet the same sourcing stresses that
**none of these minor *Artocarpus* is traded beyond the local-market level**
[1]. It is grown from seed, gathered from the wild, and also kept in
conservation/collection plantings (e.g. the Rainforest Discovery Centre at
Sepilok, Sabah) [3]. No plantation, area, yield or bearing-age data specific to
the species was found — consistent with a minor, semi-domesticated forest-fruit
crop [1][2].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Perennial evergreen tree, large — reported to **30-40
  m** in forest [2]. Fruiting season not firmly sourced.
- **Harvest pattern:** Perennial bearing; a large heavy syncarp picked/gathered
  when the skin colours bright orange. Specific window: Not sourced.
- **Days/years to first bearing:** Not sourced (no first-fruiting age found; by
  analogy to marang, which bears in ~4-6 years, but not confirmed for this
  species) [5].
- **Climate zones:** Wet equatorial tropics, evergreen forest at ~500-1,000 m;
  wants warmth and high humidity (a seed vendor gives ~24-32 °C) [2][4]. No
  chill requirement; frost-tender.
- **Composition:** Not sourced (no per-100 g nutrition figures found for the
  pulp or seed).
- **Edibility:** Pulp eaten **raw/fresh**, sweet and creamy; **seeds edible
  cooked** (boiled or roasted) [1][2].
- **Pollination:** Not sourced. (*Artocarpus* are monoecious with separate
  male and female heads; no species-specific pollination detail found.)
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced; not expected to apply to a tropical
  forest tree fruit.
- **Yield:** Not sourced.
- **Water use:** Not sourced; "moderate water," full sun, high humidity are the
  only guidance found [2][4].
- **Propagation:** **By seed** — fresh seed germinates readily (a vendor gives
  ~2-5 weeks) in warm, moist conditions [4]. *Artocarpus* seed is generally
  recalcitrant (short-lived), so fresh sowing is implied; grafting/air-layering
  and any true-to-type concern were **not sourced** for this species [4][5].
- **Labor:** Not sourced.
- **Soil:** Fertile, well-drained soil; full sun [2][4]. pH, texture and
  **salinity tolerance:** Not sourced.
- **Storage (postharvest):** Not sourced. By analogy to marang the ripe fruit
  is likely very perishable, but no species-specific shelf-life was found [5].
- **Establishment:** Planted from seedlings; spacing and nursery practice: Not
  sourced [4].
- **Cultivars:** No named cultivars found; fruit quality varies between trees
  and it is grown as seedling selections [1][3].
- **Weather response:** Frost-tender wet-tropics tree; drought/wind tolerance
  not quantified [2].
- **Water regime:** Ordinary tropical rainfall; no paddy requirement [2].
- **Harvest stages / windows:** Picked/gathered on skin colour (bright orange);
  specific seasonal windows not sourced [2].
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** Native to Borneo and nearby islands; **no
  invasive listing found** in this pass. Kept in conservation collections
  (Sepilok) [3]. No cultivation/movement restriction specific to the species
  found.
- **Controlled environment:** Not sourced as a greenhouse crop; maintained as
  botanical/conservation specimens [3].

## Hazards

- No specific human-toxicity hazard was found; the fruit and cooked seeds are
  common foods [1][2]. Like other *Artocarpus*, the tree exudes **sticky latex**
  when cut, a handling nuisance rather than a food hazard [5]. **No hazards
  found in this pass** beyond the latex note.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain (fruit):** ripe hairy fruit → opened → **white sweet pulp eaten
  fresh** [1][2].
- **Chain (seed):** seeds → **boiled or roasted** → eaten like peanuts [1][2].
- **Chain (timber):** trunk → sawn timber → **furniture, house and boat
  building** [1].
- **Not otherwise detailed here:** any processed fruit products (cakes/ice
  cream as for marang were not sourced for this species); composition; the
  genus-level peel/residue adsorbent research noted under `marang.md` [5].

## Open research questions

- **Pulp quantity vs quality:** sources agree peluntan is **better-flavoured and
  less smelly** than marang, but one calls its **pulp thinner** — so whether it
  is genuinely a "better fruit" overall, or just better-tasting with less
  flesh, is unresolved [1][3].
- **Bearing age, yield, season and composition** are all unsourced; only
  genus/marang analogues exist [1][2][5].
- **Propagation beyond seed** (grafting, air-layering) and seed storage
  behaviour were not found [4][5].
- **The "tastiest *Artocarpus*" claim** is popular/anecdotal
  (citation-needed) and needs a measured sensory or Brix comparison [1][2].
- **Deeper sources not read this pass:** the PROSEA *A. sericicarpus* entry in
  full and Lim's *Edible Medicinal and Non-Medicinal Plants* chapter (2012)
  would likely supply composition, bearing age and uses [1][5].
