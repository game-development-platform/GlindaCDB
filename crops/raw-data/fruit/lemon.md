## Naming and scope

The acid lemon, *Citrus × limon* (Rutaceae), a hybrid of citron and
bitter orange [1]. Origin is uncertain; northwestern or northeastern
India has been proposed [1][3].
- **Covered here:** the commercial acid lemons, chiefly 'Eureka' and
  'Lisbon' (the main US cultivars) and the Mediterranean 'Femminello'
  types [1][3][4][8].
- **Separate entries:**
  - Meyer lemon (`meyer_lemon`, *C. × meyeri*), `meyer-lemon.md`;
  - sweet lemon (`sweet_lemon`, *C. limetta*), `sweet-lemon.md`;
  - citron (`citron`) and bitter orange (`bitter_orange`), the parents,
    in `citron.md` and `bitter-orange.md`;
  - bergamot (`bergamot`), also a mal secco host [8], `bergamot.md`;
  - Persian lime (`persian_lime`), in `lime.md`.
- **Rootstocks only:** rough lemon, Volkamer lemon and *C. macrophylla*
  (alemow) appear here only as rootstocks [4][8].

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — flowers almost all year, so it is picked several times a
year.**
- **Year-round season:** California grows lemons year round [3]. In cool
  coastal climates citrus may flower throughout the year [2].
- **Picks:** Ventura County groves are typically picked three times a
  season, with the biggest pick in winter [4].
- **USDA marketing year:** California lemons run August-July and Arizona
  September-February [5].
- **Size picking:** fruit is picked by size, and fruit picked dark
  green stores much longer than fruit picked yellow [3].
- **Maturity standard:** a minimum juice content of 28-30% by volume,
  depending on grade [3].

**Finding 2 — water stress can force an off-season crop.**
- **Forzatura (Verdelli effect):** in Italy, irrigation withheld for
  nine weeks from June took predawn leaf water potential to -2.7 MPa
  and induced an off-season bloom [2].
- **Yield:** the regime gave 30 kg per tree in each of the summer and
  winter harvests [2].
- **Limits:** too little stress gave no off-season bloom; too much
  caused leaf drop, flower abortion, winter fruit drop and poorer winter
  fruit [2].
- **General citrus thresholds:** stem water potential above -1.0 MPa
  means no stress, -1.0 to -1.5 MPa mild stress, and -2.0 to -2.5 MPa
  visible stress symptoms [2].

**Finding 3 — the longest-storing citrus, but chilling-sensitive.**
- **Storage:** 7-12 °C at 85-95% RH, for up to 6 months [3]. FAO gives
  "up to six months or more" [2].
- **Chilling injury:** do not store below 10 °C for long; 3-4 weeks at
  3-5 °C is usually tolerated [3]. Peteca pitting and membrane stain are
  reduced by storing susceptible fruit above 13 °C [3].
- **Ethylene:** production is under 0.1 µL/kg/h at 20 °C, but lemons
  are ethylene-sensitive [3]. Degreening uses 1-10 µL/L for 1-3 days at
  20-25 °C [3].
- **Respiration:** 10-12 mg CO₂/kg/h at 10 °C, 14-24 at 15 °C and 20-28
  at 20 °C [3].
- **Controlled atmosphere:** 7.5-10% O₂ and up to 10% CO₂ delays
  senescence but is rarely used because of the injury risk [3].
- **Storage improves the fruit:** proper storage improves juice content,
  flavour and colour [3].

**Finding 4 — a high-yielding, high-cost orchard.**
- **Ventura County, 2020:** 'Eureka' on *C. macrophylla* rootstock at
  20 × 14 ft (155 trees per acre) [4].
  - **Bearing:** first fruit in year 3, full production by year 8 [4].
  - **Yield at full production:** 60.3 bins of 900 lb, or 54,270 lb per
    acre (about 61 t/ha; see Estimates, below), of which 80% is packed
    for fresh market [4].
  - **Life:** groves live more than 40 years if well kept [4].
  - **Costs:** establishment $36,669 per acre; annual production cost
    $17,487 per acre; harvest is almost half of production cost [4].
  - **Returns:** -$779 per acre above total cost at $16.42 per carton
    [4].
- **United States, 2025-26:** 62,500 bearing acres; 27.62 million boxes
  on 442 boxes per acre; value $748.9 million [5]. California has
  54,000 acres at 475 boxes per acre (80-lb boxes) [5].
- **World, 2024 (lemons and limes together):** about 23.2 million t,
  led by India (3.8 million t) and Mexico (3.3 million t) [1].

**Finding 5 — mal secco is the main Mediterranean threat.**
- **Disease:** mal secco (*Plenodomus tracheiphilus*) is "the most
  destructive fungal disease of lemons" in the Mediterranean [8].
- **Spread:** it enters through wounds caused by frost, wind, hail or
  cultivation; conidia need about 40 hours of moisture at 14-28 °C
  [8].
- **Losses:** up to 100% of susceptible-cultivar trees in some Tunisian
  orchards; about 20,000 lemon trees died over 15 years after the 1956
  outbreak in Mersin, Türkiye [8].
- **Resistance:** tolerant cultivars replaced 'Femminello' in parts of
  Italy and Greece but yield less and give poorer fruit [8]. Sour
  orange, rough lemon, Volkamer lemon and alemow rootstocks are very
  susceptible; Cleopatra mandarin and trifoliate orange are resistant
  [8].
- **Status:** EPPO A2 list; an EU regulated non-quarantine pest [8].
  It has not been reported from Portugal or Morocco [8].

**Finding 6 — frost-sensitive, so frost protection is routine.**
- **Thresholds:** about 7 °C is the comfortable minimum, and trees get
  hardier as they mature [1]. UC Master Gardener guidance puts lemon
  damage at about 26 °F (-3.3 °C) [9]. Fruit is more sensitive than
  wood or leaves, and duration matters [9].
- **Ventura practice:** wind machines (one per 10 acres, propane at
  about 14 gal/hour) and 3.13 acre-inches of frost-protection water in
  November-January [4].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Evergreen perennial tree; flushes in late
  February-March with more in summer and autumn; leaves last about two
  years [2].
- **Harvest pattern:** Repeated picks over a year-round season; about
  three picks a year in Ventura County (Finding 1) [4].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** First fruit in year 3; full
  production by year 8 [4].
- **Climate zones:**
  - **Climate:** subtropical; tropical growing is less productive
    because fruit ripens fast and holds poorly [2].
  - **Cold:** see Finding 6 [1][4][9].
  - **Elevation and chill:** not sourced. Chill is not applicable to an
    evergreen citrus.
- **Composition:**
  - **Raw lemon without peel, per 100 g:** water 89.0 g; energy 29 kcal
    (121 kJ); protein 1.1 g; fat 0.3 g; carbohydrate 9.32 g; fibre 2.8 g;
    calcium 26 mg; potassium 138 mg; vitamin C 53 mg; folate 11 µg [6].
  - **Raw juice, per 100 g:** water 92.3 g; 22 kcal; carbohydrate 6.9 g;
    potassium 103 mg; vitamin C 38.7 mg [6].
  - **Citric acid:** 5-6% of juice, about 47 g/L [1].
- **Edibility:** Raw-edible (juice and zest; very sour) [1].
- **Pollination:** Most citrus is self-compatible [2]. Not required in
  practice; not sourced specifically for lemon.
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced. Not applicable to storage
  organs.
- **Yield:**
  - **Ventura County:** about 61 t/ha at full production [4].
  - **California, 2025-26:** about 43 t/ha (see Estimates, below) [5].
  - **Arizona, 2025-26:** 212 boxes per acre [5].
  - **Verdelli regime:** 30 kg per tree per harvest, twice a year [2].
- **Water use:**
  - **Crop coefficient:** a single Kc of 0.52 for lemons in Ventura,
    California [2]. FAO-56 values for citrus are 0.70 in winter and 0.65
    in summer, raised by 0.1-0.2 in humid regions [2].
  - **Seasonal use for citrus:** 820-1,200 mm in Florida, 1,080-1,500
    mm in Arizona, 1,300 mm in South Africa, 800-850 mm on the coasts of
    eastern Spain and Israel [2].
  - **Applied water, Ventura County:** 4 inches in year 1, rising to 30
    inches (762 mm) a year from year 6, applied twice a week April-
    October by micro-sprinkler [4].
- **Propagation:**
  - **Method:** budded on a rootstock; *C. macrophylla* is the Ventura
    standard [4].
  - **Rootstock trade-offs:** rough lemon, Volkamer lemon and Carrizo
    citrange are vigorous but more freeze-sensitive [9]; several of
    the common lemon rootstocks are very susceptible to mal secco [8].
  - **Clonal/viral risk:** infected planting material is the main
    long-distance pathway for mal secco [8]. Viruses were not sourced.
- **Labor:**
  - **Non-harvest hours at full production (Ventura):** 26 hours
    pruning, 7.42 hours irrigation and 3.70 hours equipment operation,
    about 37 hours per acre (about 92 hours per hectare; see Estimates,
    below) [4].
  - **Harvest:** contracted by the carton; picking and hauling cost
    $3.34 per 37.5-lb carton [4].
  - **Pruning:** 2 minutes per tree a year in years 1-2, rising to 10
    minutes from year 5 [4].
- **Soil:**
  - **Preparation:** deep-ripped 4-6 ft twice before planting [4].
    Gypsum and sulfur are used where soil and water tests call for them
    [4].
  - **Salinity:** FAO lists lemon as "sensitive" [7]. Orange, the
    nearest crop with a full row, loses no yield up to ECe 1.7 dS/m,
    10% at 2.3, 25% at 3.3, 50% at 4.8, and all yield at 8.0 [7].
  - **Nitrogen:** 14 lb N per acre in year 1, rising to 186 lb (1.2 lb
    per tree) from year 7 [4].
- **Storage (postharvest):** 7-12 °C, 85-95% RH, up to 6 months
  (Finding 3) [3].
- **Establishment:** Trees planted in February at $17 each; 2-3%
  replaced in year 2; establishment costs $11,623 per acre to year 3
  and $36,669 per acre to year 6 [4].
- **Cultivars:** 'Eureka' and 'Lisbon' in the US [3][4]; 'Femminello'
  in Italy [8]; also 'Bonnie Brae', 'Femminello St. Teresa' (Sorrento)
  and 'Yen Ben' [1].
- **Weather response:** Frost (Finding 6) [4][9]; wind and hail open
  wounds for mal secco [8]; brown rot develops in cool, wet autumn
  weather [4].
- **Water regime:** Irrigated (micro-sprinkler or low-volume emitters
  at 10 gal/hour, one per tree) [4].
- **Harvest stages / windows:** Picked by size from dark green to
  yellow; minimum juice 28-30% [3].
- **Soil biology:**
  - **Citrus nematode** (*Tylenchulus semipenetrans*) [4].
  - **Phytophthora** root rot (*P. citrophthora*, *P. parasitica*) and
    gummosis, worse on replanted citrus ground [4].
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:**
  - **Asian citrus psyllid** drives area-wide spray zones in Ventura
    County [4].
  - **Mal secco** is EPPO A2 (Finding 5) [8].
  - **Fruit flies** are the main quarantine concern for lemon fruit [3].
  - No invasiveness listing was found.
- **Controlled environment:** Not sourced.

## Hazards

- **Phototoxicity:** cold-pressed lemon peel oil contains furocoumarins,
  including bergapten. IFRA limits bergapten in finished consumer
  products, and a 2% maximum for expressed lemon oil in leave-on skin
  products is widely cited [10].
- **Mal secco:** kills trees (Finding 5) [8].
- **Postharvest decay** [3]:
  - green mould (*Penicillium digitatum*) and blue mould (*P.
    italicum*), through wounds;
  - sour rot (*Geotrichum citri-aurantii*);
  - *Alternaria citri* and stem-end rots occasionally.
  - No mycotoxin finding.
- **Field pests:** Asian citrus psyllid, citrus thrips, bud mite,
  mealybug and scales [4].

## Uses and processing

- **Chain 1 (fresh fruit):** hand-picked by size into 900-lb bins [4]
  → washed, graded, sized and packed in 37.5- or 40-lb cartons [3][4]
  → stored at 7-12 °C for up to 6 months [3].
- **Chain 2 (juice):** about 20% of the picked crop goes to juice or
  culls [4]; in 2025-26, 6.31 million of 27.62 million US boxes were
  processed [5].
- **Chain 3 (peel):** peel oil for flavouring and aromatherapy; pectin
  [1].
- **Chain 4 (preserves and drinks):** marmalade, lemon curd, limoncello,
  and salt-preserved lemons in Morocco and around the Mediterranean [1].
- **Historical:** James Lind's 1747 scurvy experiments used lemon juice
  [1].

No religious or ritual use was found in the sources consulted.

## Open research questions

- **Origin:** northwestern or northeastern India is proposed; the
  encyclopedia itself is inconsistent [1].
- **Cold thresholds:** the 7 °C "minimum" [1] and the -3.3 °C damage
  point [9] measure different things (comfort against injury).
- **Yield gap:** the Ventura cost study's 61 t/ha [4] is well above the
  California average of about 43 t/ha [5]. The study models a
  well-managed orchard.
- **Salinity:** FAO gives lemon only a "sensitive" rating; the orange
  row is used as a proxy [7].
- **Not sourced:** elevation range, viruses in planting material,
  controlled-environment growing, and lemon-specific allergy data.

## Estimates and derivations

**Ventura County full-production yield** [4]:
- 60.3 bins × 900 lb = 54,270 lb per acre;
- 54,270 × 0.4536 = 24,617 kg per acre ÷ 0.4047 = 60.8 t/ha.

**California yield, 2025-26** [5]:
- 475 boxes × 80 lb = 38,000 lb per acre;
- 38,000 × 0.4536 = 17,237 kg per acre ÷ 0.4047 = 42.6 t/ha.
- 2024-25: 498 × 80 = 39,840 lb = 44.7 t/ha.

**California output, 2025-26** [5]: 26.0 million boxes × 80 lb × 0.4536
= 943,000 t.

**Tree density** [4]: 155 trees per acre ÷ 0.4047 = 383 trees/ha; check:
20 ft × 14 ft = 6.10 m × 4.27 m = 26.0 m², and 10,000 ÷ 26.0 = 384/ha.

**Bin and carton check** [4]: 24 cartons × 37.5 lb = 900 lb, matching
the bin weight.

**Applied water** [4]: 30 inches × 25.4 = 762 mm a year from year 6;
adding 3.13 inches of frost water gives 33.13 inches = 842 mm.

**Labour** [4]: 26 + 7.42 + 3.70 = 37.1 hours per acre ÷ 0.4047 = 91.7
hours per ha, excluding contract harvest.

**Frost threshold** [9]: (26 °F - 32) × 5/9 = -3.3 °C.
