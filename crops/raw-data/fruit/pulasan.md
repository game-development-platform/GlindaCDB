## Naming and scope

*Nephelium ramboutan-ake* (Labill.) Leenh. (Sapindaceae); synonyms *N.
mutabile* Blume, *N. intermedium* Radlk., *N. philippense* Monsalud et al.
[3]. Names: pulasan (Peninsular Malaysia), meritam (Sabah, Sarawak),
kapulasan (Indonesia), bulala (Philippines, Tagalog), ngoh-khonsan
(Thailand) [3]; rambutan-kafri and rambutan paroh in Malaya [1].
- **Distinguished from rambutan** (*N. lappaceum*) by "short and stubby"
  fruit spines rather than "long filiform" ones; it "may closely resemble"
  rambutan [3]. Morton: "closely allied to the rambutan and sometimes
  confused with it" [1].
- **Scope:** one cropID. Seedless clones exist in Malaya and Thailand
  [1]; the Javanese forms are noted under Cultivars.
- **Gate check (2026-10-03, re-confirmed 2026-11-23): passes,** narrowly,
  against rambutan:
  - **Fruit:** flesh "much sweeter than that of the rambutan" and
    separating "much more easily" [1][2]; seed edible raw, with an almond
    flavour [2].
  - **Farming:** cultivation "similar to *N. lappaceum*, but in general
    the trees require less space"; "said to be less productive than the
    rambutan" [3]. Morton spaces it at 8-10 m and says it gets "little or
    no fertilizer" [1].
  - **Flowering:** needs "a definite, but not excessive, dry season", and
    does "not necessarily" flower every year [3].
  - **Climate:** "ultra-tropical", very humid sites only [1].
  - Against: same genus, same male/bisexual tree problem and same
    propagation as rambutan [1][3].

**Relatives in this project:**
- rambutan (`rambutan`, `rambutan.md`), the same genus;
- longan (`longan`, `longan.md`) and lychee (`lychee`, `lychee.md`),
  other Sapindaceae fruits.

**Proposed:** none.

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — sweet, freestone flesh and an edible seed.**
- Fruit ovoid, 5-7.5 cm long, dark- or light-red or yellow; thick leathery
  rind with blunt tubercles or fleshy spines to 1 cm [1].
- PROSEA: 4-6.5 × 2.5-5 cm, spines to 1.5 cm, "reddish, yellowish, or
  blackish" [3].
- Aril white or yellowish-white, to 1 cm thick; seed 2-3.5 cm [1].
- "Much sweeter than that of the rambutan" [1]; separates from the seed
  "much more easily than the rambutan" [2]; seed "readily edible raw ...
  somewhat like that of almonds" [2].
- PROSEA: fruits "often are slightly sweeter and larger" than rambutan [3].
- Riau, Indonesia (67 trees in 6 populations): sweet, cream-white aril
  "easily peeled from the seeds", with "high diversity among populations"
  [7].

**Finding 2 — effectively dioecious.**
- Trees bear either male or bisexual flowers [2][3][8].
- **Pollen conflict:**
  - PROSEA (genus-level, "several species"): effectively dioecious "since
    little or no viable pollen is produced in the anthers of bisexual
    flowers" [3];
  - Djuita et al. (2016): "anther of hermaphrodite pulasan flowers
    naturally never open, although their pollen is viable", a "mechanical
    barrier for self-pollination" (snippet) [8].
- Djuita survey: 67 bisexual and 14 male trees; male trees carry more
  flowers per inflorescence; bisexual flowers usually 5 stamens, male
  usually 6 (snippets) [8].
- **Pollinators:**
  - PROSEA: "by insects, mainly bees" (genus) [3];
  - Junaidi et al. (2025), four trees at Mekarsari, Bogor: the potential
    pollinators were the hoverfly *Lathyrophthalmus* sp. and the moth
    *Amata huebneri* (snippet) [8].
- **Consequence:** seedlings "may be male or female", so seed planting is
  not favoured [1]; most producers avoid seed "due to variation in gender"
  [2].

**Finding 3 — a dry spell triggers flowering, which may skip years.**
- "Bears best after a long, dry season" (Malaya) [1].
- PROSEA (minor Nephelium species): flowering "depends largely on the
  seasonality of the climate and does not necessarily occur annually";
  they "need a definite, but not excessive, dry season". Example: an
  excellent Borneo crop in February-March 1987 after a very dry August and
  a moderately dry September 1986 [3].
- The "once every 4 years" observation (most Nephelium species,
  Peninsular Malaysia) is from PROSEA Timbers, snippet only [9].
- "The fruiting season is very short, resulting in low prices, e.g.
  200-250 Rp/kg in Indonesia in the 1980s" [3].

**Finding 4 — a minor, local crop.**
- Native range: India (Assam), Burma, Indonesia, Malaysia and the
  Philippines; "often cultivated" [3]. Wild trees infrequent around Perak,
  abundant in the Philippines; long cultivated in Malaya and Thailand;
  Java plantings mainly around Bogor [1].
- Introduced to Puerto Rico (1926) and Honduras (1927, "fruiting
  moderately" by 1945); occasionally marketed in Costa Rica [1].
- USDA ARS Hilo holds five pulasan accessions (including 'Bulala' and
  'Sibibat') [6].
- PROSEA: "Commercial production of *N. ramboutan-ake* could be expanded"
  [3]. "There is no information on yield levels" [3].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** evergreen tree 10-15 m, trunk 30-40 cm [1];
  PROSEA: up to 15(-36) m [3].
- **Harvest pattern:** one short season after a dry spell; not every year
  (Finding 3) [3].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** Not sourced.
- **Climate zones:**
  - "ultra-tropical", "very humid regions between 360 and 1,150 ft"
    (110-350 m; Morton prints "in") [1][2];
  - lowland primary forest, "often on river banks but rarely in swamps"
    [3];
  - needs a definite dry season to flower (Finding 3) [3].
  - Frost and chill: Not sourced.
- **Composition (per 100 g edible portion, Philippines) [1]:** moisture
  84.54-90.87 g, protein 0.82 g, carbohydrate 12.86 g, fibre 0.14 g, fat
  0.55 g, ash 0.43-0.45 g. PROSEA and ICRAF round these to water 85 g,
  protein 0.8, fat 0.6, carbohydrate 13, fibre 0.1, ash 0.4 [3][4]. Energy
  is estimated below.
- **Edibility:** flesh raw or as jam; seeds boiled or roasted for a
  cocoa-like drink [1]; seed eaten raw [2].
- **Pollination:** insects; effectively dioecious (Finding 2) [3][8].
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced.
- **Yield:** "no information on yield levels" [3]; "less productive than
  the rambutan" [3]. National statistics: not re-found (request filed).
- **Water use:** soil "must be constantly moist" (Ochse, via Morton) [1],
  yet flowering needs a dry spell [1][3]. Kc, rooting depth: Not sourced.
- **Propagation:**
  - Budding works "if it is done in the rainy season on rootstocks already
    set out in the field", because transplanting kills many, especially in
    dry weather [1].
  - "As with the rambutan, air-layers are very short-lived" [1].
  - PROSEA: where minor species are planted, propagation is by seed,
    "although vegetative propagation may be possible" [3].
  - CTAHR's table marks pulasan with one "possible with some difficulty"
    method and one "most common" method among seed, cuttings, air layer
    and grafting; column placement was lost in text extraction [5].
  - Wikipedia: seed and grafting [2].
- **Labor:** Not sourced. "Little or no fertilizer or other cultural
  attention" [1]; "harvesting of tall trees is difficult" (genus) [3].
- **Soil:** sand or clay [3][4]; constantly moist; richer Bogor soil
  thought to improve quality [1]. pH: Not sourced.
- **Storage (postharvest):** Not sourced.
- **Establishment:** 8-10 m apart each way [1] (100-156 trees/ha; see
  Estimates).
- **Cultivars:**
  - Java: 'Seebabat' (dark red, crowded tubercles, very sweet, freestone)
    and a light-red, smaller, clingstone group (Ochse, via Morton) [1];
  - 'Asmerah Tjoplok' and 'Kapoelasan mera tjoplok' (to Honduras); a fine
    variety in Jolo [1];
  - seedless trees in Malaya and Thailand, propagated vegetatively [1];
  - USDA ARS accessions 'Bulala', 'Sibibat', PI 561023, N16-29, N16-32
    [6].
  - PROSEA breeding aims: freestone fruit, early bearing, small trees via
    rootstocks [3].
- **Weather response:** dry-spell flowering trigger; transplant deaths in
  dry weather [1][3].
- **Water regime:** rain-fed (no irrigation reported) [1][3].
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** shares corky bark with rambutan (Hazards) [10]; otherwise Not sourced.
- **Controlled environment:** Not sourced.

## Hazards

- **Hydrocyanic acid** "has been detected in the bark and leaves" (not the
  fruit) [1].
- **Disease:** powdery mildew on trees and fruit, "does not seriously
  affect their quality"; "No other diseases or pests have been reported"
  (minor Nephelium species) [3].
- **Corky bark** (*Dolabra nepheliae*): UH-CTAHR reports the disease on
  pulasan as well as rambutan and lychee, inside and outside Hawaii [10].
  This contradicts PROSEA's "no other diseases" for the minor species.
- **Ergot / mycotoxin:** none reported this pass.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain 1 (fresh):** fruit twisted open by hand → flesh eaten raw [1][2].
- **Chain 2 (jam):** flesh → jam [1].
- **Chain 3 (seed drink):** seeds → boiled or roasted → cocoa-like
  beverage [1].
- **Chain 4 (seed fat):** dried kernels → 74.9% solid white fat, melting
  at 40-42 °C; "presumably" usable for soap [1]. PROSEA Timbers: seed oil
  formerly used for lighting, fat for soap and candles (genus, snippet)
  [9].
- **Other:** light-red, hard, heavy wood "of excellent quality but rarely
  available"; leaves and roots in poultices and fever remedies [1]; rind
  as a natural dye studied for organic solar cells [2].

## Open research questions

- **Bisexual-flower pollen:** "little or no viable pollen" (PROSEA, genus)
  [3] vs. viable pollen in anthers that never open (Djuita) [8].
- **Pollinators:** "mainly bees" (genus) [3] vs. a hoverfly and a moth
  at Bogor [8].
- **Diseases:** "No other diseases or pests have been reported" (PROSEA)
  [3] vs. corky bark on pulasan (CTAHR) [10].
- **Propagation:** by seed where planted (PROSEA) [3] vs. seed not
  favoured (Morton, Wikipedia) [1][2].
- **Tree height:** 10-15 m [1] vs. up to 15(-36) m [3].
- **ICRAF sheet error:** its botanical description (tree to 24 m, fruit
  3 × 2.25 cm, stamens 5-6) matches PROSEA's *N. uncinatum* entry word for
  word, not *N. ramboutan-ake* [3][4]. Only its composition figures are
  used here.
- **Not sourced:** yield, bearing age, storage, water use, pH, labour,
  frost, photoperiod.
- **Works identified, not yet read:**
  - Malaysian DOA fruit statistics (pulasan area and production);
  - Djuita et al. (2016), *Int. J. Plant Biology* 7(1): 6149 (MDPI blocked

  - Junaidi et al. (2025), *Jurnal Biodjati* 10(2): 217-228;

## Estimates and derivations

**Energy [1]:** protein 0.82 × 4 + carbohydrate 12.86 × 4 + fat 0.55 × 9
= 3.3 + 51.4 + 5.0 = 59.7 kcal/100 g, about 600 kcal/kg. Uses Atwater
factors 4/4/9; if Morton's carbohydrate includes fibre (0.14 g), the
figure is about 0.5 kcal high.

**Spacing [1]:** 8 m square → 10,000 / 64 = 156 trees/ha; 10 m square →
10,000 / 100 = 100 trees/ha. Square layout is assumed.

**Altitude [1]:** 360-1,150 ft = 110-351 m; Morton prints "110-350 in",
a printing error for m (Wikipedia prints m) [2].

**Seed fat [1]:** 104-107.6 °F = 40-42 °C, as printed.

No other estimates are made in this pass.
