## Naming and scope

'Ponderosa' lemon, *Citrus × pyriformis* Hassk. [2]; listed by UC
Riverside as *Citrus × limon* and *C. pyriformis* (CRC 294, PI 539491, VI
409) [1]. Other names: 'Wonder', 'American Wonder' [3]; Skierniewice lemon
(*cytryna skierniewicka*) in Poland [2][4].
- **Origin:** a chance seedling, presumably of lemon, grown by George
  Bowman of Hagerstown, Maryland, about 1886-87; in nursery catalogues
  from 1900 [1][3].
- **Parentage:**
  - **Older view:** "presumed lemon X citron hybrid" [3]; "little doubt
    that Ponderosa is a hybrid between citron and lemon", since tree and
    fruit "are clearly citron in most respects" [1].
  - **Genomic view (secondary):** Wikipedia, citing Curk et al. (2016),
    says it contains only pummelo (*C. maxima*) and citron (*C. medica*)
    DNA, perhaps as an F2 hybrid, so it "cannot derive from true lemons,
    which have a mandarin orange component" [2][5]. The paper's own
    Ponderosa result was not read.
- **Not the same as** 'Yuma Ponderosa', a lemon-pummelo hybrid used as a
  rootstock [2]. 'Yuma Ponderosa' is the rootstock of the UC Riverside
  Ponderosa tree [1] and of its Meyer lemon tree (`meyer-lemon.md`).
- **Scope:** one cropID, one clone.
- **Gate check (2026-11-23): passes,** against lemon (`lemon.md`) and
  citron (`citron.md`):
  - **Taxonomy:** a citron hybrid with pummelo (genomic) or lemon (older
    view), not a lemon or a citron [1][2].
  - **Climate:** "More sensitive to cold than true lemons" [3].
  - **Farming system:** grown mainly in dooryards, as an indoor potted
    plant in temperate regions, and popular in European homes. There has
    been small-scale commercial planting since 1948 [1][3][4].
  - **Harvest:** everbearing; fruit holds on the tree for months [1][2].
  - **Fruit:** 9-11 cm wide, 30-40 or more seeds, monoembryonic [1][3].
  - **Uses:** a lemon substitute "measure for measure", mainly an
    ornamental and oddity [1][2].

**Relatives in this project:**
- lemon (`lemon`, `lemon.md`), the source of any analogy (none made in
  this pass);
- citron (`citron`, `citron.md`), the parent all sources agree on;
- Meyer lemon (`meyer_lemon`, `meyer-lemon.md`), another lemon-like
  citron hybrid.

**Proposed:** none. 'Yuma Ponderosa' is a rootstock, not a fruit crop,
and is noted above only.

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — a citron-like tree with lemon-flavoured fruit.**
- "Since both the tree and the fruit resemble citron in most respects,
  there is little doubt that Ponderosa is a hybrid between citron and
  lemon" [1].
- Leaves are large, elliptical to oblong and citron-like; branches are
  thorny [1]. Wikipedia: slow-growing, 12-24 ft (3.7-7.3 m) at maturity
  [2]. Morton: "Tree small, moderately thorny" [3].
- "While they look similar to a citron, they taste like a lemon"; "the same
  flavor and acidity" as lemon [2].

**Finding 2 — everbearing, with fruit and flowers together.**
- "Everflowering"; "Fruits mature throughout year" at Riverside [1].
- Flowers and fruit can be on the tree together, and ripe fruit "may be
  left on the tree for many months" without losing quality [2].
- Two flower types: hermaphrodite and functionally male (Polish material
  studied in a Lublin botanic garden) [4]. Their nectaries differ in size
  (7-8 mm against 5-6 mm across), but they secrete nectar the same way
  [4].
- **Flower colour conflict:** "Flowers and new growth purple-tinged" [1]
  vs. "buds and flowers white or barely tinged with red-purple" [3].

**Finding 3 — more cold-sensitive than lemon.**
- "Tree sensitive to cold" [1]; "More sensitive to cold than true lemons"
  [3]; "less cold-hardy than a true lemon" [2].
- **Snippets:** USDA zones 9-11; "cold-hardy to 30 degrees F"; an extension
  table puts lemon fruit injury at 26-27 °F (general lemon, not Ponderosa)
  [6].
- In cold areas it is grown in containers, usually grafted on dwarfing
  rootstocks [2].

**Finding 4 — a house and dooryard plant, with some early bearing.**
- "Grown for home use and as a curiosity in California and Florida and in
  small-scale commercial plantings since 1948. Rather widely cultivated as
  an indoor potted plant in temperate regions" [3].
- "Of importance primarily as an oddity and ornamental"; a tubbed patio
  plant or dooryard ornamental in California and Florida [1].
- Poland: introduced by the pomologist Szczepan Pieniążek and popular in
  homes when imported fruit was scarce [2]. "Not a commercially grown
  cultivar, but in Europe it enjoys great popularity, since it enters the
  flowering and fruiting period early" [4].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** evergreen small tree or bush; needs pruning to
  control shape as an ornamental [2].
- **Harvest pattern:** everbearing, picked through the year [1][3].
- **Days/years to first bearing:** "enters the flowering and fruiting
  period early" [4]; no figure found.
- **Climate zones:**
  - more cold-sensitive than lemon (Finding 3) [1][2][3];
  - zones 9-11 and 30 °F (snippets) [6];
  - indoor or container culture where winters are cold [2][3].
  - Chill: none reported (everflowering) [1].
- **Composition:** Not sourced. Flavour and acidity as lemon [2].
- **Edibility:** juice and peel used as lemon; "enough juice for several
  lemon pies in just one large Ponderosa lemon" [2].
- **Pollination:** hermaphrodite and functionally male flowers, a nectar
  source for honey bees (genus-level statement) [4]. Seedy (30-40 or more)
  [3]. Need for pollination: Not sourced.
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced.
- **Yield:** Not sourced. "Productive" [1].
- **Water use:** Not sourced. Containers need more water than ground
  plantings (snippet) [6].
- **Propagation:** commercially available as budwood through California's
  Citrus Clonal Protection Program [1]. Seed is monoembryonic [1], so
  seedlings will not be true to type (inference; see Estimates). Clean
  stock: CCPP budwood [1].
- **Labor:** Not sourced.
- **Soil:** Not sourced.
- **Storage (postharvest):** on-tree storage for months [2]; off-tree:
  Not sourced.
- **Establishment:** container or dooryard; dwarfing rootstocks for
  containers [2]. Spacing: Not sourced.
- **Cultivars:** one clone. The fruit is 9-11 cm wide and 9-12 cm high,
  obovate, lumpy, faintly ribbed and slightly necked. The peel is 1-1.25
  cm thick and light orange-yellow; the pulp is pale green in 10-13
  segments, juicy and acid [3]. "Fruit medium-large, obovoid", with
  lemon-yellow colour [1]. Snippet: fruit "usually weighing 2-4 pounds"
  (one nursery) [6].
- **Weather response:** cold damage (Finding 3).
- **Water regime:** Not sourced.
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** Not sourced; distributed as certified
  budwood in California [1].
- **Controlled environment:** "Rather widely cultivated as an indoor
  potted plant in temperate regions" [3]; botanic-garden material in
  Lublin, Poland [4].

## Uses and processing

- **Chain 1 (lemon substitute):** fruit picked when needed → juiced or
  zested → used "measure for measure" for lemons [2].
- **Chain 2 (ornamental):** budded on dwarfing stock → potted or tubbed →
  grown for flowers, foliage and giant fruit [1][2][3].
- **Not detailed here:** candied peel (not sourced for this clone).

## Estimates and derivations

**Fruit size [3]:** 3 1/2-4 1/8 in = 8.9-10.5 cm wide; Morton prints 9-11
cm. 3 1/2-4 3/4 in = 8.9-12.1 cm high, printed as 9-12 cm.

**Fruit weight [6]:** 2-4 lb = 0.9-1.8 kg (single nursery snippet).

**Cold [6]:** 30 °F = −1.1 °C; 26-27 °F = −3.3 to −2.8 °C (general lemon
fruit injury).

**Seedlings not true to type (inference):** monoembryonic seed [1] has no
nucellar (clonal) embryos, so every seedling is sexual. This follows from
the definition, not from a Ponderosa-specific trial.

No other estimates are made in this pass.
