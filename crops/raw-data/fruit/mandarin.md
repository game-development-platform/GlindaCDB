## Naming and scope

*Citrus reticulata* Blanco (Rutaceae) [1].
- **Names:** "tangerine" could name the whole group but in the trade is
  usually kept for red-orange types [1]; it was first used in the 19th
  century for deep orange-red mandarins [2]. Also *naranjita*
  (Philippines) and *mandarina* (Spanish America) [1].
- **Taxonomy:**
  - **Species named in trade:** *C. unshiu* (satsuma), *C. deliciosa*
    (Mediterranean or willowleaf), *C. nobilis* (king) and *C.
    reticulata* (common). Only *C. reticulata* and its hybrids matter
    economically in the US [2].
  - **Genomics:** continental mandarins are consistent with a single
    species varying through hybridization. Wild mandarins are "free of
    the introgressed pomelo (*C. maxima*) DNA found in domestic
    mandarins" [8].
- **Origin:** the wild mandarin evolved in a restricted region of South
  China and Viet Nam. It was domesticated at least twice, north and south
  of the Nanling Mountains, where wild mandarins still grow [8]. Morton
  gives south-eastern Asia and the Philippines [1].
- **Scope:** common mandarins and tangerines ('Dancy', 'Ponkan',
  'Emperor', willowleaf), clementine, and the mandarin-like hybrids:
  tangors such as 'Temple' and 'Murcott', and tangelos such as
  'Minneola' and 'Orlando' [2]. Satsuma is covered in its own doc.

**Relatives in this project:**
- satsuma (`satsuma`, `satsuma.md`): Morton's "Class III" mandarin [1];
- kunenbo (`kunenbo`, `kunenbo.md`): *C. nobilis*, the king mandarin
  group [2];
- orange (`orange`, `orange.md`): tangors are mandarin × orange, and
  clementine is willowleaf × sweet orange [2][9];
- bitter_orange (`bitter_orange`, `bitter-orange.md`): sour orange is a
  mandarin rootstock [1];
- rangpur_lime (`rangpur_lime`, researched 2026-11-27, `rangpur-lime.md`): a
  mandarin × citron hybrid and a major drought-/tristeza-tolerant citrus
  rootstock (Brazil's 'Cravo'), alongside Cleopatra mandarin [7];
- tachibana (`tachibana`, researched 2026-11-27, `tachibana.md`): Japan's
  native wild mandarin and a likely wild ancestor of *C. reticulata*, kept
  as a cultural/ornamental heritage citrus rather than a fruit crop.

**Proposed:**
- clementine (`clementine`, *Citrus × clementina*): its pollination and
  water data are distinct (Findings 2 and 5) [3][9]; since researched in
  `clementine.md`;
- tangelo (`tangelo`, *Citrus × tangelo*, researched 2026-11-27,
  `tangelo.md`): mandarin × grapefruit, with 'Minneola'/Honeybell and
  'Orlando'; split out on its distinct pollination biology (self-
  incompatible, needs interplanted pollenizers, seedless-vs-yield
  trade-off), as clementine was [2].

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — hardy trees, tender fruit that ships poorly.**
- **Tree:** "much more cold-hardy than the sweet orange, and the tree is
  more tolerant of drought" [1]. "Mandarin or tangerine trees are
  considered more cold tolerant than orange trees, and orange trees are
  considered more cold tolerant than grapefruit trees" [14].
- **What raises freeze risk:** vigorous rootstocks (rough lemon, Volkamer
  lemon, Carrizo citrange); slower Cleopatra mandarin and Swingle
  citrumelo are more cold-tolerant [14]. Heavy "on-year" crops made
  Murcott trees more susceptible, as did drought stress [14]. Young trees
  are more susceptible than mature ones [14].
- **Fruit:** "tender and readily damaged by cold" [1][8]. Fruit can
  freeze at 28 °F (-2.2 °C) held for several hours; below 20 °F (-6.7
  °C) for more than 4 hours may kill most citrus trees (snippets) [11].
  The UF/IFAS guide itself gives no threshold temperatures [14].
- **Frost protection:**
  - **California:** a bare or low orchard floor, 2.2 acre-inches of water
    on the floor, and wind machines from year 4, each covering about 10
    acres and burning 15 gallons of propane an hour [12].
  - **Florida:** microsprinklers at 2,000 gallons per acre per hour are
    the main method. Wind machines cover about 10 acres in cold pockets.
    Heaters (35-40 per acre, about 1 gallon of fuel oil an hour each) are
    no longer used [14].
- **Hardiest types:** 'Changsa' survived 4 °F (-15.6 °C) at Arlington,
  Texas [1]. Satsuma survived 12 °F (-11.1 °C) [1].
- **Commercial drawbacks:** "poor holding capacity on the tree, the peel
  is tender and therefore the fruits do not stand shipping well, and the
  tree has a tendency toward alternate bearing" [1].

**Finding 2 — pollination decides seediness, and sometimes set.**
- **Group default:** citrus are "usually self-fertile" or parthenocarpic
  [8]; most citrus flowers are self-compatible [3].
- **Clementine:** the seedless type "is self-incompatible." Cross-pollen
  makes it seedy. 'Monreal' self-pollinates and has more than 10 seeds;
  ordinary clementines have at most 10 [9]. Morton gives 3-6 seeds for
  Florida clementines [1].
- **The 2006 dispute:** California growers, Paramount Citrus among them,
  "threatened to sue local beekeepers to keep bees away from their
  crops" [9].
- **Needs cross-pollination:** 'Sunburst' is "self-infertile; needs
  cross-pollination for good fruit set" [1]. It "is self-incompatible
  and will produce poorly when planted in solid blocks" [16].
  - **Pollenizers for 'Sunburst':** 'Temple', 'Orlando', 'Nova' and
    'Minneola' are rated very good, and 'Robinson' and 'Fallglo' good.
    Pollenizer trees must be close by [16].
  - **The trade-off:** "Cross pollination increases seed numbers," to
    10-20 per fruit, and seedy fruit is less wanted fresh [16].
- **Seed counts:** 'Robinson' has 10-20 seeds, rising to an average of 22
  when back-crossed with 'Orlando' pollen, which "greatly elevates
  fruit-set" [1]. Willowleaf has 15-20 and 'Dancy' 6-20 [1].

**Finding 3 — non-climacteric, with weeks of storage, not months.**
- **Storage:** 5-8 °C at 95% RH for up to 4 weeks. Chilling injury
  (peel pitting) occurs below 5 °C [2].
- **Ethylene:** under 0.1 µL/kg/h at 20 °C [2].
- **Ambient:** washed and waxed 'Dancy' decays heavily within 2 weeks
  and totally within 4 weeks at 21 °C [1].
- **Coorg, India:** unwaxed fruit shrivels in 10 days at room
  temperature. Waxing gives 14 days, perforated polyethylene bags 21
  days, and 5 °C 31 days [1].
- **Peel disorders:** zebra-skin (from excess soil moisture before
  harvest, worsened by degreening), oleocellosis (from squeezing at
  harvest), puffiness, stem-end rind breakdown (from water loss; worst in
  late-season fruit) and granulation [2].
- **FAO comparison:** citrus storage runs "from weeks for mandarins and
  up to six months or more for lemons" [3].

**Finding 4 — the peel is a product in its own right.**
- **Chenpi:** sun-dried, aged peel. Xinhui peel is "purported to be the
  best quality" [10].
  - **Prices (late 2014):** about HK$140/kg for one-year peel and
    HK$600-800/kg at ten years. Peel stored over 20 years can reach
    nearly 24,000 RMB/kg [10].
- **Peel oil:** flavours hard candy, gelatins, ice cream, chewing gum and
  bakery goods. Oil paste is a standard soft-drink flavour, and
  deterpenated oil goes into liqueurs [1].
- **Petitgrain mandarin oil:** distilled from leaves, twigs and unripe
  fruit; produced mostly in Italy, Sicily and Algiers [1].
- **Synephrine:** 'Dancy' had more than any other citrus, 97-152 mg/L,
  in 1965 [1].

**Finding 5 — clementine water research: flowering is the sensitive
stage.**
- **Spring:** flowering and fruit set are "the most sensitive to stress
  for small citrus, such as clementines" [3].
- **Summer:** "no impact on clementine yield as long as predawn leaf
  water potential... was not reduced below -1.3 MPa" [3].
- **Autumn:** irrigation at 25% and 50% of ETc in late summer to early
  autumn cut fruit size by 11% and 25%, and increased peel creasing [3].
- **Season-long:** 55% of tree water needs cut yield by only 17% [3].
- **Slope:** the clementine crop-water function slope is "greater than
  one, suggesting that this cultivar may be more sensitive to water
  deficits than navel oranges" [3].
- **Alternate bearing:** "Some cultivars, notably Valencia and
  mandarins, have an alternate bearing cycle" [3].
  - **'Dancy':** "large crops of small fruit one year, followed by small
    crops of large fruit the following year" [15]. Morton gives ethephon
    thinning of very young fruit [1]; the UF/IFAS sheet advises thinning
    by pruning in heavy years, which brings extra flushes that make
    Alternaria harder to control [15].
  - **Ventura W. Murcott:** about 200 lb per tree in heavy years, with
    "a possible reduction of 50% or more" the next year. Alternate
    bearing shows from year 5 or 6 [13].
  - **Ventura yield series (lb/tree, years 4-12+):** 30, 88.5, 125,
    100, 175, 110, 200, 100, 210 [13].

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Evergreen tree, smaller than or equal to sweet
  orange. Old trees reach 7.5 m with a greater spread; usually thorny
  [1]. Flowers are single or a few in the leaf axils [1].
- **Harvest pattern:** Perennial; one crop a year per cultivar. US
  marketing seasons run October 1-May 15 in California and September
  15-April 30 in Florida [4]. Spanish 'Nules' clementines mature
  mid-November to mid-to-late January [9].
- **Days/years to first bearing:**
  - **San Joaquin Valley (Tango):** "Commercial yields normally begin in
    the third or fourth establishment year," or the second or third at
    close spacing. Full production comes by year 8 [12].
  - **Ventura (W. Murcott):** fruit in years 2-3 is discarded or sold at
    farmers' markets; it is marketable from year 4 [13].
  - **Productive life:** 40 years assumed in the San Joaquin study [12];
    establishment cost amortized over 36 years at Ventura [13].
  - 'Sunburst' is "early-bearing" [1].
- **Climate zones:**
  - **Climate:** subtropical; esteemed in Japan, southern China, India
    and the East Indies [1]; Mediterranean production in Spain, Morocco,
    Turkey and Egypt [8][9].
  - **Heat:** clementines have "lower heat requirements" than other
    citrus, and bear early in high-heat regions [9].
  - **Colour:** persistent heat prevents natural colour break, so Florida
    degreens early fruit [2]. 'Owari' satsuma peel often stays green
    after maturity [1].
  - **Cold:** Finding 1.
  - **Chill:** not applicable; no chill requirement sourced.
- **Composition (per 100 g):**
  - **USDA SR Legacy 169105, raw:** 53 kcal (223 kJ); water 85.2 g;
    protein 0.81 g; fat 0.31 g; carbohydrate 13.3 g; fibre 1.8 g; vitamin
    C 26.7 mg; potassium 166 mg; calcium 37 mg; phosphorus 20 mg;
    magnesium 12 mg; iron 0.15 mg; folate 16 µg; vitamin A 34 µg RAE;
    β-carotene 155 µg; β-cryptoxanthin 407 µg [6].
  - **USDA 169924, canned in light syrup:** 61 kcal; vitamin C 19.8 mg;
    potassium 78 mg [6].
  - **Morton (Central American tangerines):** moisture 82.6-90.2 g,
    calcium 25.0-46.8 mg, ascorbic acid 13.3-54.4 mg [1].
- **Edibility:** Raw-edible, peeled by hand; seeds not eaten [1][2].
- **Pollination:** Mostly not required, but cultivar-dependent (Finding
  2). Bees lower quality in seedless clementine and are needed by
  'Sunburst' and 'Robinson' [1][9].
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced. Not applicable to storage
  organs.
- **Yield:**
  - **California, 2025-26:** 438 boxes per acre on 70,000 bearing acres,
    31.0 million boxes [4]. Tango and W. Murcott alone: 943 40-lb
    cartons per acre on 35,000 acres, with 504 fruit set per tree [5].
  - **California, earlier:** 400 boxes per acre (2023-24) and 441
    (2024-25) [4].
  - **Florida:** 67 boxes per acre (2023-24), 74 (2024-25) and 107
    (2025-26) [4].
  - **San Joaquin Valley (Tango, from W. Murcott data):** 38 field bins
    of 900 lb per acre from year 7, with 70% packing out as 4,788 5-lb
    cartons [12]. Years 3-6 give 4.8, 13.4, 25.0 and 33.0 bins [12].
  - **Ventura (W. Murcott):** 135 lb per tree on average over years 5-12,
    or 918 25-lb cartons per acre, 70% of it grade One [13].
  - **Per tree:** "up to 79 kilograms" for a mature tree [8].
  - **See Estimates** for t/ha.
- **Water use:**
  - **Crop coefficient:** a single Kc of 0.44 for clementines in
    Mazagón, Spain. FAO-56 citrus values are 0.70 in winter and 0.65 in
    summer [3].
  - **Ground cover:** for drip clementines, Kc = 0.006 × ground cover (%)
    + 0.272 [3].
  - **Seasonal ETc (citrus):** 820-1,200 mm in Florida, 1,080-1,500 mm in
    Arizona, 1,300 mm in South Africa and 800-850 mm on the coasts of
    eastern Spain and Israel [3].
  - **Applied water, San Joaquin Valley:** 3.3 acre-inches in year 1,
    rising through 7.4, 11.5, 17.2 and 23.0 to 30 acre-inches (762 mm)
    at maturity, April-October, plus 2.2 acre-inches for frost [12].
  - **Applied water, Ventura:** 6 acre-inches in years 1-2, 12 in years
    3-4, 18 in years 5-6 and 24 (610 mm) from year 7, by micro-sprinkler
    [13].
  - **Drought:** trees are more drought-tolerant than sweet orange [1].
    Drought-stressed trees are more freeze-susceptible [14].
  - **Waterlogging:** excess soil moisture before harvest causes
    zebra-skin [2]. Waterlogging tolerance itself was not sourced.
- **Propagation:**
  - **Budding:** onto rootstocks. 'Emperor' goes on rough lemon or,
    better, *Poncirus trifoliata* [1]. 'Sunburst' suits sour orange,
    rough lemon, 'Carrizo' and 'Cleopatra' [1], and Swingle citrumelo
    [16].
  - **Seed:** willowleaf "reproduces true from seed" [1].
  - **Cleopatra mandarin:** "much used as a rootstock in Japan and
    Florida" [1]; the most popular rootstock for 'Dancy' [15].
  - **Clonal/viral risk:** satsuma is more resistant than sweet orange to
    psorosis, a graft-transmissible disease [1]. No other virus data
    were sourced.
- **Labor:**
  - **Harvest:** clipped by hand, to avoid peel tearing or "plugging"
    [15]. Excess squeezing causes oleocellosis [2].
  - **Picking rate:** "120 pounds (4.8 cartons) per hour" (Ventura)
    [13].
  - **Non-harvest hours, mature San Joaquin orchard:** 9.20 machine hours
    and 16.74 non-machine hours per acre. Pruning (topping, hedging, hand
    pruning) and harvest are contracted [12].
  - **Ventura:** 33.6 machine hours per acre, including 6.5 hours of
    harvest equipment time; picking, hauling and pruning are contracted
    [13].
  - **Establishment suckering and pruning (San Joaquin):** 4.42, 5.50 and
    6.00 hours per acre suckering in years 1-3; 3.14 and 6.00 hours
    pruning in years 4-5 [12].
  - **Harvest schedule:** about one-third of the orchard is picked at each
    of three harvests (San Joaquin) [12]; late January to April at
    Ventura [13].
  - **See Estimates** for hours per hectare.
- **Soil:**
  - **Texture and pH:** not sourced for mandarin. Gypsum, sulfur, lime
    and other amendments are used for infiltration and pH "based on soil
    and water tests"; the San Joaquin study injects one ton of gypsum per
    acre a year [12].
  - **Preparation:** deep ripping to 4-6 ft (San Joaquin) [12]; cross
    ripping to 36 in (Ventura) [13].
  - **Nitrogen:** 0.1 lb N per tree in year 1, rising by 0.1 lb a year to
    0.7 lb (126.7 lb per acre) from year 7 [12]. Ventura uses 415 lb of
    urea per acre [13].
  - **Salinity:** sweet orange, the nearest full FAO row, loses no yield
    up to ECe 1.7 dS/m and 50% at 4.8 dS/m [7].
  - **Chloride by rootstock:** Cleopatra and Sunki mandarins tolerate 25
    me/L in the root zone (16.6 me/L in irrigation water). Ponkan
    mandarin, sour orange and rough lemon tolerate 15 (10). Trifoliate
    orange, sweet orange and the citranges tolerate 10 (6.7) [7].
- **Storage (postharvest):** Finding 3.
  - **Respiration (mg CO2/kg/h):** 4-8 at 5 °C, 6-10 at 10 °C, 12-20 at
    15 °C and 20-30 at 20 °C [2].
  - **Packing:** 4/5-bushel cartons, sizes 56-210 fruit per carton [2].
    Standard boxes are 80 lb in California and 95 lb in Florida [4].
  - **Decay control:** TBZ or imazalil in coatings [2].
- **Establishment:**
  - **Density:** 'Ponkan' can be kept dwarf and planted at 900 per acre
    (2,224/ha) in China [1].
  - **California:** 12 × 20 ft, 181 trees per acre (San Joaquin), with
    closer new plantings for earlier crops [12]. Ventura densities range
    from 100 to 300 trees per acre; its study uses 14 × 18 ft, 170 per
    acre [13].
  - **Replanting:** about 2% of trees are replaced in year 2 [12][13].
- **Cultivars:**
  - **US popular:** 'Dancy', 'Fallglo', 'Robinson', 'Sunburst' and
    clementine; tangors 'Temple' and 'Murcott'; tangelos 'Minneola' and
    'Orlando' [2]. 'Dancy' is "no longer widely planted" in Florida,
    replaced by 'Robinson', 'Sunburst' and 'Murcott' (Honey) [15].
  - **California forecast:** Tango and W. Murcott Afourer [5].
  - **Morton's classes:** I, mandarin ('Changsa', 'Emperor', 'Oneco',
    willowleaf); II, tangerine (clementine, 'Cleopatra', 'Dancy',
    'Ponkan', 'Robinson', 'Sunburst'); III, satsuma; plus 'Kara' (an
    'Owari' × 'King' hybrid) [1].
  - **Clementine types:** seedless, clementine and 'Monreal' [9].
- **Weather response:**
  - **Heat:** severe, prolonged early-summer heat causes heavy "June
    drop" in citrus [3].
  - **Wet soil:** zebra-skin before harvest (Finding 3) [2].
  - **Cold:** Finding 1.
- **Water regime:** Irrigated in Mediterranean and arid production
  (Spanish drip clementines; California). Florida groves are
  supplementary [3][4].
- **Harvest stages / windows:**
  - **Maturity:** a minimum SSC:TA ratio and at least 50% colour break
    [2].
  - **On the tree:** holding is poor [1]. Gibberellic acid at colour
    break reduces clementine peel blemish and permits late harvest in
    Spain [1].
  - **'Sunburst':** matures by mid-November and stays acceptable through
    late December. Fruit with no colour break decays more after
    degreening, so harvest waits for partial colour change [16].
  - **Ethephon:** 'Robinson' sprayed with Ethrel colours and loosens on
    the tree, cutting coloring-room time [1].
  - **Drop control:** 2,4-D (Citrus Fix) is sprayed on Tango in mid-to-
    late October to reduce pre-harvest drop [12].
  - **Grades (Ventura):** Number One and Number Two, with seed count used
    by marketers though not in USDA standards [13].
  - **Degreening:** 1-5 µL/L ethylene at 28-29 °C and 95% RH, for 12 h
    to 3 days, with one air change an hour [2].
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:**
  - **Canker:** citrus canker (*Xanthomonas axonopodis* pv. *citri*)
    has restricted movement of Florida mandarins [2].
  - **Fruit flies:** cold treatment is approved for Caribbean fruit fly
    areas [2].
  - **Naturalization:** none was sourced.
- **Controlled environment:** Not sourced.

## Hazards

- **Drug interaction:** a 2017 study suggested clementine phytochemicals
  may act like grapefruit's. A 2019 follow-up "called these results
  into question" [9].
- **Diphenyl pads:** a historical fungistat in cartons. Washed and waxed
  'Dancy' and 'Sunburst' with 2 pads absorbed more than the 110 ppm
  federal limit within 2 weeks at 21 °C [1].
- **Pests:** chaff scale (*Parlatoria pergandii*) leaves green feeding
  marks that make 'Dancy' unmarketable [1]. San Joaquin orchards are
  treated for citrus thrips, katydids, orange worms, California red and
  citricola scale, ants and snails. Citrus nematode and phytophthora root
  rot "can be severe problems" [12]. Gophers damage roots at Ventura
  [13]. 'Sunburst' is resistant to Alternaria and very tolerant of snow
  scale [1], and resistant to scab [16].
- **Mites on 'Sunburst':** Texas citrus mite and citrus rust mite cause
  severe leaf and stem blistering and premature leaf drop; leaves show
  damage before fruit [16].
- **Citrus greening (HLB):** 'Sunburst' "is highly susceptible", and its
  popularity has declined for that reason [16].
- **Alternaria brown spot:** 'Dancy' tree and fruit are susceptible,
  with defoliation and crop loss unless each flush is protected with
  fungicide [15].
- **Diseases:**
  - **Stem-end rots** (*Diplodia natalensis*, *Phomopsis citri*): worst
    where early fruit is degreened [2].
  - **Anthracnose** (*Colletotrichum gloeosporioides*); **brown rot**
    (*Phytophthora citrophthora*); **green and blue moulds**
    (*Penicillium digitatum*, *P. italicum*) [2].
  - **Satsuma resistance:** more resistant than sweet orange to canker,
    gummosis, psorosis and melanose [1].
- **Ergot / mycotoxin:** none reported. *Penicillium* is listed only as a
  rot [2].

## Uses and processing

- **Chain 1 (fresh):** clipped → TBZ drench where needed → degreened
  (early fruit) → washed, coated with TBZ or imazalil → 4/5-bushel cartons
  → 5-8 °C [2]. California shipped 22.0 of 31.0 million boxes fresh in
  2025-26 [4].
- **Chain 2 (canned):** very small types are canned in syrup [1].
- **Chain 3 (peel oil):** expressed oil for candy, ice cream and soft
  drinks; deterpenated oil for liqueurs; petitgrain oil from leaves, twigs
  and unripe fruit; oils and tinctures for perfume and colognes [1].
- **Chain 4 (chenpi):** peel sun-dried and aged. For cooking it is soaked
  in cold water for no more than half an hour and the pith scraped off
  [10]. Used in red-bean soup, tong sui, porridge, duck, mooncakes and
  tea, and in Japanese shichimi togarashi [10].
- **Chain 5 (processed juice):** Florida processed 159,000 of 460,000
  boxes in 2025-26 [4].
- **Medicinal:** chenpi is believed in Chinese folk medicine to regulate
  qi and reduce phlegm [10].
- **Ritual and religious:** No religious use sourced. Mandarins were
  given as New Year gifts among Japanese immigrants in Canada and the US
  from the 1880s, and go into Christmas stockings in North America [8].
  This came only from the summary portion of the encyclopedia fetch.

## Open research questions

- **Harvest hours:** picking is contracted in both UC studies; only the
  Ventura picking rate (120 lb/h) is sourced, so harvest hours are
  estimated.
- **Net return:** the San Joaquin study's Table 4 gives $6,459 above
  operating costs, but its ranging table gives $6,354 at the same yield
  and price [12].
- **Soil pH and texture:** not sourced for mandarin.
- **World production:** about 50 million t in 2024, 51% Chinese, against
  37.8 million t in 2016 [8][9]. Harvested area and a world yield were
  not sourced.
- **Fruit freeze threshold:** only a general citrus figure (28 °F for
  several hours) was found, at snippet level [11]. The UF/IFAS guide
  gives none [14].
- **Clementine seeds:** 3-6 seeds (Morton, Florida) [1] against at most
  10 (encyclopedia) [9]; these are different clementine types.
- **'Sunburst' origin:** Morton says it was selected in 1967 from
  'Robinson' × 'Osceola' seedlings [1]; UF/IFAS says the cross was made
  in 1961 [16]. Both give 1979 for release.
- **'Dancy' disease and origin:** Morton calls 'Dancy' "disease-resistant"
  [1], but UF/IFAS lists Alternaria susceptibility as a main reason it
  was abandoned [15]. Origin: Morton says it was found in Col. G. L.
  Dancy's grove at Buena Vista and brought into cultivation in 1871 or
  1872 [1]; UF/IFAS says it came from a 'Moragne' seed in 1867 in Col.
  F. L. Dancy's grove at Orange Mills [15].
- **Separate entries:** clementine now has its own doc,
  `clementine.md`. Tangelo is proposed as its own crop; this doc keeps it
  as a cultivar until it is researched.
- **Cultural detail:** the New Year gift custom came only from the
  summary portion of a truncated fetch [8].

## Estimates and derivations

**California yield** [4]: 438 boxes × 80 lb = 35,040 lb/acre × 1.12085 =
39,274 kg/ha ≈ 39.3 t/ha (2025-26). 2023-24: 400 × 80 = 32,000 lb/acre =
35.9 t/ha. 2024-25: 441 × 80 = 35,280 lb = 39.5 t/ha.

**Florida yield** [4]: 67 boxes × 95 lb = 6,365 lb/acre = 7.13 t/ha
(2023-24). 2025-26: 107 × 95 = 10,165 lb = 11.4 t/ha.

**Tango and W. Murcott** [5]: 943 cartons × 40 lb = 37,720 lb/acre =
42.3 t/ha. Fruit per carton: from the 1.359-inch survey diameter, not
derivable without a weight; not estimated.

**Yield tiers** (per hectare):
- Poor: 7.1 t/ha (Florida 2023-24) [4].
- Typical: 35.9 t/ha (California 2023-24) [4].
- Good: 39.3 t/ha (California 2025-26) [4].
- Exceptional: 42.3 t/ha (California Tango and W. Murcott 2025-26) [5].
- A single-grower W. Murcott claim of 2,500 cartons/acre (112 t/ha) is
  snippet-level and not adopted [11].
- Cost-study cross-check: Ventura's 25.7 t/ha average and 40.0 t/ha
  year-12 figure, and the San Joaquin 38.3 t/ha mature figure, fall within
  the typical-to-exceptional range [12][13].

**Cost-study yields** [12][13]:
- San Joaquin mature: 38 bins × 900 lb = 34,200 lb/acre × 1.12085 =
  38,333 kg/ha ≈ 38.3 t/ha picked. Packed: 4,788 × 5 lb = 23,940 lb/acre =
  26.8 t/ha (70%). Year 3: 4.8 × 900 = 4,320 lb = 4.8 t/ha.
- San Joaquin per tree: 34,200 ÷ 181 = 189 lb = 85.7 kg.
- Ventura average (years 5-12): 22,950 lb/acre = 25.7 t/ha. Year 12+:
  35,700 lb = 40.0 t/ha. Heavy-year trees at 200 lb = 90.7 kg.

**Per-tree check** [8][13]: 79 kg × 170 trees/acre × 2.471 = 33.2 t/ha,
consistent with the typical tier.

**Density** [1][12][13]: 900/acre × 2.471 = 2,224/ha (matches Morton).
14 × 18 ft = 252 ft²; 43,560 ÷ 252 = 173/acre (the study states 170) =
427/ha. 12 × 20 ft = 240 ft²; 43,560 ÷ 240 = 181.5/acre = 448/ha. The
Ventura range of 100-300 per acre is 247-741/ha.

**Morton's temperatures** [1]: 4 °F = -15.6 °C and 12 °F = -11.1 °C, both
correct. "69º F (20.26º C)" is wrong: 69 °F = 20.6 °C.

**Snippet thresholds** [11]: 28 °F = -2.2 °C; 20 °F = -6.7 °C.

**Water** [3]:
- Kc at 50% ground cover: 0.006 × 50 + 0.272 = 0.57; at 70%, 0.69. The
  0.44 Mazagón value implies about 28% cover by the same formula
  ((0.44 - 0.272) ÷ 0.006).
- Yield response: 45% less water for 17% less yield gives a ratio of
  0.17 ÷ 0.45 = 0.38. This is season-long and low compared with the
  slope-greater-than-one crop-water function, which used full ETc
  variation.

**Fresh share** [4]: California 22.0 ÷ 31.0 = 71% fresh (2025-26);
Florida 301 ÷ 460 = 65%.

**Value** [4]: California $40.12 per box × 438 boxes = $17,573 per acre
at the packinghouse door (2025-26) = $43,420/ha.

**Labour** [12][13]:
- San Joaquin non-harvest: 9.20 + 16.74 = 25.94 h/acre ÷ 0.4047 = 64.1
  h/ha, excluding contracted pruning and harvest.
- Ventura: 33.6 h/acre = 83.0 h/ha of machine time, including 6.5 h/acre
  (16.1 h/ha) for harvest equipment; picking and pruning are contracted.
- Picking at 120 lb/h: San Joaquin 34,200 ÷ 120 = 285 h/acre = 704 h/ha;
  Ventura average 22,950 ÷ 120 = 191 h/acre = 473 h/ha. Range about
  470-700 h/ha for picking alone at 26-38 t/ha.

**Applied water** [12][13]: 30 acre-inches × 25.4 = 762 mm, plus 2.2 for
frost = 32.2 acre-inches = 818 mm (San Joaquin). 24 × 25.4 = 610 mm
(Ventura). Both sit at or below the 820-1,500 mm citrus ETc range for
hot inland regions [3].

**Frost irrigation** [14]: 2,000 gal × 3.785 L ÷ 4,047 m² = 1.87 L/m²,
or about 1.9 mm an hour.

**Cost-study returns** [12][13]:
- San Joaquin: 4,788 cartons × $3.95 = $18,913 per acre gross (2011),
  against $12,559 operating costs.
- Ventura: 643 × $25 + 275 × $12.50 = $16,075 + $3,438 = $19,513 per
  acre gross (2005).

**Chenpi price ratio** [10]: HK$600-800 ÷ 140 = 4.3-5.7 times for ten
years of ageing.

**Storage** [1][2]: a typical 21 days sits below the 28-day (4-week)
handbook maximum and the 31-day Coorg trial at 5 °C, and matches the
21-day ambient bag trial.

**World share** [8]: 51% × 50 million t ≈ 25.5 million t for China
(2024).
