## Naming and scope

*Garcinia madruno* (Kunth) Hammel (Clusiaceae, also Guttiferae), formerly
*Rheedia madruno*, a fruit tree of Central and South America [1][2]. It is one of
the former *Rheedia* fruits that `mangosteen.md` flagged as now folded into
*Garcinia*; these Neotropical species are collectively called the **"New World
mangosteens"** [2][6]. Like button mangosteen it is an instance of the loose
"mangosteen = whole genus" naming noted in `mangosteen.md` [6].

- **Names:** charichuelo / charichuela (Peru and the Amazon); **madroño** and
  madrono (Colombia and Venezuela); "bumpy lemon" in the nursery trade for the
  warty yellow fruit [1][2][3].
- **Range:** widely distributed across the Neotropics — from Costa Rica and Panama
  (one source extends it to southern Mexico) south through Colombia, Venezuela,
  the Guyanas, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia and Brazil (the Amazon) [1][5]. Recorded in
  Colombia up to about 1,200 m [5].
- **Taxonomy is unsettled — a species complex.** A 2024 Brazilian study treats *G.
  madruno* as a **species complex**, giving a morphological delimitation and
  describing three new Amazonian species split out of it [5]. So data filed under
  "*G. madruno*," especially from Amazonian material, may mix more than one
  species — a parallel to the marang / `artocarpus_mutabilis` split in this
  project [5].
- **Another New World mangosteen, not proposed this pass:** *Garcinia intermedia*
  (lemon-drop mangosteen) surfaced as a related cultivated Neotropical *Garcinia*,
  but it was not researched here; it is a candidate for its own row if a later
  pass confirms it as a distinct crop [2].

**Gate check (2026-11-26): passes.** Proposed from `mangosteen.md` as "a tropical
American *Garcinia* fruit... no farming data were read this pass" [6]. It is
distinct enough for its own row on every count, and sits in a different hemisphere
from its researched siblings:
- **Taxonomy:** a validly distinct species (indeed a species complex), *G.
  madruno* / ex-*Rheedia madruno*, not a form of true mangosteen or button
  mangosteen [1][2][5].
- **Range:** Neotropical (Central and South America) [1][5] — wholly separate from
  the Malesian range of true mangosteen and button mangosteen [6].
- **Reproduction:** dioecious, so a male and a female tree are generally both
  needed to set fruit and seed, though like some dioecious *Garcinia* it may set
  viable seed without fertilisation (Finding 2) [1][4].
- **Farming system / harvest / uses:** a cultivated and wild-gathered minor fruit
  of the New World tropics, with a warty yellow rind and juicy subacid whitish
  pulp eaten fresh and made into preserves and drinks (Finding 1) — distinct from
  both the purple thick-rinded true mangosteen and the smooth orange button
  mangosteen [1][3][6].

**Relatives in this project:** `mangosteen.md` (the proposing doc), the now-
researched `button-mangosteen.md` (another edible *Garcinia*), and the souring-
spice *Garcinia* `kokum.md` (*G. indica*) and `malabar-tamarind.md` (*G.
gummi-gutta*) [6]. The sibling `PROPOSED` row `garcinia_dulcis` (*G. dulcis*)
remains unresearched.

## Research findings

**Finding 1 — a warty yellow "New World mangosteen" eaten fresh and preserved.**
The fruit is yellow with a rough, bumpy/warty rind (hence "bumpy lemon"), holding
juicy, slightly aromatic, **subacid whitish pulp** [1][3]. It is eaten mostly
fresh, and also made into jams, preserves and drinks [1]. The tree is an evergreen
with dark, leathery leaves, roughly 6-12 m tall, a slow grower that tolerates poor
soils and heavy clay and takes about **5-7 years to first fruit** [1][3] — earlier
than true mangosteen's 10-20 years but still a long wait [6].

**Finding 2 — dioecious, but perhaps facultatively apomictic like its cousins.**
*G. madruno* is dioecious: male and female flowers are on separate trees, so an
orchard generally needs both sexes to set fruit and seed [1][4]. However, as in
other dioecious *Garcinia* (including true mangosteen), at least some plants may
produce **viable seed without fertilisation**, giving seed genetically identical
to the mother [4]. This places it between strictly sexual button mangosteen (which
needs hand-pollination, `button-mangosteen.md`) and fully apomictic, male-less
true mangosteen (`mangosteen.md` Finding 1) [4][6]. Whether charichuelo orchards
rely on apomixis or on planting both sexes was not sourced.

**Finding 3 — seed-propagated, because cuttings and layers fail.** Rooting
cuttings and air-layering are reported as ineffective for this species, leaving
seed as the practical propagation route [4]. No grafting practice was found for
*G. madruno* (contrast its Asian cousin button mangosteen, which is itself used as
a mangosteen rootstock) [6]. Seed-handling detail (is the seed recalcitrant, as in
true mangosteen?) was not sourced.

## Agronomic data

- **Life form / season:** Evergreen tree, ~6-12 m, dark leathery leaves; slow-
  growing [1][3]. Carries *Garcinia* yellow latex (used medicinally in Panama,
  Uses) [3].
- **Harvest pattern:** Perennial bearing; fruit eaten fresh. Number of seasons and
  months not sourced for this species.
- **Days/years to first bearing:** about 5-7 years, slow (Finding 1) [3].
- **Climate zones:** humid Neotropical lowland to lower montane; recorded in
  Colombia up to about 1,200 m [5]. Frost-intolerant (equatorial/tropical). Chill:
  none. Mean-temperature and rainfall bands: Not sourced to figures.
- **Composition:** Not sourced. No fruit-chemistry or nutrition analysis specific
  to *G. madruno* fruit was found (nursery "digestion/immunity" claims are
  marketing and are not relied on) [1]. The genus and this species are noted in
  phytochemical literature for guttiferones/biflavonoids, but no fruit-composition
  figures were sourced this pass.
- **Edibility:** the subacid whitish pulp is raw-edible [1]. Seed edibility not
  sourced.
- **Pollination:** dioecious; generally both sexes needed, though viable seed may
  form without fertilisation (Finding 2) [1][4]. Pollinator species not sourced.
- **Photoperiod sensitivity:** Not sourced. Not applicable to any storage organ.
- **Yield:** Not sourced. No per-tree or per-hectare figures were found.
- **Water use:** Not sourced. A wet-tropics tree.
- **Propagation:** by seed; cuttings and air-layering are ineffective (Finding 3)
  [4]. Clonal/viral degeneration: Not sourced.
- **Labor:** Not sourced.
- **Soil:** tolerates poor soils and heavy clay [1][3]. pH, texture and **salinity
  tolerance: Not sourced.**
- **Storage (postharvest):** Not sourced.
- **Establishment:** Not sourced (planting method, spacing). Both sexes generally
  needed to crop unless apomictic (Finding 2) [1][4].
- **Cultivars:** None sourced; the species-complex status (Finding, Naming) means
  even the species boundary is unsettled [5].
- **Weather response:** Not sourced to figures. Frost-intolerant; tolerant of poor
  and heavy soils [1][3]. Frost-as-a-quality-event: not applicable.
- **Water regime:** rain-fed wet-tropics tree; no flooded/paddy requirement known.
- **Harvest stages / windows:** picked when the warty rind colours yellow; window
  and successiveness not sourced [1][3].
- **Soil biology:** Not sourced.
- **Invasiveness / biosecurity:** native across the Neotropics [1][5]; no invasive
  listing was found, and as a slow, seed-propagated forest/orchard tree it has no
  weedy reputation. IUCN status not sourced for this species. Movement/quarantine
  rules: not sourced (true mangosteen is a regulated fruit-fly host, but that is
  not confirmed here) [6].
- **Controlled environment:** Not sourced; grown outdoors in the tropics and as a
  rare-fruit specimen elsewhere [3].

## Hazards

- **Ordinary fruit:** no toxicity sourced for the pulp, which is eaten raw [1].
- **Latex staining:** *Garcinia* yellow latex stains and is used medicinally in
  Panama; not separately measured here (a genus trait, cf. `mangosteen.md`) [3][6].
- **Medicinal claims:** the yellow latex is used traditionally against ulcers and
  sores in Panama, and leaf preparations are claimed for health, but clinical
  evidence is lacking [1][3]; nursery health claims are not relied on [1].
- **Ergot / mycotoxin, allergy:** none sourced.
- **TO BE ADDRESSED** — nothing specific for this crop beyond the genus latex note.

## Uses and processing

- **Chain (fresh):** warty yellow fruit picked ripe → peeled → subacid whitish
  pulp eaten fresh [1][3].
- **Chain (preserves and drinks):** pulp → jams, preserves and drinks [1].
- **Latex (medicinal):** yellow latex → traditional treatment of ulcers and sores
  in Panama [3].
- **Wood:** used for furniture and construction [3].
- **Not otherwise detailed here:** any oil, dye or tannin use; composition and
  processing literature is lacking for this species.
